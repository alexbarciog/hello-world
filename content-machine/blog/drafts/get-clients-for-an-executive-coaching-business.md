> - Executive coaching is bought in a window: a leader's first weeks in a bigger role, a founder admitting the company has outgrown how they manage, a Head of People given a budget.
> - Seven of those moments are visible on LinkedIn with a free account; the table below gives the search and the message for each.
> - Run the week-one plan by hand for an hour a day, or let Intentsly watch LinkedIn and open the conversations for you.

The fastest way to get clients for an executive coaching business is to reach a leader, or whoever sponsors coaching for them, in the week a transition is announced: a new COO in her first month, a first-time VP posting about it, a founder saying the company has outgrown how he runs it. All three post on LinkedIn, and the sponsor is often in the comments. Message them within a day with one observation about the transition, not a coaching philosophy, and you are the first coach in the conversation.

## Why does the usual executive coach advice stall?

Most pages on how to get clients for an executive coaching business list the same channels: pick a niche, ask past clients for referrals, join an association, write articles, speak at events, offer a free session, earn a credential. None of it is wrong, and most of it is written by coach-training schools and coaching software vendors. The problem is timing. A referral arrives when someone in your network happens to be sponsoring coaching this quarter, and a weekly article reaches whoever is reading that day, not whoever is deciding.

The demand is real and it is crowded. The International Coaching Federation's 2025 Global Coaching Study counts [122,974 coach practitioners worldwide and $5.34 billion in industry revenue, with 54% of coaches specialising in leadership or executive coaching](https://coachingfederation.org/blog/coaching-industry-continues-global-growth-with-5-34-billion-usd-revenue-new-research-reveals/), and most coaches expecting next year's growth to come from more clients rather than higher fees. Every one of those coaches is asking for referrals and posting articles.

What a coach usually lacks is the moment. McKinsey's review of leadership transitions puts [the share judged a failure or a disappointment two years later at between 27 and 46 percent, finds 74 percent of US leaders and 83 percent of global leaders feel unprepared for the move, and notes that tailored coaching doubles the odds of success while only 32 percent of organisations use it](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/successfully-transitioning-to-new-leadership-roles). A buying signal is a public action that marks that moment; the [trigger event lesson](/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales) explains the idea, and for coaches the triggers are unusually loud, because leaders announce their transitions on LinkedIn the day they happen.

## Where do executive coaching clients show up on LinkedIn?

Each row is a moment you can find with a free LinkedIn account. The search column is what to type into the search bar before switching to the Posts tab (or the Jobs tab, where stated) and filtering to the past week. The last column is the message, written to quote the signal and offer one piece of work, not a programme.

| Signal | Where | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A leader starting a bigger role ("I'm happy to share that I'm starting a new position as COO", "excited to join as VP of Engineering", "stepping into the CEO role") | Posts tab: `"new position" COO` or `"starting a new position" "VP of"`, past week; job-change alerts on saved leads | The first ninety days decide whether the transition sticks, and the leader knows it; a sponsor is often watching too | Congratulate, ask one question about what they inherited, and offer a 30-minute conversation on their first-quarter plan, no pitch attached |
| A first-time executive promotion ("promoted to VP", "my first executive role", "joining the leadership team") | Posts tab: `"promoted to" "VP of"` or `"first executive role"` or `"joining the leadership team"`, past week | The person now manages managers, usually without having done it before, and HR has a development budget for exactly this | Offer a scoped first-ninety-days engagement; message the person, and ask who in People or HR supports new leaders there |
| A founder posting that the company has outgrown their management ("I'm the bottleneck", "learning to let go", "we went from 15 to 60 people and I had to change how I lead", "hired our first VP") | Posts tab: `founder "the bottleneck"` or `"let go" founder "my team"` or `"first VP" founder`, past week | A self-diagnosis in public, often with a board or investor pressing for it; the founder is the buyer and the budget | Reply in the thread with one specific question about the hand-off, connect with a note that quotes the post, and offer a single session on one decision they are stuck on |
| A first Head of People, L&D or leadership development hire ("Head of People", "Leadership Development Manager", "Learning & Development Lead", "Talent Development Partner") | Jobs tab: "Head of People" or "leadership development", filtered to companies under about 500 people; check the People tab for an existing L&D team | Leadership development has just been given an owner and a budget; the hire takes months and the leaders need coaching now | Message the hiring manager (often the CEO or CHRO) and offer to coach two or three leaders until the hire lands, then hand over the material |
| A funding round that adds a leadership layer ("we raised our Series A", "closed our Series B", a hiring plan that lists VP roles) | Posts tab: `"we raised" "Series A"` or `"closed our Series B"`, past week; the [funding signal guide](/guides/find-companies-that-just-raised-funding) covers the sources | New money means new executives, first-time managers and a board that expects the founder to scale; investors often fund coaching | Congratulate, ask what the leadership team looks like in six months, and offer a founder-to-CEO or new-executive engagement scoped to the hiring plan |
| Someone asking for a coach by name ("can anyone recommend an executive coach for a new VP?", "looking for a leadership coach for our founder") | Posts tab: `recommend "executive coach"` or `"leadership coach" recommend "our"`, past week | An open evaluation with the shortlist forming in the comments the same day | Answer in the thread with one usable point about their situation, connect, and offer a chemistry call within the week |
| Leaders and People teams engaging under leadership authors', coaching platforms' and rival coaches' posts (a VP commenting "this is exactly what I'm facing", a CHRO saving a post on first-time managers) | Open the post, read the comments, filter commenters by title; watch the followers of three competing coaches | Early research: the person is thinking about the problem and has not chosen a coach | Reply to their comment with a specific observation, connect, and send a short piece of your own on the same topic before asking for anything |

Two filters keep the list honest. A transition post older than three weeks is cold: the leader has either found support, decided to tough it out, or been given a mentor internally. And a post from another coach asking "who needs a coach?" is not a buyer, nor is a manager venting about her own boss with no sponsor in the thread. Reddit and X carry the same requests, and the [Reddit method](/blog/find-b2b-leads-on-reddit) has the queries; treat them as manual channels, because Intentsly monitors LinkedIn only.

The second row is the one most coaches skip, and it is where sponsored work hides. DDI's 2025 HR Insights Report, from [2,185 HR professionals and 10,796 leaders, is headlined "Only 20% of CHROs Have Leaders Ready to Fill Critical Business Roles", finds about 49% of critical leadership roles could be filled immediately by an internal candidate, and reports 86% of HR respondents expecting a greater need to develop new leadership skills over the next five years](https://www.ddi.com/about/media/hr-insights-report-2025). A CHRO in that position has a budget line for the first-time VP who just posted about the promotion; the coach who messages both in the same week gets the engagement. ## How to get clients for an executive coaching business by hand: a week-one plan

The plan takes about an hour a day with a free LinkedIn account. Sales Navigator makes the job-change and job-post steps faster but is not required.

1. **Day 1: write the one-sentence offer and list the voices.** Pick the leader and the company, for example "first-time VPs at software companies of 50 to 500 people, sponsored by the CEO or the Head of People". Then list ten leadership authors, coaching platforms and investors your buyers follow, and five coaches who compete with you. The [ideal customer profile lesson](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) is the longer version of this step.
2. **Day 2: build the saved searches.** Create the Posts-tab searches from the table with your industry words added, each filtered to the past week, plus two Jobs searches: one for Head of People and L&D roles, one for VP roles at companies that just raised. Bookmark them. The [hiring-signals routine](/guides/track-hiring-signals-for-sales) goes deeper on the job-post signal.
3. **Days 3 to 5: thirty minutes of reading, thirty of replying.** Run the searches, read the new posts and listings, and for each real buyer do the "what to do" step from the table. Reply in public first, connect second. Send at most 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day; LinkedIn tolerates more, your acceptance rate does not.
4. **Day 6: follow up and find the sponsor.** For every accepted connection with no reply, send one message that adds something: a question about the team they inherited, a line about the hand-off they described. Never "just checking in". Where the person who posted is the executive and the budget sits with HR, the [decision-maker guide](/guides/find-decision-makers-on-linkedin) has the steps to find who signs.
5. **Day 7: count.** Signals found, replies, chemistry calls booked. Fewer than ten relevant posts and listings in a week means the industry words are wrong or your leaders are quiet on LinkedIn; widen the search terms before changing the niche.

Message template for the new executive, for copying:

```
Congratulations on the COO role. The pattern I see most in the
first quarter is that the team you inherited was built for the
last leader's style, and the hardest calls are about people, not
strategy. If it would help, I can spend 30 minutes with you on
what you walked into and where the first ninety days usually go
sideways. No programme, no proposal.
```

```message
Saw your post about hiring your first VP and stepping out of the day-to-day. What is the one decision you are still holding on to that you know should be theirs?
```

Finding the right fifteen people each day is where the hour goes; if you want that step done for you, the [LinkedIn signals feature](/features/linkedin-signals) runs the same post searches continuously and hands you the list already scored against your customer profile.

## Other ways to get executive coaching clients, and when they beat signals

Signals produce conversations with strangers. Three other routes earn their place, and for most coaches one of them comes first.

**People you have coached** beat signals once you have a few engagements behind you. A VP you coached becomes a CEO at another company in three years and brings you in for her whole team; a Head of People who sponsored you changes jobs and sponsors you again. Keep a list of every client and every sponsor, and watch their job changes, because this network compounds and a cold list never does. The cost is time: it pays in year three, not month three.

**Associations and credentials** beat signals when the buyer is a procurement-driven corporate L&D team that shortlists from a vendor list. An ICF credential, a listing in the directories HR teams search, and a presence at the association's local chapter get you onto the list. They do not pick the moment, and they put you beside every other credentialed coach, which is why they pair with the plan above instead of replacing it.

**Content and speaking** beat signals when the goal is to be the obvious choice once a leader starts looking rather than to start the conversation. A weekly post that dissects one leadership moment, a talk at a founder community, a slot on a People-leader podcast: each earns calls from leaders you would never have searched for. It is slow, and the people it reaches are mostly not in a window this month.

Cold lists are the one tactic signals replace outright. Almost every VP could use a coach in theory, which is why a scraped list of them converts so badly; a transition post or a Head of People listing says which of them has a reason this month. ## FAQ

### How do I get my first executive coaching client?
Ask the last two companies you worked for, and any leader you have already helped informally, whether one of their executives is in a transition this quarter: a new role, a first leadership team, a founder stepping back. That is where most coaches' first paid engagement comes from. Run the week-one plan in parallel, so strangers are already in conversation once the network has been asked.

### Who pays for executive coaching, the executive or the company?
Both, and the message differs. A founder or a senior executive often pays personally and decides fast. In companies with an HR or People function, the sponsor is usually the CEO, the CHRO or a Head of Leadership Development, and the executive is the user. Message the executive about the transition, then ask who supports new leaders there, so the sponsor hears about you from the person they are funding.

### How do I find executive coaching clients on LinkedIn?
Search for the moment, not the title. Use the Posts tab with phrases leaders write when a transition starts, such as "starting a new position as COO" or "hired our first VP", filtered to the past week, and the Jobs tab for Head of People and leadership development roles. Reply in public first, connect with a note that quotes the post, and offer one scoped conversation rather than a programme.

### Do I need an ICF credential to get executive coaching clients?
A credential helps most when the buyer is a corporate L&D team that shortlists from a vendor list, and it rarely decides an engagement a founder or a new VP buys for themselves. Leaders in the signals above ask whether you have been through their situation before. One transition you helped land, with the leader willing to say so, usually counts for more than the letters.

### Should I offer a free session to get executive coaching clients?
Yes, if it is scoped: a 30-minute conversation on one decision the leader is facing, ending in one thing they will do differently this week. An unscoped free session trains the buyer to expect free coaching. Offer it to a leader who has posted a signal from the table, because the session then answers a question they are already asking, not one you had to raise.

### How long does it take to build an executive coaching practice?
Weeks rather than quarters when the first client comes from a former employer or a sponsor who already knows you. Signal outreach adds strangers to that: week one should produce replies and a chemistry call or two, and a leader who announced a new role this week is usually deciding on support within a month. Count weekly, and expect a scoped engagement before a retainer.
