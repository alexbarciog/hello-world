Here is how to track job changes on LinkedIn for sales without buying a tracker: list the people whose move would matter (past champions, customers, decision makers at target accounts), connect with them so LinkedIn's free Catch Up tab and job-change notification show their moves, save the same people as Sales Navigator leads so its alert fires, run the Job Changes spotlight search weekly for strangers, sweep your CRM monthly for quiet movers, and log every hit in a scored sheet. Message the mover inside a week.

## What you need to track job changes on LinkedIn for sales

- A list of names. Everything below watches people you have named in advance, so start with your CRM's closed-won contacts and the people who championed you in deals that stalled. If "champion" is a loose word in your team, the lesson on [what a champion in sales is](/learn/what-is-a-champion-in-sales) gives a four-part test before you add someone.
- A LinkedIn account with connections to those people. The free notifications only cover first-degree connections.
- Sales Navigator, optional. Steps 3 and 4 use it. Steps 1, 2, 5 and 6 work on a free account. If you are deciding whether to pay for it, the guide on [setting up LinkedIn Sales Navigator alerts](/guides/set-up-linkedin-sales-navigator-alerts) covers the plan gate and prices from LinkedIn's own pages.
- A spreadsheet with the columns in Step 6. Time: about two hours to set up, then twenty minutes a week and one hour a month.

A job change, for this guide, is a person you care about taking a new title at a new company, or a new title with wider ownership at the same company. It is the trigger event with the shortest fuse, because a new leader reviews vendors in the first weeks and LinkedIn's own product page says decision makers are [62% more receptive to outreach](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator/how-to-use) after a move (LinkedIn's claim, no method shown). The lesson on [what a trigger event in sales is](/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales) places it next to the others.

## Step 1: Build the watch list before you touch any tool

Every method below is blind to people you have not named, so the list comes first. Pull three cohorts from your CRM into one sheet:

| Cohort | Where to find them | Why they matter when they move |
|---|---|---|
| Past champions and current customers' users | Closed-won deals, support tickets, renewal contacts | They know the product. A move is a warm intro to a new budget. |
| Lost-deal champions | Closed-lost deals where one person pushed for you | The blocker was often the company, not the person. A new company resets that. |
| Decision makers at target accounts | Your account list, the titles that sign for what you sell | Their arrival or departure changes who you sell to there. |

Aim for a list you can actually watch: 100 to 300 names for one person, more if the whole team shares the sheet. Salesmotion's guide quotes a UserGems estimate that [about 20% of CRM contacts change jobs each year](https://salesmotion.io/blog/champion-tracking-job-changes), which it uses to argue for tooling; the same number tells you that a 200-name list produces a move every week or so, which is exactly enough to handle by hand.

For each name, record company, title, LinkedIn URL, the cohort, and whether you are connected. The connection column decides which of the next two steps covers them.

## Step 2: Turn on LinkedIn's free job-change notifications

LinkedIn tells you, for free, when a first-degree connection updates their position. Two places show it.

1. **The Catch Up tab.** Open **My Network** and click **Catch up**. LinkedIn added this tab in February 2024 to surface a connection's new job, work anniversary or hiring post, with a one-tap message prompt, per [TechCrunch's report on the launch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/06/linkedins-new-feature-nudges-users-to-reach-out-to-people-in-their-network). Check it twice a week and ignore the anniversaries.
2. **The job-changes notification.** Click **Me**, then **Settings & Privacy**, then **Notifications** in the left column, then **Network catch-up updates**, then **Job changes**, and switch on **In-app notifications** (and push, if you want it on your phone). That click path is documented in [Guideflow's walkthrough](https://www.guideflow.com/tutorial/how-to-turn-on-in-app-job-changes-notifications-on-linkedin); the labels can shift with LinkedIn's redesigns, so look for the same words if a menu has moved.

Then connect with everyone on the Step 1 list you are not yet connected to, a few a day, with a one-line note that says why ("we worked together on the Northwind rollout in 2024"). Without the connection, neither surface sees them.

What this misses: anyone who is not a connection, anyone who updates their profile without posting (the feed is driven by profile edits, but LinkedIn chooses what to show you), and anyone whose move you only notice three weeks late because the tab was busy. Treat it as the alarm; the sheet in Step 6 is the record.

## Step 3: Save the list as leads in Sales Navigator so the alert fires

If you have Sales Navigator, the job-change alert is the most reliable of its alerts, but it only fires for leads you have saved. LinkedIn's how-to page is explicit: save the lead, and Sales Navigator will "send you alerts when they make a move, like changing jobs" ([LinkedIn, how to use Sales Navigator](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator/how-to-use)).

1. Create a lead list called `Watch: champions` and another called `Watch: target accounts`.
2. Search each name from Step 1, open the profile, click **Save**, and pick the list. Bulk-save from a search result page where you can.
3. Open your alert settings (profile photo, **Settings**, the email and notification section) and put **Changed jobs** on email. This is the one alert you want the same day; the alerts guide linked above covers which others to leave in the product.
4. Each week, open the alerts feed, filter by **Lead** and alert type **Changed jobs**, and copy every hit into the sheet.

Two honest limits. CatchIntent's guide says Sales Navigator's alerts can [lag the real update by one to four weeks](https://catchintent.com/blog/track-job-changes-linkedin-sales/) (its observation, not a measured figure), which matches the general pattern that LinkedIn only knows about a move once the person edits their profile. And the list is the ceiling: an alert never tells you about someone you did not save.

## Step 4: Run the Job Changes spotlight search weekly for the strangers

The alert covers the people you know. The spotlight search covers the people you do not: new decision makers who just arrived at your target accounts. LinkedIn lists the **Job Changes** filter under the **Spotlights** section of Lead filters on its [how-to-use page](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator/how-to-use), and third-party write-ups such as [KeepSync's comparison](https://www.keepsync.io/post/track-linkedin-job-changes-automatically-tools-methods-compared) call it "Changed Jobs in the Past 90 Days"; the label you see depends on the version you are on, but the filter is the same.

Open **Lead filters+** and build one saved search per segment:

| Filter | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Account lists | `Watch: target accounts` (or Current company = your top 50 names) | Keeps the result to companies you would sell to anyway |
| Seniority level | Director, VP, CXO, Owner or Partner | The arrival that changes budget, not every new hire |
| Function | The function that buys what you sell (Sales, Marketing, Operations) | Cuts the noise from unrelated departments |
| Spotlights | **Job Changes** (changed jobs in the past 90 days) | The trigger itself |
| Geography | Your territory | Optional, but it shrinks the list fast |

Save the search with alerts on and name it `New leaders at target accounts`. Run it weekly even if the alert is on, because the alert only tells you there are new results and you want to catch arrivals in their first two or three weeks, well inside the 90-day window.

A second search catches departures, which matter when the mover was your champion: open the **Account page** for each key account. LinkedIn's page says Account pages show which decision makers have "joined or left" and the hiring trend, which is the quickest way to see that a contact you relied on is gone before their profile tells you.

## Step 5: Sweep the CRM once a month for the quiet movers

Some people change their title six weeks after they start, and some never post. The monthly sweep catches them with two searches that need no alert.

**The Past company search (Sales Navigator).** This is the method Josh Asbury documented in 2023 and it still works: set **Past company** to one of your customers' names and **Current company** to exclude that same company, so the results are people who used to work there and now work somewhere else. His own run went from [over 44,000 results to 156](https://www.joshasbury.com/posts/tracking-people-who-changed-jobs-with-linkedin/) once he added a shared connection, so add a title or a connection filter before you read the list. If your CRM is Salesforce and connected, the same post describes a system list called **Past Customers at New Companies** that does this for closed-won contacts without a search.

**The Google query (free).** For each key account, two queries once a month:

```
"Acme Logistics" ("joins as" OR "appointed" OR "named" OR "promoted to") (VP OR director OR head)
site:linkedin.com/in "Acme Logistics" "new position"
```

Getcleed's guide suggests the same idea as a standing [Google Alert for "[Company] hires" or "[Company] appoints"](https://www.getcleed.com/blog/job-change-sales-trigger), which is worth setting for your top twenty accounts so the sweep comes to your inbox.

**The profile check (free, slow).** For the fifty names on the list whose move would matter most, open the profile once a month and compare the headline to the sheet. Salesmotion estimates a quarterly check of [200 to 300 contacts at two to four hours](https://salesmotion.io/blog/champion-tracking-job-changes); a monthly check of fifty is under an hour and catches the move months earlier.

This is also the step where a tool earns its place, with one caveat about which tool. [Intentsly's LinkedIn signals](/features/linkedin-signals) watch posts and engagement, so they catch the "excited to start as Head of Sales" announcement and the pain posts that follow a new role, and score each poster against your ideal customer profile; they do not keep a job-change database, so keep the saved-lead alerts and this sweep for the people who move without posting.

## Step 6: Log it, score it, and message within the week

One sheet, one row per move. Columns:

```
date_spotted | name | cohort | old_company | old_title | new_company | new_title
| source (catch-up / sn-alert / sn-search / sweep) | score | message_sent | reply | next_step
```

Score each move on four yes-or-no questions, one point each:

1. **Fit.** Is the new company inside your ideal customer profile (size, industry, the problem you solve)?
2. **Authority.** Does the new title own the budget or the workflow you sell into, or is it a lateral move or a promotion with the same scope?
3. **Relationship.** Did this person use your product or push for it, or are they a stranger from the spotlight search?
4. **Recency.** Is the move under thirty days old by the profile start date?

Four points: message today. Three: message this week. Two: add to the normal outbound list. One or zero: log it and move on. Embers' playbook uses a similar [ten-point model with a 48-hour review](https://useembers.com/blog/job-change-buying-signal/); the four-question version exists so a sheet filter can sort the week in a minute.

The message depends on the cohort. Two templates, both under 60 words so they survive on a phone:

```
Past champion, new company (score 4):
Congrats on the move to {new_company}, {first_name}. When you were at {old_company} you
pushed for {product} because {the problem it solved}. If {new_company} has the same gap,
happy to show you what changed since, or just answer questions. No rush in week one.
```

```
New decision maker at a target account (score 3):
Hi {first_name}, saw you joined {new_company} as {new_title}. Most new {function} leads we
talk to spend the first month auditing {the workflow you sell into}. If it helps, I can
share how two companies your size set it up. Either way, good luck with the first 90 days.
```

Send the first one inside a week of spotting the move. Then one follow-up on day five to seven with something useful (a comparison, a checklist, a relevant post of theirs you reacted to), and a last touch around day twenty. After that, stop until a new signal appears; the templates page on [LinkedIn messages for buying signals](/blog/linkedin-message-templates-for-buying-signals) has the follow-up variants.

## A worked example (hypothetical)

Northwind Payroll (a labelled hypothetical company) sells payroll software to UK firms with 50 to 500 staff. Dana, their only seller, exports 180 names: 60 past users from closed-won accounts, 25 champions from lost deals, and 95 finance directors at target firms. She is connected to 70 of them.

Week one. She turns on the job-changes notification, connects with 20 of the unconnected names with a one-line note, saves all 180 as leads in two Sales Navigator lists, builds the `New leaders at target firms` search (account list, Director and above, Function = Finance, Job Changes spotlight) and sets Google Alerts for her top 20 accounts. Setup takes an afternoon.

Week three, Tuesday triage (eighteen minutes). The Catch Up tab shows a former payroll manager at a customer now "Head of Finance Operations" at a 300-person logistics firm: fit yes, authority yes, relationship yes, recency yes, score 4, message sent that morning. The Changed jobs alert shows a finance director at a target firm who moved to a 2,000-person bank: fit no, score 1, logged. The spotlight search returns three new finance directors at target firms: one started two weeks ago, score 3, message sent Thursday with a two-page comparison; two started ten weeks ago, score 2, added to the normal sequence.

Month one sweep (fifty minutes). The Past company search on her largest customer finds a former user now "Finance Director" at an in-ICP firm who never posted about the move; score 4, and the message mentions the rollout they ran together. The numbers in this example are illustrative, not measured.

## Where this goes wrong

1. **Congratulating and pitching in the same message.** The first touch on a day-one mover is a short note. Say why you are writing, offer one useful thing, and leave the ask for the follow-up. A new leader's inbox in week one is full of vendors who did not wait.
2. **Scoring every move the same.** A lateral move, a promotion with the same scope, and a contractor-to-permanent profile edit all trigger the alert and none of them resets a budget. The authority question in Step 6 exists to stop you messaging them like a new decision maker.
3. **Watching the alert and skipping the sweep.** Alerts only know what you saved, and they only fire once the profile is edited. Teams that rely on them alone find the champion who moved in March in September, when their new company already bought. The monthly sweep is the part that feels optional and is not.

## What to do with the list

Treat the sheet as a queue. Every Monday, sort by score and date, work the fours and threes first, and move anything older than ninety days without a reply into the normal outbound list, where it is a warmer name than a cold one. Keep the source column honest, because after two months it tells you which of the four methods finds the moves that turn into meetings, and you can drop the one that never does.

Pair the job-change rows with what the same people post. A mover who writes about the process they inherited is a better first message than a mover who stays quiet, and that is the part a signal tool can read for you. [Try Intentsly's AI](/try-ai) with no account to see which people in your market are posting about the problem you solve right now, and run this guide's alerts alongside it for the ones who moved without saying a word.

## FAQ

### Does LinkedIn notify you when a connection changes jobs?

Yes, for first-degree connections. The Catch Up tab under My Network lists new jobs, and the Notifications settings have a "Job changes" switch under "Network catch-up updates" that puts the same updates in your notification feed or on your phone. Both depend on the person editing their profile, and LinkedIn decides which updates it shows, so pair them with a monthly check of the names that matter most.

### Can you track job changes on LinkedIn without Sales Navigator?

Yes, if you are connected to the people and the list is small enough to check by hand. The free notification covers connections, a monthly Google query covers announcements at key accounts, and a profile check covers the fifty names you cannot afford to miss. Sales Navigator adds the saved-lead alert and the Job Changes spotlight for strangers at target accounts, which is where a free account runs out.

### How soon after a job change should you reach out?

Within the first week of spotting it, with a short note and no pitch, then a useful follow-up a week later and a last touch around day twenty. Most pages on this topic treat ninety days as the window in which a new leader reviews vendors, and the early touches get the replies. After ninety days, treat the person as a warm name on the normal outbound list.

### How do I track past customers who moved to new companies?

Save them as leads in Sales Navigator so the Changed jobs alert fires, and run the Past company search monthly with the customer's name in Past company and excluded from Current company. If your Salesforce is connected, the Past Customers at New Companies list does this for closed-won contacts. On a free account, connect with them and rely on the Catch Up tab plus a monthly profile check.

### Does the Sales Navigator Job Changes filter show every move?

No. It returns people who changed jobs in the past ninety days within whatever other filters you set, and it only knows about a move once the person updates their profile. Someone who starts in January and edits their headline in March appears in March. That delay is why the guide keeps a monthly sweep and a profile check next to the filter instead of trusting one source.
