# Brief: how to warm up a linkedin account for outreach

Section: guides. Type: guide. Cluster: outbound. Researched 2026-10-29.

## Intent
Procedural. The searcher has a new, dormant or second LinkedIn account and wants a schedule they
can start today that gets the account to a working outreach volume without a restriction. The
top results are all week-by-week ramps (4 weeks is the norm, Closely says 4 to 6), so the page
must be a calendar with daily numbers, plus the rules for when to move up or hold.

## What every page covers (table stakes)
Finish the profile first; week 1 is engagement only; requests ramp from about 5 a day to 20 to
30 a day over four weeks; withdraw old pending invitations; do not automate early; one device and
location; watch acceptance rate and the "too many invitations" warning.

## What no page covers (the gap)
- Nobody says who to engage with. Every page says "like and comment"; none says to spend the
  warm-up on the exact people and competitor posts you will later message, so the four weeks
  produce a target list and a signal log, not just an aged account.
- Nobody gives a hold rule that does not depend on a percentage they cannot source. Thresholds
  range from 20% to 70% across the pages, often inside the same article.
- Every page is a vendor pitch with its own tool at the end-state, and none states its own caps.
- No page has a worked example or a daily routine in minutes.
- No page ties the warm-up to the message you send in week 3 (the signal you saw while warming up).

## Unlike the pages ranking now, this one ...
... turns the four warm-up weeks into target-list building: every like, comment and follow goes to
the people and competitor posts you will message later, so you finish with a logged list of
engaged prospects; it gives one copy-paste calendar with a hold-or-advance rule based on counts
you can see (accepted out of sent, warnings), not an unsourced percentage; it names the caps

accounts per plan) instead of promising unlimited volume; it includes a labelled hypothetical
worked example, a 20-minute daily routine, and only community-reported limits, each linked to
the page opened today and labelled as such.
