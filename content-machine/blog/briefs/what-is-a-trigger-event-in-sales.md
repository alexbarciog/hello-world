# Brief: what is a trigger event in sales (learn, lesson)

Date: 2026-10-27. Searched "what is a trigger event in sales", "sales trigger events examples list",
"trigger event selling research reply rate study".

## Who ranks (opened today)
- HubSpot, "29 Types of Trigger Events and How to Track Them" (updated 2025-10-08, first published 2017):
  definition "any occurrence that creates an opening", 29 numbered examples, tracking via earnings calls, SEC
  filings, LinkedIn and Google alerts, newsletters, social listening. No statistics. HubSpot product plugs.
- Crunchbase blog (updated 2025-11-12): "an occurrence that leads to a sales opportunity", examples grouped as
  business events, company changes, broader events; 10 tracking methods ending in Crunchbase Pro. No stats.
- UserGems (updated 2026-01-26): 23 examples each with action and tracking note; ends in UserGems pitch. No stats.
- Salesforge (updated 2026-08-10): separates triggers from buying signals and intent data, "decay window",
  24 to 48 hour response window with no source; recommends Leadsforge/Salesforge throughout.
- Autobound guide (2026): 60 signals in six categories, urgency tiers, many statistics that are self-reported
  or carry no source (70%+, 3-5x, 40-60%, 45 days, 60-70%).
- Komo glossary: compact definition, repeats the 74% vs 16% "Forrester 2012 via Craig Elias" figure and
  vendor reply-rate claims, itself notes they are directional.
- Craig Elias, CustomerThink 2008: origin of the three types (Bad Experience, Transition, Awareness) and
  the "Window of Dissatisfaction". The 74% close-rate figure traces to a 2013 InsideSales article quoting
  Elias quoting Forrester; no primary Forrester document is reachable online.

## Intent
Definition plus examples. Format of the top results: definition paragraph, then a long list of event types,
then tracking tools, then the vendor. Searcher is new to the term, wants to know what counts and what to
do with one.

## Table stakes
Definition; the common examples (new executive, funding, M&A, hiring, relocation, new product, lost
customer, new legislation); how to spot them (alerts, LinkedIn, filings).

## What no page covers
- No page says what a trigger event does NOT tell you (which person, whether anyone is acting yet).
- Statistics are unsourced vendor claims or a 2012 figure nobody can open; no page says so.
- Lists of 23 to 60 event types assume a paid database; none says which events a 1 to 200 person company
  can actually see without one, or which to drop.
- No decision rule for choosing the two or three triggers to track.
- Every page ends in its own product.
