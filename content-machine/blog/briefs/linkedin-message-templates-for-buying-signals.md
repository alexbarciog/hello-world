# Brief: linkedin message templates for buying signals (blog, templates, 2026-10-24)

## Intent
Copy-paste. The searcher has spotted (or is about to track) a buying signal on LinkedIn and wants the message
to send for it, today. Top results are vendor template dumps (Valley "10 templates that fit the limits",
Coffee "25 templates" that are actually 14, Hublead 4 openers) and signal guides without templates (Valley
"signal-based outbound" guide, Salesforge "signal based linkedin outreach", Expandi trigger-based outreach,
RevBoss trigger playbook). Format to match: a library of templates, one per signal, with limits and a FAQ.

## What every page covers (table stakes)
- Reference the specific thing the person did; one question; no pitch in the first message.
- Connection note vs message vs InMail; note limit 200 characters (300 on paid, per several vendor pages).
- Two follow-ups, then stop. Act within a day or two of the signal.
- Placeholders in braces.

## What no page covers (the gap)
- Nobody pairs the template with the full sequence per signal. Valley's 10 templates are grouped by stage
  (connection, opener, follow-up), not by signal; Coffee's are grouped by intent (sales, recruiting,
  partnership). Salesforge gives five openers tied to signals, with no follow-ups. Expandi's triggers are
  mostly actions on YOUR content (profile view, like on your post, webinar), not buying signals.
- Nobody says when a signal is too weak to message (a like, a page follow, a funding round with no link to
  what you sell) or what the message should do in that case (connect without a note, wait for a second signal).
- Nobody gives a "do not send if" rule per signal (the misread: a job-change post that is actually a
  farewell post; a competitor-post comment that is the competitor's employee; a tool request already answered).
- Numbers are vendor claims without data pages (Valley 15-45% vs 2%; RevBoss "7x", "400%"; Tyler Willis
  18% from a self-reported Indie Hackers post, no sample size). The two primary sources found and opened:
  Expandi benchmarks (13,218,869 connection requests, 6,730,447 messages, 13,302 accounts, May 2025 to April
  2026: 28.5% acceptance, 3.0% connection-note reply, 10.4% message reply) and LinkedIn's own InMail data
  (recruiter InMails, May 2021 to April 2022: under 400 characters 22% above average response, over 1,200
  11% below, individually sent 15% higher than bulk, Saturday 8% fewer responses).
- No page prints the character count next to each template, so readers cannot tell which ones fit a note.
- LinkedIn Help (answer/46662) could not be opened from this run (robots), so the article tells the reader to
  trust the counter in the invite box and keeps every note under 200 characters.

## Questions people ask (FAQs on ranking pages, related searches)
- Should I mention the competitor's post in my message?
- How soon after a buying signal should I send the message?
- What if the person is not connected to me?
- How many messages should I send before I stop?
- Can I automate these templates?
- What is a good reply rate for signal-based LinkedIn messages?
- Do these templates work for InMail?

## Unlike the pages ranking now, this one ...
... gives one complete sequence (connection note, first message, follow-up) for each of seven LinkedIn buying
signals instead of a pile of openers sorted by stage, prints the character count next to every note, adds a
"do not send if" rule and a timing window per signal, says which signals are too weak to message at all,
sources its two numbers to LinkedIn's own data and a 13.2-million-request benchmark instead of vendor claims,
and uses only the caps we actually run (15 a day, 30 max, 2 accounts).
