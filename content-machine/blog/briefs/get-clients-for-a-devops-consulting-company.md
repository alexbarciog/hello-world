# Brief: how to get clients for a devops consulting company (solutions, landing)

Checked 2026-11-23.

## Intent
Commercial. A founder or partner at a small DevOps consultancy (or an engineer going independent) wants
a repeatable way to find companies that will pay for DevOps work this quarter. The ranking pages are
generic "how to get consulting clients" lists; nothing on page one is specific to the moment a company
decides to hire outside DevOps help.

## Who ranks (opened today)
- devopsboys.com "How to Start DevOps Consulting" and "Finding Clients" guides: refused automated fetch
  (client error), could not be read. Listed in the SERP as the two most specific pages.
- clickup.com/p/small-business/how-to-get-clients-for-devops-consultant: a product pitch for ClickUp
  (track inquiries in one platform). No channels, no numbers, no method.
- omkar.cloud/blog/how-to-get-devops-clients (2023-04-19, vendor): cold email CEOs/CTOs at 11 to 1,000
  person companies with "ideas for their business"; email companies hiring a DevOps engineer; follow up
  after two days. Email only, no LinkedIn, no timing, no downsides, no sources.
- dev.to/chrisachard "How to Find Consulting Clients" (2019): meetups and talks, public proof of work,
  asking the network for introductions, watching for companies hiring your skill, subcontracting to
  5 to 10 person consultancies, emailing the technical lead on a job post. Two short scripts. No LinkedIn
  search strings, no buying-signal framework beyond hiring posts.
- teamblind.com thread (cloud/DevOps engineer going part time): your existing network; Upwork and Fiverr
  are very competitive (one commenter applied to 20+ projects with no reply); check your employment
  contract first.
- r/devops "how to do freelance work in devops" (Apr 2025, via libreddit mirror): referrals; small
  one-off jobs for startups (CI/CD setup, VPS hosting, architecture reviews); specialise (Kubernetes,
  Jenkins to modern CI, one cloud); clients want experts, juniors are seen as a risk.

## What every page covers
Network and referrals first, a portfolio or GitHub, marketplaces (with a warning), cold email to
companies hiring, subcontracting, specialising in a niche.

## What no page covers
- The moment of purchase. DevOps consulting is bought in the week after something broke or a date
  landed: an outage and its post-mortem, a cloud bill that jumped, a Kubernetes version leaving
  support, a SOC 2 or ISO deadline, the only DevOps engineer resigning, a migration announced in public,
  an AI workload that nobody can cost. No page names these or shows how to find them on LinkedIn.
- Exact LinkedIn Posts-tab and Jobs-tab search strings per signal.
- A message per signal that quotes the post and offers one scoped piece of work.
- A daily time budget and the caps that keep an account safe.
- An honest line between signals and referrals, subcontracting, marketplaces.
- Sources. No ranking page links a primary source for any number.

- Should I take a retainer or project work?

- How do I find companies that need DevOps help?
- How much should a DevOps consultant charge? (not answered with a number; no verified source)

## Original angle
Unlike the pages ranking now, this one names the seven public moments that make a company of 10 to
500 people hire a DevOps consultancy this quarter (an outage or post-mortem post, a cloud bill or
"29% waste" post, a Kubernetes or runtime version leaving support, a SOC 2 / ISO 27001 deadline, the
sole DevOps engineer leaving or a first "DevOps engineer" job post, a migration announced in public,
an AI workload with no cost control), gives the LinkedIn search and the message for each, a week-one
plan with a daily time budget, the honest cases where referrals and subcontracting win, and uses only
primary sources opened today (Flexera 2026 State of the Cloud, FinOps Foundation State of FinOps 2026,
Google Cloud DORA 2025 announcement, kubernetes.io release pages) plus our own 14-day run.
