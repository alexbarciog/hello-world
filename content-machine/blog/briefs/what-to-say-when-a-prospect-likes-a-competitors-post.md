# Brief: what to say when a prospect likes a competitor's post

Date: 2026-11-23. Section: blog. Type: templates. Cluster: templates.

## Intent
The searcher has a name (someone who liked a competitor's or an industry leader's post) and wants the words to send. They want
scripts, plus the answer to "is a like even worth a message?" Format of the top results: vendor how-tos that end in the vendor's
tool, with zero to two templates each. The page should be a short decision rule followed by copy-paste scripts.

## What every page covers
Find the engagers, filter by ICP, send a topic-led message, do not name the competitor, automate it with the vendor's tool.

## What no page covers
- The difference between a like and a comment in the WORDS you send (every template is the same for both).
- A decision rule for when a like deserves a message, a silent connection, or nothing.
- Scripts for the four situations a like actually comes in (post about your problem, launch post, repeat liker, like plus
  a second signal), with character counts.
- What to write back when the person says "just following".
- The buyer's-side objection (Carta) and where it is right.
- Honest reply-rate expectations with a sourced baseline instead of a vendor band.

## Questions people ask (from the pages' FAQs, comments and related searches)
- Should I mention that they liked the post? Should I name the competitor?
- Is a like even a buying signal, or just the visible trigger?
- How soon should I message after the like? How long is it warm?
- What if they liked the post because they are a customer of the competitor?
- Can I automate this without getting restricted?
- What reply rate should I expect?

Vendors (Common Room, Expandi, Unify, Valley, AiSDR, Reply.io, Fuzzy) and two individuals (Koka Sexton, Carta's Peter

## Unlike the pages ranking now, this one ...
treats the like as the weakest usable signal and gives a 30-second check that sorts it into message, connect silently or
skip; then four scripts matched to the kind of post that was liked (problem post, launch post, repeat liker, like plus a
second signal), each under 300 characters and none naming the competitor or the like; a reply script for "just following";
the exact phrases that read as surveillance; a sourced baseline (13.2M-request benchmark) instead of a vendor reply band;
and the buyer's-side argument that reaction-triggered outreach never works, with where it is right.
