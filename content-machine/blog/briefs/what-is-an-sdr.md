# Brief: what is an sdr (learn, lesson) — 2026-11-24

## Intent
Definition. The searcher wants to know what the role is, what the person does, how it differs from a BDR and an account executive, and (for a founder) whether they need one. Page one is definition articles from vendors (Salesforce, TechTarget, LinkedIn Sales Solutions, Pipedrive, Apollo, ZoomInfo) and a careers piece (Coursera). Format: definition, responsibilities, skills, tools.

## What every page covers
Definition sentence, four duties (prospect, reach out, qualify, nurture/hand off), skills list, tools list, a short "SDR vs AE" line, and a product pitch.

## What no page covers
- Numbers. Salesforce, TechTarget, LinkedIn and Pipedrive give no salary, quota, activity or tenure figures at all. Coursera gives a Glassdoor base salary only. Apollo and ZoomInfo quote activity ranges without sources.
- Agreement on SDR vs BDR. LinkedIn and ZoomInfo put inbound on the SDR and outbound on the BDR; Apollo's page inverts it; Pipedrive defines the SDR as outbound. No page tells the reader the titles are not standardised.
- A decision rule for a company under 200 people: do it yourself, hire, or use signals plus software.
- Where the AI SDR fits next to the human role.

## Questions people ask
What does SDR stand for in sales? Is an SDR an entry-level job? SDR vs BDR? How many meetings should an SDR book a month? What does an SDR earn? Do small companies need an SDR?

## Who ranks
Vendors and large publishers, but all thin on data. The keyword is kept as is; the lesson wins on sourced benchmarks and the decision rule rather than on authority.

## Angle
Unlike the pages ranking now, this one defines the SDR by the handoff (a booked, qualified meeting for a closer), gives the sourced benchmark numbers for the role (daily activities, monthly meeting quota, share at quota, ramp, tenure, pay) from one primary survey, states plainly that SDR and BDR are not standardised titles and shows the disagreement, places the AI SDR next to the human role, and gives a founder of a company under 200 people a decision rule for doing the job themselves, hiring, or feeding signals to software.
