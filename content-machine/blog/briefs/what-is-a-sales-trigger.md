# Brief: what is a sales trigger (learn, lesson)

Searched 2026-11-05. Opened: Yesware (updated 12 Aug 2025), Close glossary (undated), DealHub glossary
(updated 9 Apr 2026), Persana (7 May 2025), Crunchbase (updated 12 Nov 2025), Bounce Watch (2 Mar 2026),
CUFinder (27 Jun 2026, modified 31 Oct 2026), SalesHive glossary (reviewed Jun 2026), Leadfeeder (15 Sep 2026).

## Intent
Definition plus a list of examples. Every ranking page answers "what is a sales trigger" with one sentence
("any event that creates a good opportunity to reach out") and then a list of 6 to 29 trigger events
(funding, new executive, hiring, M&A, product launch, legislation, relocation, trade shows, complaints
about a competitor). Format to match: definition in bold, types, a table, how to find them, FAQ.

## What every page covers
- The one-line definition (an event at the prospect's company that gives a reason to reach out).
- A list of trigger events grouped by company / product / industry / customer (DealHub) or by growth /
  leadership / risk (Bounce Watch) or eight groups (CUFinder).
- Where to find them: Google Alerts, LinkedIn, press releases, job boards, the vendor's own database.
- Advice to act fast and to mention the trigger in the first line.

## What no page covers
- The word is used for three different things: a change at the prospect's company (trigger event),
  a person's action (buying signal; Close and SalesHive fold website visits and pricing-page views in
  under the same word), and an automation rule in a CRM or outreach tool (Persana's FAQ "what triggers
  the sales order system" and Close's "triggers in marketing" show the search catches that meaning too).
  No page says which one the reader means or that the three chain together.
- No page grades the triggers. The lists treat a funding round and a public complaint as equals, when
  they differ in whether they name a person, how long the window stays open, how many sellers get the
  same alert the same morning, and whether you can see them free.
- The statistics are secondhand. Persana: "74% higher win rates" (Forrester, no document), "8x more
  likely to buy after funding" (Crunchbase link), "10x more likely" for new executives (no source).
  Bounce Watch: Forrester 35-50% first-vendor win, HBR 70% of vendor decisions in six months, HubSpot
  3-4x reply rates, none linked to a document. CUFinder itself says the 74% figure is secondhand.
  Crunchbase, Leadfeeder, SalesHive, DealHub, Close: no trigger statistics at all. Nobody has a primary
  source, and nobody says so plainly except CUFinder in one sentence.
- No page gives a first-hand example of which trigger produced which result.

## Relationship to the existing trigger-event lesson
/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales already covers the company-change type in depth (Elias's three
types, what a trigger does not tell you, the five-deals rule). This page must not repeat it. It owns the
umbrella word: the three meanings, how they chain, the scorecard that grades common triggers, and the
rule for choosing. It links to the trigger-event lesson as the deep dive on the company-change type.

## Angle
Unlike the pages ranking now, this one says the word "sales trigger" is used for three different things
(a change at a company, an action by a person, a rule in your software), shows how the three chain into
one message, grades twelve common triggers on four questions nobody's list asks (does it name a person,
how long the window stays open, how many sellers get it the same morning, can you see it free on
LinkedIn), says plainly that the win-rate statistics quoted for triggers have no primary source, and
reports the one trigger that produced Intentsly's own 14 demos, labelled as one company over two weeks.
