## Intent
Definition. The searcher works in B2B sales or marketing at a small company, has seen "dark social" in a LinkedIn
post or a vendor article and wants to know what it is, whether it matters for them, and what to do. Every ranking
page is a definition plus marketing advice. Format to match: lesson (definition, why, how, comparison, actions, FAQ).

## Table stakes
A one-sentence definition; the channels (Slack, Teams, WhatsApp, DMs, forwarded email, communities, calls); shows
as direct traffic; Madrigal coined it in 2012; the "how did you hear about us" question; UTMs cannot fix it.

## What no page covers
- Every page is written for the marketer who owns attribution. None says what a seller should do differently
  tomorrow, beyond "expect unseen influence".
- No page separates the part of dark social you cannot see (the private thread) from the part you can (the public
  post that got forwarded, the "anyone using" question, the comment under a competitor's post). That split is the
  whole practical answer for a sales team.
- Statistics are either vendor consensus ("40-70%"), undated, or from third-party round-ups. None cites the
  SparkToro test directly or dates the Refine Labs sample.
- Nobody states the limits: the self-reported question is biased by memory, a seller still cannot read a Slack
  thread, and "dark social" is not a channel you can post into.
