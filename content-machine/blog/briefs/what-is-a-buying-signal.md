# Brief: what is a buying signal (learn / lesson)

Researched 2026-10-16. Pages opened: clay.com/glossary/buying-signal, salesforce.com/blog/sales/what-are-buying-signals,
blog.hubspot.com/sales/buying-signals-sales, salesloft.com "Defining Buying Signals", dealhub.io/glossary/buying-signals,
salesmotion.io/blog/what-is-a-buying-signal-in-sales, revenue.io glossary "What are Buying Signals?",
about.crunchbase.com "10 Verbal and Nonverbal Buying Signals", Gartner press releases 2026-03-09 and 2026-05-20.

## Intent
Definitional. The searcher is new to the term and wants a one-line definition, the types, examples, and
what to do when one shows up. Top results are glossary entries (Clay, DealHub, Revenue.io, Saber) and vendor
blog posts (Salesforce, HubSpot, Salesloft, Salesmotion) of 750 to 2,200 words, plus one 6,000-word
Salesmotion listicle of 40 signals. H2s are phrased as questions. Format to match: lesson with a quotable
definition, a types section, a short comparison table, a "how to use it" section and a FAQ.

## What every page covers
- A definition built on "cue / behaviour / event that shows a prospect is considering a purchase".
- A split into verbal, non-verbal (body language), digital and company-level or "situational" signals.
- The same examples: pricing questions, demo requests, pricing-page visits, funding, new hires,
  complaints about a current vendor, new stakeholders joining the thread.
- "How to respond": act fast, personalise, bring in the next step.

## What no page covers
- Almost every page describes signals that happen inside a sales conversation the seller already has
  (nodding, asking tough questions, bringing in legal). None separates those from the signals that
  happen before any conversation exists, in public, which is the only kind that helps a founder who has
  no pipeline yet.
- A test for whether something is a buying signal at all. The pages list 5 to 40 examples with no rule
  for deciding if a new observation counts.
- False positives and how to handle a single weak signal. Clay has a one-line "cons" list; HubSpot,
  Salesloft, DealHub, Revenue.io have nothing or one sentence. Salesmotion treats stacking as the answer
  but its conversion numbers are mostly unsourced.
- Statistics: Clay, Salesforce, Salesloft, DealHub, Revenue.io and Crunchbase cite no data at all.
  HubSpot cites one vendor (Lantern). Salesmotion cites many figures, several without a source.
- A copy-paste asset. None gives a table the reader can reuse to score what they saw.
