# Brief: what is a hiring signal in sales (learn, lesson)

Searched 2026-11-17. Pages opened: Komo glossary (komo.ai/glossary/hiring-signal), Autobound glossary
(autobound.ai/glossary/hiring-signals), Crustdata blog (crustdata.com/blog/hiring-signals), FirstSales
"job posts vs hiring signals" (firstsales.io/blog/job-posts-vs-hiring-signals), Valley "hiring signals for
sales prospecting" (joinvalley.co/blog/hiring-signals-for-sales-prospecting). Firmbase result was an
unrelated UK company-list page. Primary sources opened for statistics: Greenhouse 2024 State of Job
Hunting post (greenhouse.com/uk/blog/...), SHRM recruiting benchmarking page (shrm.org, 2026 data brief),
BLS JOLTS release for September 2026 (bls.gov/news.release/jolts.nr0.htm, released 3 November 2026).

## Intent
Definitional. The searcher is new to signal-based selling and wants a one-sentence definition, the kinds
of hiring signal, how it differs from a job posting and a buying signal, and what to do with one. Page one
is vendor glossaries and vendor blogs (Komo, Autobound, Crustdata, FirstSales, Valley), all short-to-medium
definition pages with a types list and a tool pitch. Format to match: definition page with a types table.

## What every page covers
- Definition as "data from job postings, headcount changes and recruiting activity that reveals priorities".
- A list of types: first hire in a function, hiring surge, senior leader hire, tool named in the posting,
  roles in a new location, hiring slowdown.
- "Contact the hiring manager, not the recruiter", "act fast".
- A pitch for the vendor's own monitoring product.

## What no page covers
- None separates what a hiring signal *proves* (a decision was made and money exists) from what it only
  *suggests* (that the company will buy what you sell). Inferences are presented as facts.
- Nobody gives a test to tell a budget signal from a backfill or a ghost job; Crustdata names ghost jobs
  but offers no method. Greenhouse's own figure (18 to 22% of postings on its platform are ghost jobs)
  appears on none of them.
- Timing is asserted without a source (Komo: 7 to 14 days; FirstSales: 14 days; Crustdata: 24 to 48 hours).
  SHRM's median time to fill (39 calendar days, 4,600+ organisations) gives a sourced window; nobody uses it.
- The headline statistics are vendor-reported or unsourced: Komo's "73% of openings posted within 30 days
  of budget approval (Origami)", "2 to 3x higher purchase intent (Prospeo)", "18% response rate";
  Autobound's "2.4x more likely to buy software" is labelled by Autobound itself as having no identifiable
  primary source. Valley admits there is no independent study linking postings to win rates.
- Nobody says which hiring signals a one-person team can see for free on LinkedIn versus which need a
  job-board scraper.

## Questions people ask (from the ranking pages' FAQs and headings)
- What is a hiring signal? / What are hiring signals in B2B sales?
- Is a job posting a buying signal?
- What is the difference between a hiring signal and a buying signal?
- Who do you contact when a company is hiring?
- How soon should you reach out after a job posting?
- How do you tell a real hiring signal from a ghost job?
- Do hiring signals work for small companies / enterprises?

## Who ranks
Mid-size vendor sites, no large publications. The exact keyword is winnable with a lesson page; our own
guide (/guides/track-hiring-signals-for-sales) already ranks for the how-to version and answers "what is a
hiring signal" in its FAQ, so this lesson defines the term and sends readers to the guide for the routine.

## Unlike the pages ranking now, this one ...
... says a hiring signal is three different pieces of evidence (a job posting, a person's hiring post, a
headcount change) and states for each what it proves and what it only suggests; gives a four-question test
that separates a budget signal from a backfill or a ghost job, using Greenhouse's own 18 to 22% ghost-job
figure and the JOLTS gap between 7.5 million openings and 5.4 million hires; sets the outreach window from
SHRM's sourced median time to fill (39 days) instead of a vendor's guess; says plainly that the win-rate
and response-rate statistics on the ranking pages have no primary source we could open; and separates
the hiring signals you can see free on LinkedIn from those that need a job-board scraper, including the
honest note that Intentsly watches LinkedIn hiring posts, not job boards.
