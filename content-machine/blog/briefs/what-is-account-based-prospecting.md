# Brief: what is account-based prospecting

Section: learn. Type: lesson. Cluster: fundamentals. Researched 2026-11-07.

## Intent
Definition first. The searcher is new to the term, has seen "account-based" in front of
marketing, selling and prospecting, and wants to know what prospecting-by-account means, how
it differs from the other two, and whether it applies to a team their size. Ranking pages are
long vendor playbooks (7 steps, tool lists, cadences); the definition part is one paragraph.

## Who ranks (opened 2026-11-07)
- Woodpecker, "Account-Based Prospecting: How to Build a Focused Target List" (13 Jul 2026).
  Definition: a targeted approach focused on a defined group of high-value accounts; starts with
  the company, not the contact. Seven steps. No sourced statistics. ABP = sales-led, ABM =
  marketing-led, ABS used loosely as the same thing.
- Skylead, "Account-Based Prospecting: The Secret Weapon of B2B Sales" (29 Apr 2026, updated
  15 Sep 2026). Definition: finding and researching high-value companies that match the ICP,
  then the decision-makers inside them, before outreach. Seven steps. Two statistics pointed at
  Gartner and Demandbase pages (the Gartner URL slug says 61%, the text says 73%).
- Tomba, "Account-Based Prospecting in 2026: A Complete Playbook" (2 Jun 2026). Definition:
  pick a finite list of companies first, then the people. H2s as questions. Cites Gartner's
  "six to ten decision-makers" page. Table figures (ACV, cycle length) unsourced.
- GTM Works, "What is Account Based Prospecting (ABP)?" (15 Feb 2026). Two-minute page.
  Defines ABP as top-of-funnel identify/activate/nurture until a demo; compares with ABM and ABX.
- Reply.io, "5 Ways To Level Up Your Account-Based Prospecting Automation" (undated). Treats
  ABP, ABM and ABS as "similarly named approaches"; cites Gartner's 6 to 10 decision-makers
  with no link; a 21.5% reply rate from its own campaign.
- Pipedrive, "Account-based sales" (14 Oct 2026): the one page that says when the approach
  does NOT fit (low-value high-volume sales, very short cycles, single decision-maker,
  one-off purchases).
- Wikipedia "Account-based selling": no mention of prospecting; cites a 2017 Gartner note.
- LinkedIn top-content page: robots-blocked. Gartner pages: could not be opened this run, so
  no Gartner figure is used.

## What every page covers
Company-first vs person-first; ICP; target account list; tiering; mapping the buying
committee; multichannel outreach; CRM; metrics. Table stakes, covered briefly.

## What no page covers
- A one-sentence definition that separates prospecting (finding and preparing) from selling
  (running the deal) and marketing (campaigns to the account).
- An honest fit test a 1 to 200 person company can answer in a minute. Pipedrive gives four
  "when not" bullets; nobody turns them into a scored test.
- Where the people-signals fit: every page builds the list from firmographics and then
  researches each account by hand. None says that a public post from someone inside a target
  account is the cheapest way to pick which account to work this week.
- The decision-maker statistic every page quotes lives on a Gartner page; this lesson does not
  repeat a number it could not open.

## Questions people ask
- What is the difference between account-based prospecting and account-based marketing?
- Is account-based prospecting the same as account-based selling?
- How many accounts should a rep work at once?
- Does account-based prospecting work for small companies / small deals?
- What is the difference between account-based and lead-based prospecting?
- What tools do you need for account-based prospecting?

## Unlike the pages ranking now, this one ...
... defines account-based prospecting in one sentence as the finding-and-preparing stage of
account-based selling, separates it from ABM, ABS and lead-based prospecting in one table, gives
a six-question fit test with a pass mark so a small team knows in a minute whether to switch,
shows how to pick the week's accounts from public LinkedIn signals instead of a static tier
list, reports that Intentsly's own launch was signal-led rather than account-led and what that
implies, and repeats no statistic it could not open.
