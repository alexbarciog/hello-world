# Brief: what is a sales accepted lead (learn, lesson)

Date: 2026-11-27. Searched "what is a sales accepted lead" and "sales accepted lead SAL definition MQL SQL handoff SLA"; opened the pages below through the web-fetch agent.

## Intent
Definition. The searcher wants one sentence they can quote, the place of SAL between MQL and SQL, and a way to decide whether their team needs the stage. Top results are glossary entries and vendor blog posts, so the format is a lesson, not a how-to.

## Who ranks (opened 2026-11-27)
- Forrester blog "Sales accepted leads: the most important and most overlooked step..." (both forrester.com and go.forrester.com URLs redirect to the generic /revenue/ landing page; article not readable today).
- Nutshell, "What Are SALs? Best Criteria for Qualification" (Will Gordon, published 2023-02-09, updated 2026-07-31): SAL = MQL that passed a formal sales review; BANT plus "readiness"; "sales has 24 hours to contact"; healthy acceptance 70 to 90% in the FAQ but "lower than 90% means ambiguity" in the body; no sources, no example, CRM pitch.
- Mountain (MNTN, CTV ad vendor): generic definition, five loose criteria, no numbers, product pitch at the end.
- Pedowitz Group, "difference between MQLs, SQLs and SALs" and "how do you measure sales acceptance of MQLs": the clearest operational definition (SAL = MQL the assigned rep accepts within the SLA: reachable, right account, not junk; SQL = qualified after contact); "commonly 24 hours"; rejection reason taxonomy; no benchmarks.
- Rework, "SQL definition and acceptance" (updated 2026-05-04): SAL review window "typically 48 to 72 hours"; rejection codes R1 to R6; all statistics second-hand (MarketingSherpa, HBR 2011, Drift) and not opened here.
- Dreamdata glossary: SAL = lead marketing and sales both agree is ready; formula SAL rate = SALs / leads; no numbers.
- Forrester (SiriusDecisions) blog "Measuring the impact of successful sales handoffs" (go.forrester.com/blogs/measuringtheimpactofsuccessfulsaleshandoffs, loads, no date shown): SAL "signals a successful change in control" and is "a commitment to contact the lead within a specified period of time, rather than a reflection of the quality of the lead"; 72% of surveyed companies had a formal SAL stage; sales accepted 42% of marketing-sourced leads; 9.3 closed/won deals per 1,000 inquiries with a formal SAL stage vs 4.6 without; target SAL rate at or above 85%. The only primary research on the first two pages.
- First Page Sage, "MQL to SQL conversion rate by industry: 2026 report" (published 2026-11-19): average 16.1%, median 15%, B2B SaaS 13%, IT and managed services 13%, staffing 12%; client data 2019 to November 2026, 30 industries; never mentions SAL; its SQL means a meeting was booked.
- HBR 2011 "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads" (Oldroyd, McElheran, Elkington): only the BYU repository abstract could be opened; no figures, so none are used.

## What every page covers
Definition, MQL then SAL then SQL order, "why it matters" (alignment, fewer leaked leads), BANT-style criteria.

## What no page covers
- The point Forrester makes and every vendor page misses: acceptance is a handoff commitment, not a quality verdict. Vendor pages mix "accepted" with "qualified" and then recommend BANT for acceptance, which is the SQL test.
- A copy-paste acceptance rule small enough to apply in 60 seconds, with a rejection-code table.
- When a team should skip the stage (one person does both jobs, or leads come from outbound signals rather than forms).
- What the "accept" decision looks like in outbound, where no marketing team hands anything over.
- Numbers with sources. Nutshell contradicts itself (70 to 90% vs 90%); Pedowitz and Mountain give none.

## Questions people ask (from the ranking pages' FAQs)
- What is the difference between SAL and SQL? Between MQL and SAL?
- What is a good sales accepted lead rate?
- How long does sales have to accept a lead?
- What happens when sales rejects a lead?
- Does a small team need an SAL stage?
- Who decides whether a lead is sales accepted?

## Angle
Unlike the pages ranking now, this one defines the sales accepted lead as a handoff commitment rather than a quality judgement (with Forrester's research on what that changes), gives a three-check acceptance rule and a six-code rejection table the reader can paste into a CRM today, uses only sourced numbers, tells one-person teams and outbound teams when to skip the stage and what replaces it, and uses our own launch (14 demos in 14 days, no marketing handoff at all) as the first-hand case.
