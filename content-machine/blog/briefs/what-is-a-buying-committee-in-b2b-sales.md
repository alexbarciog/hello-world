# Brief: what is a buying committee in b2b sales (learn, lesson)

Date: 2026-11-10. Section: learn. Type: lesson. Cluster: fundamentals.

## Intent
Definition query. Searcher is new to signal-based selling or deal qualification and wants: a one-line
definition, the roles on the committee, how big it usually is, and what to do about it. Top results are
glossary-style explainers with a role list; two add "how to sell to one" sections.

## Who ranks (opened 2026-11-10)
- abmatic.ai (a dozen near-duplicate programmatic pages fill most of page one): definition, 5 roles
  (exec sponsor/champion, economic buyer, technical buyer, user buyer, procurement/legal), illustrative
  size tiers with no data, a 3-question FAQ, no statistics, no table.
- flowla.com: Bonoma 1982 six roles (initiator, influencer, decider, purchaser, user, gatekeeper) plus
  Miller Heiman buyer types; one stat (Gartner "six to ten", second-hand); vendor case studies; no table,
  no FAQ.
- callboxinc.com: long guide, 7 roles + legal/procurement, many stats (Forrester 13 stakeholders, Gartner
  6-10, Gartner conflict), coverage table, deal-size table, 7-question FAQ. Strongest page. Vendor is an
  outsourced appointment-setting firm.
- bullseye.so glossary: 6 roles, four headline stats with no sources for three of them, 5-question FAQ.
- belkins.io: original study of 1,871 ICP submissions, titles per deal by company size (4.6 / 5.6 / 6.0).
No large publications on page one; the keyword is winnable as written.

## Table stakes
Definition; list of roles with what each cares about; the committee is bigger than one person; a
line on multithreading; FAQ on size and identification.

## What no page covers
- Nobody distinguishes a buying committee from the neighbouring terms (buying center, buying group,
  decision maker, champion) in one table.
- Size numbers are quoted second-hand and mixed up (13 vs 6-10 vs 4.6). No page says WHY the figures
  differ (Forrester counts everyone involved, Gartner counts the team, Belkins counts titles sellers target).
- No page says what a committee looks like at a 15-person company, where the whole committee is one
  founder, or gives a decision rule by company size.
- No page shows how committee members show up in public on LinkedIn before the first call.
- Role lists give titles, not the question each role asks and what they say when they are about to kill
  the deal.

## Angle
Unlike the pages ranking now, this one explains why the committee-size figures disagree (13 vs 6-7 vs
4.6, each counting something different), gives every role as the question it asks and the sentence it
says before killing a deal, separates buying committee from buying center, buying group, decision maker
and champion in one table, adds a decision rule by company size (under 50 people the committee is usually
the founder, which is what our 14-demo run showed), and shows how committee members appear on LinkedIn
before the first call.
