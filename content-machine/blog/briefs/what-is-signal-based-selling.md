## Intent
Definition. The searcher is new to the term, has seen it in a vendor's pitch or a LinkedIn post, and wants to know
what it means, how it differs from intent data and ABM, and whether it needs a tool. Format of the top results:
definition paragraph, list of signal types, a workflow, a tool list, the vendor. Several pages lead with a "30-second
answer" block, which is what answer engines quote.

## Table stakes
A one-sentence definition; the common signal types (job changes, funding, hiring, website and content behaviour,
tech changes, social engagement, competitor activity); contrast with static lists and fixed cadences; a
detect-prioritise-act loop; "act fast" because signals decay.

## What no page covers
- Every definition is written by a company that sells signals, so none says what a signal is NOT, except Common
  Room (one line). No page gives a test the reader can apply to a data point.
- The reply-rate and close-rate multiples (2-4x, 3-5x, 5-10x, 15-25% vs 3-5%, 33-41% vs 18-25%) differ from page
  to page and trace to vendor reports, a LinkedIn post or nothing. No page says so.
- No page separates the method (a rule for when and whom to contact) from the data products sold under the name.
  Every page's "how to implement" step assumes you buy a feed.
- No page shows a hand-run version a 1 to 20 person team can do on LinkedIn this week, with the search strings.
- No page reports what happened when the author ran it, with the before/after number.
