# Brief: what is lead qualification (learn, lesson)

Date: 2026-11-18. Section: learn. Cluster: fundamentals.

## Intent
Definitional. The searcher is new to sales vocabulary and wants: a one-sentence definition, why it
matters, how it is done (the frameworks everyone names), how it differs from lead scoring and from
MQL/SQL labels, and what to do with it. Top results are long vendor explainers (2,000 to 4,000 words)
with a definition, a frameworks section, a step list, a question list and FAQ. Format to match: a
lesson with a bold definition, a short comparison table, a copy-paste asset and FAQ.

## What every page covers (table stakes)
Definition; MQL vs SQL; why it matters (rep time); BANT and two to five other acronyms; a step list;
qualifying questions; a checklist; FAQ.

## What no page covers
- A rule for picking a framework. Every page lists four to six acronyms and says "choose what fits".
  None says plainly that MEDDIC and GPCTBA were written for enterprise deals and are overkill for a
  team of one to twenty.
- Qualification before the conversation. All five pages treat qualification as something done to a
  lead that has already arrived (a form fill, a demo request). For an outbound team the harder
  question is which of 200 strangers deserves a message, and most of that is answerable from public
  information before anyone talks.
- Disqualification as the point, not a side effect. Only RevenueHero and Bigin give it a section.
- A short, usable asset. The checklists are 8 to 20 items; nobody gives a card that fits one screen.
- Honest limits: when qualification is not worth formalising, and what it gets wrong (buyers
  without a line-item budget, people who buy later).
