## What every page covers
Why X is "underused" for B2B, the three or five signal types (complaint, recommendation request,
comparison, pain description, milestone), "reply before you DM", private lists of ICP accounts, a daily
check. Most give two to six example phrases.

## What no page covers
- X's own rules. None links X's automation policy, which forbids unsolicited bulk or automated DMs and
  automated replies triggered by keyword searches, or X's limits page (500 DMs a day, 400 follows a day,
  50 posts and 200 replies a day for unverified accounts). Several pages recommend tools that do exactly
  what the policy forbids.
- X's own help pages on search: advanced search works only when logged in on x.com; saved searches are
  "currently not available on x.com" (app only). Every page says "save your searches" as if it were a web
  feature.
- What monitoring costs at the platform level: X API reads are pay-per-use ($0.005 per post read, capped at
  3 million reads a month), which is why third-party X monitoring is priced the way it is and why the free
  route is the search box.
- A triage step. A matching post is a sentence, not a lead; nobody says how to check the account is a buyer
  (bio, company, role) before spending time.
- Where to reply. Most pages push X DMs; only one says to move to LinkedIn and none says when.
- Honest fit: which industries actually talk about tools on X and which do not.
- Every statistic on the page is a vendor's claim about itself or a round-up figure without a primary source.
