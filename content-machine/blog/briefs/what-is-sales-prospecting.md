# Brief: what is sales prospecting (learn, lesson)

Date: 2026-11-25. Keyword difficulty: high. Page one is all large vendors.

## Intent
Definition first ("what is X"), then the reader wants to know how it is done, how it differs from
lead generation and from a lead, and which methods exist. Top results are long vendor explainers
(3,000+ words) with a definition, inbound vs outbound, methods lists and a FAQ. Format to match:
definition, why, how, vs table, FAQ. The lesson shape in page-write.md fits.

## What every page covers (table stakes)
Definition; lead vs prospect; inbound vs outbound; a list of methods (call, email, LinkedIn,
referrals, events); why it matters; FAQ.

## What no page covers
- A time budget: how many hours a founder or a one-person sales function should give prospecting
  each week, and what to cut when the budget is blown.
- Numbers from primary sources on what prospecting produces per day (Bridge Group) and per 100
  emails (Hunter), in one place, with links.
- LinkedIn's practical daily limits stated as numbers.
- A prospecting vs lead generation vs lead qualification table (every page does this in prose).
- When prospecting is the wrong first move.
- Timing: starting from what a buyer just did in public instead of a database filter.

## Unlike the pages ranking now, this one ...
... defines prospecting by the two things it must produce (a named person and a reason to contact
them now), gives a weekly time budget with a cut rule, puts prospecting, lead generation and lead
qualification side by side in one table, cites only primary sources opened today (Bridge Group's
351-company SDR report, Gartner's 645-buyer survey, Hunter's 11-million-email analysis), states
LinkedIn's practical daily caps as numbers, and uses Intentsly's own 14-demos-in-14-days launch as
the first-hand example of signal-first prospecting.
