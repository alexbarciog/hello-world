# Brief: how to find clients as a fractional cto

Section: solutions. Type: landing. Slug: find-clients-as-a-fractional-cto. Researched 2026-11-15.
(Second keyword of the run: "how to get clients for an ai consulting business" was blocked by a gate bug, see that draft.)

## Intent
Commercial how-to. A senior engineer or former CTO going fractional wants a repeatable way to find paying
engagements. Top results are "how to become / how to go fractional" guides from recruiters, coaching
businesses and consultant-tooling vendors, with a short "finding clients" section each.

## Who ranks (opened today)
- Maxwell Bond (UK tech recruiter, 1 Jul 2025): define value proposition, LinkedIn profile, network,
  partner with the recruiter. "Most fractional work comes from word of mouth or warm introductions." No
  numbers.
- ConsultKit (vendor, 24 Mar 2026): network, startup ecosystems, VC referrals via technical due
  diligence, content, a discounted 90-day first engagement, communities. Pricing tables and third-party
  statistics (FractionUS, "State of Technology Leadership Report") quoted without links.
- CTO Coach (coaching business, 23 Mar 2026): existing network, marketplaces, content, VC/PE networks
  (free technical assessment of a portfolio company). Typical clients: due diligence, migration, team
  building, interim leadership. "3 to 6 months to first paying engagement", author's guidance.
- AltexSoft (dev agency, 29 Oct 2025) and Stephan Schmidt / amazingcto.com (CTO coach, 12 Jan 2026):
  buyer-side "when to hire a fractional CTO" pages. Schmidt's seven signs: non-technical founder burned by
  outsourced development, overwhelmed promoted tech lead, investors asking hard questions, scaling with
  tech as the bottleneck, confusion about AI, cannot afford a full-time CTO, needs experience not hours.
- Medium (Sergio Pereira, "4 steps I use") and salesso.com: fetch failed (client error), not used.
- The Next Web (27 Aug 2026): "fractional demand up 9% over 90 days" per Go Fractional, role scope unclear.

## What every page covers (table stakes)
Define a niche or value proposition; warm network and former colleagues first; LinkedIn profile and
content; VC and investor relationships via due diligence; communities and marketplaces; retainer pricing.

## What no page covers (the gap)
- The supply-side pages tell the CTO how to present; the buyer-side pages tell the founder when to hire.
  No page joins them: which of the founder's "signs" are visible in public, and what to search to find the
  founder in that week.
- No page separates a company that could use a CTO (every non-technical founder) from one that has decided
  this month (a hire posted, a round closed, an agency fired, a CTO gone).
- Statistics are quoted second-hand without links; none links the marketplace's own live data or says the
  engineering market is balanced, not a shortage, which is why timing matters more than for finance.
- Nobody says when outreach is the wrong tool and the investor route or a referral partner is better.

## Questions people ask
How do I get my first fractional CTO client? How many clients can a fractional CTO take? Do I need to have
been a CTO already? Should I use a fractional marketplace? How do founders find a fractional CTO? What
should I charge? (Rates answered without numbers; no first-hand rate data.)

## Unlike the pages ranking now, this one ...
names the six LinkedIn moments that show a company of 5 to 100 people is about to pay for a part-time CTO
(a non-technical founder asking how to judge developers or find a technical cofounder, a first senior
engineering job post at a company with no CTO, a pre-seed or seed round at a company whose team page shows
no technical leader, a burned-by-the-agency or rebuild post, investor or board due-diligence pressure
including "what is our AI strategy", a CTO leaving or a developer promoted into the title), gives the
Posts-tab or Jobs-tab search and the message for each, a week-one plan with a daily time budget and
LinkedIn's sending caps, cites only sources opened today (Go Fractional live demand data, the FractionUS
round-up of US research, Stephan Schmidt's buyer-side signs) or our own 14-day run, and says when
investors, agencies and fractional-CFO partners beat signal outreach.
