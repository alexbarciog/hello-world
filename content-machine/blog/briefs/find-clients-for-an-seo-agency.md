# Brief: how to find clients for an seo agency (solutions, landing)

Searched 2026-11-18 (web search, two query variants). Opened: Search Engine Land guide (Jon Nastor,
19 tips), DesignRush (7 steps, updated Dec 2025), Traffic Think Tank (10 + 7 tips), Reply.io
(2026), Snov.io (updated Sep 2026), plus three data sources: Search Engine Journal's report on the
SEOFOMO 2024 survey, SparkToro's 2025 State of Digital Agencies write-up, Momentic's State of Agency
Discoverability 2026.

## Intent
Commercial how-to. The searcher runs or is starting an SEO agency (or freelances) and wants a list
of ways to get clients, ideally ones that work this month. Top results are long tip lists (7 to 19
items) with a "how to convert" tail. Format to match: list-shaped, scannable, with a plan.

## What every page covers
Niche down, publish SEO content and rank for "SEO agency in <city>", post on LinkedIn, ask for
referrals, partner with web design / PPC / content agencies, list on Clutch or DesignRush, cold
email with a free audit as the hook, networking events, Upwork and similar, discovery calls,
value-based proposals.

## What no page covers
- Timing. Every page tells the reader where to pitch, none tells them *when* a company is about
  to buy SEO. No page names a trigger event (traffic drop after a core update, a site migration,
  a new head of marketing, an in-house SEO job ad, a "recommend an SEO agency" post).
- Search strings. Zero LinkedIn or Boolean searches across all five how-to pages. Templates: none.
- Honest downsides. The tactic lists have no "when this beats that" rule. SEL/TTT admit cold
  LinkedIn messages get ignored and that rejection is constant, but offer no alternative to volume.
- Sourced numbers. The how-to pages cite self-reported case studies or unsourced percentages
  (Reply.io: "80% of B2B leads from social come from LinkedIn", no source). The two surveys that
  exist (SEOFOMO via SEJ; SparkToro) are not cited by any of the ranking how-to pages.
- Vendor bias: SEL closes on Semrush One, TTT on its course, DesignRush on its directory, Reply.io
  and Snov.io on their tools.

## Questions people ask (from the pages' FAQs and related searches)
- How do I get my first SEO client with no case studies?
- How do I get SEO clients without cold calling or cold email?
- Is cold email still worth it for an SEO agency?
- Should an SEO agency pick a niche?
- How long does it take to land an SEO client?
- How do I get local SEO clients?

## Who ranks
Search Engine Land (Semrush-owned), DesignRush, Traffic Think Tank, Reply.io, Snov.io,
ManyRequests, Surfer. Vendor blogs and one trade publication; no page is unbeatable on depth, all
are generic. Keep the keyword as is.

## Unlike the pages ranking now, this one ...
names the seven LinkedIn behaviours that show a company is about to buy SEO (traffic-drop and
migration complaints, in-house SEO job ads, a new marketing leader, funding or new-market posts,
recommendation requests, questions under SEO tool vendors' posts, a DIY-SEO post by a founder),
gives the exact Posts-tab or Jobs search and the message for each, a week-one plan with a daily
time budget and LinkedIn's sending caps, says when referrals, partnerships and directories beat
signals with the SEOFOMO (via SEJ) and SparkToro figures linked, and uses only numbers with a
source: those two surveys and our own 14-day run.

## Numbers available (opened today)
- SEJ on SEOFOMO 2024 survey (337 self-selected respondents): referrals 77%, ranking in search
  39%, social media 26%, industry events 25%, cold outreach 14%, paid outreach 9%.
  https://www.searchenginejournal.com/survey-shows-where-seos-get-their-clients/525399
- SparkToro, 2025 State of Digital Agencies (published 17 Feb 2026): 59% have tried outbound; 9%
  very effective, 58% moderately, 33% not effective at all; 73% say LinkedIn is their most
  effective social channel; 70% have nobody dedicated to sales full-time; 55% close in 1 to 6
  weeks; 15% named partner referrals as their top referral source.
  https://sparktoro.com/blog/digital-agency-new-business-still-a-concern-referrals-still-rule-for-lead-gen-and-are-sales-pipelines-finally-recovering/
- Momentic (secondary compilation, no sample size): referrals as top source fell from 73% (2024)
  to 58% (2025) per RSW/US. Not used: second-hand and unsampled.
