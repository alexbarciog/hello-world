# Brief: what is a marketing qualified lead (learn, lesson)

Searched 2026-11-26. Opened: Tableau (~1,000 words, modified 2026-09-28), Adobe (~1,200 words,
2022), HubSpot (~1,900 words, updated 2023, originally 2018), TechTarget (~1,000 words, 2019
revised 2023), Pipedrive SMB guide (~2,300 words, dated 2026-09-23), ZoomInfo (~4,500 words,
updated 2026-07-01), CallRail (~1,800 words, 2019). Intercom URL returned the blog index, not an
article. Primary sources opened for numbers: First Page Sage MQL-to-SQL by industry (updated
2026-11-19), First Page Sage lead-to-MQL benchmarks (2025-08-01), Forrester "Saying Goodbye To
MQLs" blog (2023, Buyers' Journey Survey 2023), Forrester Palo Alto Networks client story,
MarketScale write-up of the Belkins 2026 B2B conversion survey (2026-11-01). The Belkins report
itself could not be opened (fetch permission), so its figures are cited through MarketScale.

- **Intent.** A definition first ("what does MQL mean"), then "how do I decide what counts as
  one", then "MQL vs SQL". Top results are glossary-style explainers with a criteria list, an
  MQL vs SQL section and, on the longer pages, a lead-scoring example.
- **What every page covers.** The definition (a lead marketing has judged more likely to buy),
  example actions (ebook download, webinar, pricing page, form fill, cart), the point-score
  idea with a threshold, MQL vs SQL, "align sales and marketing".
- **What no page covers.** Every page assumes a marketing team with forms and a nurture track
  feeding a separate sales team. None says what an MQL is when the company is one founder or a
  five-person team with no inbound funnel, where the "marketing" evidence is public LinkedIn
  behaviour rather than a download. None states the Forrester case that the MQL counts the
  wrong unit (a person, when 93% of buyers buy in groups) and lets the reader decide. Most
  numbers are unlinked (ZoomInfo cites only its own case studies; Pipedrive's scores are made
  up; TechTarget's "24 hours" has no source). Nobody gives the three-line MQL rule (fit, action,
  threshold) a small team can write in ten minutes, or a decision rule for when to skip the
  stage altogether.
- **Questions people ask.** What does MQL stand for? What is the difference between an MQL and
  an SQL? What is a good MQL to SQL conversion rate? How do you qualify an MQL? What is a sales
  accepted lead? Is the MQL dead? How many MQLs do I need?
- **Who ranks.** Tableau, Adobe, HubSpot, TechTarget, Pipedrive, ZoomInfo, CallRail. Large
  sites, but mostly dated glossary pages; the query is a definition, so the lesson can earn
  long-tail variants and answer-engine citations, and it completes the MQL/SQL/PQL set.

Unlike the pages ranking now, this one defines the marketing qualified lead for a company with
no marketing department, gives a three-line MQL rule (fit, action, threshold) with the public
LinkedIn evidence that stands in for a form fill, puts the 2026 MQL-to-SQL and lead-to-MQL
benchmarks next to each other with their sources, states Forrester's buying-group objection
honestly and gives a decision rule for when to skip the MQL stage, and uses our own 14-demos
launch as the first-hand case of selling without one.
