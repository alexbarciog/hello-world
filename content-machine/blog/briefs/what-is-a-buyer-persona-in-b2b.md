# Brief: what is a buyer persona in b2b (learn / lesson)

Searched 2026-11-13. Opened: ZoomInfo (buyer-persona-guide, updated 30 Jun 2026), Miro (17 Apr 2026),
EBQ (31 Oct 2024, modified 14 Apr 2025), Adience (18 Sep 2026), B2B International (modified 27 Aug 2025),
HubSpot (definition page, updated 18 Mar 2026). Primary sources opened for numbers: Gartner press release
20 May 2026 (645 buyers, Aug to Sep 2025), Gartner press release 7 May 2025 (632 buyers, Aug to Sep 2024),
Forrester investor release 21 January 2026.

## Intent
Definitional. Every top result opens with a one-sentence definition, then "why it matters", then a field
list ("what to include"), then steps to build one, then persona examples, then persona vs ICP, then FAQ.
Format to match: lesson with bold definition, field table, short build method, comparison table, FAQ.

## What every page covers (table stakes)
- Definition: a research-based (or "semi-fictional") profile of a decision-maker or influencer in target
  accounts; describes a role, not a company.
- Field list: role, goals, KPIs, pain points, buying role, objections, information sources, channels.
- Build steps: mine CRM, interview customers, segment, draft, validate, roll out.
- Persona vs ICP (company vs person); buying committee has several personas.
- Named example personas (ZoomInfo: Alex the SDR, Jordan the SDM, Morgan the CRO; Miro: PM, VP Ops,
  IT lead; Adience: "Pragmatic Operations Leader").

## What no page covers (the gap)
- None gives a build method sized for a company with ten customers and no research budget; all assume
  interviews at scale or a data platform (ZoomInfo's method is its own product).
- None has a "says no when" (veto) row, or a "public evidence" row (what this role posts, comments on or
  changes publicly that shows the persona is live right now). Nobody connects personas to timing.
- Numbers: ZoomInfo cites its own case studies; EBQ cites a vendor stats round-up and a 2017 survey;
  Miro and B2B International cite nothing. Only Adience links a primary source (Gartner 2026).
- "How many personas" is answered with "3 to 5" and no reason (ZoomInfo). No decision rule.
- Nobody says plainly where personas mislead (fictional names and stock photos, built from the marketing
  team's imagination, never validated against lost deals).
