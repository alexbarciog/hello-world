## Intent
Definition query. Searcher wants a plain definition, where the data comes from, how it differs from first-party,
and whether it is worth buying. Top results are short glossary pages (Abmatic, 450 and 1,300 words) and two
vendor guides (Leadinfo 1,100, FullEnrich 2,100). Format to match: definition first, then sources, then
first-party comparison, then limits and how to use, then FAQ.

## Who ranks
Abmatic holds most of page one with near-duplicate programmatic glossary pages; Leadinfo and FullEnrich are
vendor blogs; Bombora's own "what is intent data" page appears. No large publication, so the full keyword is
reachable.

## What every page covers (table stakes)
Definition (behaviour recorded on sites you do not own, aggregated and sold); source list (publisher co-ops,
bidstream, review sites, content syndication); first-party vs third-party; account-level nature; brief "how
to use".

## What no page covers (the gap)
- Not one statistic is linked to a source on any ranking page. Every number is unlinked or a vendor claim.
- Nobody shows what the big providers actually say about their own data: Bombora scores the last 3 weeks
  against a 12-week baseline, G2 reports Buying Stage and Activity Level updated daily, 6sense scores 0 to 100
  and predicts an opportunity in 90 days and says it identifies the account, not the individual. The
  providers' own docs are the best evidence of what you receive, and no explainer quotes them.
- Nobody gives the reader a question to ask a vendor before paying, or a decision rule by company size.
- Privacy: all four pages are thin. Bombora's own pages describe the consent model (publisher tag, network
  consent), which nobody repeats.
- Nobody covers public, person-level signals (LinkedIn) as the category a small team can actually use.
