# Brief: how to find b2b clients online (solutions, landing)

Date: 2026-10-20. Section: solutions. Cluster: find-customers.

## Intent
Commercial-informational. The searcher runs or sells for a small B2B business (agency, consultancy,
services firm, software company) and wants a list of places and methods that produce clients, with
enough detail to start. Every page on the first page is a numbered list of channels. Match the list
format for the channels, but lead with the sequencing decision the lists skip.

## Who ranks (opened 2026-10-20)
- Plezi, "10 great ways to find new B2B clients" (2022): SEO, social, content, website trust, events,
  phone, partnerships, forums, freelance platforms, own tool. One unsourced stat (80% of journey online).
- Assembly, "8 Powerful Ways To Find More B2B Clients" (Aug 2025): LinkedIn content, SEO, newsletter,
  cold email, LinkedIn messaging, creator sponsorship, Google/Meta ads, partnerships. No stats, no templates.
- Tomba, "How to Find and Keep B2B Clients in 2026" (Jun 2026): ICP, sources, data accuracy, tools,
  outreach, retention, 90-day plan. Vendor-leaning toward email finders; prices for list types.
- Reply.io, "How to Find B2B Leads in 2026" (updated Jan 2026): tool roundup plus LinkedIn playbook
  and an intent-data table. No sources.
- Instantly, "How To Get B2B Clients: 8 Proven Strategies" (updated May 2026): fundamentals plus eight
  strategies, most pointing at Instantly features.
- Callbox, "How to Search for B2B Clients in the USA" (updated Apr 2026): planning framework, channel
  list, second-hand stats, outsourcing pitch.
All are vendor blogs, none is a large publication, so the keyword is winnable with a page that is more
specific.

## Table stakes (cover briefly)
LinkedIn, cold email, SEO/content, communities and forums, referrals and partnerships, paid ads,
freelance marketplaces, define an ICP first.

## Gaps
- No page sorts channels by whether they reach people buying now or people who could buy someday.
- No page says which public behaviours mark a B2B buyer who is in-market, or what to send for each.
- No templates or exact searches; no daily time budget; no sending limits.
- Statistics are unsourced or second-hand. 6sense's 2025 Buyer Experience Report is primary and
  was opened this run (https://6sense.com/report/buyer-experience/): first contact at 61% of the
  journey, 94% ranked their shortlist before engaging sellers, buyers initiated 79% of engagements,
  5.1 vendors evaluated on average, nearly 4,000 buyer responses.
- Nobody says when a channel is the wrong choice.

## Questions people ask
- Where can I find B2B clients for free?
- Cold email or LinkedIn first?
- How long does it take to find B2B clients online?
- Do I need a website before I start?
- Should I buy a lead list?
- How many LinkedIn messages a day is safe?

## Other sources opened
- Close, "Customer acquisition strategy for startups" (Steli Efti): first 10 by any means, network
  first, cold-call a targeted list (Crunchbase companies that just raised: seven customers in two weeks).
- Indie Hackers thread on first 1/10/100 customers: Reddit, Product Hunt, X, LinkedIn, personal
  network most mentioned.

## Angle
Unlike the pages ranking now, this one sorts the channels by whether they reach buyers who are
in-market this week or buyers who might be someday, gives the LinkedIn behaviours that mark an
in-market B2B client as a table with the message for each, a week-one plan with exact searches and
a daily time budget, two copy-paste message templates, the sending limits, and says when referrals,
communities or paid beat signals. Numbers only from 6sense's 2025 report and our own 14-day run.
