# Brief: what is an ai sdr (learn / lesson)

Researched 2026-10-21. Pages opened: salesforce.com/sales/ai-sales-agent/ai-sdr, artisan.co/blog/ai-sdr
(Aug 2024), ibm.com/think/topics/ai-sdr (Apr 2026), qualified.com/plus/articles/ai-sdr (Mar 2025),
aisdr.com/blog/what-is-an-ai-sdr (Aug 2026, reviewed Oct 2026), autobound.ai/glossary/ai-sdr.
Primary sources opened for statistics: Gartner press releases 2025-06-25 (agentic AI cancellations) and
2026-05-20 (B2B buyer survey), Bridge Group "SDR tenure and ramp time" (2025 report, 351 companies),
SaaStr SDR tenure post (community poll, 2023; too informal, not used).

## Intent
Definitional. The searcher is a founder or sales lead who keeps hearing the term and wants to know what
the software actually does, how it differs from a human SDR and from "AI sales agent", whether it works,
and what it costs. Top results are vendor explainers (Salesforce, IBM, Artisan, Qualified, AiSDR,
Autobound) of 1,500 to 4,000 words with question H2s and, on two of them, an FAQ. Format to match:
lesson with a quotable one-line definition, a "how it works" section, a comparison table, honest
limits, a short "how to use it this week".

## What every page covers
- Definition: software that does the top-of-funnel work of a human SDR (find, research, write, send,
  follow up, qualify, book).
- A how-it-works list (research, message generation, sending, reply handling, handoff).
- AI SDR vs human SDR, mostly framed as cost and 24/7 availability.
- A benefits list, then the vendor's own product.
- Two of six pages distinguish AI SDR from AI BDR, AI sales agent and AI sales assistant.

## What no page covers
- The split that decides whether the thing works: WHO the AI SDR writes to. Every page describes
  the AI SDR as sending to a list; none says where the list comes from or that an AI SDR pointed at a
  cold list is cold outreach with better grammar, while one pointed at people who just did something
  (a buying signal) has a first line that is true.
- The channel question in plain terms: email AI SDRs live and die by deliverability infrastructure
  (AiSDR hints at it); LinkedIn AI SDRs live and die by daily account limits. No page gives a reader
  the limits to expect or says that a vendor who promises unlimited LinkedIn sends is a risk.
- A decision rule for a 1-to-200-person company: when an AI SDR is the wrong purchase (tiny
  addressable market, no proof the offer converts when a human sends it, nobody to own the handoff).
- A neighbouring-terms table that a reader can use in a minute (AI SDR vs human SDR vs sequencer vs
  AI sales assistant vs AI sales agent).
- A questions-to-ask-a-vendor checklist (copy-paste asset).
- Statistics with primary links. Most pages cite each other or their own reports.

## Questions people ask (from the FAQ sections on Salesforce and AiSDR, and the H2s)
- Will AI SDRs replace human SDRs?
- What is the difference between an AI SDR and an AI sales agent (or AI BDR)?
- Can an AI SDR make cold calls?
- How much does an AI SDR cost?
- How long does an AI SDR take to set up?
- Does an AI SDR integrate with my CRM?
- Do AI SDRs work?

## Who ranks
Large vendors (Salesforce, IBM) and AI SDR vendors (Artisan, Qualified, AiSDR, Autobound). No
independent source on page one. The keyword is kept as is because the /learn section exists to define
terms and the gap (signals vs lists, channel limits, decision rule) is real; the page is not trying
to outrank Salesforce on brand, it is trying to be the page people and answer engines quote for the
honest definition.
