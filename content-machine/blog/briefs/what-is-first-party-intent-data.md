# Brief: what is first-party intent data (learn, lesson) — 2026-11-11

## Intent
Definition query. The searcher wants a plain definition, the difference from second- and
third-party intent data, examples, and a sense of whether it is worth collecting. Top results are
glossary entries and vendor explainers with a three-way split (first, second, third party).

## Who ranks (opened today)
- Leadinfo "The Value of First-party Intent Data": definition (data collected on your own
  properties), five source types, five hypothetical uses, one unsourced "10-15% of visitors"
  figure, no limits, no consent, no cost.
- UserGems "Types of Intent Data": first/second/third split with one example each, provider
  list, customer ROI claims only, no independent data.
- TrustRadius "3 Types of Intent Data": same split plus "downstream" data; first-party limit
  stated (covers only the part of the journey on your own properties).
- intentdata.io: vendor page, "1.8 billion websites" unsourced, sells contact-level data.
- Lusha: same split, funnel-stage mapping, no statistics, one stated downside (only prospects
  already on your site).
- Rover Insights: shallow/medium/deep source ladder (digital behaviour, engagement events,
  conversation data), "decays 30% per year" with no source, Forrester cited without a figure.
- Knowlee glossary: GDPR angle (Art. 6(1)(a) consent, 6(1)(f) legitimate interest),
  illustrative scoring numbers, no FAQ.
- Bombora page returned only generic intent-data copy.
Mid-size vendor and glossary pages, not large publications: a lesson can compete.

## Table stakes
Definition, the first/second/third-party split, a list of first-party sources, an example,
"why it matters".

## What no page covers
- A one-question test for whether a given signal is first-party.
- What each source actually proves (a pricing-page visit vs a demo request) in one table.
- The honest coverage problem with numbers: how much of the buying journey happens before a
  buyer touches your properties (6sense 2025 report, Gartner survey), stated with the source.
- The grey zone small teams ask about: is a LinkedIn comment under your post first-party? Under a
  competitor's post? Nobody answers it.
- What a company with no traffic can do (the first-hand point: our launch pipeline came from
  public LinkedIn signals, not from first-party data we did not yet have).
- A decision rule for first-party vs public vs bought data by company stage.

## Angle
Unlike the pages ranking now, this one gives a one-question ownership test, a source table
that states what each first-party signal proves, puts a sourced number on how much of the
journey happens before a buyer reaches your properties, answers whether a LinkedIn comment
counts, and says plainly that a company with no traffic has to start with public signals
(which is how our own first 14 demos were booked).
