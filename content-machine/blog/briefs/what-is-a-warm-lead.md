## Intent
Definitional. Every top result opens with a one-line definition, then cold vs warm vs hot, then signals
or indicators, then how to convert, then FAQ. Format to match: lesson with bold definition, comparison
table, a classification asset, three actions, FAQ.

## What every page covers (table stakes)
- Definition: a prospect who has shown interest in your company (downloaded content, visited pricing,
  opened emails, followed you) but has not asked to buy.
- Cold vs warm vs hot by engagement level, urgency and sales motion.
- Lists of warm indicators: repeat site visits, content downloads, webinar attendance, email clicks,
  LinkedIn follows and post engagement, demo or trial requests (hot).
- Scoring models with point values and thresholds (ZoomInfo, Prospeo, monday.com).
- Speed-to-lead advice: minutes for hot, 24 hours for warm.
- MQL/SQL placement inside the warm band (Zeliq, Warmly).

## What no page covers (the gap)
- Every page defines warmth as engagement with YOUR brand. None treats the person who acted in public
  about the PROBLEM (asked which tool to buy, complained about a vendor) but never heard of you. For a
  company with no inbound, that is the only warm lead available, and it is the one the first message can
  quote. ZoomInfo gets closest with its "can you name the action in the first line" test, but limits
  warmth to the lead's history with you plus company events.
- Numbers: cold 1-3%, warm 5-15%, hot 20-40% appear on monday.com, ZoomInfo, Zeliq and Prospeo, with
  no named source anywhere (ZoomInfo: "industry benchmarks"; Zeliq calls them "indicative"; monday.com
  and Prospeo cite nothing). No page tells the reader to measure their own.
- No page separates "warm because they know you" from "warm because they are deciding now". A
  newsletter subscriber of three years and a founder asking for CRM recommendations this morning both
  count as warm on every list.
- No decision rule for a solo founder with no CRM, no scoring model and 20 leads.
