**An AI SDR is software that does the first stage of sales on its own: it decides who to contact, writes and sends the first message, handles the replies and books the meeting for a human to run.** SDR stands for sales development representative, the person who opens conversations so that a closer can finish them. The short answer to "what is an AI SDR" is a program that takes over that opening job, on email or LinkedIn, and hands you a calendar invite instead of a list of names.

## Why does an AI SDR matter?

Because the opening job is expensive to staff and the people who do it leave. The Bridge Group's 2025 report on 351 B2B companies puts average SDR tenure at 1.9 years and average ramp time at 3.0 months ([Bridge Group, February 2025](https://bridgegroupinc.com/research/sdr-tenure-and-ramp-time)). A company of ten people cannot carry a role that takes a quarter to learn and turns over every two years, so for most small teams the choice was never "human SDR or AI SDR". It was "founder does outbound at night or nobody does".

A labelled example. Suppose a four-person agency sells HubSpot onboarding. The founder has a list of 300 marketing managers and sends twenty messages a week when client work allows, which is most weeks never. An AI SDR pointed at that list and the founder's calendar sends every day, answers "what does this cost?" and puts the people who say yes into Thursday afternoon slots. The founder's job shrinks to showing up for the meetings.

The example also shows the limit. The AI SDR did not decide that those 300 people were the right people, and it did not know which of them had a reason to buy this month.

## How does an AI SDR work?

Every AI SDR, whatever the vendor calls it, makes three decisions that a human SDR used to make. Judge a product by how it makes each one.

1. **Who to contact.** The weakest products take a CSV. Better ones build a list from a database using your ideal customer profile (company size, role, industry). The best ones start from a buying signal: a specific person did something recently that shows a need, such as asking in public which tool to buy or complaining about a competitor. The lesson on [what a buying signal is](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal) defines the term; the point here is that the first decision fixes the ceiling on everything after it.
2. **What to say.** The software writes a first message and a follow-up sequence from the data it has about the person. If the data is a job title and a company name, the message is a template with two fields filled in. If the data is the sentence the person wrote yesterday, the message can quote it.
3. **How to reply.** When the person answers, the AI SDR classifies the reply (interested, question, objection, not now, wrong person), responds in natural language within the limits you set, and either books the meeting through a calendar link or hands the thread to you with a summary.

Then it logs the conversation to your CRM, if it integrates with yours, and repeats the loop the next day.

### A signal-fed AI SDR and a list-fed AI SDR are different products

The pages that rank for this term describe the AI SDR as working a list. That is the common design, and it has a predictable result: cold outreach with better grammar, sent at higher volume. A list-fed AI SDR asks the same question of 300 people and hopes a few are in-market.

A signal-fed AI SDR reverses the order. It watches a place where buyers show themselves, such as the comments under a competitor's LinkedIn posts or posts containing a pain-point keyword, scores each person against your ideal customer profile, and messages only the ones who both fit and just acted. The first line of the message is true because it refers to something the person did. Fewer messages go out, and more of them get an answer.

We ran the signal-fed version on ourselves at launch: our own LinkedIn signals, aimed at founders complaining about cold-outreach burnout, with the conversation handled by our AI SDR. The result was 14 qualified demos in 14 days with zero manual outreach, and a reply rate 4x our previous cold campaigns. The part of that story worth copying is the first decision: the people were chosen because of what they had just posted.

## What are the limits of an AI SDR?

Four, and a vendor who does not mention them is selling you the demo, not the product.

**It cannot invent a reason to buy.** If the offer does not convert when a human sends it to a well-chosen list, it will not convert when software sends it to a bigger one. Prove the message by hand first, with twenty sends, before you automate it.

**The channel sets the volume.** On email, deliverability is the constraint: cold domains, warm-up, bounce rates and spam complaints. On LinkedIn, the constraint is the daily action limit per account. Any vendor promising unlimited LinkedIn sends is asking you to risk the account. Intentsly's [AI SDR](/features/ai-sdr), for example, works on LinkedIn only, allows up to 2 sender accounts per workspace and caps each at 30 messages and 30 connection requests a day, with 15 recommended; it does not send email. Expect numbers in that range from any vendor who wants your account to survive the quarter.

**It needs a human on the other end of the handoff.** The AI books the meeting; someone has to run it, and someone has to read the threads the AI flagged as "unsure". Teams that assign nobody to this watch meetings evaporate.

**Buyers know.** In Gartner's survey of 645 B2B buyers, run from August to September 2025, 69% said they prefer to validate AI-generated insights with a sales rep, and 51% said they are more likely to meet misleading information from generative AI, against 49% who said the same of a rep ([Gartner, 20 May 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-20-gartner-survey-finds-sixty-nine-percent-of-b-two-b-buyers-turn-to-sales-reps-to-validate-ai-generated-insights)). An AI SDR that opens a conversation is tolerated; one that pretends to be a person through the whole deal is not.

One market caution. Gartner predicted in June 2025 that over 40% of agentic AI projects would be cancelled by the end of 2027, estimated that only about 130 of the thousands of vendors claiming agentic AI were real, and called the rest "agent washing": chatbots and sequencers rebranded as agents ([Gartner, 25 June 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)). The checklist below separates the two in a demo.

## AI SDR vs human SDR vs sequencer vs AI sales agent

The terms get used as synonyms by people selling them. They are not.

| | What it does | Who decides who to contact | Who handles the reply | Where it stops |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Human SDR | Researches, writes, sends, calls, qualifies | The SDR, from a territory or list | The SDR | Hands qualified meetings to a closer |
| Sequencer (email or LinkedIn automation) | Sends a fixed series of messages on a schedule | You, by uploading a list | You | Stops when the sequence ends or the person replies |
| AI sales assistant (copilot) | Drafts messages, summarises calls, suggests next steps | You | You | Never sends on its own |
| AI SDR | Chooses contacts, writes, sends, replies, books | The software, from a list, a database or signals | The software, within limits you set | Hands the booked meeting to a human |
| AI sales agent | Broader label; may cover inbound chat, qualification, outbound and support | Depends on the product | The software | Varies; read the product page |

"AI BDR" is the same thing as an AI SDR with the job title changed. If a product only sends and never replies, it is a sequencer with a writing model attached, whatever the pricing page calls it.

## How to use this lesson this week

Three actions, in order.

1. **Decide what the AI SDR should be fed.** Write down the five buying signals that preceded your last few deals. If you have none yet, the free [Buying Signal Playbook](/signal-playbook) lists twelve worth chasing and eight to ignore, with the LinkedIn trigger for each. If every signal on your list happens on LinkedIn, you want a LinkedIn AI SDR; if your buyers only answer email, you want an email one and a deliverability plan.

2. **Prove the message by hand.** Send twenty messages yourself to people who fit your [ideal customer profile](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) and just gave a signal. If nobody answers, fix the offer before you buy software to send it faster.

3. **Run the vendor checklist in the demo.** Copy this into your notes and ask every question.

| Question for the vendor | A good answer sounds like | A warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Where do the contacts come from? | "From signals you choose, scored against your ICP" or "from your list" | "We have a database of everyone" |
| What are the daily sending limits? | A specific number per account per channel | "Unlimited" |
| What happens when someone replies with a question you did not script? | "It answers within the rules you set, or hands the thread to you with a summary" | "It always answers" |
| Can I see every message before it goes out, at least at first? | "Yes, approval mode is on by default" | "You will not need to" |
| Does it tell the buyer it is automated when asked? | "Yes" | A pause |
| Which CRM and calendar does it write to, natively? | Named tools | "Via Zapier" only |
| How do I get my data out? | An export the rep can show you | "Why would you want to" |

## FAQ

### What is an AI SDR in simple terms?

An AI SDR is software that does the job of a sales development representative: it picks people to contact, writes and sends the first messages, answers their replies and books a meeting into a human's calendar. The human then runs the meeting. It replaces the opening of a sales conversation, not the conversation itself.

### What is the difference between an AI SDR and an AI sales agent?

An AI SDR is a specific job: outbound prospecting, reply handling and meeting booking. "AI sales agent" is a broader label that vendors use for anything from inbound website chat to full outbound to post-sale support. Every AI SDR is an AI sales agent; most AI sales agents are not AI SDRs. Read the product page for what it actually sends and to whom.

### Will AI SDRs replace human SDRs?

They replace the repetitive part of the role: the research, the first message, the follow-ups and the scheduling. They do not replace the judgement that handles an unscripted objection or the trust built across a long deal. Teams that get value from one keep a human owning the handoff and point the software at people who gave a signal rather than at everyone.

### Can an AI SDR make cold calls?

Some products offer AI voice calling, but most AI SDRs are text-based, working on email or LinkedIn. Automated voice brings consent rules that vary by country and a buyer reaction that is often worse than an email. For a small company, treat voice as a separate decision with its own legal check rather than a box to tick in a demo.

### How long does an AI SDR take to set up?

Setting up the software is usually hours: connect the sending account, the calendar and the CRM, and write the rules for replies. Deciding what to feed it takes longer and matters more. Before you connect anything, you need an ideal customer profile, a list of the signals you want it to act on and a message that has already earned replies when sent by hand.

### Does an AI SDR need a CRM?

Not to run, but you will want one within a month. The AI SDR produces conversations and meetings; without a CRM, the record of who said what lives inside the vendor's tool. Ask which CRMs are native integrations rather than connected through a third-party automation, and ask how you export the conversations if you leave.
