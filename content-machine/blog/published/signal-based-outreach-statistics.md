## What reply rate does signal-based outreach get compared with cold email?

The honest answer: the cold email side is measured, the signal-based side mostly is not. [Instantly's 2026 benchmark report](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026) puts the average reply rate at 3.43% across its platform for 2025, with the top 25% of senders at 5.5% or better and the top 10% above 10.7%. That report does not split campaigns by whether they used a signal.

The "15 to 25% reply rate for signal-based outreach" figure is on most of the pages ranking for this query. [twelfth.agency](https://twelfth.agency/2026/04/01/signal-based-outbound/) attributes it to [Autobound's signal-based selling guide](https://www.autobound.ai/blog/signal-based-selling-complete-guide). I opened that guide: its only "15-25%" is an expected meeting booking rate for a scenario the guide itself labels illustrative, not measured. [LinkedOtter](https://www.linkedotter.com/articles/signal-based-outreach-5x-reply-rate-vs-cold-email-june-2026) credits the same range to "Salesforce and industry benchmarks" with no title or link, and [Salesforce's State of Sales 2026 announcement](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/state-of-sales-report-announcement-2026/) contains no reply-rate figure at all.

> [!WARNING]
> A reply-rate range that is a different vendor's illustration two citations back is not a benchmark. If you put that range in a plan, write "unverified vendor claim" next to it.

What I can give you is dated, first-hand LinkedIn numbers. [Our launch campaign](https://intentsly.com/case-studies) ran on our own signals and AI SDR and booked 14 qualified demos in 14 days with a reply rate 4x our previous cold campaigns. Across Intentsly customers in October 2026, AI SDR outreach triggered by LinkedIn engagement signals got a 21% reply rate, and accounts typically book 2 to 4 meetings a week; that is one product, one channel, one month, and it is ours, so weigh it accordingly.

## How many buyers are in-market at any given time?

About one in twenty. [LinkedIn's B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/it/it/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule) states the 95-5 rule as "95% of your potential buyers aren't ready to buy today", and backs it with purchase frequency: 75% of companies buy computers once every 4 years and 80% change banking services once every 5 years. The page is written for advertisers, which is why it argues for brand spend on the 95%.

For outbound the [same 95-5 number](https://business.linkedin.com/it/it/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule) cuts the other way. If 5% of a list is in-market, a signal is the only thing that tells you which 5%, and a list with no signal is 95% noise before you write a word. That is the whole case for [signal-based selling](/learn/what-is-signal-based-selling) in one statistic.

## How far along are buyers before they talk to a seller?

More than halfway, and with a favourite already picked. [6sense's 2026 Global B2B Buyer Experience Report](https://6sense.com/buyer-experience-report-2026) says buyers first engage sellers roughly 59% of the way through an average 10.3-month cycle, after 91.4% have already ranked their shortlist, and the pre-contact favourite wins 82% of the time. The buyer's team initiated 82% of first vendor contacts.

The [2025 edition](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251112018032/en/), from more than 4,000 buyers, had the preferred vendor winning 80% of deals and 94% of buying groups ranking vendors before first contact, with cycles shortening from about 11 months in 2024 to 10 in 2025. Two years of the same vendor's survey pointing the same way is not independent confirmation, but it is consistent.

> [!NOTE]
> 6sense sells intent data, so a finding that buyers decide before sales hears from them is also its sales pitch. The sample sizes are large and the questions are published, which is more than most of the numbers in this category offer.

The practical reading: a buyer who is [59% through their cycle](https://6sense.com/buyer-experience-report-2026) when they email you has been leaving signals for months. Liking a competitor's post, commenting on a pain-point thread, following a vendor page, posting a "what do people use for X" question. Those are the moments to show up, and [the 12 LinkedIn buying signals worth chasing](/blog/linkedin-buying-signals) lists the ones we act on.

## Do sellers have the time to work signals by hand?

Not according to the sellers. [Salesforce's State of Sales 2026](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/state-of-sales-report-announcement-2026/), a survey of 4,050 sales professionals in 22 countries run in August and September 2025, found the average seller spends 40% of their time selling, nearly one full day a week on prospecting, and 48% say they lack the bandwidth for adequate cold outreach. In the same survey 55% already use AI for prospecting.

On the buyer side, [Gartner's March 2026 release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience) (646 B2B buyers, surveyed August to September 2025) found 67% prefer a rep-free experience and 45% used AI during a recent purchase. Fewer buyers want a rep in the early stages, so the rep who does reach out needs a reason the buyer recognises, which is what a signal gives you.

This is the step where a tool earns its place: watching the engagement on competitor and industry posts every day, scoring each person against your customer profile and sending the first message is work a two-person team cannot do by hand at any volume, and it is exactly what [Intentsly's LinkedIn signals](/features/linkedin-signals) do for an account.

## What do the sourced numbers mean for a small team?

Put the surviving figures together and a plan writes itself. Around 5% of your market is in-market now, buyers pick a favourite before they contact anyone, and cold email to the other 95% averages a 3.43% reply rate even for teams that send at scale, per the [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026), [LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/it/it/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule) and [6sense](https://6sense.com/buyer-experience-report-2026) pages above.

1. **Pick two or three signals you can see every day.** For most small teams that is engagement on competitor and industry posts on LinkedIn, plus posts that name your problem. The [7-step setup guide](/guides/set-up-signal-based-outbound) covers the choice.
2. **Set a reply-rate floor before you start.** Use 3.43% as the floor a cold list would give you and 10.7% as what the best cold senders reach, both from [Instantly's report](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026). A signal-based campaign that cannot beat the floor is a targeting problem, not a copy problem.
3. **Measure your own number and date it.** Our launch campaign booked 14 qualified demos in 14 days with zero manual outreach and a reply rate 4x our previous cold campaigns. That line is useful because it has a date and a method behind it; make yours the same.
4. **Keep volume low and relevance high.** Intentsly caps each LinkedIn account at 30 messages and 30 connection requests a day and recommends 15, because a signal-based list is small by design. The [small-team model](/blog/signal-based-outbound-for-small-teams) shows how 2 to 5 people run it.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> A founder comments under a competitor's pricing announcement: "We looked at this last quarter, the per-seat model killed it for us." That person is in-market, has a shortlist, and has told you the objection. One message that answers the objection beats a hundred to the list.

## Where these numbers come from, and what I did not find

I opened each source on 26 November 2026. Instantly's report gives no count of emails or accounts, only "billions" of interactions. 6sense and Salesforce publish sample sizes but not margins of error. Gartner's full report is behind a client wall, so the press release is the only public figure. The 2011 Harvard Business Review study on lead response time is cited everywhere for "respond within an hour"; I could only open its catalogue record, not the paper, so its numbers are not on this page.

If you run signal-based outreach on LinkedIn and have a dated reply rate from your own accounts, that is more useful than anything in the second table. ## FAQ

### What is a good reply rate for signal-based outreach?

There is no published benchmark that isolates signal-based campaigns. The nearest measured reference is cold email, where [Instantly's 2025 platform data](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026) shows a 3.43% average and 10.7% or more for the top 10% of senders. Across Intentsly customers in October 2026, signal-triggered LinkedIn outreach replied at 21%; treat that as one vendor's own figure.

### Is the headline reply-rate range on other pages real?

I could not trace it to a measurement. The pages that cite it point to [Autobound's guide](https://www.autobound.ai/blog/signal-based-selling-complete-guide), where 15-25% is an illustrative meeting booking rate for a scenario, or to unnamed "industry benchmarks". It may describe some teams' results, but nobody has shown their data for it, so do not plan around it.

### How many B2B buyers are in-market right now?

About 5%. [LinkedIn's B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/it/it/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule) states the 95-5 rule as 95% of potential buyers not being ready to buy today, based on how rarely companies purchase in a category. For outbound that means a list without a signal is mostly people who will not reply for a good reason.

### How early do buyers decide on a vendor?

Before they contact one, most of the time. [6sense's 2026 report](https://6sense.com/buyer-experience-report-2026) found 91.4% of buying groups rank a shortlist before first contact, first contact happens about 59% of the way through a 10.3-month cycle, and the pre-contact favourite wins 82% of the time. Reaching a buyer earlier means reading their signals, not waiting for a form fill.

### Are these statistics biased because vendors produced them?

Partly, and the table says which ones. Instantly sells email tooling, 6sense sells intent data, Salesforce sells sales software, Intentsly sells LinkedIn signals. Gartner and the LinkedIn B2B Institute have less direct skin in the game. Sample sizes and dates are listed so you can decide how much weight each deserves.

### Does signal-based outreach work for a team of one or two?

The sourced numbers argue it works better for small teams than volume does, because 5% of a market is a list a small team can actually work. Sellers already report lacking bandwidth for cold outreach ([Salesforce, 2026](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/state-of-sales-report-announcement-2026/)), and a signal-based list is small by design. Our own launch was run by the product, not a team.
