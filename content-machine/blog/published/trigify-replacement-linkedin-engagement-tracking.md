A Trigify replacement for LinkedIn engagement tracking has to do one thing: tell you who liked or commented on the posts you care about, soon after it happened, so you can start a conversation. Trigify's Social Signals stop for most customers at 11:59pm BST on 22 October 2026. If you want the signal and the LinkedIn outreach in one tool, look at Intentsly, Gojiberry or Valley. If your team's own posts are the source, Teamfluence. If you feed Clay or a sequencer, Jungler or PhantomBuster.

This article covers that single job. For the whole product, including Listening searches and workflows, see our list of [Trigify alternatives](/blog/trigify-alternatives). For exports and dates, see [what ends when Trigify shuts down](/blog/trigify-shutting-down).

## Which Trigify signals are you actually replacing?

Trigify's help centre lists 16 Social Signals types, and six of them are engagement on posts. [The "What is Social Signals?" article](https://help.trigify.io/articles/1828448-what-is-social-signals) names them as "Liked competitor content", "Liked tracked company content", "Liked tracked person content", "Commented on tracked content", "Engaged with tracked topic" and "Competitor engagement". The other ten are role changes, hiring, topic posts and company-level events, which this article leaves alone.

Three details of how Trigify ran that job decide what a replacement must match. [The set-up guide](https://help.trigify.io/articles/4257490-set-up-social-signals-monitoring) says you added people and companies by pasting profile URLs or uploading a CSV, chose a cadence of daily, weekly or hourly, and the first run looked back 30 days. [The limits article](https://help.trigify.io/articles/9028799-what-are-the-limits-on-social-signals-and-why-did) says a Starter plan monitored up to 50 profiles with 2 signal types and Max up to 5,000 profiles with 10 signal types. And the signal could start a workflow: [the Social Signals trigger](https://help.trigify.io/articles/9139430-social-signals-trigger) fired a payload with the signal type, the originating post and the matched person into whatever came next, such as the HTTP Request node to Clay or the native HubSpot, Attio, Salesforce and Airtable nodes described in [Trigify's export guide](https://help.trigify.io/en/articles/3268880-how-do-i-get-my-data-into-clay-my-crm).

Write down your answer to three questions before you compare tools: whose posts (competitors, industry leaders, your own team, or a handful of specific post URLs), how many profiles, and who sends the message afterwards. The table below is organised around those three.

One more thing to know before you shop: Trigify's pricing page is gone. [trigify.io/pricing](https://www.trigify.io/pricing) now shows the HubSpot announcement with no plan figures. The ranking pages disagree about what it cost, with [Octolens](https://octolens.com/trigify-alternatives) and [Valley](https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/best-tools-to-message-linkedin-post-engagers) quoting $40 to $199 a month and [Clearcue](https://clearcue.ai/blog/trigify-alternatives-linkedin-intent-signal-tools) quoting $149 to $549, so use your own last invoice as the budget line.

**You tracked competitor and industry-leader profiles and messaged the people who engaged.** This is the set-up Intentsly is built for. Valley does it for profiles you track and your own posts, with every message reviewed before it goes.

**Your team's own posts were the source, and leads belonged in HubSpot or Slack.** Teamfluence is the closest shape. Its [pricing page](https://teamfluence.com/pricing) lists a Trigify price match and a free migration service for new customers, which is worth asking about before 22 October.

**Trigify fed a Clay table or a sequencer and you want to keep that.** Jungler is the only option here that both monitors profiles continuously and says it needs no login or cookie from you; its [homepage](https://jungler.ai/) lists Clay, Slack, Google Sheets, HeyReach, lemlist, Instantly, Smartlead and Expandi as destinations. PhantomBuster is cheaper but works post by post from URLs you supply, and the [Post Commenters guide](https://support.phantombuster.com/hc/en-us/articles/26970963368338-How-to-Use-the-LinkedIn-Post-Commenters-Export) says it needs your connected LinkedIn account, takes about 20 seconds per 10 comments and may not fully extract posts with tens of thousands of comments. Clay's own Find Post Audience action works the same way, one post at a time with your account connected, and [Clay's pricing page](https://www.clay.com/pricing) puts webhooks, CRM sync and the HTTP API on Growth, not Launch, so budget for that if your Trigify workflow pushed data onward.

**You only ever pulled the engagers of a few specific posts.** PhantomBuster or Clay alone. Do not pay for monitoring you will not use.

**Your Listening searches ran on Reddit, X, YouTube, podcasts or Hacker News.** None of the seven above replaces that from what their own sites say, and Intentsly does not monitor those sources. Ask each vendor for its source list in writing.

## What do you say to someone who engaged with a competitor's post?

The signal is public and recent, so the message should be short and should not pretend you were not watching. This template is ours; the brackets are the only parts you change.

```
Hi [first name], saw your comment on [competitor or leader]'s post about [topic]
yesterday. You asked about [the specific point they raised]. We hit the same
problem with [your customers' job] and ended up [one sentence on what you do
differently]. Happy to share what we learned if useful, no pitch attached.
```

Rules that keep it working: send within two days of the engagement, name the post, never quote their comment back to them in full, and make the first ask a conversation, not a demo. If the person only liked the post and did not comment, drop the second sentence and lead with the topic of the post instead. For the signals worth chasing in the first place, [the Buying Signal Playbook](/signal-playbook) lists 12 to prioritise and 8 to ignore.

If you would rather not write and send these by hand, Intentsly's AI SDR does this step: it opens the LinkedIn conversation with each scored engager, keeps it going and books the meeting through your calendar link, inside the 30-message daily cap per account, with 15 a day recommended. [See how the AI SDR works](/features/ai-sdr).

## What Trigify did well, and what you should keep from it

The team built a signal product people liked, and two habits from it are worth carrying over whatever you choose. First, filter before you act: Trigify's own export guide tells users to put an If Condition node before every integration so irrelevant posts never reach the CRM. Every tool above either scores leads for you (Intentsly, Gojiberry, Valley, Jungler's enrichment) or expects you to filter in a table (Clay, PhantomBuster); skip the filter and you will message people who liked a post for reasons that have nothing to do with buying. Second, keep the person-level view: Trigify's engagement signals named the person who liked or commented, and the message template above only works because you know which person did what.

## FAQ

### Can I export who liked or commented on a post from LinkedIn itself?
Not as a file. Clicking the reaction count on a post opens the list of people, and you can read it and copy names by hand, but LinkedIn offers no export button for likers or commenters, as [Valley's guide to messaging post engagers](https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/best-tools-to-message-linkedin-post-engagers) also notes. That is why the tools above either connect your account to read the list for you or, in Jungler's case, say they collect public engagement without your login. Decide which of those you are comfortable with first.

### Does a Trigify replacement need my LinkedIn login or cookie?
Most do. Intentsly, Gojiberry and Valley connect your LinkedIn account because they also send from it. PhantomBuster's guide requires a connected account or session cookie, and every action in Clay's LinkedIn Community integration is marked "Bring Your Own Account Required". Jungler is the one tool here whose site says it needs no cookies, extension or login. Ask any vendor not listed to state this in writing.

### Which tools track competitor posts continuously rather than one URL at a time?
Intentsly, Gojiberry, Valley (for profiles you track), Teamfluence (for posts you choose) and Jungler monitor profiles and collect new engagement as it appears. PhantomBuster and Clay work from post URLs or URNs you supply, although PhantomBuster's Watcher mode re-checks the same posts for new comments on a schedule. If you tracked more than a handful of profiles in Trigify, the first group saves you the most time.

### Will my Trigify Social Signals move to HubSpot?
No. [Trigify's announcement](https://www.trigify.io/) says the standalone platform is being retired, accounts will not be migrated to HubSpot, and monitoring, signal collection, integrations and API access stop when access ends. Save what you want from the Signals feed and your targets list before your end date, and email hugo@trigify.io if you need help exporting.

### How much will a Trigify replacement for engagement tracking cost?
From the vendors' own pages on 16 October 2026: Gojiberry Pro $99 a month, Jungler from $99, PhantomBuster Start $69 a month, Valley Starter $199 per seat, Teamfluence 449€ a month for a team, Clay from a free plan with paid tiers from $167 a month as shown on [its pricing page](https://www.clay.com/pricing). Intentsly's price is on [our pricing page](/pricing) and there is a 7-day free trial. Compare against your last Trigify invoice rather than the figures in alternatives lists, which disagree with each other.

### Is a like on a competitor's post really a buying signal?
On its own, no. It tells you the person follows the topic and the competitor, which makes them worth a look, not a pitch. Combine it with fit (do they match your ideal customer) and with a second signal where you can, such as a comment that describes a problem or a post of their own on the topic. The scoring step, whether a tool does it or you do, is what turns the list into a pipeline.
