> [!SUMMARY]
> - Fintech is bought when money moves differently than it did last quarter: a first finance hire, a round, a new country, a frozen account, a rail deadline, a new CFO, or a public "who do you use?".
> - All seven moments are visible on LinkedIn with a free account; the table below has the search and the message for each.
> - Run the week-one plan by hand for two weeks, count the replies, then decide whether to automate the finding.

The fastest way to find customers for a fintech startup is to reach a company in the week its money starts moving differently: a founder posting the first controller job, a finance lead complaining that a wire took five days, a payments head announcing a go-live date for instant payments. Those moments are public on LinkedIn, and whoever messages within a day, with one observation about the post, is in the conversation before a shortlist exists. Below: seven signals, a week-one plan, and when partners beat outreach.

## Why does the usual fintech customer acquisition advice stall?

Most pages on how to find customers for a fintech startup give the same list: define a niche, build trust with compliance badges, publish content, run founder-led LinkedIn posts, ask for referrals, find integration partners, buy ads. None of that is wrong, and most of it is slow. A finance team does not change its payment processor, its card programme or its lender because it read a good article. It changes when something forces the date: a new hire who owns the problem, money that arrived or stopped arriving, an entity in a new country, a regulator, or a provider that failed them in public.

Those forcing events are common. The Federal Reserve's 2026 Report on Employer Firms, built on 6,525 responses from small employer firms surveyed between September and November 2025, found that [60% of firms applied for financing in the prior twelve months](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/2026-report-on-employer-firms), 56% of them to cover operating expenses and 46% for expansion. The share of applicants who went to online lenders rose from 17% in the 2020 survey to 29% in 2025, and 60% of those online borrowers said the cost was higher than expected, against 37% at small banks. Read that as a market description: most small firms are shopping for money every year, a growing share tries a fintech, and many of them come away ready to switch again.

A buying signal is a public action that shows a company is in-market now; the [trigger event lesson](/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales) covers the general idea. Fintech has an advantage most categories lack: the people who buy it (founders, controllers, heads of finance, payments leads at banks and other fintechs) post about hiring, fundraising, expansion and provider failures as a matter of routine. The job is to read those posts before your competitors do.

Two filters keep the list honest. A signal older than two weeks is cold: the company has picked someone or decided to live with the problem. And a post from another fintech, a bank or an agency asking "who needs better payments?" is not a buyer, however many comments it has. Reddit, Slack communities and X carry the same requests, and the [Reddit method](/blog/find-b2b-leads-on-reddit) has the queries; treat them as manual channels, because Intentsly monitors LinkedIn only.

The deadline row has more volume than most founders expect, because the dates are set by regulators and rail operators and they are public. In the US, the Federal Reserve's FedNow team wrote on 15 July 2026 that [more than 1,800 financial institutions are live on the FedNow Service](https://www.frbservices.org/news/fed360/issues/soaring-volume-participation-three-years-fednow-service/), three years after launch, and new participants usually announce their go-live. In Europe, Finextra's timeline of the Instant Payments Regulation puts [sending instant payments and verification of payee from 9 October 2025 for euro-area payment providers](https://www.finextra.com/the-long-read/1471/europes-instant-payments-regulation-and-the-9-october-deadline-explained), with e-money and payment institutions in the euro area due to receive and send by 9 April 2027, and non-euro member states due to send by 9 July 2027. A payment institution posting about its April 2027 readiness is a buyer for anyone who sells payee verification, sanctions screening or instant-payment connectivity, and the deadline is the close date.

## How to find customers for a fintech startup by hand: a week-one plan

The plan takes about an hour a day with a free LinkedIn account. Sales Navigator makes the headcount and job-post steps faster but is not required. Pick one buyer type for the first two weeks; the three types below post in different places and need different first lines.

1. **Day 1: pick the buyer type and write the one-sentence offer.** Finance teams at companies of 20 to 500 people (spend, AP, treasury, payroll), owners of small firms (lending, invoicing, payments), or banks, payment institutions and other fintechs (rails, compliance, infrastructure). Then write the offer as "we do X for companies that just did Y". The [ideal customer profile lesson](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) is the longer version of this step.
2. **Day 2: build the saved searches.** Create the seven Posts-tab searches from the table with your market and product words added, each filtered to the past week, plus one Jobs search for the first-finance-hire roles. Bookmark them. The [hiring-signals routine](/guides/track-hiring-signals-for-sales) goes deeper on the job-post signal and the [search operators guide](/guides/linkedin-search-operators-for-prospecting) explains the quoting rules.
3. **Days 3 to 5: thirty minutes of reading, thirty of replying.** Run the searches, read the new posts, and for each real buyer do the "what to do" step from the table. Reply in public first, connect second. Send at most 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day; LinkedIn tolerates more, your acceptance rate does not.
4. **Day 6: follow up and find the person who signs.** For every accepted connection with no reply, send one message that adds something: a question about which countries they are collecting in, a line about the settlement window they mentioned. Never "just checking in". Where the person who posted is a finance analyst rather than the controller or founder, the [decision-maker guide](/guides/find-decision-makers-on-linkedin) has the steps to find who signs.
5. **Day 7: count.** Signals found, replies, calls booked. Fewer than ten relevant posts in a week means the product words are wrong, or your buyers do not post; widen the terms and add the Jobs tab before changing the buyer type.

Message for the provider-complaint signal, for copying:

```message
Saw your post about the payouts being held. The usual trigger at a company your size is a volume spike the processor's risk model did not expect, and it can be shortened with a reserve agreement. Happy to show how we handle that case for marketplaces, no demo deck.
```

> [!WARNING]
> Compliance is a trust question before it is a sales question. If your product touches customer funds or data, have the licence, the sponsor-bank relationship or the security report ready before you message a controller, because that is the first thing they will ask, and "in progress" ends the conversation.

A hypothetical example, to make the plan concrete: a six-person spend-management startup in the Netherlands adds "Dutch", "Benelux" and "expense" to the seven searches, runs a Jobs search for "controller" at companies of 50 to 500 people in the region, and sets alerts for new finance leaders at companies in its investors' portfolios. Its week-one list is a handful of funding posts, two first-controller job posts, one complaint about a frozen account and one request for a spend tool; the counts depend on how much the region posts. Finding those fifteen people each day is where the hour goes. If you want that step done for you, the [LinkedIn signals feature](/features/linkedin-signals) runs the same searches continuously and hands you the list already scored against your customer profile.

## Other ways to find fintech customers, and when they beat signals

Signals produce conversations with strangers. Three other routes earn their place, and for most fintech startups one of them comes first.

**Integration partners and marketplaces** beat signals when your product plugs into something the buyer already uses: an accounting platform, a commerce platform, a payroll provider, a bank's app store. The partner's listing puts you in front of buyers at the moment they search for "payments" or "expenses" inside a tool they already trust, and the partner has done the compliance vetting for them. The cost is that the partner sets the terms and the listing sits next to your competitors, which is why most fintechs run partner listings and signal outreach together. The [B2B SaaS page](/solutions/find-customers-for-b2b-saas) covers the partner route in more detail.

**Accountants, fractional CFOs and bookkeepers as referral partners** beat signals when you sell to owners of small firms, because the owner asks their accountant before they ask LinkedIn. A fractional CFO sees every client that outgrows its bank, every cash-flow gap and every botched payroll, and wants a product to hand the problem to. The [fractional CFO page](/solutions/find-clients-as-a-fractional-cfo) describes that partner's side of the relationship.

**Referrals from the first customers** beat signals for the first customer, almost always: an investor's portfolio, a former employer, a founder friend who will tolerate a product with rough edges. Ask after every successful month-end close or every clean payout run. The ceiling arrives when that network has been asked once, and the plan above is what you run when it has. Cold lists are the one tactic signals replace outright. Every company moves money, which is why a scraped list of "companies with 50 to 200 employees" converts so badly; a post says which of them changed how it moves money this month. ## FAQ

### How do fintech startups get their first customers?
Usually from the founders' own network: an investor's portfolio, a former employer, a founder friend who needs the product and will forgive early bugs. Ask that network for one scoped pilot, not a contract. Run the week-one plan in parallel from day one, so that when the network has been asked, companies that posted a first finance hire or a frozen account this week are already in conversation with you.

### Who is the buyer for a B2B fintech product?
It depends on the company's size. Under about 50 people, the founder signs and often runs finance from a bank login. From 50 to 500, a controller or head of finance evaluates and the founder or CFO signs. At banks and payment institutions, a payments product lead evaluates and a compliance officer can veto. Message the person who posted the signal, then use the company page to find who signs; they are often different people.

### How long is the fintech sales cycle?
A deadline-driven purchase (a payouts problem, an instant-payments date, a first payroll in a new country) can close in weeks because an outside event set the date. A platform change at a bank, or anything that moves customer funds, takes a quarter or more and includes a security review. Lead with the deadline product, and let the wider rollout follow once you are a vendor on file.

### Do you need SOC 2 or a licence before selling fintech?
You need whatever your product's regulatory position requires before you touch customer money or data, and the security evidence a controller will ask for on the first call. A fintech that only reads data can often sell on a security questionnaire and a clear data-handling answer; one that holds or moves funds needs the licence or the sponsor bank first. Have the honest answer ready before you message anyone.

### Is LinkedIn worth it for fintech sales?
For fintechs selling to founders, controllers and finance leaders at companies of 20 to 500 people, yes: those buyers post about hiring, rounds, expansion and provider failures every week. For fintechs selling to large banks, less so; the buyer rarely posts a need and the route is partners, conferences and procurement. Run the searches for two weeks and count before deciding.

### How do you sell to banks and credit unions as a startup?
Start with a public date. Rail go-lives, instant-payment deadlines and messaging migrations are announced by the institution and set by someone else, so the buyer has a budget and a calendar. Message the payments or product lead with the deadline in the first line and a clear statement of what your product covers. Expect a security review, a vendor-management process and a longer cycle than a startup buyer, and bring a reference institution as soon as you have one.
