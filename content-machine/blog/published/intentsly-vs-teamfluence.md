Intentsly vs Teamfluence comes down to whose network the signals come from and who answers the reply. Teamfluence captures what happens on your team's own LinkedIn accounts (profile visits, post reactions, company page follows), qualifies each person against your ideal customer profile and routes them to HubSpot or Salesforce, with unlimited seats. Intentsly watches other people's posts for buyers, scores them, and its AI SDR runs the LinkedIn conversation and books the meeting. Pick Teamfluence for a team that already posts; pick Intentsly to reach buyers you have never met.

Everything about Teamfluence below comes from teamfluence.com, opened on 30 October 2026, with a link on each claim. Everything about Intentsly comes from what the product does today, limits included. If you arrived here from Trigify's shutdown, [Trigify vs Teamfluence](/blog/trigify-vs-teamfluence) covers what moved over; this page covers the Intentsly pairing in depth.

## Where is Teamfluence the better choice?

Teamfluence does several things Intentsly does not do at all. If any of these is why you are shopping, Intentsly is the wrong pick.

**Your team already posts and you want the engagement turned into pipeline.** This is Teamfluence's whole premise: "Your team's Network is your team's Sales Pipeline" ([homepage](https://teamfluence.com/)). It captures who visited a teammate's profile, who followed the company page, who reacted to a colleague's post, and runs each one through ICP qualification. Intentsly does not watch your own accounts for visitors or followers; it watches other people's posts. If you have five people publishing every week, Teamfluence sees a stream Intentsly never looks at.

**You need more than two senders.** Teamfluence's pricing page sells unlimited seats on one plan and says every teammate can be added at no extra cost ([pricing page](https://teamfluence.com/pricing)). Its homepage example is a team of five sending 125 connection requests a day between them. Intentsly runs up to two LinkedIn accounts per workspace. For a sales team of six, that is one Teamfluence workspace or three Intentsly workspaces.

**You run Salesforce, Pipedrive or Clay.** Teamfluence names HubSpot and Salesforce as native integrations, with Slack alerts and Clay and generic webhooks ([homepage](https://teamfluence.com/)); its Pulse page adds Pipedrive for CRM sync, an API that can send connection requests and messages, and a native MCP server so Claude or ChatGPT can call your workspace directly ([Pulse](https://teamfluence.com/pulse)). Intentsly integrates natively with HubSpot, Cal.com, Google Calendar and LinkedIn, supports export, and lists no API.

**You want phone numbers as well as emails.** Teamfluence's pricing page lists work email for 9 in 10 leads plus phone numbers and company and role details, with phone lookups at 40 credits each ([pricing page](https://teamfluence.com/pricing)). Intentsly enriches with verified emails only.

**You want to build your own routing.** Teamfluence's help centre documents workflows with an ICP step, an enrichment step and a CRM sync step, plus templates such as "New Profile Visit - ICP Routing to DM or Network Campaign" ([help index](https://teamfluence.com/help)). Intentsly's path is fixed: signal, score, AI SDR, calendar link.

**You want a person on every send.** Teamfluence's homepage puts it plainly: "Then we tell you who to reach out to. You decide how." ([homepage](https://teamfluence.com/)). Its campaigns send a connection request and one follow-up message after acceptance ([networking campaigns](https://teamfluence.com/help/networking-campaign)); the conversation after that is yours. If your compliance team or your own nerves want a human in every thread, that design is the feature.

**You want EU hosting stated up front.** Teamfluence's Trigify comparison lists European hosting in Estonia as a difference ([Teamfluence vs Trigify](https://teamfluence.com/teamfluence-vs-trigify)). Hosting region is not a fact this page can state for Intentsly, so ask before you assume.

## Where is Intentsly the better choice?

**Buyers you have never met.** Teamfluence's signals are mostly people who already touched your team: visited a profile, followed the page, reacted to a post. That is warm, and it is also small if your team does not post much. Intentsly's [LinkedIn signals](/features/linkedin-signals) start from the other end: people liking, commenting on or sharing your competitors' and industry leaders' posts, people posting about the pain points and tools you care about, people following competitor pages. None of them has heard of you yet, which is the point of outbound.

**The lead gets an answer without you.** This is the row Teamfluence's pages leave blank. Its campaigns send a connection request and one follow-up after acceptance ([networking campaigns](https://teamfluence.com/help/networking-campaign)), and its homepage says you decide how to reach out. In Intentsly, the [AI SDR](/features/ai-sdr) holds the LinkedIn conversation in your place and sends your Cal.com or Google Calendar link so the lead books the meeting. If your inbox is the bottleneck, this is the difference to weigh.

We ran this on ourselves at launch: our own signals and AI SDR, targeting founders who posted about cold outreach burnout, booked 14 qualified demos in 14 days with zero manual outreach, and the reply rate was 4x our previous cold campaigns. Most of those leads came from signals caught in real time rather than from a list.

**Monitoring without a per-user cap.** Teamfluence's help centre caps each user at 60 tracked profiles and 40 tracked posts ([FAQ](https://teamfluence.com/help/faq)), allows six influencers per active seat ([influencers](https://teamfluence.com/help/v2/pulse-monitor-influencers)), tracks a post for 2 months before you renew it ([post tracking](https://teamfluence.com/help/how-to-post-track)), and matches keywords only against posts from the profiles you track ([keyword tracking](https://teamfluence.com/help/v2/pulse-how-to-track-keywords)). Those are sensible limits for a team watching a short list of thought leaders. They are tight for a solo founder who wants to watch thirty competitors and a dozen topics. Intentsly's competitor, leader and keyword sources run continuously, and keyword posts are matched across LinkedIn rather than inside a tracked list.

**One person can run it.** Teamfluence's pricing and homepage are written for teams: unlimited seats, team-wide capture, a five-person example ([pricing page](https://teamfluence.com/pricing)). Intentsly is built for one or two accounts, and setup is self-serve with no sales call. If you are the whole sales team, you are the typical Intentsly user and an edge case for Teamfluence.

**A ceiling with a number on it.** Intentsly caps each account at 30 messages and 30 connection requests a day and recommends 15. Teamfluence's homepage example works out to 25 requests a day per person, but the help pages opened today print no per-account limit for campaigns ([homepage](https://teamfluence.com/)). If you want to know the ceiling before you connect your account, Intentsly tells you.

**Four outreach languages.** Intentsly monitors and writes in English, Romanian, German and Dutch. Teamfluence's pages opened today do not state supported languages, so ask before you assume either way.

**You can see your buyers before you sign up.** [/try-ai](/try-ai) shows people matching your profile who are engaging on LinkedIn right now, with no account. Teamfluence's pricing page points to a demo and a sales conversation ([pricing page](https://teamfluence.com/pricing)), and its Gojiberry comparison offers a 7-day trial ([Teamfluence vs Gojiberry](https://teamfluence.com/teamfluence-vs-gojiberry)).

## What does each tool cost?

Teamfluence's pricing page, headed "Pay for leads, not for seats" and marked "Starting October 1st, 2026", lists two plans, a Regular Unlimited Plan and a Small Unlimited Plan, and prints one price: 449 EUR a month for the Regular plan, billed monthly, with 1,000 qualified leads a month, unlimited seats, 20,000 credits and up to 100,000 contacts in your network. The Small plan shows no figure on the page. Extra leads and credits come as additional monthly buckets with no price shown, and a fair usage policy applies ([pricing page](https://teamfluence.com/pricing)).

Credits are how the real cost moves. The same page lists a new lead at 1 credit (repeat detections free), a signal such as a profile visit, like or follow at 1 credit, a workflow job at 1, an AI job at 1, an email at 4 and a phone number at 40, with bring-your-own API keys as an option ([pricing page](https://teamfluence.com/pricing)). If your team's accounts generate a lot of engagement, the credit budget is the number to watch rather than the plan price.

Two other Teamfluence pages still show different figures today. The Pulse page says "Starting at $99/month", and the Gojiberry comparison prints an Economy plan at 89 EUR per seat and a Business plan at 129 EUR per seat ([Pulse](https://teamfluence.com/pulse), [Teamfluence vs Gojiberry](https://teamfluence.com/teamfluence-vs-gojiberry)). Treat the pricing page as current and confirm on the demo call; prices change, and the third-party reviews that rank for this query all quote the older per-seat numbers.

Intentsly: see the [pricing page](/pricing). There is a 7-day free trial, setup is self-serve with no sales call, and you can cancel anytime.

The useful comparison is cost per booked meeting, not per month. For Teamfluence, add the time your team spends answering replies and the credits each lead, signal and email consumes. For Intentsly, the ceiling is two accounts at 30 messages a day, so the question is whether that volume of AI-run conversations books the meetings you need.

## Can you use both?

On the same LinkedIn account, be careful. Both tools send from your profile, and LinkedIn counts every action against one set of limits whichever tool sent it. Intentsly's 30-a-day caps are per account for that reason. Teamfluence's homepage example is 25 requests a day per person ([homepage](https://teamfluence.com/)). Run both on one profile and neither can keep you under the line.

On different jobs, yes, and the split is natural. Teamfluence covers inbound engagement on your team's accounts and routes it to the CRM; Intentsly covers buyers engaging elsewhere on LinkedIn and has the AI SDR talk to them. Both connect to HubSpot, so the CRM stays the single record. What you would not get is one inbox: Teamfluence's campaigns and DMs run from your teammates' accounts, and Intentsly's AI SDR runs its own conversations from the account you give it.

The manual version of Intentsly's half is also worth knowing: the [competitor monitoring guide](/guides/monitor-competitors-on-linkedin) shows how to watch competitor posts by hand in an hour a week, and [tracking competitor post engagers](/blog/track-competitor-linkedin-post-engagers) covers the three ways to pull the people who reacted. If that routine is where your week goes, Intentsly runs those sources continuously and scores the results so you only see the people worth a message.

## How do you switch from Teamfluence to Intentsly?

Teamfluence's Prospect Tracker exports selected or all prospects to CSV from the bulk action menu, and a workflow can sync qualified leads to your CRM ([Prospect Tracker](https://teamfluence.com/help/prospect-tracker/)). Before you cancel, pull out:

1. Your prospects with their status and tags, as CSV or into HubSpot, so the history stays in the CRM.
2. Your ICP criteria from the AI Agent: the persona and company rules that produced the matches.
3. The list of tracked profiles, influencers and keywords, which is the part that carries over.
4. The follow-up message that got the best acceptance rate, for reference.
5. Any open conversation from a campaign, so nobody waiting on a teammate goes cold.

Intentsly finds people from live signals rather than an imported list, so items 2 and 3 carry over, not the prospect file. Set the same competitors, leaders and keywords up as [LinkedIn signals](/features/linkedin-signals), connect the same HubSpot, and the scoring runs against the profile you already know works.

## Which should you pick?

If your team already posts and you want the resulting engagement qualified and routed to Salesforce or HubSpot, need more than two senders, want phone numbers, want to build your own workflows or want a person in every thread: Teamfluence. If you are one or two people, your buyers are reacting to other people's posts rather than yours, and you want the reply answered and the meeting booked without you in the inbox: Intentsly.

Still unsure? The shortest test is to see your own buyers. Open [/try-ai](/try-ai), which shows matching people engaging on LinkedIn right now, no account needed. If the list is thin, your buyers are not where Intentsly looks and Teamfluence's inbound capture is the better bet. If it is full, start the 7-day trial and let the AI SDR take the first conversations. Either way you have an answer this week. Other pairings are on the [compare index](/compare).

## FAQ

### Does Teamfluence send LinkedIn messages?
Partly. Its networking campaigns send connection requests and one follow-up message after a request is accepted, workflow templates can route a lead to a DM step, and the Pulse page says the API can send requests and direct messages ([networking campaigns](https://teamfluence.com/help/networking-campaign)). No page opened today describes it answering replies. Intentsly's AI SDR holds the conversation and sends your calendar link.

### How much does Teamfluence cost per month?
Its pricing page, marked as starting 1 October 2026, prints 449 EUR a month for the Regular Unlimited Plan with 1,000 qualified leads, unlimited seats and 20,000 credits, and shows no price for the Small plan ([pricing page](https://teamfluence.com/pricing)). Other Teamfluence pages still show older per-seat figures, so confirm on the demo call. Intentsly's plans are on its [pricing page](/pricing).

### Does Teamfluence work for a solo founder?
It can, but its pricing and product pages are written for teams: unlimited seats, team-wide signal capture and a five-person example ([pricing page](https://teamfluence.com/pricing)). Its per-user caps of 60 tracked profiles and 40 tracked posts also matter more when one person does all the watching. Intentsly is built around one or two accounts and self-serve setup.

### Can Teamfluence track competitor posts?
Yes, within limits. You add competitor or influencer profiles, choose which of their posts to track, and everyone who reacts or comments is captured for 2 months before you renew ([influencers](https://teamfluence.com/help/v2/pulse-monitor-influencers)). Each user can track 60 profiles and 40 posts, and six influencers per active seat. Intentsly watches competitor and leader posts continuously without a per-user list.

### Can I export my leads from Teamfluence?
Yes. The Prospect Tracker help page describes exporting selected or all prospects to CSV from the bulk action menu, and a workflow step can sync qualified leads to HubSpot or Salesforce ([Prospect Tracker](https://teamfluence.com/help/prospect-tracker/)). Intentsly also supports export of leads and signals. Moving between them means rebuilding ICP rules and signal sources, not importing the prospect file.

### Which is safer for my LinkedIn account?
Neither publishes independent restriction statistics, so judge by what each states. Intentsly prints its ceiling: 30 messages and 30 connection requests per account per day, with 15 recommended. Teamfluence's homepage example is five people sending 125 requests a day, and its help pages opened today print no per-account campaign limit ([homepage](https://teamfluence.com/)). Running both on one profile is the riskiest option.
