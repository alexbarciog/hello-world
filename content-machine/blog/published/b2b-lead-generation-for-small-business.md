B2B lead generation for small businesses works when you start from buyers who have already said what they need. Every week, owners post on LinkedIn about the problem you solve: a founder asking who to call about their website, an operations lead complaining about their IT provider, a company announcing a hire your service supports. Message them the week they post and you are answering, not interrupting. Below: six signals that show a company is buying now, a week-one plan that fits in five hours, and when other routes win.

## Why does B2B lead generation for small businesses stall?

Time, not tactics. In Fiverr's [2025 small business survey](https://www.fiverr.com/news/small-business-survey-2025) of nearly 6,000 owners across 25 countries, 70% said they spend less than five hours a week on marketing. The pages that rank for this query list twelve or fifteen channels, each of which assumes a person whose job it is. For a company of one to twenty people, the owner is that person, between delivering the work and sending the invoices.

The second problem is the list. Buying a database of 2,000 companies that fit your profile tells you who could buy; it says nothing about who is buying this quarter. Most of those companies are fine with their current supplier, so the replies that come back are mostly "not now". A buying signal is a public action that shows someone is in-market now; the [buying signal lesson](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal) has the definition and a test you can apply to any post.

Small businesses have one advantage here. Your buyers are mostly small businesses too: the SBA's Office of Advocacy counts [36,207,130 small businesses](https://advocacy.sba.gov/2026/02/03/advocacy-releases-frequently-asked-questions-about-small-businesses-2026/) in the United States, 99.9% of all businesses, employing 45.9% of private-sector workers. In a company that size, the person posting about the problem is usually the person who signs. There is no procurement team between you and the decision.

## Where do small business B2B buyers show up on LinkedIn?

Each row is a behaviour you can see with a free LinkedIn account. The search column is what to type in the LinkedIn search bar before switching to the Posts tab and filtering to the past week; replace the bracketed words with your own category. The last column is the message, written to quote the post rather than introduce your company.

| Signal | Where | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| An owner or manager describes the problem in public ("our books are a mess", "third outage this month", "our site takes forever to load") | Posts tab: `"anyone else" [problem words]` or `"so frustrated with" [category]`, past week | The pain is current and unassigned; the buyer has not yet decided whether to fix it in-house or hire | Reply in the thread with one specific thing to check, then connect with a note that quotes the post. No pitch until they ask what you do |
| Someone asks for a recommendation ("can anyone recommend a bookkeeper for a small agency?") | Posts tab: `"can anyone recommend"` or `"looking for a"` or `"who do you use for"` plus [category], past week | The decision to buy is made; they are choosing between names this week | Answer with one useful criterion for choosing, not your name; connect the same day; offer a 20-minute call with no proposal attached |
| A company hires for the role your service supports or replaces (first marketing hire, office manager, IT lead) | Jobs search for the title, sorted by date; Posts tab: `"we're hiring" [title]` | Budget exists for the function; a vendor can start before the hire does, or cover what one hire cannot | Message the owner, not HR; name the role and offer to cover the first deliverable while they recruit |
| A buyer comments or asks a question under a competitor's post or an industry voice's post | The comment sections of the ten competitors and ten industry accounts your buyers follow | They are researching your category in public; a competitor has their attention but not yet their business | Reply to their comment with something the post left out; connect with a note that refers to the thread, never to the competitor |
| A new business, new location, funding or "we just launched" post | Posts tab: `"excited to announce" [industry]` or `"opening our"` or `"we raised"`, past week | New money and a plan that outruns the team; the first outside help they buy is usually operational | Wait a day for the congratulations to pass, then send one observation about the thing they will hit first and ask who owns it |
| A complaint about a tool or vendor in your category ("switching from [tool]", "our agency ghosted us") | Posts tab: `"switching from"` or `"looking to replace"` or `"anyone moved off"` plus [tool or category] | They have decided to change supplier; the window is days to weeks | Ask what broke, in one sentence, before saying anything about yourself; the answer tells you whether you are a fit |

Two filters keep the list short. A post older than two weeks is cold: the buyer has chosen someone or moved on. And a post from another vendor describing the same problem is marketing, not a lead. Reddit and X carry the recommendation and complaint signals too and are worth a weekly manual pass; Intentsly monitors LinkedIn only.

The words in brackets decide whether the searches return buyers or noise. "Business services" returns nothing usable; "Xero bookkeeping for agencies" or "managed IT for dental practices" does. The [ideal customer profile lesson](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) is the longer version of choosing them.

## A week-one plan that fits in five hours

The plan assumes a free LinkedIn account and one person. Sales Navigator helps with the jobs step and saved searches, but it is not required in week one.

1. **Day 1, one hour: write the one-sentence offer and list twenty accounts.** "Monthly bookkeeping for marketing agencies with 5 to 30 staff" works; "accounting services" does not, because the searches only return buyers when they contain the words buyers use. Then list ten competitors and ten industry voices your buyers follow. The people commenting under those twenty accounts are your first list.
2. **Day 2, one hour: build the saved searches.** Create the six Posts-tab searches from the table, each filtered to the past week, plus one Jobs search for the titles your service supports, sorted by date. Bookmark all seven. Open the latest posts from the twenty accounts and note which commenters run companies in your profile.
3. **Days 3 to 5, forty minutes a day: read, then reply.** Run the searches, read the new posts, and for each real buyer do the "what to do" step from the table. Reply in public first, connect second. Send at most 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day. LinkedIn tolerates more; your acceptance rate does not, and in a small business your profile is the company.
4. **Day 6, thirty minutes: follow up with something new.** For every accepted connection with no reply, send one message that adds something: a benchmark from your category, a one-paragraph look at their public footprint, a question only someone who has done the work would ask. Never "just checking in".
5. **Day 7, thirty minutes: count.** Signals found, replies, calls booked. Fewer than ten relevant posts in a week means the bracketed words are wrong or your buyers do not post; widen the words before changing anything else. The [qualification check](/guides/qualify-leads-before-outreach) is the five-minute test to run before each call.

Message template for the recommendation signal, for copying:

```
Saw your post asking about [category]. Whoever you pick, two things
worth checking first: [check A] and [check B]. If either comes back
the way I expect, the fix is usually [one sentence]. Happy to walk
through it on a 20-minute call, no proposal attached.
```

The [LinkedIn outreach message guide](/guides/write-linkedin-outreach-message) covers the follow-up sequence and the mistakes that get a small vendor ignored. The hour that disappears every week is finding the right fifteen people; if you want that step done for you, the [LinkedIn signals feature](/features/linkedin-signals) runs the same searches continuously and hands you the list already scored against your profile.

## Other ways to find B2B leads for a small business

Signals produce conversations with strangers who need you now. Three other routes earn their place, and for an established business one of them probably comes first.

**Referrals and past customers** beat signals once you have a dozen happy clients. Ask at the moment the result lands, and ask for a specific introduction ("do you know another agency owner who does their own books?") rather than "anyone you know". The ceiling is the size of the network, which is why small businesses add outbound once the referral list is tapped.

**Partnerships with adjacent vendors** beat signals when another small business already talks to your buyers every month: the accountant who sees every agency's cash flow, the web developer who hears about every slow site. One or two partners who send each other work can outproduce a quarter of outreach. They are slow to set up and depend on the partner's goodwill, so they rarely cover a dry month.

**Paid tools and agencies** beat signals when you have budget but no hours at all. LinkedIn's own [Sales Navigator plans page](https://business.linkedin.com/sales-solutions/compare-plans.html) lists Core at US$119.99 a month and Advanced at US$159.99, with 50 InMails a month on Core and more than 50 search filters; the [alerts guide](/guides/set-up-linkedin-sales-navigator-alerts) shows how to turn the week-one searches into notifications. Outsourcing the whole job costs more: Belkins, an outbound agency, says on its [own site](https://belkins.io/blog/small-business-lead-generation-companies) that its packages start from US$5,000. An agency makes sense when one new customer is worth several times that; for a business selling a few hundred dollars a month, the arithmetic rarely works.

Cold lists are the one tactic signals replace outright. A list of 2,000 company owners says who could hire you; a post about the vendor who just let one of them down says who is hiring this month. ## FAQ

### How do small businesses generate B2B leads without a sales team?
Pick one channel and one hour a day. The six LinkedIn searches in the table above surface companies that have said in public they need what you sell, and a reply that quotes their post gets a conversation without a cold pitch. One person running the week-one plan books calls; the remaining work is turning up to them. Add a second channel only once the first produces replies every week.

### Is LinkedIn worth it for a small business, and do I need Sales Navigator?
LinkedIn is worth it if your buyers post there; the week-one plan tells you within seven days. Sales Navigator is not required: the Posts tab, past-week filter and Jobs search are free. Pay for it when you want the searches to alert you instead of running them by hand, or when you are tracking more than twenty accounts. Its pricing is listed on LinkedIn's own plans page, linked above.

### How much does B2B lead generation cost a small business?
Done by hand, it costs about five hours a week and nothing else. The paid routes, with the prices linked in the section above, run from a Sales Navigator licence to an agency retainer in the thousands. The right comparison is against what one customer is worth to you over a year: if that figure is in the hundreds, keep it manual or use a tool; if it is in the tens of thousands, an agency can pay for itself.

### Should a small business buy a B2B lead list?
Usually not as a first step. A purchased list tells you who fits your profile, not who is buying now, so most replies are "not now" and the time goes into chasing them. Build your own list from signals instead; the [prospect list guide](/guides/build-b2b-prospect-list) shows how to keep it to companies that have shown one. Buy a list only to fill a profile you already know converts.

### How many leads a month does a small business need?
Work backwards from one customer. If one in three replies becomes a call and one in three calls becomes a customer, one new customer a month needs about nine replies, which is a few relevant posts a day with a good message. Count signals found, replies and calls each week, and fix the earliest step that falls short before changing anything downstream.

### How long before lead generation produces paying customers?
Week one should produce replies and a call or two, not a signed contract. Someone who asked for a recommendation this week decides within days; a company that just made a hire takes a month or two to buy around it. Once calls book every week, the first customer is weeks away. Keep the searches running while you deliver, so the pipeline does not empty when the project ends.
