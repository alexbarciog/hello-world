LinkedIn keyword monitoring for leads means being told when someone posts "what do you use for X" or "alternative to [competitor]", so you can reply while the post is live. LinkedIn will not do this for you: its notifications fire only when you are tagged. You have three routes: bookmark a few post searches and read them daily (free), use Sales Navigator's content filters if you already pay for it, or pay a watcher tool. Which one depends on how many matching posts your market produces a week.

## What LinkedIn itself will and will not tell you about keyword posts

Start with the limits, because they explain why this category of tools exists. [Buska's LinkedIn page](https://www.buska.io/platforms/linkedin) puts it plainly: LinkedIn's native notifications fire when someone tags your name or your company page. A post that says "we're replacing [your competitor] next quarter" without tagging anyone reaches nobody's inbox. [Octolens makes the same point](https://octolens.com/linkedin-monitoring) and builds its LinkedIn pitch on it.

Sales Navigator's Buyer Intent does not close the gap. [LinkedIn's own guide to Buyer Intent](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/resources/sales-terms/buyer-intent) lists what feeds it: following your company, visits to your company page and website, profile visits to your sellers or leadership, new connections to your team, lead-form completions, ad engagement and accepted InMails. Every item is interest in you. Nothing on the list is a keyword in a post.

Third-party tools work with public posts only. [Brand24's LinkedIn monitoring article](https://brand24.com/blog/?p=155185) states that LinkedIn's API does not give tools access to private groups, restricted posts or direct messages, and [Linked API's social listening guide](https://linkedapi.io/guides/linkedin-social-listening) adds that LinkedIn's official APIs centre on your own Page's content, so anything that searches posts across LinkedIn by keyword is using a logged-in search or a public-web sample, not an official keyword feed. That matters for the "does it need my login" column in the table below.

## Step 1: Write the keyword list before you choose a route

The keywords decide whether you get buyers or noise, and the list is the same whichever route you take. Write ten to fifteen phrases in four groups. These are examples for a company selling outbound software; swap in your own category, competitors and job.

| Group | What it catches | Example phrases |
|---|---|---|
| Asking for a tool | A buyer with a budget and a deadline | "what do you use for", "any recommendations for", "looking for a tool" plus your category word |
| Leaving a competitor | A buyer already in motion | "alternative to [competitor]", "moving off [competitor]", "[competitor] pricing" |
| Describing the pain | A buyer who has not named a solution yet | "cold outreach isn't working", "reply rates dropped", "tired of buying lists" |
| Category terms | Vendors, students and your peers, mostly | "intent data", "signal-based selling" |

Track the first three groups. Track category terms only if the other three return almost nothing, because category words are where the vendors and the job posts live. On LinkedIn the biggest noise sources are hiring posts, event promotion and announcements, so whichever route you use, plan to exclude "hiring", "we're hiring", "webinar", "join us", "excited to announce" and "thrilled to". Boolean support varies by tool, so where you cannot use NOT, read past them.

Keep the list in a text file, not inside a tool. Monitoring tools in this category change and close often: the [Trigify shutdown](/blog/trigify-shutting-down) is the most recent example we have written up, and one page still ranking for this exact search on 31 October 2026 (a "monitor keywords on LinkedIn" page on triggify.io) now redirects to an unrelated website. The list is the asset; the tool is the delivery.

## Step 2: The free route, LinkedIn post search as a daily routine

LinkedIn's post search is a usable monitor if you accept that you have to open it. The routine:

1. In the LinkedIn search bar, type one phrase in quotes, for example `"alternative to hubspot"`, and press Enter.
2. Click **Posts** in the filter row.
3. Open **Date posted** and choose **Past 24 hours**. Open **Sort by** and choose **Latest**.
4. Bookmark the results page. Repeat for each phrase, so you end with eight to twelve bookmarks in a folder.
5. Each morning, open the folder and read. A phrase with no new posts takes five seconds; a busy one takes a minute. Log each match (author, headline, post link, phrase, date) in a sheet before you do anything else.

Two behaviours of LinkedIn search shape how you read the results. First, [Linked API's guide](https://linkedapi.io/guides/linkedin-social-listening), which is built on the same logged-in search, says LinkedIn widens a narrow query on its own and returns loosely related posts, so a non-empty result page does not mean every post contains your phrase. Read the post, not the count. Second, [Favikon's post-search article](https://www.favikon.com/blog/how-to-search-for-any-post-on-linkedin) notes that native keyword matching is literal and that post results have no filters for follower count, engagement, industry or seniority. Fit has to be judged by hand, from the author's headline, which is why the log sheet has a headline column.

If your bookmarks fill with posts from one or two people (usually influencers who repeat your phrase every week), skip them; they are not buyers, and their comment sections are a better place to look than their posts. If a bookmark returns nothing for two weeks, replace the phrase. If a bookmark returns twenty posts a day, that phrase is too broad or your market is busy enough for the next route.

A second free input is Google. Searching `site:linkedin.com/posts "alternative to hubspot"` returns public LinkedIn posts Google has indexed, and the same query can be saved as a Google Alert. The same Favikon article states the limit: coverage is incomplete, because Google indexes only part of LinkedIn. Treat it as a weekly supplement, not the monitor.

## Step 3: If you pay for Sales Navigator, use the content filters

Sales Navigator approaches the problem from the person's side rather than the post's side. Vendor walkthroughs of its lead search, such as [Findymail's filter guide](https://www.findymail.com/blog/sales-navigator-search-filters) (updated May 2026) and [Skylead's help article](https://help.skylead.io/hc/en-us/articles/28810343073937-How-to-Use-LinkedIn-and-Sales-Navigator-Filters), describe a **Posted content keywords** filter that returns leads who have posted content containing your keywords, and a Spotlight for leads who have **posted on LinkedIn in the last 30 days**. LinkedIn renames and moves filters, so check both names in your own account before building a routine on them. [LinkedIn's own how-to page](https://business.linkedin.com/sales-solutions/sales-navigator/how-to-use-linkedin-sales-navigator) says saved lead searches notify you when there are new results, which is the alert mechanism.

The practical setup is one saved search per keyword group from Step 1, combined with your geography, headcount and seniority filters, with the Posted content keywords filter carrying the phrases joined by OR. The order in which saved searches, lists and alerts should be built so they actually fire is in [the Sales Navigator alerts guide](/guides/set-up-linkedin-sales-navigator-alerts).

Two limits. This route tells you that a person who matches your profile has posted about the topic; it does not hand you the post the hour it went live, so the "reply while it is fresh" part still depends on how often you open the saved search. And it costs a seat: [LinkedIn's plan comparison](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator/compare-plans) lists Core at US$119.99 a month and Advanced at US$159.99 a month per licence, which is more than most of the watcher tools below. If you already pay it, use it. If you do not, do not buy it for keyword monitoring alone.

## Step 5: Score the match, then reply to the post, not the person

A match is a post, not a lead. Before you message anyone, score it in the log sheet. This is a rule to copy, not a measured model:

| Question | Points |
|---|---|
| Phrase group was "asking for a tool" or "leaving a competitor" | 3 |
| Phrase group was "describing the pain" | 2 |
| Author's headline fits your ideal customer profile (role and company type) | 3 |
| Post is under 48 hours old | 2 |
| Post already has a reply from a competitor | 1 |

Act on 7 or more today, 5 to 6 this week, under 5 never. The decay matters: the value of a "what do you use for" post is mostly gone once the author has picked. The definitions behind the fit question are in [what is a buying signal](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal), and the full set of LinkedIn signals worth chasing, with their own scoring table, is in [LinkedIn buying signals](/blog/linkedin-buying-signals).

Then reply where the post is. A public comment that answers the question earns a connection request that gets accepted; a direct message that opens with "I saw your post about X" works when the post is a complaint rather than a question. Either way, quote the post, say one specific thing, ask one question, no link and no pitch in the first message. Working templates per signal, including the "asked for recommendations" case, are in [LinkedIn message templates for buying signals](/blog/linkedin-message-templates-for-buying-signals). If you would rather not write and send these by hand, Intentsly's AI SDR takes the scored keyword post, sends the LinkedIn message and keeps the thread going until the lead books; see [how the conversational AI handles replies](/features/conversational-ai).

## Which route? A rule by weekly volume

Run the free bookmarks for two weeks first, because they give you the number this rule needs: matching posts per week from authors who fit.

1. **Under five a week.** Keep the bookmarks. Ten minutes a day. No tool earns its fee here.
2. **Five to twenty a week, and you will write every reply yourself.** A watcher priced for one person: [Buska Starter at $49 a month](https://www.buska.io/platforms/linkedin) if you want intent scoring and Slack, [ParseStream Pro at $67](https://parsestream.com/linkedin-keyword-monitoring) if ten keywords are enough, per their pages today. Keep the bookmarks for a week in parallel and check the tool catches what you catch.
3. **Five to twenty a week, and the replies are the bottleneck.** This is where Intentsly is the right buy and a feed-only tool is not, because the match is only worth something if someone answers it the same day. When we ran our own signals and AI SDR on LinkedIn at launch, we booked 14 qualified demos in 14 days with zero manual outreach, and most of those leads came from intent signals caught in real time, including people posting about cold outreach burnout.
4. **Twenty or more a week across platforms, with a brand or community team reading.** Octolens or a listening suite. You are paying for comment coverage, boolean filters and reporting.
5. **You mostly need to know who engages with competitor posts, not who posts keywords.** Different job; see [how to track competitor LinkedIn post engagers](/blog/track-competitor-linkedin-post-engagers).

Where each route is the wrong choice: bookmarks fail when nobody owns the morning routine; Sales Navigator fails when you buy it only for this; Buska and ParseStream fail when the replies pile up unanswered; Octolens fails for a one-person budget; Intentsly fails when your buyers are not on LinkedIn, when you need more than two sending accounts, or when you want a feed to read rather than conversations started.

## FAQ

### Does LinkedIn notify you when someone posts about a keyword?
No. LinkedIn's notifications fire when your name or company page is tagged, as Buska's and Octolens's LinkedIn pages both state. An untagged post that names your competitor or describes your problem reaches you only if you search for it, run a saved Sales Navigator search, or use a tool that searches posts on your behalf.

### Can you monitor LinkedIn keywords for free?
Yes, with bookmarks. Search a quoted phrase, filter to Posts, set Date posted to Past 24 hours and Sort by to Latest, and bookmark the page. Read eight to twelve such bookmarks each morning and log the matches. Add a Google Alert on `site:linkedin.com/posts "your phrase"` as a weekly supplement, knowing Google indexes only part of LinkedIn.

### Does Sales Navigator do LinkedIn keyword monitoring?
Partly. Vendor walkthroughs describe a Posted content keywords lead filter and a "posted in the last 30 days" Spotlight, and LinkedIn says saved searches notify you of new results. That finds people who fit your profile and have posted about a topic. It does not push you the post the hour it appears, and Buyer Intent covers interest in your company only.

### Do LinkedIn monitoring tools need my LinkedIn login?
Some do. Buska asks you to paste your li_at session cookie, so the searches run as you. Octolens says no LinkedIn login is needed. ParseStream and Mentionkit do not say on the pages we opened. Intentsly connects your LinkedIn account for outreach, with up to two accounts and daily caps of 30 messages and 30 connection requests each. Ask before the trial, not after.

### Is monitoring LinkedIn posts for keywords allowed?
Reading public posts through LinkedIn's own search is ordinary use of the site. The risk sits on the sending side, in volume and templated messages, which is why Intentsly caps outreach at 30 messages and 30 connection requests per account per day and recommends 15. Keep volumes there or lower, write each first message about the post, and never automate public comments.

### How fast do you need to reply to a keyword post?
Same day for a post that asks for a recommendation or names a competitor, because the author usually decides within days and the first useful answers get the connection. A pain post without a question can wait a day or two. A post older than two weeks is a research note about that account, not a reason to message.
