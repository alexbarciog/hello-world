The fastest way to get clients for an HR consulting firm is to reach a company in the week its owner stops being able to run HR alone, and that week is usually visible on LinkedIn: a first people-operations job post, a founder asking for a handbook template, a layoff post, a head of people leaving. Message that person within a day with one specific observation instead of a service list, and you are the first consultant in the conversation. Below: six signals, a week-one plan, and when referrals win.

## Why does the usual HR consultant advice stall?

Most pages on how to get clients for an HR consulting firm give the same list: pick a niche, polish the LinkedIn profile, offer a free HR audit, ask for referrals, partner with a payroll provider. None of it is wrong. The problem is timing. A referral arrives when someone in your network happens to need HR help this month, and a weekly post reaches whoever is scrolling that day, not whoever is deciding.

The buyers exist, and most of them are not HR people. In FrankCrum's August 2026 survey of 1,000 small-business owners and employees who handle HR, [39% of small businesses had no dedicated HR department or employee](https://www.frankcrum.com/research/accidental-hr-manager-smb-study); the work sat with the owner, an office manager or whoever was free. In the same survey, 72% of the people running HR had felt unqualified for at least one high-stakes situation, most often an employee dispute, a harassment complaint or a discrimination complaint, and 25% had brought in outside HR help in the past year as a result.

That outside help is you, and the moment they go looking is short. The company either finds a consultant that week, calls a lawyer, or decides to muddle through. A buying signal is a public action that shows a company is in-market now; the [trigger event lesson](/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales) explains the idea in general, and for HR consultants the triggers are unusually easy to see, because the people who hire you are founders and operators, and they post about hiring, firing and growing.

## Where do HR consulting clients show up on LinkedIn?

Each row is a moment you can find with a free LinkedIn account. The search column is what to type into the search bar before switching to the Posts tab (or the Jobs tab, where stated) and filtering to the past week. The last column is the message, written to quote the signal and offer one piece of HR work, not a menu of services.

| Signal | Where | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A first people-operations or HR job post at a company with no HR team ("HR manager", "people ops generalist", "office manager and HR") | Jobs tab: "HR manager" or "people operations", filtered to companies under about 200 people in your niche; check the company page for an existing HR head | The owner has admitted the work needs a person; the hire takes months, and the handbook, the onboarding and the open complaint cannot wait that long | Message the founder or COO: what a fractional HR lead covers while the role is open, and what the hire should inherit on day one |
| A founder asking for a template or a how-to ("anyone have an employee handbook template?", "how do you run performance reviews at 30 people?", "PTO policy for a remote team?") | Posts tab: `"employee handbook" template` or `"performance review" founder "how do you"` or `"PTO policy" startup`, past week | An open evaluation with a short list forming in the comments the same day, and a founder doing HR work by hand | Answer in the thread with one usable point, then connect with a note that quotes their post; offer to review what they have, not a proposal |
| A headcount or compliance threshold ("we just passed 50 employees", "hired our 15th person", "opening an office in Texas", "first hire in Germany") | Posts tab: `"50 employees" "we"` or `"15th hire"` or `"first hire in"` plus a country or state, past week; also `"new office"` with your region | New obligations arrive with headcount: under US federal law, most anti-discrimination rules apply from 15 employees and the Family and Medical Leave Act from 50 (sources below); other countries and states add their own | Congratulate, name one obligation that just switched on for them, and offer a 30-minute compliance check against a short list |
| A layoff, restructuring or "hard conversation" post ("we had to let people go", "restructured the team", "my first time firing someone") | Posts tab: `"let go" founder "team"` or `"layoffs" "we made the decision"` or `"first time" firing`, past week | The riskiest HR work of the year is happening without an HR person: documentation, severance, the people who stayed | Do not pitch into a layoff post. Connect quietly, acknowledge the post, and offer a conversation about the survivors and the paperwork in the next two weeks |
| An HR leader leaving, or a first head of people arriving ("last day as Head of People", "excited to join as People Lead") | Posts tab: `"last day" "Head of People"` or `"stepping down" HR`; also `"excited to join" "Head of People"` at companies of 20 to 200 people | A gap at the top of the people function, or a new leader who audits everything in the first quarter and has budget for projects they cannot staff | Wish them well, ask who covers payroll questions and open cases meanwhile, and offer to bridge for a defined number of weeks; for the arrival, offer one scoped project (handbook, levelling, comp bands) |
| Rapid hiring after a raise ("we raised", "hiring 20 people this quarter", a company page with ten open roles and no recruiter) | Posts tab: `"we raised" hiring` or `"open roles" "we're hiring"` plus your industry words; Jobs tab: companies with many postings and no HR title on the team | Onboarding, contracts and manager training are about to be needed at a pace the founder has never run | Offer the onboarding and manager-basics setup as a fixed-scope project, timed to the first new-hire start date |

Two filters keep the list honest. A signal older than two weeks is cold: the company has either found someone or decided to wait. And a post from another HR consultant or a PEO asking "who needs help with HR?" is not a buyer, however many comments it has. Reddit and X carry the same requests, and the [Reddit method](/blog/find-b2b-leads-on-reddit) has the queries; treat them as manual channels, because Intentsly monitors LinkedIn only.

The compliance row rests on two public pages. The EEOC states that the laws it enforces cover private employers with [15 or more employees, or 20 or more for age discrimination](https://www.eeoc.gov/employers/coverage_private.cfm), and the US Department of Labor's fact sheet puts FMLA coverage at [50 or more employees in 20 or more workweeks](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/28d-fmla-employer+notification). If you work outside the US, replace those with the thresholds in your jurisdiction; the signal is the same, a company crossing a line it did not know was there.

## How to get clients for an HR consulting firm by hand: a week-one plan

The plan takes about an hour a day with a free LinkedIn account. Sales Navigator makes the headcount and job-post steps faster but is not required.

1. **Day 1: write the one-sentence offer and list the voices.** Pick the company type and the size, for example "venture-backed software companies of 20 to 100 people in the UK with no HR hire". Then list ten payroll providers, employment lawyers, accountants and founders your buyers follow, and five HR consultancies that compete with you. The [ideal customer profile worksheet](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) is the longer version of this step.
2. **Day 2: build the saved searches.** Create the six Posts-tab searches from the table with your industry words added, each filtered to the past week, plus one Jobs search for the HR-manager or people-ops role. Bookmark them. The [hiring-signals routine](/guides/track-hiring-signals-for-sales) goes deeper on the job-post signal, which has the most volume for HR consultants.
3. **Days 3 to 5: thirty minutes of reading, thirty of replying.** Run the searches, read the new posts, and for each real buyer do the "what to do" step from the table. Reply in public first, connect second. Send at most 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day; LinkedIn tolerates more, your acceptance rate does not.
4. **Day 6: follow up and find the budget owner.** For every accepted connection with no reply, send one message that adds something: a question about their onboarding, a line about the threshold they just crossed. Never "just checking in". Where the person who posted is an office manager rather than the owner, the [decision-maker guide](/guides/find-decision-makers-on-linkedin) has the six steps to find who signs.
5. **Day 7: count.** Signals found, replies, calls booked. Fewer than ten relevant posts in a week means the industry words are wrong or your buyers are quiet on LinkedIn; widen the search terms before changing the niche.

Message template for the handbook request, for copying:

```
Saw your question about the handbook. The two sections founders
at your size most often get wrong are [leave and overtime
classification], and they are the two that come up first in a
dispute. Happy to look at what you already have and mark up the
gaps in 20 minutes, no proposal attached.
```

Finding the right fifteen people each day is where the hour goes; if you want that step done for you, the [LinkedIn signals feature](/features/linkedin-signals) runs the same searches continuously and hands you the list already scored against your customer profile.

## Other ways to get HR consulting clients, and when they beat signals

Signals produce conversations with strangers. Three other routes earn their place, and for most HR consultants one of them comes first.

**Payroll providers, employment lawyers and accountants as referral partners** beat signals when you serve one region and one company size. A payroll provider hears every "how do I classify this person" question; an employment lawyer sees every dispute that a handbook would have prevented; an accountant sees every client cross a headcount line. Each of them wants an HR consultant to hand the problem to, and none of them wants to do the work. The cost is dependence on partners who also hear from your competitors, which is why most consultancies run partnerships and signal outreach together.

**Former employers and colleagues** beat signals for the first client, almost always. Every page that ranks for this question says so, and for once the consensus is right: the company you left knows exactly what you can do and already has the problem. The ceiling arrives when that network has been asked once; the plan above is what you run when it has.

**Local HR communities, chambers and founder groups** beat signals when the goal is to be the obvious choice once a company starts looking, rather than to start the conversation. Answering questions in a founders' Slack or speaking at a chamber breakfast earns calls from owners you would never have searched for. It is slow; pair it with the plan above rather than waiting for it.

Cold lists are the one tactic signals replace outright. Almost every company of 15 to 200 people without an HR hire could use an HR consultant in theory, which is why a scraped list of them converts so badly. A post says which of them has decided to get help this month. ## FAQ

### How do I get my first HR consulting client?
Ask the last two companies you worked for, and any founder you helped informally, whether they need a defined project this quarter: a handbook, an onboarding process, a compliance check. That is where almost every consultancy's first client comes from. Run the week-one plan in parallel from day one, so that when the network has been asked, strangers who posted a signal this week are already in conversation.

### Do I need an HR certification to get HR consulting clients?
No client in the signals above asks for one; they ask whether you have handled their situation before. A certification helps when a partner such as a law firm refers you, because it reassures the referrer, and in some countries it is expected. Evidence of outcomes, such as a handbook you wrote or a dispute you kept out of court, wins the call more often.

### Should I offer a free HR audit to get clients?
Yes, if it is scoped: 30 minutes against a fixed checklist, ending in a one-page list of gaps. Unscoped free consulting trains the buyer to expect free work. Offer the audit to a founder who has posted a signal from the table, because the audit then answers a question they are already asking, rather than to a cold list that has to be persuaded there is a problem.

### Which businesses should an HR consulting firm target?
Companies of roughly 10 to 200 people that have crossed, or are about to cross, a line that creates HR work: a headcount threshold, a first manager layer, a new country, a funding round with a hiring plan. The searches in the table only work with your buyers' words in them, so pick a company type and a region first, then an industry if the niche still produces more than ten relevant posts a week.

### How long does it take to get clients for a new HR consultancy?
Weeks rather than quarters when the first client comes from a former employer or a partner firm. Signal outreach adds strangers to that: week one should produce replies and a call or two, and a company that posted a threshold or a handbook question this week is usually deciding within a month. Count weekly, and expect a scoped project before a retainer.

### Can I get HR consulting clients without a website?
For the plan above, yes. Every message in the table points the buyer to your LinkedIn profile, not a site, and the profile needs a headline that names the company type you serve and one visible example of your work. A website matters later, when referrals and partners send people who want to check you before they reply.
