> [!SUMMARY]
> - A sales accepted lead (SAL) is a marketing qualified lead that a salesperson has agreed, against a written rule and within a set time, to work. Nobody has qualified it yet.
> - Acceptance is a promise to make contact, not a verdict on quality. The verdict comes later, when the lead becomes a sales qualified lead or goes back with a reason.
> - The stage exists so marketing and sales count the same leads. One person doing both jobs does not need it.
> - If your leads come from LinkedIn signals rather than forms, the same decision still happens: is this person worth a message today?

**A sales accepted lead is a lead, usually a marketing qualified lead, that a salesperson has formally agreed to take ownership of and contact within an agreed time, because it meets the handoff rule that marketing and sales wrote down together.** That is the short answer to "what is a sales accepted lead". The acronym is SAL. The word that matters is accepted: sales has taken the lead, and nothing else has happened yet.

## Why does the sales accepted lead matter?

Because without it, the two teams argue about a number neither can check. Marketing reports 200 leads sent; sales remembers five worth calling. The SAL stage forces a decision on each lead, accept or return, so both sides look at the same count.

The clearest research on the stage comes from SiriusDecisions, now part of Forrester. In its survey, 72% of companies had a formal SAL stage, sales accepted 42% of the leads marketing sourced, and companies with a formal stage produced 9.3 closed-won deals per 1,000 inquiries against 4.6 for companies without one ([Forrester, measuring the impact of successful sales handoffs](https://go.forrester.com/blogs/measuringtheimpactofsuccessfulsaleshandoffs)). The same post sets the target: with a shared lead definition, acceptance should sit at or above 85%.

The reason the stage pays off is ownership. An accepted lead has a name next to it and a clock running; an unaccepted lead has a row in a spreadsheet.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> A labelled, hypothetical example. A 12-person IT services firm gets 60 marketing leads a month. The founder writes a handoff rule: a company of 20 to 500 staff, a named person with a work email, and a form field that mentions a project. The one salesperson accepts or returns each lead within a working day. In the first month she accepts 38, returns 22 with a reason code, and by week three marketing has stopped passing on student sign-ups.

## How does a lead become sales accepted?

A lead is accepted when it passes a short check that sales can run from the record alone, without talking to anyone. This is where several of the pages ranking for the term go wrong: they recommend BANT (budget, authority, need, timing) as the acceptance test, which needs a conversation, so the acceptance rule quietly turns into the qualification test and the stage stops measuring the handoff. Forrester's post draws the line: acceptance is a commitment to contact the lead within a set time, not a judgement about its quality after that contact ([Forrester](https://go.forrester.com/blogs/measuringtheimpactofsuccessfulsaleshandoffs)).

An acceptance rule needs three checks, each answerable in under a minute:

| Check | Question | Passes | Fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Right account | Does the company match the ideal customer profile on size, industry and region? | A 40-person logistics software firm in Germany | A student, a competitor, a country you do not serve |
| Reachable person | Is there a real name, a role and a working channel? | Head of Sales with a work email or a LinkedIn profile | An info@ address, a first name only |
| Not already in play | Is the lead new to the pipeline? | No open deal, no colleague already working it | Duplicate of an active opportunity, an existing customer |

Then the time limit. Pedowitz Group gives 24 hours as the common window ([Pedowitz Group, the difference between MQLs, SQLs and SALs](https://www.pedowitzgroup.com/difference-between-mqls-sqls-and-sals)); Rework's playbook puts the SAL review window at 48 to 72 hours ([Rework, SQL definition and acceptance](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/marketing-sales-alignment/sql-definition-and-acceptance)). Pick one and put it in the CRM as a due date. A lead that nobody accepted or returned by the deadline is a process failure, and gets counted as one.

### What happens when sales rejects a lead?

It goes back with a reason code, never free text, because the codes are what marketing fixes. Pedowitz Group and Rework both publish taxonomies ([Pedowitz Group, how to measure sales acceptance of MQLs](https://www.pedowitzgroup.com/how-do-you-measure-sales-acceptance-of-mqls); [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/marketing-sales-alignment/sql-definition-and-acceptance)); the table below merges them into six codes with a destination for each.

| Code | Reason | What marketing does with it |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Outside the ideal customer profile | Tighten targeting; disqualify the record |
| R2 | Wrong person, no role in the purchase | Nurture; find the buyer at the same account |
| R3 | Bad or missing data | Fix the form; re-enrich |
| R4 | Duplicate or existing customer | Merge with the open record |
| R5 | Good fit, no project this cycle | Long-cycle nurture; revisit next quarter |
| R6 | Competitor, student, not a real buyer | Suppress |

If more than a third of returned leads carry R1 or R3, the marketing qualified lead rule is the problem, not the salesperson. The lesson on [what a marketing qualified lead is](/learn/what-is-a-marketing-qualified-lead) covers the three-line rule that fixes it.

## Sales accepted lead vs MQL, SQL and buying signal

| | Who decides | Based on | Has anyone spoken to the lead? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing qualified lead (MQL) | Marketing, by score | Fit fields plus an action such as a download | No |
| Sales accepted lead (SAL) | Sales, from the record | Right account, reachable person, not a duplicate, inside the time limit | No |
| Sales qualified lead (SQL) | Sales, after contact | Fit, need, authority and timing confirmed in a conversation | Yes |
| Buying signal | Nobody yet | A public post, comment, hire or job ad that suggests need or timing | No |

The SAL is the only stage that measures a process rather than a lead. The MQL measures interest, the [sales qualified lead](/learn/what-is-a-sales-qualified-lead) measures a buyer, and the SAL measures whether the handoff worked: did sales look, and did it look in time?

This is also why MQL to SQL conversion looks so low. First Page Sage's 2026 report, built on client data gathered from 2019 to November 2026 across 30 industries, puts the average at 16.1% with a median of 15%, and B2B SaaS at 13% ([First Page Sage, MQL to SQL conversion rate by industry](https://firstpagesage.com/reports/mql-to-sql-conversion-rate-by-industry/)). An SAL stage splits that drop into two numbers: the leads sales would not even take, and the leads it took but could not qualify. Each has a different fix.

> [!NOTE]
> The 24-hour rule in most SAL definitions is a review deadline, not a contact deadline. Accept within a day, then the first touch gets its own clock. Teams that merge the two end up accepting leads nobody has looked at, and the stage stops meaning anything.

## Do you need a sales accepted lead stage?

Not always. The stage pays for itself when two conditions hold: different people run marketing and sales, and leads arrive faster than one person can eyeball them. Below that, it is paperwork.

A founder who does both jobs already decides, lead by lead, whether to work it, and writing that decision down as a stage adds nothing. Apply the three questions from the lesson on [lead qualification](/learn/what-is-lead-qualification) directly and skip the handoff.

Outbound teams have the opposite problem: no marketing team hands anything over, so there is no MQL to accept. The acceptance decision still exists, just earlier. A [buying signal](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal) on LinkedIn, such as a post asking for a vendor or a job ad for the role you replace, is the raw lead, and accepting it means deciding from the profile and the post alone that the person is worth a message today. The same three checks apply: right account, reachable person, not already in play. Intentsly runs those checks for you on LinkedIn, scoring each person who posts about a pain point or engages with a competitor against your ideal customer profile, and [Try the AI](/try-ai) shows the matching buyers for your service with no account.

That is how we launched. We had no inbound funnel and nobody to accept leads from. The system booked 14 qualified demos in 14 days with zero manual outreach, at a reply rate 4x our previous cold campaigns, and most of those leads came from intent signals caught in real time.

## How to use the sales accepted lead this week

1. **Write the acceptance rule in three lines.** Right account, reachable person, not already in play, with one example of a pass and a fail for each, plus the time limit. Put it in the CRM's description of the lead stage so whoever accepts sees it every time.
2. **Add the six rejection codes as a required field.** A lead cannot leave the SAL stage as "returned" without one. After two weeks, count the codes; the pattern tells you what to fix upstream. The [guide to qualifying leads before outreach](/guides/qualify-leads-before-outreach) turns the next stage, the conversation, into a five-minute check per lead.
3. **Report two rates, not one.** Leads to accepted, and accepted to qualified. If the first sits far below the 85% Forrester describes for aligned teams ([Forrester](https://go.forrester.com/blogs/measuringtheimpactofsuccessfulsaleshandoffs)), fix the lead definition. If the second is low, fix the conversation.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between a sales accepted lead and a sales qualified lead?

A sales accepted lead has been taken on by a salesperson from the record alone: right account, reachable person, not a duplicate, inside the time limit. Nobody has spoken to the person. A sales qualified lead has been through that conversation and confirmed to fit, have a need, hold some authority and have a reason to act. Acceptance is a promise to look; qualification is the result of looking.

### What is a good sales accepted lead rate?

Forrester's post on sales handoffs reports that sales accepted 42% of marketing-sourced leads in the SiriusDecisions survey and says aligned teams should reach 85% or more ([Forrester](https://go.forrester.com/blogs/measuringtheimpactofsuccessfulsaleshandoffs)). Nutshell's own guidance calls 70 to 90% healthy ([Nutshell, SALs explained](https://nutshell.com/blog/sals-explained)). A rate near 100% usually means the marketing bar is set too high or sales is accepting without looking.

### How long does sales have to accept a lead?

Most published definitions use 24 hours; Pedowitz Group calls that the common window and Rework's playbook allows 48 to 72 hours for the review ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/marketing-sales-alignment/sql-definition-and-acceptance)). The number matters less than the fact that it exists and is tracked. Accepting is a review deadline; the first contact attempt should have its own, shorter one.

### What happens when sales rejects a lead?

It goes back to marketing with a reason code such as "outside the ideal customer profile", "wrong person", "bad data", "duplicate", "no project this cycle" or "not a real buyer". Each code has a destination: tighten targeting, nurture, fix the form, merge, revisit next quarter or suppress. Free-text rejections get ignored; coded ones change what marketing sends next month.

### Does a small team need a sales accepted lead stage?

No, if one person runs both marketing and sales or fewer than a few dozen leads arrive a month. The stage exists so two teams count the same leads, and one person already does. Qualify each lead directly instead. Teams running outbound from LinkedIn signals make the same accept-or-skip decision on every signal, just without a marketing team upstream.

### Who decides whether a lead is sales accepted?

The salesperson who will own it, applying a rule both teams wrote. Marketing can score, route and nurture, but acceptance means a named rep has agreed to contact this lead within the time limit. If nobody owns the decision, or if it is made automatically when a lead is assigned, the stage records routing rather than acceptance and the numbers stop meaning anything.
