The fastest way to find clients as a fractional CMO is to reach a company in the week it decides it needs marketing leadership but not a full-time hire, and that week is usually visible on LinkedIn: a funding post, a head-of-marketing job ad, a founder asking which agency to use, a marketing lead's farewell post. Message those people within a day with one specific observation about their marketing, and you are the first CMO in the conversation. Below: seven signals, a week-one plan, and when referrals win.

## Why does the usual fractional CMO advice stall?

Every page on how to find clients as a fractional CMO gives the same sequence: fifteen to twenty-five quiet conversations with your network, an announcement post, three to five posts a week, referral swaps with fractional CFOs and agencies, a founder community, a marketplace listing. None of it is wrong, and for the first client it is usually right. The problem is timing. Your network produces a client only when someone in it happens to need a CMO this month, and a LinkedIn post reaches whoever is reading that day, not whoever is deciding.

The marketplaces that rank for this question say so themselves. In Fractional Jobs' own survey, [84% of fractional leaders found their first client through their network](https://www.fractionaljobs.io/help/how-to-get-your-first-fractional-clients), and in Go Fractional's April 2026 survey of 213 fractional professionals, [92% named personal networks as a main client channel, 54% used LinkedIn to find clients, and 90% called client acquisition their biggest challenge](https://www.gofractional.com/docs/2026_State_of_Fractional_Work-Go_Fractional.pdf). Everyone is working the same network and the same feed. The second and third clients, the ones your network does not contain, have to be found at the moment they start looking.

The demand is there and it is small-company demand. Lightcast counted [nearly 1,000 US fractional job postings in the first seven months of 2026, more than in all of 2025, and 97% of them came from small and medium companies](https://lightcast.io/blog/rise-of-fractional-leadership); marketing and PR make up 10% of those postings. A survey of 113 fractional marketing leaders by The Slice Network and Robert Half, reported by Marketing Week, puts the buyers at [scale-ups (41%) and early growth-phase businesses (38.5%)](https://www.marketingweek.com/fractional-cmos-demand-expertise/), with B2B SaaS the biggest sector. Those are founder-led companies, and founders post. A buying signal is a public action that shows a company is in-market now; the [trigger event lesson](/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales) explains the idea, and for fractional CMOs the triggers are unusually easy to see.

## Where do fractional CMO clients show up on LinkedIn?

Each row is a moment you can find with a free LinkedIn account. The search column is what to type into the search bar before switching to the Posts tab (or the Jobs tab, where stated) and filtering to the past week. The last column is the message, written to quote the signal and offer one piece of marketing work, not a menu of services.

| Signal | Where | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A funding announcement ("we raised", "excited to announce our seed round") | Posts tab: `"we raised" seed` or `"announce" "Series A"` plus your industry words, past week | A hiring plan with a marketing line in it, investors asking about pipeline, and usually nobody senior owning marketing yet | Wait a day for the congratulations, then send one observation about what the first marketing hire should and should not be, and offer to sketch the quarter's plan in 20 minutes |
| A job post for a head of marketing, marketing manager or VP marketing at a company under about 100 people | Jobs tab: "head of marketing" or "marketing lead", filtered by company size and your niche | Budget exists; the company will wait months to fill the role and often needs CMO-level judgement for a few hours a week more than a full-time hire | Message the founder or CEO: what a fractional CMO covers while the role is open, and what the hire should own when they arrive |
| A job post titled "fractional CMO" or "part-time marketing director" | Jobs tab: "fractional CMO" or "fractional marketing", past month | The company has already decided on the model; it is choosing the person, and the post has a short list forming | Apply through the post and message the poster the same day with one thing you noticed about their marketing; speed matters more than polish here |
| A founder asking for a recommendation or a how-to ("anyone know a good fractional CMO?", "which agency for B2B paid?", "how do you pick a positioning?") | Posts tab: `"fractional CMO" recommend` or `"agency" "any recommendations"` or `positioning "any advice"`, past week | An open evaluation, with a short list forming in the comments the same day | Answer the question in the thread with one usable point, then connect with a note that quotes their post; offer a look at their funnel, not a proposal |
| A marketing leader leaving, or a new CEO or first sales hire arriving | Posts tab: `"last day" "head of marketing"` or `"stepping down" marketing`; also `"excited to join" "first sales"` at companies of 20 to 200 people | A gap at the top of marketing, or a new sales motion that needs pipeline the founder cannot produce alone | Congratulate or wish them well, ask who owns demand generation now, and offer to bridge the gap for a defined number of weeks |
| A founder doing marketing in public ("our ads stopped working", "rebranding", "launching on Product Hunt", "trying to crack outbound") | Posts tab: `"CAC" founder` or `"rebrand" "lessons"` or `"launch" "what we learned"`, past week | The founder is running marketing by hand and finding it hard; the launch or the budget review has a date on it | Reply with one specific, non-obvious point about their situation; connect; mention what you do only in the second message |
| An agency break-up or a new market ("parting ways with our agency", "bringing marketing in-house", "expanding to the US") | Posts tab: `"agency" "in-house" founder` or `"expanding to" "new market"`, past week | Execution exists or is being rebuilt, and nobody is setting strategy; a one-off project with a deadline, which is the easiest fractional engagement to start | Offer the project, scoped for the deadline (a 90-day plan, a go-to-market for the new market), with the retainer left for later |

Two filters keep the list honest. A signal older than two weeks is cold; the company has either found someone or decided to wait. And a post from another fractional CMO or a marketing agency asking "who needs help with their marketing?" is not a buyer, however many comments it has. Reddit and X carry the same requests, and the [Reddit method](/blog/find-b2b-leads-on-reddit) has the queries; treat them as manual channels, because Intentsly monitors LinkedIn only.

## How to find clients as a fractional CMO by hand: a week-one plan

The plan takes about an hour a day with a free LinkedIn account. Sales Navigator makes the job-post and company-size steps faster but is not required.

1. **Day 1: write the one-sentence offer and list the voices.** Pick the company type and the stage, for example "seed to Series A B2B software companies in the DACH region that have just hired their first salespeople". Then list ten investors, agency founders and marketing writers your buyers follow, and five fractional CMOs or agencies that compete with you. The [ideal customer profile worksheet](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) is the longer version of this step.
2. **Day 2: build the saved searches.** Create the seven Posts-tab searches from the table with your industry words added, each filtered to the past week, plus two Jobs searches: the head-of-marketing role and "fractional CMO". Bookmark them. The [funding-round guide](/guides/find-companies-that-just-raised-funding) and the [hiring-signals routine](/guides/track-hiring-signals-for-sales) go deeper on the two signals with the most volume.
3. **Days 3 to 5: thirty minutes of reading, thirty of replying.** Run the searches, read the new posts, and for each real buyer do the "what to do" step from the table. Reply in public first, connect second. Send at most 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day; LinkedIn tolerates more, your acceptance rate does not.
4. **Day 6: follow up and find the budget owner.** For every accepted connection with no reply, send one message that adds something: a question about their funnel, a number from their own post. Never "just checking in". Where the person who posted is not the founder or CEO, the [decision-maker guide](/guides/find-decision-makers-on-linkedin) has the six steps to find who signs.
5. **Day 7: count.** Signals found, replies, calls booked. Fewer than ten relevant posts in a week means the industry words are wrong or your buyers are quiet on LinkedIn; widen the search terms before changing the niche.

Message template for the funding post, for copying:

```
Congratulations on the round. One thing most founders at your stage
get wrong in the next quarter is hiring a marketing manager before
anyone has decided what marketing is for. Happy to sketch what the
first 90 days of marketing should contain in 20 minutes, no proposal
attached.
```

Finding the right fifteen people each day is where the hour goes; if you want that step done for you, the [LinkedIn signals feature](/features/linkedin-signals) runs the same searches continuously and hands you the list already scored against your customer profile.

## Other ways to find fractional CMO clients, and when they beat signals

Signals produce conversations with strangers. Three other routes earn their place, and for most fractional CMOs one of them comes first.

**Your former employers, colleagues and agencies** beat signals for the first client, almost always. Every page that ranks for this question says so, and the surveys above agree. The ceiling arrives when that network has been asked; after the first month of conversations it is a referral source, not a pipeline.

**Fractional CFOs, COOs and agencies as referral partners** beat signals when you serve one stage and one sector. A fractional CFO sees every company whose founder has stopped understanding the pipeline numbers; a paid-media agency sees every client whose strategy is missing; a startup lawyer sees every raise. Each of them wants a CMO to hand the problem to. The cost is dependence on partners who also hear from your competitors, which is why most fractional CMOs run partnerships and signal outreach together. The [fractional CFO page](/solutions/find-clients-as-a-fractional-cfo) is the mirror image of this one and a useful read for a partner.

**Content and speaking** beat signals when the goal is to be the obvious choice once a company starts looking, rather than to start the conversation. A weekly post that shows how you think about positioning or the first marketing hire earns replies from founders you would never have searched for. It is slow; pair it with the plan above rather than waiting for it.

Cold lists are the one tactic signals replace outright: almost every company on a scraped list of 20-to-200-person B2B companies could use marketing leadership in theory, and a post says which of them has decided to get it this month. The [marketing agency page](/solutions/find-clients-for-a-marketing-agency) covers the same signals from the execution side; the [solutions index](/solutions) covers other business types.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to get the first fractional CMO client?
Weeks rather than quarters if the first client comes from a former employer, an agency you worked with or a partner firm. Signal outreach adds strangers to that: week one should produce replies and a call or two. A company that posted a funding announcement or a head-of-marketing job this week is usually deciding within a month, so count weekly and expect a project before a retainer.

### How do fractional CMOs find clients without paying for ads?
The plan above costs an hour a day and a free LinkedIn account: seven saved searches for the moments when a company decides it needs marketing leadership, a public reply and a connection note for each real buyer, and one follow-up that adds something. Referral partners and your own network come first and cost nothing either; the pages that rank for this question agree that ads are the last channel a fractional practice needs.

### How do I find startup clients as a fractional CMO?
Startups are the easiest buyers to see, because founders post. Funding announcements, Product Hunt launches, a first sales hire and "our ads stopped working" posts are all in the table above, and seed to Series A companies rarely have a marketing leader yet. Add your sector words to the searches, filter to the past week, and offer a scoped 90-day plan rather than a retainer.

### How many clients can a fractional CMO manage at once?
In Go Fractional's 2026 survey, [half of fractional professionals ran two or three clients and about a quarter ran one](https://www.gofractional.com/docs/2026_State_of_Fractional_Work-Go_Fractional.pdf); marketing was among the functions where four or five was common. A practical test: if a new client's first month would push your weekly hours past what you can deliver with a proper plan for each, offer a project instead of a retainer.

### Should I specialise or stay general as a fractional CMO?
For outreach, specialise. The searches in the table only work with your buyers' words in them: "we raised" returns every company on earth, "we raised" plus "SaaS" plus "Berlin" returns yours. Niche by stage and company type first, sector second. You can still take a general client who finds you through a referral; you cannot search for one.
