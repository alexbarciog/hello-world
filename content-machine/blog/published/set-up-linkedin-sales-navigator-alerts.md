Here is how to set up LinkedIn Sales Navigator alerts so they fire for the right people: save your target accounts into a list first, save at least five leads at each account, save three searches with alerts on, switch on Buyer Intent if your plan has it, then choose which alerts reach your email and which stay in the product. Alerts only fire for what you have saved, so the saving is the setup. The whole thing takes about an hour, plus fifteen minutes a week to triage.

## What you need to set up LinkedIn Sales Navigator alerts

- A Sales Navigator licence. Core gets lead and account alerts; Buyer Intent and content-engagement alerts need Advanced or Advanced Plus. Step 1 has the table. The free trial covers Core and Advanced, needs a card and reminds you by email seven days before it ends, per [LinkedIn's compare-plans page](https://business.linkedin.com/sales-solutions/compare-plans.html).
- A written ideal customer profile, because every alert you set up filters on it. The lesson on [what an ideal customer profile is](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) takes ten minutes if you do not have one.
- A list of 10 to 50 target companies. A spreadsheet export from your CRM is enough.
- Admin access to your company website if you want website visits to count towards Buyer Intent (Step 5 explains the Insight Tag).
- A fixed slot in the calendar. Alerts that nobody reads are noise.

An alert, in Sales Navigator, is a notification that something changed for a lead or account you saved: a job change, a post, a profile view, a hiring surge, a visit to your company page. It is one way to catch a trigger event; the lesson on [what a trigger event in sales is](/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales) covers the ones Sales Navigator does not see. If you are still deciding whether Sales Navigator is the right tool for this, the [Intentsly vs LinkedIn Sales Navigator comparison](/compare/intentsly-vs-linkedin-sales-navigator) sets out which jobs each one does.

## Step 1: Check which alerts your plan can fire

Before saving anything, know what the feed can show you. LinkedIn's product page gates alerts by plan, and the gate explains most "my alerts are empty" complaints. The table below is compiled from [LinkedIn's Sales Navigator page](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator) and a [LinkedIn Community post on buyer intent alerts](https://scommunity.linkedin.com/sales-navigator-features-9/earn-trust-by-leveraging-these-buyer-intent-alerts-6001), both opened on 30 October 2026.

| Alert | What triggers it | Plan |
|---|---|---|
| Lead changed job or role | A saved lead moves company or gets promoted | All plans |
| Account alerts (shared updates, hiring and headcount changes) | A saved account posts, hires or changes headcount | All plans |
| Lead viewed your profile | A saved lead opens your LinkedIn profile | All plans |
| Lead accepted your connection | A saved lead accepts your request | All plans |
| Potential lead viewed your profile | Someone at a saved account, not yet saved, views you | All plans |
| Lead engaged with your content | A saved lead likes, comments or shares your post or ad | Advanced Plus (Advanced on some LinkedIn pages) |
| Lead viewed your Smart Link | A lead opens content you shared as a Smart Link | Advanced, Advanced Plus |
| Buyer Intent (employees and leadership) | People at a saved account view your company page or website | Advanced, Advanced Plus |
| Shared-list activity | A colleague adds to or comments on a shared list | Advanced, Advanced Plus |
| CRM alerts, Microsoft Teams alerts | CRM record changes; saved-lead changes pushed to Teams | Advanced Plus |

Prices, for the decision: Core is listed at US$119.99 a month and Advanced at US$159.99 a month, with Advanced Plus by demo, on [LinkedIn's compare-plans page](https://business.linkedin.com/sales-solutions/compare-plans.html) today. If the alerts you want are all in the "All plans" rows, Core is enough.

## Step 2: Save your target accounts into one list

Alerts are generated from saved leads and saved accounts, so an empty list means an empty feed. Start with accounts, not people, because saving an account switches on account-level alerts (hiring, growth, new decision makers) for everyone who works there, and because saving a lead later also saves its company automatically, per [LinkedIn's Community post on saving leads](https://scommunity.linkedin.com/sales-navigator-features-9/save-your-key-prospects-why-is-it-important-to-save-leads-early-5972).

The click path, from [LinkedIn's how-to-use page](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator/how-to-use?ss=1):

1. Click **Account filters+** next to the search bar to open Account Search.
2. Set the filters that describe your ideal customer: company headcount, industry, geography, and, if useful, the hiring and headcount-growth filters.
3. On each result, click **Save**, then choose an existing list or **Create new list**. Name the list by segment ("UK accountancy firms 20-200") rather than "Targets".
4. For a longer list, tick the results and save them in bulk. LinkedIn's release notes put bulk actions at 25 leads or accounts at a time and the list limit at 1,000 per list ([Q3 2020 release notes](https://scommunity.linkedin.com/new-sales-navigator-features-64/new-sales-navigator-features-q3-2020-lists-and-alerts-enhancements-1139)).

Two shortcuts depend on plan. CSV upload of an account list needs Advanced or Advanced Plus, and auto-saving accounts from the CRM needs Advanced Plus, per [LinkedIn's Community post on saving accounts](https://scommunity.linkedin.com/sales-navigator-features-9/save-your-key-companies-why-is-it-important-to-save-accounts-early-5935). The same post recommends starting with at least ten companies and adding more weekly, and claims that people who save at least 25 accounts engage with 5x more alerts, without giving a methodology.

## Step 3: Save at least five leads at each account

Account alerts tell you a company moved. Lead alerts tell you who to message. LinkedIn's own guidance is to save at least five leads per account so you can see the buying committee change, in its [four-step workflow post](https://scommunity.linkedin.com/tips-from-linkedin-experts-80/4-proven-steps-to-elevate-your-sales-navigator-workflow-6258).

1. Click **Lead filters+** to open Lead Search.
2. Add the **Current company** filter and pick your saved account list, so the search only returns people at companies you already track.
3. Filter by **Seniority level** and **Function** for the roles that buy from you, and the two roles that influence them.
4. Click **Save** next to each profile and put them in a lead list named like the account list.
5. While browsing LinkedIn.com, the **Save in Sales Navigator** button on a profile does the same thing without switching tabs, per [LinkedIn's product page](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator).

A rough budget: 30 accounts with five leads each is 150 saved leads. That is enough to keep the feed moving and few enough that you can act on every alert.

## Step 4: Save three searches with alerts on

Saved-search alerts are the part most walkthroughs skip, and they are the only alerts that bring you people you have not saved yet. Saving an Advanced Lead Search notifies you when it has new results, per [LinkedIn's how-to-use page](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator/how-to-use?ss=1). The click path from a [LinkedIn Community post of August 2025](https://scommunity.linkedin.com/tips-from-linkedin-experts-80/search-like-you-mean-it-5-navigator-tips-that-deliver-7812): run the search, click **Save search** in the upper right, name it, and set the alert to daily, weekly or monthly.

Which three searches:

| Saved search | Filters | What a new result means | Alert frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| New decision makers at target accounts | Current company = your account list; Seniority = Director and above; **Changed jobs** (last 90 days) | Someone with budget just arrived and is reviewing vendors | Weekly |
| Buyers talking about your problem | Keywords = your problem phrase in quotes; **Posted on LinkedIn** (last 30 days); Seniority = Manager and above | A person is publicly thinking about what you sell | Daily |
| Warm strangers | **Following your company**; Current company headcount = your ICP range | Someone outside your lists already knows you | Weekly |

The **Changed jobs** filter returns people who moved in the last 90 days, and LinkedIn says decision makers are 62% more receptive to outreach after a move, on its [how-to-use page](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator/how-to-use?ss=1). Keep the keyword search tight with Boolean: `"cold outreach" AND (burnout OR "reply rate") NOT recruiter` is a complete query, and LinkedIn documents AND, OR and NOT in the same [August 2025 post](https://scommunity.linkedin.com/tips-from-linkedin-experts-80/search-like-you-mean-it-5-navigator-tips-that-deliver-7812).

When the alert arrives, click the count of new results rather than the name of the search; the name reopens the whole result set.

This is also the step where the manual work piles up. A saved search only sees people's own posts and job changes, not who liked or commented on a competitor's post or an industry leader's thread, and you still have to read each new result and decide whether it fits. ## Step 5: Switch on Buyer Intent and install the Insight Tag

On Advanced and Advanced Plus, Buyer Intent shows which saved accounts are researching you. Two alerts come from it: one when leadership (VP, CXO, Partner or Owner) at a saved account views your company page or website, one when other employees do, per the [Community post on buyer intent alerts](https://scommunity.linkedin.com/sales-navigator-features-9/earn-trust-by-leveraging-these-buyer-intent-alerts-6001).

The prerequisite that most guides leave out: website visits only count if the LinkedIn Insight Tag is installed on your site. Without it, only visits to your LinkedIn company page trigger the alert, per [LinkedIn's release notes](https://scommunity.linkedin.com/new-sales-navigator-features-64/new-sales-navigator-features-q3-2020-lists-and-alerts-enhancements-1139). The tag is a snippet from Campaign Manager that your web team pastes into the site template; ask for it before you trial Advanced, not after.

To read the alerts: on the homepage, filter alerts by **Accounts** and choose **Buyer Interest**, then open the account to see the "Buyer insights" and "Growth insights" sections, per [LinkedIn's Community post on buyer interest](https://scommunity.linkedin.com/tips-from-linkedin-experts-80/buyer-interest-where-can-you-identify-top-buyers-in-sales-navigator-1800). Expect vague detail: LinkedIn hides an attribute such as function or seniority when fewer than ten employees match it, so you will often learn that "several people in finance" visited, not who. The alert tells you which account to work, and your saved leads at that account tell you whom to message.

## Step 6: Choose where each alert type reaches you

Alerts appear in the homepage feed, the mobile app and email digests. The setting lives under your profile photo, then **Settings**. LinkedIn's help centre article "Manage Sales Navigator email alerts" documents the labels; vendor walkthroughs from [Evaboot](https://evaboot.com/blog/linkedin-sales-navigator-alerts) and [Skylead](https://skylead.io/blog/sales-navigator-alerts/) describe the same path as **Settings**, then the email and notification section, then a "Receive emails?" toggle per category and individual toggles per alert type. Labels move between releases; the logic does not.

A split that works for one person:

- Email: lead job changes, Buyer Intent, saved-search results. These are the alerts you act on the same day.
- In-product only: profile views, connection accepts, account news, content engagement. You read these in the weekly slot.
- Off: shared-list activity if you work alone, and anything you have not acted on in a month.

If email is noisy, set a filter in your inbox that labels anything from Sales Navigator and skips the main view, then open the label during your triage slot.

## Step 7: Triage the feed once a week and train it

The feed sorts the most urgent alerts to the top and lets you filter by Lead, Account, shared activity and alert type, per [LinkedIn's release notes](https://scommunity.linkedin.com/new-sales-navigator-features-64/new-sales-navigator-features-q3-2020-lists-and-alerts-enhancements-1139). You can also sort by lead list, search for one prospect, bookmark an alert for later and remove irrelevant ones, which LinkedIn says trains the feed, per its [post on saving leads](https://scommunity.linkedin.com/sales-navigator-features-9/save-your-key-prospects-why-is-it-important-to-save-leads-early-5972).

A fifteen-minute routine:

1. Filter by **Lead**, alert type **Changed jobs**. Message each one the same day (template below).
2. Filter by **Account**, alert type **Buyer Interest**. Open the account, pick the most senior saved lead, message them.
3. Filter by alert type **Shares** or **Posted**. Comment on two posts before you message anyone.
4. Switch to saved searches and open the new-results count on each of the three searches. Save anyone who fits to the lead list; this is how the lists grow.
5. Bookmark anything you want to come back to. Remove alerts from accounts that turned out to be wrong, and remove the account from the list too.

The message for each alert type:

| Alert | Opening line (adapt, do not paste) | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Lead changed job | "Saw you moved to {company} as {role} in {month}. In your first quarter there, is {problem} on the list or already handled?" | One question, no pitch |
| Lead posted about your problem | Comment on the post first, then: "Your post on {topic} matched what we hear from {segment}. One thing we tried that worked: {one line}." | Offer the full note |
| Buyer Intent on an account | "A few people at {company} have been looking at how we handle {problem}. Happy to send the two-page version if useful; no call needed." | Send an asset |
| New decision maker at a saved account | "Congratulations on the {role} seat. When you review the {category} stack, we have a short comparison that saved {segment} leaders a week." | Send the comparison |
| Lead viewed your profile | Wait one day. If they viewed twice: "Noticed you stopped by. Anything I can point you to on {topic}?" | Open door only |

More sequences, written per signal, are in the [LinkedIn message templates for buying signals](/blog/linkedin-message-templates-for-buying-signals).

## Worked example: Northwind Payroll (hypothetical)

Northwind Payroll is a made-up 40-person payroll software company selling to UK accountancy firms with 20 to 200 staff. Its sales lead, on Advanced, sets alerts up like this.

Step 2: Account Search with headcount 11-200, industry Accounting, geography United Kingdom, plus the hiring filter. She saves 40 firms into "UK accountancy 20-200".

Step 3: Lead Search with Current company = that list, Seniority = Director, Partner, Owner, Function = Finance and Operations. She saves 190 people into "UK accountancy buyers".

Step 4: Three saved searches. "New partners at target firms" (account list, Partner or Director, Changed jobs, weekly). "Payroll pain posts" (keywords `payroll AND ("month end" OR "manual" OR "bureau")`, Posted on LinkedIn, Manager and above, daily). "Following Northwind" (Following your company, headcount 11-200, weekly).

Step 5: She asks the web developer for the Insight Tag the same day. It goes live a week later; until then, Buyer Intent only shows company-page visits.

Week two, Tuesday triage: two "Changed jobs" alerts (a new Operations Director at a saved firm; she messages him with the job-change line), one Buyer Intent alert (three people in finance at a 90-person firm viewed the company page; she sends the two-page comparison to the saved Finance Partner there), and six new results on the payroll-pain search, of which two are at firms in the ICP and get saved. Total time: eighteen minutes. The numbers in this example are illustrative, not measured.

## Where this goes wrong

**Saving leads before accounts, or saving nobody.** The most common reason the feed is empty is that nothing is saved. The second most common is a lead list with one person per company, which gives you job-change alerts but no account-level hiring or intent signal. Save the account, then five people at it.

**Expecting alerts about people you did not save.** Lead and account alerts are about your saved lists. The only alerts that bring strangers are saved-search results and the "potential lead viewed your profile" alert, and the second one still requires the account to be saved. If your pipeline depends on finding new people who are showing intent across LinkedIn, Sales Navigator alerts are the wrong instrument on their own; the guide on [how to set up signal-based outbound](/guides/set-up-signal-based-outbound) covers the sources that do that.

**Letting the feed become a newspaper.** Account news and content-engagement alerts are interesting and rarely actionable. If you do not remove the irrelevant ones and switch off the categories you never act on, the three alerts that matter each week sit under thirty that do not. Treat the "remove" action as training, because that is what LinkedIn says it is.

## What to do with the list

Alerts produce a short daily list of people with a reason to hear from you. Three rules keep it a pipeline.

Message within 48 hours of a job-change or intent alert; after that the reason has gone cold and the message reads as generic. Use one connection request or one InMail per person, with the alert as the first line and a single question as the ask. Log each alert you acted on in a sheet with four columns (date, person, alert type, outcome) so that after a month you can see which alert types turn into replies and switch the others off.

Qualify before you spend an InMail: the [five-minute check for qualifying leads before outreach](/guides/qualify-leads-before-outreach) is built for exactly this list. When you have more alerts than you can read, that is the point to try [Intentsly's matching buyers with no account](/try-ai): it shows who on LinkedIn is engaging with your topic right now, scored against your profile, so you spend the hour on the messages instead of the feed. ## FAQ

### Why am I not getting any Sales Navigator alerts?

Almost always because nothing is saved. Alerts are generated from saved leads, saved accounts and saved searches, so a new licence with empty lists produces an empty feed. Save at least ten accounts and a few leads at each, wait a day, and check the homepage feed. If the feed fills but email stays quiet, check the email toggles under Settings.

### Do Sales Navigator alerts work on the free trial or on Core?

Yes for the basic set. Lead and account alerts, profile views and connection accepts are listed for all plans on LinkedIn's product page. Buyer Intent, content-engagement alerts and Smart Link alerts are listed for Advanced and Advanced Plus. The trial covers Core and Advanced, so a trialist on Advanced sees the fuller set until it ends.

### How do I get Sales Navigator alerts by email?

Click your profile photo, open Settings, and find the email and notification section. There is a toggle to receive emails per category and individual toggles per alert type; LinkedIn's help article "Manage Sales Navigator email alerts" lists the current labels. Keep job changes, Buyer Intent and saved-search results on email and leave the rest in the product.

### How many leads and accounts can I save?

LinkedIn's release notes state a limit of 1,000 leads or accounts per list and bulk actions of 25 at a time. Vendor blogs quote an overall cap of 10,000 saved leads and 1,000 accounts, but none of them links a LinkedIn source, so plan around the per-list figure and keep lists segmented rather than one giant list.

### Can I get alerts about people I have not saved?

Only two ways. A saved search with an alert returns new people who match your filters, and the "potential lead viewed your profile" alert covers unsaved people at a saved account. Anyone engaging with other people's posts, including your competitors' posts, is invisible to Sales Navigator alerts; that needs a separate signal tool or a manual check.

### What is the difference between a saved-search alert and a lead alert?

A lead alert reports a change to a specific person you saved: a new job, a post, a profile view. A saved-search alert reports that new people now match a set of filters you saved, whether or not you know them. Lead alerts tell you when to message someone you already track; saved-search alerts feed new names into the lists.
