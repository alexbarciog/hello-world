> [!SUMMARY]
> - Sales prospecting is the work of finding specific people who could buy from you and making the first contact.
> - It is done when you have two things: a named person who fits your customer profile, and a reason to contact them this week.
> - Prospecting is the seller-starts route; lead generation is the buyer-starts route. Both feed lead qualification.
> - Give it a fixed weekly budget, measure replies per 100 contacts, and cut list size before you cut research.

**Sales prospecting is the work of finding specific people who could buy what you sell and making the first contact with them.** That is the short answer to "what is sales prospecting". The output is not a spreadsheet of companies; it is a list of named people, each with a reason to contact them now, and a first message sent to each one. Everything else in prospecting, from the lists to the follow-up schedule, exists to produce those two things: the right person and the right moment.

## Why does sales prospecting matter?

Prospecting matters because it is the only part of selling that creates conversations out of nothing. Marketing brings in the people already looking. Prospecting reaches the larger group who have the problem, would pay to fix it, and have not started looking yet. If you sell something new, or something buyers do not know to search for, prospecting is the whole top of your pipeline.

It is also the step that gets skipped first. A founder running sales alongside product and hiring will always find something more urgent than writing to 20 strangers, and the pipeline goes quiet six weeks later. Prospecting has a long delay between effort and result, which is why it needs a fixed budget rather than spare time.

A labelled example (hypothetical, not a customer): a two-person firm sells scheduling software to physiotherapy clinics. Nobody searches for it, because clinics assume the practice-management system they already have is the only option. The founder lists 60 clinics with three or more practitioners, finds the practice manager at each, and notes which ones posted a job ad for a receptionist in the last month. Those 14 get the first messages, because a clinic hiring front-desk staff is a clinic feeling the scheduling load. Six reply, three book a call. None of that could have come from inbound.

## How does sales prospecting work?

Sales prospecting runs as five steps, repeated every week. The steps are the same for one founder doing an hour a day and for a team of sales development representatives (SDRs, the people whose whole job is prospecting and qualifying before handing over to a closer).

1. **Define the ideal customer profile.** One page naming the company size, the industry, the role that buys, the problem they have and one public sign that a company has it this month. The lesson on the [ideal customer profile](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) has a worksheet.
2. **Build the list.** Named people at named companies that match the profile, with a working contact route for each: a LinkedIn profile, a verified email or a direct number. The [guide to building a B2B prospect list](/guides/build-b2b-prospect-list) gives the exact steps.
3. **Research each person for a reason to write now.** A post they published, a role they just took, a tool their company adopted, a job they are hiring for. This step is where most of the time goes, and where [Intentsly's LinkedIn signals](/features/linkedin-signals) do the reading for you by surfacing the people in your market who just engaged with a competitor's post or wrote about the problem you solve.
4. **Make the first contact.** A short message on the channel the person actually reads, built on the reason from step 3, followed by two or three follow-ups on a fixed schedule.
5. **Qualify the replies and hand them over.** In the first reply or call, check that the person has the problem, the authority and a timeline. Pass the ones who do into a sales conversation. The lesson on [lead qualification](/learn/what-is-lead-qualification) covers the frameworks.

What this produces at a staffed team is documented. The Bridge Group's 2025 report on 351 B2B companies puts the median SDR at 112 activities a day (44 phone, 41 email, 19 LinkedIn, 8 text or other) for 4.1 quality conversations a day, with an average ramp of 3.0 months, one SDR supporting 2.4 account executives, and 60% of reps at quota, the lowest figure in the study's history ([The Bridge Group, 2025 SDR Metrics Report](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)). Divide the two medians and it comes to roughly 27 touches for every real conversation, which is the going rate when the list comes from a database filter.

> [!NOTE]
> A prospect is a named person who fits the profile and has been contacted or is about to be. A lead is anyone who has shown some interest, such as a form fill or a webinar sign-up, whether or not they fit. Prospecting produces prospects; it does not wait for leads.

## What are the main sales prospecting methods?

Every sales prospecting method is a different way of doing steps 2 and 3, finding the person and finding the reason. The channel used for the first contact matters less than where the list came from. The table gives the methods a small team can run, with the limit each one carries.

| Method | How the list is built | Best when | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Database prospecting | Filter a contact database by industry, size and title | Your buyers are silent in public and you need volume | Everyone has the same list; messages read as generic |
| LinkedIn prospecting | Search profiles, posts and engagement; message or connect | Your buyers post and comment about their work | Practical daily caps: Intentsly limits each connected account to 30 messages and 30 connection requests a day and recommends 15 |
| Cold email | Verified addresses from a database or enrichment tool | You can write 25 different first lines a day | Reply rates fall sharply as the list grows (see below) |
| Cold calling | Direct numbers from a database, dialled in blocks | Buyers answer phones and the deal is large enough to pay for the time | Highest cost per conversation; needs blocks of uninterrupted time |
| Referral prospecting | Ask customers and partners for named introductions | You have 10 or more happy customers | Does not scale on your schedule |
| Signal-based prospecting | Start from a public action: a post, a hire, a funding round, a comment on a competitor's post | Your market talks in public and timing decides the reply | Volume is limited by how much your buyers say out loud |
| Event prospecting | Attendee and speaker lists from events your buyers attend | A few events a year gather your whole market | Seasonal; lists go stale fast |

The cold email numbers are worth knowing before picking a method. Hunter analysed 11 million cold emails sent in 2024 and found an average reply rate of 4.1%, rising to 5.8% for campaigns of 50 or fewer recipients and falling to 2.1% for campaigns of 1,000 or more; campaigns with follow-ups averaged 4.9% against 3% without, and in the same report's survey of 217 decision-makers, 71% named lack of relevance as the reason they do not reply ([Hunter, The State of Cold Email 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)). Small batches and a specific reason to write are not tips. They are the difference between the top and the bottom of that range.

A [buying signal](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal) is the unit the last two rows run on: a public action by a named person that suggests timing. When we launched Intentsly we prospected our own market this way, targeting founders who had posted about cold outreach burnout, and booked 14 qualified demos in 14 days with zero manual outreach and a reply rate 4x our previous cold campaigns (see [case studies](/case-studies)). Every one of those people was a stranger. Every message referred to something they had just written.

## Sales prospecting vs lead generation vs lead qualification

The three terms get used as if they were one, and most explainers separate them in paragraphs. The table is shorter.

| | Sales prospecting | Lead generation | Lead qualification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who starts | The seller | The buyer, prompted by marketing | Either |
| Input | A customer profile and a list | Content, ads, forms, events | A reply or a form fill |
| Output | Named people contacted with a reason | Contacts who raised a hand | A yes or no on problem, authority and timing |
| Who does it | Founders, SDRs, account executives | Marketing | SDRs or the closer |
| Measured by | Replies and meetings per 100 contacts | Leads per month and cost per lead | Share of leads that become opportunities |
| Fails when | The list is broad and the message generic | Nobody is searching for what you sell | The checklist is applied after the demo instead of before |

Prospecting and lead generation feed the same qualification step from opposite directions. The lesson on [outbound sales](/learn/what-is-outbound-sales) covers the seller-starts side as a whole; prospecting is its first and largest chunk.

Buyers have changed what they expect from that first contact. In a Gartner survey of 645 B2B buyers fielded in August and September 2025, 67% said they prefer a rep-free buying experience, 45% had used generative AI during a recent purchase, buyers consulted an average of seven information sources, and 69% said they prefer to validate AI-generated insights with a sales rep ([Gartner, 20 May 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-20-gartner-survey-finds-sixty-nine-percent-of-b-two-b-buyers-turn-to-sales-reps-to-validate-ai-generated-insights)). A buyer who would rather not talk to a rep still makes room for one who shows up at the moment they need a check on what they have read. Prospecting that starts from what the buyer just did in public lands at that moment; prospecting that starts from a title filter lands at random.

## How much time should sales prospecting take?

Give prospecting a fixed weekly budget and spend it in the same order every week. A workable budget for a founder or a one-person sales function is five hours a week, split as one hour on the list, two hours on research, one hour on first messages and one hour on follow-ups and logging. That is a starting rule, not a measured optimum; the point is that the number is written down and defended.

When the budget runs out before the list does, cut the list, not the research. Hunter's numbers above show that 50 well-read contacts outperform 1,000 skimmed ones, so the week with 25 contacts and 25 real first lines is the better week. The order of cuts: drop the lowest-fit segment first, then the channel with the worst reply rate, then the volume. Never drop the follow-ups; they carry a large share of the replies.

> [!TIP]
> Keep a scoreboard with four columns: contacts, replies, meetings, week. After three weeks you have your own reply rate, and every decision about the list, the channel or the message can be made against it instead of against an industry average.

Prospecting is the wrong first move in two cases. If buyers already search for what you sell and you can afford to wait for them, build inbound first and prospect only the accounts you cannot afford to miss. And if you cannot yet name one public sign that a company has your problem, go back to the profile, because every message you send before that will be a guess.

## How to use sales prospecting this week

Three actions, each under an hour:

1. **Write the public sign.** Finish this sentence for your business: "A company needs us when it publicly ...". A hire, a post, a launch, a complaint, a funding round. If you cannot finish it, your list will be a database filter and your batches must stay small.
2. **Pick 25 people and find one reason each.** Use the search strings in the [LinkedIn search operators guide](/guides/linkedin-search-operators-for-prospecting) to find people who posted about the problem this month. Write the first line of each message from what they said.
3. **Book the budget.** Put five hours in next week's calendar under "prospecting", in the split above, and set the scoreboard up before the first message goes out.

If step 2 is where the hour disappears, the free [Buying Signal Playbook](/signal-playbook) lists 12 signals worth chasing and 8 to ignore, with a script for each, so the reason in every first line comes from something the buyer just did rather than from an hour of searching.

## FAQ

### What is sales prospecting in simple terms?
Sales prospecting is finding specific people who could buy what you sell and contacting them first. You decide who fits, find a reason to write to each person now, send a short message and follow up. The result is a conversation with someone who was not looking for you, which is where most new pipeline comes from when buyers do not search for your product.

### What is the difference between prospecting and lead generation?
Who starts. In prospecting the seller picks the person and makes the first contact. In lead generation marketing attracts people who raise a hand, through content, ads, forms or events, and sales follows up. Both feed lead qualification. Prospecting is measured in replies and meetings per 100 contacts; lead generation in leads per month and cost per lead.

### What is the difference between a lead and a prospect?
A lead is anyone who has shown some interest, such as a form fill or a webinar sign-up, whether or not they fit your customer profile. A prospect is a named person who fits the profile and has been, or is about to be, contacted by you. Leads come in from marketing and get checked for fit; prospects are chosen for fit and then contacted.

### What are the main sales prospecting methods?
Database prospecting, LinkedIn prospecting, cold email, cold calling, referrals, signal-based prospecting and event prospecting. They differ mainly in where the list comes from and how specific the first message can be. Small, well-researched batches outperform large generic ones on every channel, and LinkedIn carries practical daily caps on messages and connection requests that limit volume.

### Who is responsible for sales prospecting?
At companies with a sales development team, SDRs prospect and qualify, then hand qualified conversations to account executives; [The Bridge Group's 2025 report](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group) puts the median at one SDR for every 2.4 account executives. At companies of one to twenty people the founder or the first salesperson does it, usually inside a fixed weekly budget alongside everything else.

### What is an example of sales prospecting?
A labelled hypothetical: a founder selling compliance software to small accounting firms lists 40 firms with five or more staff, finds the managing partner at each, and notes which ones posted about a regulatory deadline this month. She writes to those 12 first, each message naming the post, with a follow-up four days later. Nobody asked to hear from her. That is prospecting.
