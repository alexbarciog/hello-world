Intentsly vs Clay is a choice between a workbench and a worker. Clay is a spreadsheet-shaped platform for building lists, enriching them from a marketplace of data vendors, running AI research on each row and pushing the result into cold email. Intentsly watches LinkedIn for the people engaging with competitor and industry posts right now, scores them against your ideal customer profile, and its AI SDR sends the messages, answers the replies and books the meeting. Pick Clay to build the machine; pick Intentsly to have the conversation handled.

Everything about Clay below comes from clay.com and university.clay.com, opened on 31 October 2026, with a link on each claim. Everything about Intentsly comes from what the product does today, limits included. If the enrichment side of Clay is what you are weighing, [what lead enrichment is and where the data comes from](/learn/what-is-lead-enrichment) defines the terms; this page compares the two products in depth.

## Where is Clay the better choice?

Clay is a general-purpose tool and Intentsly is a narrow one. If any of these is why you are shopping, Intentsly is the wrong pick.

**You need to build and enrich lists.** This is Clay's core job: import or source a list, then run waterfall enrichment so that when one provider misses an email the next one tries, buying from "200+ data and AI vendors" under one contract ([homepage](https://www.clay.com/)). Intentsly enriches the people its signals found with verified emails and nothing else. It is not a list builder and has no marketplace.

**You want signals beyond LinkedIn.** Clay's signals doc lists job changes, promotions, new hires, job postings, news and fundraising, topic intent and web intent, and you can turn any enrichment into a custom signal ([signals doc](https://university.clay.com/docs/signals)). Intentsly watches LinkedIn engagement only. If your trigger is "they just raised a round" or "they posted a CISO role", Clay has it and Intentsly does not.

**You run AI research per row.** Claygents, Clay's web research agents, "surface the insights reps spend hours hunting for" and can be customised with the Claygent builder on every plan, including Free ([outbound](https://www.clay.com/use-cases/outbound), [pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing)). Intentsly scores against your ICP; it does not run open-ended research on each prospect.

**Email is your main channel.** Clay's Sequencer sends cold email from accounts you connect or buy in Clay, with four emails per sequence, sentiment-tagged replies and an inbox per rep ([sequencer](https://university.clay.com/docs/getting-started-with-sequencer), [sequencer announcement](https://www.clay.com/blog/clay-email-sequencer)). Intentsly's AI SDR sends LinkedIn messages and connection requests only. It does not send email at all.

**You want the workflow to be yours.** Clay's homepage sells orchestration: workflows, a CLI and API, reusable functions, and webhooks that automate "any signal" on Growth and above ([homepage](https://www.clay.com/), [pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing)). Intentsly is a fixed pipeline: signal, score, message, reply, meeting.

**You need Salesforce, a warehouse or an API.** Clay's pricing page lists CRM auto-sync, HTTP API integration and native warehouse support for "Snowflake, Fivetran, Postgres, Databricks, and BigQuery" on Growth and above ([pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing)). Intentsly integrates natively with HubSpot, Cal.com, Google Calendar and LinkedIn, and supports export; it lists no Salesforce connection and no API.

**You want a free plan with no clock on it.** Clay's Free plan includes 500 actions and 100 data credits a month, unlimited seats and tables, up to 200 rows per table, and "Enrich with Claygent" ([pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing)). Intentsly's trial is 7 days; the free part is [/try-ai](/try-ai), which shows matching buyers without an account but is a preview, not a plan.

## Where is Intentsly the better choice?

**The lead gets an answer without you.** This is the row to weigh first. In Clay, a reply lands in a rep's inbox: the Sequencer announcement says "Each rep gets their own permissioned inbox in Clay to see and manage replies", and the automation on offer is to "have Claygents draft replies that go to Slack for rep approval" ([sequencer announcement](https://www.clay.com/blog/clay-email-sequencer)). A person still reads, approves and sends. In Intentsly, the [AI SDR](/features/ai-sdr) holds the LinkedIn conversation in your place and sends your Cal.com or Google Calendar link so the lead books the meeting. If your inbox is the bottleneck, this is the difference to weigh.

We ran this on ourselves at launch: our own signals and AI SDR, targeting founders who posted about cold outreach burnout, booked 14 qualified demos in 14 days with zero manual outreach, and the reply rate was 4x our previous cold campaigns. Most of those leads came from signals caught in real time rather than from a list. Across Intentsly customers in October 2026, a typical account books 2 to 4 meetings a week.

**Clay does not send on LinkedIn.** Clay's own docs call the Sequencer "Clay's cold email tool", and its outbound page says to "Export directly to email and multi-channel sequencers such as Outreach, Instantly, and HeyReach or use Clay's sequencer" ([sequencer](https://university.clay.com/docs/getting-started-with-sequencer), [outbound](https://www.clay.com/use-cases/outbound)). The HeyReach integration adds a lead "to an existing HeyReach campaign from a professional profile URL", and "Campaigns cannot be created from within Clay" ([HeyReach doc](https://university.clay.com/docs/heyreach-integration-overview)). So a LinkedIn play in Clay is three tools: Clay for the list, HeyReach or similar for the sending, and you for the replies. Intentsly's AI SDR sends the connection request, sends the message and carries the thread from one place.

**Engagement is the product, not a column.** In Clay, post engagement is one action among hundreds: give it a post URL and it returns the top reactions, comments or shares, with a default of 10 and a maximum of 30 per call ([professional posts](https://www.clay.com/university/guide/professional-posts-integration)). You pick the posts, run the table, score the rows and push them somewhere. Intentsly watches the competitor and industry-leader posts you name, the keywords people post about, competitor page followers and hashtag engagers, continuously, and scores every person against your ICP. When you would otherwise be pasting post URLs into a table every morning, [LinkedIn signals](/features/linkedin-signals) is the feature that does that step for you. To decide which behaviours deserve a message before you set any of this up, the free Buying Signal Playbook lists 12 worth chasing and 8 to ignore at [/signal-playbook](/signal-playbook).

**No credit meter to manage.** Clay bills in two currencies: Actions, where "Each record enriched or exported counts as 1 Action", and Data Credits, which "start at $0.05 each", with unused Actions expiring each cycle and one-time top-ups carrying "a 30% premium" ([credits doc](https://university.clay.com/docs/actions-data-credits), [pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing)). That is fair for a marketplace, and it means someone on your team owns the meter and the budget per run. Intentsly's only limits are the LinkedIn caps: 30 messages and 30 connection requests per account per day, 15 recommended.

**Nothing to build.** Clay rewards people who like building tables; its Free plan lets you learn it before you pay. Intentsly is self-serve, live in about five minutes, with no sales call and cancel anytime. If nobody on the team wants to own a workbook, that matters more than any feature row.

## What does each tool cost?

Clay's pricing page, opened today, lists four plans: Free, Launch, Growth and Enterprise, with annual billing marked "Save 10%" ([pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing)). Its plans and billing doc gives Launch as "Starting at $185/month" with 15,000 actions and 2,500 to 10,000 data credits a month, and Growth as "Starting at $495/month" with 40,000 actions and 6,000 to 100,000 data credits a month; Enterprise is custom at 100,000 or more of each ([plans and billing](https://university.clay.com/docs/plans-and-billing)). The pricing page itself shows a slider per plan with lower monthly starting figures at the smallest tier, so open the page and set the slider to your volume before you compare.

Three rules on Clay's pages change the real cost. Unused Data Credits roll over on monthly plans but are "capped at 2× your plan's monthly credit limit", and on annual plans only 15% rolls over, "provided you renew on the same or a higher-tier plan" ([plans and billing](https://university.clay.com/docs/plans-and-billing)). Actions do not roll over at all. One-time top-ups cost 30% more than plan credits ([credits doc](https://university.clay.com/docs/actions-data-credits)). The 14-day trial includes 1,000 data credits and access to webhooks, CRM integrations, email sequencers and the HTTP API, but not phone enrichment ([plans and billing](https://university.clay.com/docs/plans-and-billing)).

Intentsly: see the [pricing page](/pricing). There is a 7-day free trial, setup is self-serve with no sales call, and you can cancel anytime.

The useful comparison is cost per booked meeting, not per seat. Clay's plan price is the smaller part of its cost; the larger part is the Data Credits each row consumes, the LinkedIn tool you attach, and the person who builds the tables and answers the replies. Price that person's time before you compare plans.

## Can you use both?

On different jobs, yes, and for a team that already runs Clay this is the sensible setup. Clay for list building, enrichment, Claygent research, email sequences and the non-LinkedIn signals. Intentsly for the LinkedIn conversation: signals, scoring and the AI SDR from your own profile. Both connect to HubSpot, so the CRM stays the single record; Clay's HubSpot integration can "import, create, update, and manage HubSpot objects directly in Clay" for companies, contacts, deals and leads ([HubSpot doc](https://university.clay.com/docs/hubspot-integration-overview)).

There is no sending conflict, because Clay does not touch LinkedIn. The one thing to watch is the HeyReach or similar tool you may have attached to Clay for LinkedIn steps. Both it and Intentsly would send from the same profile, and LinkedIn counts every action against one set of limits whichever tool took it. If Intentsly runs on a profile, keep the other tool off that profile. Intentsly's 30-a-day caps are per account for that reason.

## How do you switch from Clay to Intentsly?

You will probably not switch outright, because Intentsly does not replace Clay's list building, enrichment or email. What moves is the LinkedIn engagement play. Clay's docs describe a CSV export from the Actions menu, with each exported record counting as one Action, and a HubSpot integration that writes contacts back to the CRM ([CSV lesson](https://university.clay.com/lessons/download-as-a-csv-clay-101), [credits doc](https://university.clay.com/docs/actions-data-credits), [HubSpot doc](https://university.clay.com/docs/hubspot-integration-overview)). Before you retire the Clay table that scrapes post engagers:

1. Export the table as CSV so you keep the people who already replied and know who is mid-conversation.
2. Write down which posts, authors, competitors and keywords produced those replies. These become your signal definitions in Intentsly.
3. Keep the HubSpot connection; Intentsly connects to the same HubSpot, so the record stays whole.
4. Pause the HeyReach or other LinkedIn campaign on the profile Intentsly will use. Leave Clay's email sequences running.
5. Finish any open LinkedIn threads yourself before the AI SDR starts, so nobody gets two voices.

Intentsly finds people from live signals rather than from an imported list, so step 2 carries over, not the export. Set up the same competitors, keywords and hashtags as [LinkedIn signals](/features/linkedin-signals), connect HubSpot, and the scoring runs against the profile you already know works. The [seven-step signal-based outbound setup](/guides/set-up-signal-based-outbound) is the order to do it in, and if you built the Clay version from the [post-engager scraping methods](/blog/scrape-linkedin-post-likers-and-commenters), the same post list is your starting signal set.

## Which should you pick?

If you need to build and enrich lists, want signals beyond LinkedIn, run AI research per row, send email as the main channel, want to own every step of the workflow, need Salesforce, a warehouse or an API, or want a free plan with no clock: Clay. If your buyers are on LinkedIn, you want the person who engaged this morning rather than a column you refresh, and you want the reply answered and the meeting booked without you in the inbox: Intentsly. A team that already runs Clay can keep it and add Intentsly for the LinkedIn side, with one rule about which tool touches the profile.

## FAQ

### Does Clay send LinkedIn messages?
No, not on the pages opened today. Clay's docs describe the Sequencer as "Clay's cold email tool", and its outbound page routes LinkedIn work to tools such as HeyReach, where the campaign must already exist ([sequencer](https://university.clay.com/docs/getting-started-with-sequencer), [HeyReach doc](https://university.clay.com/docs/heyreach-integration-overview)). Intentsly's AI SDR sends connection requests and messages on LinkedIn itself and carries the thread.

### Does Clay reply to leads for you?
Not without a person in the loop. The Sequencer announcement says each rep gets a permissioned inbox to manage replies, and Claygents can draft replies "that go to Slack for rep approval" ([sequencer announcement](https://www.clay.com/blog/clay-email-sequencer)). Intentsly's AI SDR continues the LinkedIn conversation on its own and sends your calendar link when the lead is ready to book.

### Does Clay track LinkedIn post engagement?
Partly. Clay's post engagement page says it can "Track who's liking and commenting on professional posts" and calls this "a soft intent signal"; the professional-posts actions take a post URL and return at most 30 reactions, comments or shares per call ([post engagement](https://www.clay.com/providers-signal-type-subtype/post-engagement-monitoring), [professional posts](https://www.clay.com/university/guide/professional-posts-integration)). Intentsly watches named competitors, keywords, pages and hashtags continuously and scores every engager.

### What are Clay credits?
Two meters. Actions count platform work: "Each record enriched or exported counts as 1 Action", and unused Actions expire each cycle. Data Credits buy data and AI from the marketplace, "start at $0.05 each", and a fully enriched record "typically costs 6–20 Data Credits" ([credits doc](https://university.clay.com/docs/actions-data-credits), [pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing)). Intentsly has no credit meter; its limits are the LinkedIn caps.

### Does Clay have a free plan?
Yes. Clay's pricing page lists a Free plan with 500 actions and 100 data credits a month, unlimited seats and tables, up to 200 rows per table and Claygent enrichment, plus a 14-day trial of the paid plans ([pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing)). Intentsly offers a 7-day trial and a no-account preview of matching buyers at [/try-ai](/try-ai).

### Can I export my data from Clay?
Yes. Clay's docs describe opening the Actions menu, choosing Export and clicking "Download CSV", with past files kept under Available Exports; each exported record counts as one Action ([CSV lesson](https://university.clay.com/lessons/download-as-a-csv-clay-101), [credits doc](https://university.clay.com/docs/actions-data-credits)). Export the people who replied on LinkedIn and note which posts and keywords produced them; those become your signal definitions in Intentsly.
