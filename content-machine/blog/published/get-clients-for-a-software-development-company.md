The fastest way to get clients for a software development company is to reach a business in the week it decides to pay an outside team instead of hiring one, and that decision is usually visible on LinkedIn: a founder asking who built your app, a raise announced by a team with no engineers, a CTO's last day, a senior developer vacancy reposted again. Message that person within a day with one observation about their build, not a deck. Below: seven signals, a week-one plan, and when referrals win.

## Why does the usual dev shop advice produce so few clients?

Most pages on how to get clients for a software development company give the same list: pick a niche, put case studies on the site, write technical content, get Clutch reviews, bid on Upwork, run cold email, ask for referrals. None of it is wrong, and the "other ways" section below says when each of them beats outreach. The problem is timing. A cold list of companies that could use developers says nothing about which of them has decided to buy this quarter, so most messages land on people with no open project and no budget.

The buyers exist, and most of them are not software companies. In Clutch's November 2025 survey of 405 US small and medium-sized business owners, [55% planned to increase technology spending in the coming year](https://clutch.co/resources/invest-tech-meet-customer-expectations) and only 5% planned to cut it; web development (39%), mobile app development (38%) and software development (37%) were the top priorities for 2026. In the same survey the most common obstacle was limited internal technical expertise (26%), 45% of the companies relied solely on internal teams, and 21% ran a hybrid of in-house and outside help. That gap between what they plan to build and who they have to build it is your pipeline.

The moment a company moves from "we should build this" to "we need someone to build this" is short, and it is public more often than people expect, because the people who make that decision post about hiring, funding, launches and departures. A buying signal is a public action that shows a company is in-market now; the [trigger event lesson](/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales) explains the general idea. For a development shop the triggers are unusually clear, because almost every one of them is a company admitting it cannot build something itself.

## Where do software development clients show up on LinkedIn?

Each row is a moment you can find with a free LinkedIn account. The search column is what to type into the search bar before switching to the Posts tab (or the Jobs tab, where stated) and filtering to the past week. The last column is the message, written to quote the signal and offer one scoped piece of work, not a menu of services. Add your own stack and vertical words to every search.

| Signal | Where | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A direct ask for a recommendation ("can anyone recommend a development agency?", "who built your app?", "looking for a dev shop for an MVP") | Posts tab: `"recommend" "development agency"` or `"who built your app"` or `"looking for a dev shop"`, past week | The shortlist is forming in the comments that day; the buyer has a scope in mind and a rough budget | Answer in the thread with one useful point about scoping their build, then connect with a note that quotes the post and offers a 20-minute scoping call |
| A raise announced by a team with no engineers ("we raised our pre-seed to build", "seed round closed, now we build") where the company page lists a handful of people and no engineering titles | Posts tab: `"we raised" "build"` or `"pre-seed" "build"` plus your vertical; open the company page and check the People tab for engineering titles | The money has a product promise attached and a deadline the investors know; hiring a team takes months the runway does not have | Congratulate, name the first thing you would scope for the promise in their post, and offer a fixed-scope first milestone |
| A technical co-founder search ("looking for a technical co-founder", "CTO wanted, equity") | Posts tab: `"technical co-founder"` or `"looking for a CTO"`, past week | A non-technical founder with a product idea and no one to build it; the co-founder search usually takes longer than the founder expects | Do not argue against the co-founder. Offer to build the first version while they search, so the eventual CTO inherits working code instead of a slide deck |
| A non-software company announcing a customer-facing build ("launching our customer portal", "we're building an app for our customers", "digitising our ordering") | Posts tab: `"customer portal" "we're building"` or `"our new app" "customers"` plus your industry words; exclude software companies by eye | An operations or product lead at a logistics, healthcare, retail or finance company owns a build their IT team has never done | Ask what the first release has to do, then offer a review of their scope or a one-month discovery with a named engineer |
| A technical leader leaving, or a first CTO arriving ("last day as CTO", "stepping down as Head of Engineering", "excited to join as VP Engineering") at companies of 10 to 200 people | Posts tab: `"last day" CTO` or `"stepping down" "head of engineering"`; also `"excited to join" CTO` | The roadmap does not stop when the leader does; the new arrival audits every vendor and has budget for work they cannot staff in the first quarter | For the departure, ask who owns the roadmap meanwhile and offer to bridge for a defined number of weeks; for the arrival, offer one scoped project the new leader can show in their first 90 days |
| A platform deadline or a forced rebuild ("end of life", "migrating off", "rebuilding from scratch", "our agency ghosted us", "technical debt is killing us") | Posts tab: `"end of life"` plus the platforms you work in, or `"migrating" "from"`, or `"our agency"`, past month | A current supplier or platform is on notice and the date is fixed; a second opinion is wanted before the next contract | Ask what went wrong before offering anything; offer a code or architecture review with a written result, which earns the rebuild conversation |
| An engineering vacancy that will not fill (a senior or lead role reposted, open for two months, or a founder posting "struggling to hire developers") | Jobs tab: your stack's titles at companies in your segment, sorted by date, watching for reposts; Posts tab: `"struggling to hire" developers` or `"can't find" engineers` | The work is budgeted and already late; augmentation starts next week, a hire starts in a quarter | Message the engineering lead with the one role you can fill this month and a named engineer's profile, and offer a two-week paid trial |

Two filters keep the list honest. A signal older than about two weeks is cold: the founder who asked for an agency a month ago has usually chosen one, and the role that was reposted has sometimes been filled. And a post from another agency asking "who needs an app built?" is not a buyer, however many comments it collects. Reddit and X carry the same requests; the [Reddit method](/blog/find-b2b-leads-on-reddit) has the queries, and both are manual channels, because Intentsly monitors LinkedIn only.

The vacancy row rests on a public number. In ManpowerGroup's February 2026 talent shortage release, based on interviews with 39,063 employers in 41 countries, [72% of employers reported difficulty filling roles](https://investor.manpowergroup.com/node/68796), engineering was among the hardest skills to find (named by 19% of employers) behind AI model and application development (20%), and the Information sector reported the highest shortage of any sector at 75%. A reposted senior engineer vacancy is the visible end of that statistic, and a shop with a bench is the fastest answer to it.

## How to get clients for a software development company by hand: a week-one plan

The plan takes about an hour a day with a free LinkedIn account. Sales Navigator makes the company-size and job-post steps faster but is not required.

1. **Day 1: write the one-sentence offer and list the voices.** Pick the build type and the company type, for example "MVPs for seed-stage fintech founders in the UK" or "Flutter apps for logistics companies of 50 to 500 people". Then list ten accelerators, investors, no-code communities and design agencies your buyers follow, and five development shops that compete with you. The [ideal customer profile lesson](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) is the longer version of this step; a shop that says "full-stack development for anyone" produces searches that match everyone and messages that land with no one.
2. **Day 2: build the saved searches.** Create the seven Posts-tab searches from the table with your stack and vertical words added, each filtered to the past week, plus one Jobs search for your stack's senior titles. Bookmark them. The [hiring signals routine](/guides/track-hiring-signals-for-sales) goes deeper on the vacancy signal, and the [funding guide](/guides/find-companies-that-just-raised-funding) covers the raise signal, which has the shortest window.
3. **Days 3 to 5: thirty minutes of reading, thirty of replying.** Run the searches, read the new posts, and for each real buyer do the "what to do" step from the table. Reply in public first, connect second. Send at most 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day; LinkedIn tolerates more, your acceptance rate does not.
4. **Day 6: follow up and find the budget owner.** For every accepted connection with no reply, send one message that adds something: a question about their first release, an observation about the platform they named. Never "just checking in". Where the person who posted is a marketing lead or an office manager rather than the person who signs, the [decision-maker guide](/guides/find-decision-makers-on-linkedin) has the six steps to find who does.
5. **Day 7: count.** Signals found, replies, scoping calls booked. Fewer than ten relevant posts in a week means the stack and vertical words are wrong, or your buyers do not post; widen the words before changing the niche.

Message template for the direct ask, for copying:

```
Saw your post asking who built [company]'s app. Before you pick
a team, the two scope questions that decide the budget are
[offline mode and the payment flow]; most quotes you get will
assume different answers. Happy to spend 20 minutes on your
scope and send you the questions to ask every agency, no
proposal attached.
```

Finding the right fifteen people each day is where the hour goes; if you want that step done for you, the [LinkedIn signals feature](/features/linkedin-signals) runs the same searches continuously and hands you the list already scored against your ideal client.

## Other ways to get software development clients, and when they beat signals

Signals produce conversations with strangers who have a project this month. Three other routes earn their place, and for most shops one of them comes first.

**Past clients and referrals** beat signals for the first client and for the cheapest next one. The last company you shipped for knows what you can do, already has a backlog, and will introduce you to the founder they had coffee with last week if you ask at the end of every project rather than when the pipeline is empty. Every page that ranks for this question says so, and the consensus is right. The ceiling arrives when that network has been asked once; the plan above is what you run when it has.

**Partners** beat signals when you serve one platform or one build type. A design agency that does not code, a fractional CTO who scopes but does not build, and a platform's own partner programme all meet buyers before they post anything, and each of them wants a shop to hand the build to. The cost is dependence on partners who also hear from your competitors, and a referral fee or a white-label margin, which is why most shops run partnerships and signal outreach together.

**Directories and marketplaces** beat signals when you have no reviews yet and need cash flow this quarter. Clutch's own guide for buyers says [most software development projects on the platform cost between $10,000 and $49,999](https://clutch.co/developers/service-guide) and most listed companies charge $25 to $49 an hour, which is the honest picture of what a directory sends you: many small, price-compared projects. Take a few for the testimonials, then let the plan above find the projects a buyer never posts to a marketplace. The [software company page](/solutions/find-leads-for-a-software-company) covers the neighbouring case where you sell a product as well as services, the [SaaS company page](/solutions/get-clients-for-a-saas-company) covers the product-only case, and the [solutions index](/solutions) covers other business types.

Cold lists are the one tactic signals replace outright. Almost every company of 5 to 500 people could use outside developers in theory, which is why a scraped list of them converts so badly. A post says which of them has decided to buy this month.

## FAQ

### How do I get my first client for a software development company?
Ask the last two companies you worked for, and any founder you have helped informally, whether they have a scoped project this quarter: an MVP, a feature their team cannot staff, an integration. That is where almost every shop's first client comes from. Run the week-one plan in parallel from day one, so that when the network has been asked, strangers who posted a signal this week are already in conversation.

### Is Upwork or Clutch worth it for a software development company?
Yes, early, and for a specific job: reviews and cash flow. A marketplace sends small, price-compared projects to many bidders, so take a few, deliver well, collect the reviews, and reduce your dependence on it as the signal plan and referrals grow. A directory listing without reviews rarely produces anything; the reviews are what buyers filter on.

### Should a software development company niche down to one industry or one technology?
One of the two, and the searches in the table decide which. If your buyers describe their problem in industry words ("patient intake", "load tendering"), niche by industry. If they describe it in technology words ("migrating off Magento", "a Flutter rebuild"), niche by technology. Either way, a niche that produces fewer than ten relevant posts a week is too narrow for outreach alone; pair it with partners.

### How long does it take to get clients for a software development company?
Weeks for the first conversations and a quarter for the first signed build from a stranger, because a scoping call turns into a contract only after a proposal, a budget approval and sometimes a procurement step. Referrals are faster. Count weekly: signals found, replies, scoping calls. Expect a small fixed-scope first milestone before a long engagement.

### Do I need case studies before I can get software development clients?
Not before the first conversation; the messages in the table point to one observation about the buyer's build, not to a portfolio. You need one proof of work by the time a buyer compares you with two other shops, and a short written post-mortem of a past project with what went wrong is more convincing than a logo wall. Write one after every project, with the client's permission.

### How do I get software development clients from the US or Europe from another country?
Run the same plan with the region in the searches and the time zone in the message. Marketplace bidding rarely works for this, because a buyer there compares you on price with teams it cannot tell apart. Reply in public first so the buyer sees how you think, write in the buyer's language, offer a short paid trial with a named engineer, and state your overlap hours. The co-founder search and the vacancy rows are the most open to remote teams, because the buyer has already accepted that the work will not sit in their office.
