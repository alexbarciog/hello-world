> [!SUMMARY]
> - A marketing qualified lead (MQL) is a lead that marketing has judged more likely to buy than the rest, because the person fits your customer profile and did something that shows interest.
> - Marketing judges it, usually with a score and a threshold. Nobody has spoken to the person yet, which is what separates it from a sales qualified lead.
> - Benchmarks put MQL to SQL conversion in the low to mid teens, so most MQLs never become sales conversations.
> - If you are a team of one to ten with no inbound funnel, write a three-line rule (fit, action, threshold) or skip the stage and qualify from public buying signals instead.

**A marketing qualified lead is a lead that the marketing team has judged more likely to become a customer than other leads, based on who the person is and what they have done, and has marked as ready to hand to sales.** That is the short answer to "what is a marketing qualified lead". The acronym is MQL. The judgement comes from data, not a conversation: a title that fits, a company of the right size, and an action such as a demo request or a pricing page visit.

## Why does the marketing qualified lead matter?

Because it is the first filter between "gave us an email address" and "worth a salesperson's hour". Without it, sales either calls everyone, which wastes the day, or calls nobody, which wastes the leads.

The filter is coarse by design, and the numbers show it. First Page Sage's 2026 report, built on client data from 2019 to November 2026 across 30 industries, puts the average MQL to SQL conversion rate at 16.1% with a median of 15%; B2B SaaS and IT and managed services sit at 13%, staffing and recruiting at 12% ([First Page Sage, MQL to SQL conversion rate by industry](https://firstpagesage.com/reports/mql-to-sql-conversion-rate-by-industry/)). The same agency's lead-to-MQL benchmark, from a different and older dataset, puts the average share of leads that become MQLs at 31% ([First Page Sage, lead to MQL conversion rate benchmarks](https://firstpagesage.com/reports/lead-to-mql-conversion-rate-benchmarks/)). Chain the two and roughly five in a hundred raw leads reach a sales conversation.

Many teams never find this out. A write-up of Belkins' 2026 B2B conversion survey reports that 38% of teams do not track MQL to SQL conversion at all, rising to 44% at companies with one to ten employees, and that 54% of outbound-led teams convert fewer than 10% of their MQLs into SQLs ([MarketScale, Belkins finds 38% of B2B teams don't measure MQL-to-SQL conversion](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/marketing-tech/belkins-finds-38-of-b2b-teams-dont-measure-mql-to-sql-conversion)).

> [!EXAMPLE]
> A labelled, hypothetical example. A four-person IT consultancy runs a guide download on its site and gets 60 sign-ups a month. The founder used to call all 60. She writes an MQL rule: company with 20 to 300 staff, a title in IT or operations, and either a second visit to the services page or a reply to the follow-up email. Eleven of the next 60 pass. She calls eleven, books three meetings, and stops calling students and competitors.

## How does a lead become marketing qualified?

Three things have to be true, and every ranking page lists variations of them. Written as a rule, they fit on three lines.

| Line | What it checks | Form-based evidence | What it keeps out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fit | The person and company match your [ideal customer profile](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) | Title, company size, industry, country fields | Students, job seekers, competitors, the wrong country |
| Action | The person did something that shows interest, not just presence | Pricing page visit, demo request, webinar attended, guide downloaded, email clicked | One page view, a cookie banner click, an unsubscribe |
| Threshold | Enough fit and action, added up, to be worth a call | A score above a line you set, or two named actions within 30 days | Interest with no fit, fit with no interest |

The threshold is where [lead scoring](/learn/what-is-lead-scoring) comes in. Teams assign points to each fit attribute and each action, and the lead becomes an MQL when the total crosses a line. The number itself matters less than the fact that marketing and sales agreed on it.

1. **Write the fit line from your last ten customers.** Size, industry, role of the person who signed. If those ten have nothing in common, the lesson on the [buyer persona](/learn/what-is-a-buyer-persona-in-b2b) comes first.
2. **Pick the actions your customers took before buying.** Look at what the last ten did on the site or in email before the first call. Those actions become your action line.
3. **Set the threshold low and raise it.** Start with "fit plus one action". If sales rejects more than a third of what it gets, add a second action or tighten the fit.

### What counts as an MQL when leads come from LinkedIn, not forms?

Every page that ranks for this term assumes gated content and a marketing team scoring it. A founder or a five-person agency usually has neither. The three lines still hold; the evidence comes from public behaviour instead of a form.

| Line | Public LinkedIn evidence |
|---|---|
| Fit | Headline and current company on the profile, company page headcount, industry |
| Action | A post asking for a vendor, a comment under a competitor's post, a like on a post about the problem you solve, a "starting a new position" update |
| Threshold | Fit plus one action in the last 14 days, or two actions in the last 30 |

This is the MQL that a [buying signal](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal) produces. The person never filled in a form, but they said who they are and what they want in public, and the date is on the post. Reading those profiles and grading each one against the fit line is the slow part; Intentsly does that on LinkedIn, scoring each person who posts about a pain point or engages with a competitor against your ideal customer profile, and [Try the AI](/try-ai) shows the matching buyers for your service with no account.

That is how we launched, with no inbound funnel and no MQL stage at all. We ran our own signal agents on LinkedIn, targeted founders posting about cold outreach burnout, and let the conversational AI handle the replies. The system booked 14 qualified demos in 14 days with zero manual outreach, at a reply rate 4x our previous cold campaigns, and most of those leads came from intent signals caught in real time.

## Marketing qualified lead vs SQL, SAL and PQL

These four labels describe one lead at different moments, judged by different people with different evidence.

| | Who judges | Evidence | Typical moment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing qualified lead (MQL) | Marketing, usually by score | Fit fields plus downloads, visits, clicks | After a form fill or an engagement |
| Sales accepted lead (SAL) | Sales, on paper | A rep agrees the MQL meets the handoff rule and will work it | Before any contact |
| Sales qualified lead (SQL) | Sales, after contact | Fit, need, authority and timing confirmed in a conversation | After the first exchange |
| Product qualified lead (PQL) | Product analytics | A usage threshold crossed in a trial or free plan | After sign-up |

The MQL shows interest; the [sales qualified lead](/learn/what-is-a-sales-qualified-lead) shows a buyer. The gap between them is the conversation, which is why MQL to SQL conversion is the ratio most teams argue about. The [product qualified lead](/learn/what-is-a-product-qualified-lead) replaces the MQL in companies with a free trial, because usage is stronger evidence than a download.

> [!NOTE]
> The MQL has a serious critic. Forrester has argued since 2023 that the MQL counts the wrong unit: it scores one person, while its Buyers' Journey Survey found 93% of B2B buyers bought as part of a group of two or more and 71% in a group of four or more ([Forrester, Saying goodbye to MQLs](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/saying-goodbye-to-mqls-whats-the-big-deal-about-getting-rid-of-mqls)). Its Palo Alto Networks client story reports opportunities with several people attached were eight times more likely to advance, and a 17% higher closed-won rate after the switch to buying groups ([Forrester, Palo Alto Networks client story](https://www.forrester.com/client-stories/palo-alto-networks-buying-groups)). For large deals with many stakeholders, that argument is strong. For a ten-person company selling to a founder who decides alone, the MQL still describes the situation well enough.

## How to use the marketing qualified lead this week

1. **Decide whether you need the stage at all.** If fewer than 30 leads a month arrive and one person does both marketing and sales, an MQL rule is paperwork. Qualify every lead directly with the four-line [sales qualified lead](/learn/what-is-a-sales-qualified-lead) test and skip the handoff. Above that volume, or with two people in the loop, write the three lines.
2. **Write the three lines and put them where sales can see them.** Fit, action, threshold, one sentence each. The guide on [qualifying leads before outreach](/guides/qualify-leads-before-outreach) turns the rule into a five-minute check per lead.
3. **Count two ratios for a month.** Leads to MQL and MQL to SQL. If the second sits far below the low-teens figures in the benchmark ([First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/reports/mql-to-sql-conversion-rate-by-industry/)), the fit line is too loose; if sales accepts almost everything, the threshold is too high and leads are waiting in nurture for no reason.

## FAQ

### What does MQL stand for?

MQL stands for marketing qualified lead. It is a lead that the marketing team has judged more likely to buy than other leads, based on fit (the right kind of person at the right kind of company) and behaviour (an action that shows interest, such as a demo request or a pricing page visit). The label means marketing checked; it does not mean anyone has spoken to the person.

### What is the difference between an MQL and an SQL?

A marketing qualified lead has been judged by marketing from data: fit fields and actions such as downloads or visits, usually added up as a score. A sales qualified lead has been checked by a salesperson, normally in a conversation, and confirmed to fit, have a need, hold some authority and have a reason to act. The MQL shows interest; the SQL shows a buyer.

### What is a good MQL to SQL conversion rate?

First Page Sage's 2026 report, based on its client data from 2019 to November 2026, gives a 30-industry average of 16.1% and a median of 15%, with B2B SaaS and IT and managed services at 13% ([First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/reports/mql-to-sql-conversion-rate-by-industry/)). Treat the figures as a range from one agency's clients. A rate far above them often means the MQL bar is too high; far below, the wrong people are arriving.

### What are examples of MQL criteria?

Fit criteria: job title, company size, industry, country. Action criteria: requested a demo, visited the pricing page, attended a webinar, downloaded a guide, clicked a campaign email, returned to the site within a week. A common rule is fit plus one strong action, or fit plus two weaker actions within 30 days. The best criteria come from what your last ten customers did before their first call.

### What is a sales accepted lead?

A sales accepted lead (SAL) is a marketing qualified lead that a salesperson has agreed, against a written handoff rule, to work. It sits between MQL and SQL and exists so that marketing and sales count the same leads: sales must accept or reject each one, usually within a day, before any qualifying conversation. Teams where one person does both jobs can skip the stage.

### Is the MQL dead?

Forrester has argued since 2023 that companies should replace MQLs with buying groups and opportunities, because B2B purchases are made by groups and the MQL scores one person ([Forrester, Saying goodbye to MQLs](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/saying-goodbye-to-mqls-whats-the-big-deal-about-getting-rid-of-mqls)). That case is strongest for large deals with many stakeholders. For a small company selling to a founder or a single department head, a three-line MQL rule still does the job.
