The fastest way to get clients for an IT consulting company is to reach a company in the week a deadline it did not choose becomes its problem: a server platform leaving support, an ERP go-live that slipped, an auditor's list, an acquisition that doubled the systems, a new IT director with ninety days to show a plan. The people who sign post those moments on LinkedIn, and a message that quotes the deadline arrives before any shortlist exists. Below: seven deadlines, a week-one plan, and when referrals win.

## Why does the usual advice for IT consulting clients stall?

Most pages on how to get clients for an IT consulting company list the same channels: a better website, referrals, LinkedIn posting, vendor partnerships, webinars, cold email, a freelance marketplace to start. None of it is wrong, and most of it is slow in the same way. A referral arrives when a past client happens to meet someone with a project this quarter. A blog post is read by whoever searched, whenever they searched.

A consulting project is different from managed IT support, and the difference decides where the clients are. An MSP sells a standing arrangement, so its buyers are companies whose arrangement broke; the [MSP page](/solutions/get-clients-for-an-msp) covers those signals. A consultancy sells a scoped piece of work with a date on it, and the date is almost always set by someone else: a software vendor, a regulator, an acquirer, a board. The buyer does not wake up wanting a consultant. The buyer wakes up with a deadline and discovers the team cannot meet it alone.

That is why the window matters more than the size of the market. Gartner's July 2026 forecast puts [worldwide IT spending at $6.37 trillion in 2026, up 14.2%](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-07-27-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-it-spending-to-grow-14-point-2-percent-in-2026-totaling-6-point-37-trillion), but IT services inside it at $1,570 billion, growing 5.3%. The hardware and software budgets are growing almost three times faster than the budget for people to implement them, which is exactly the gap a company feels when the vendor's date arrives and nobody internal has done the migration before. A [trigger event](/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales) is the general name for that moment; for IT consulting the triggers come with calendar dates attached.

## Where do IT consulting clients show up on LinkedIn?

Each row is a deadline you can find with a free LinkedIn account. The search column is what to type into the search bar before switching to the Posts tab (or the Jobs tab, where stated) and filtering to the past week. The message column quotes the deadline and offers one scoped piece of work, not a capabilities deck. Reddit and X carry the same posts and the [Reddit method](/blog/find-b2b-leads-on-reddit) has the queries; treat them as manual channels, because Intentsly monitors LinkedIn only.

| Signal | Where | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A vendor end-of-support date on their stack ("still on Windows Server 2016", "Exchange on-prem", "our ERP version goes out of support next year") | Posts tab: `"Windows Server 2016" upgrade` or `"end of support" migrate` or `"out of support" ERP`; Jobs tab: roles that list the legacy product by name | A date the company cannot move is now months away, extended security updates cost money and buy only time, and the in-house team has never done this migration | Message the IT lead or founder with the date, what breaks after it (support, compliance questionnaires, insurance), and a fixed-scope assessment of what has to move first |
| An ERP or CRM selection, or a slipping go-live ("evaluating NetSuite vs Dynamics", "six months into our ERP implementation", "go-live moved to Q2") | Posts tab: `"ERP implementation" "go-live"` or `evaluating NetSuite OR Dynamics OR SAP "we"` or `"ERP" "over budget"`, past month | Either a decision is being made without an independent view, or a project is in trouble and the vendor's partner is the only adviser in the room | Offer a short, independent review: requirements against the shortlist, or a two-week health check of the stalled project, with a written finding they can take to the vendor |
| A first IT director or a new CIO ("excited to join as Head of IT", "first 90 days as CIO", a company's first "IT Director" job post) | Posts tab: `"new role" "Head of IT"` or `"first 90 days" CIO`; Jobs tab: "IT Director" or "Head of IT" at companies with no existing IT leader on the page | A new leader has one quarter to produce a roadmap and no team to produce it; a first IT hire means the founder has admitted the stack is now a business risk | Congratulate, then offer the one deliverable a new leader needs in month one: a current-state assessment or a roadmap they can present, scoped and dated to their first board meeting |
| A cloud cost or data-centre exit post ("our AWS bill doubled", "moving off the colo before the lease ends", "lift and shift did not save us anything") | Posts tab: `"cloud bill" OR "AWS bill" OR "Azure bill" "we"` or `"data center" lease exit` or `"lift and shift"`, past month | The company has already spent on infrastructure and is now paying for the architecture decisions nobody made; a lease end or a renewal is the date | Name the two causes of that bill you see most often, offer a cost and architecture review with a date tied to their lease or renewal |
| An acquisition or merger ("thrilled to announce we have acquired", "joining forces with", a founder posting about integrating two teams) | Posts tab: `"we have acquired" OR "we've acquired" "team"` or `"joining forces" integration`, past month; the company page shows the new headcount | Two of everything: two identity systems, two ERPs, two email tenants, and a deal timeline that assumed integration would be quick | Offer an integration assessment scoped to the thirty days after close: identity, email, finance systems, and the order to merge them |
| An audit or compliance deadline ("SOC 2 by Q2", "ISO 27001 certification", "NIS2 applies to us", "DORA", "cyber insurance renewal wants MFA everywhere") | Posts tab: `"SOC 2" "we need"` or `"ISO 27001" certification "this year"` or `NIS2 OR DORA "we"` or `"cyber insurance" renewal`, past week | A third party has written the checklist and set the date; the owner needs someone to own the list and the evidence, and the internal team is already full | Name the two controls that usually fail first on that framework and offer a gap assessment mapped to the questionnaire or standard they named |
| A stalled AI pilot or a public request for a partner ("we rolled out Copilot and nothing changed", "board wants an AI roadmap", "anyone recommend an Azure partner / ERP consultant?") | Posts tab: `"Copilot" rollout "we"` or `"AI roadmap" board` or `recommend "IT consultant" OR "Azure partner" OR "ERP consultant"`, past week; read the comments, where other buyers add "following" | The pilot proved the data and process were not ready, and the board question is now a deadline; a recommendation request is an open evaluation with a shortlist forming in the thread the same day | For the pilot: offer a data and process readiness review with a written scope. For the request: answer in the thread with one useful question, then connect with a note that quotes the post and offers a twenty-minute comparison call |

Two filters keep the list honest. A deadline post older than a month is usually spoken for: the company picked a partner or decided to pay for extended support. And a post from another consultancy or a vendor asking "who is still on an old server?" is not a buyer, however many comments it collects.

The end-of-support row is not a corner case this winter. Microsoft's Windows IT Pro blog states that [Windows Server 2016 reaches end of support on January 12, 2027](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/t5/windows-it-pro-blog/plan-for-windows-server-2016-and-windows-10-2016-ltsb-end-of/ba-p/4496136), that Windows 10 Enterprise LTSB 2016 reached it on October 13, 2026, that Extended Security Updates can be bought for up to three years, and that the ESU programme "is not intended to be a long-term solution but rather a temporary bridge". Every company that posts about a 2016-era server between now and January is a project with a date on it.

The ERP row has its own evidence. Panorama Consulting's 2026 ERP Report, released on 4 March 2026, found that [more than a quarter of organizations exceeded their project budgets](https://www.panorama-consulting.com/panorama-consulting-group-releases-latest-study-of-erp-implementation-outcomes-across-the-globe/), with additional technology needs the leading cause. An owner posting "we are six months in and the budget is gone" is describing the most common ERP outcome, and the consultancy that answers with a two-week health check is the only independent adviser they will hear from.

## How to get clients for an IT consulting company by hand: a week-one plan

The plan takes about an hour a day with a free LinkedIn account. Sales Navigator makes the headcount and job-post steps faster but is not required.

1. **Day 1: write the one-sentence offer and list the voices.** Pick the company type, size and stack, for example "manufacturers of 50 to 300 people in the Midlands still running on-premises Windows Server and a 2016-era ERP". Then list ten vendors, analysts, auditors and founders your buyers follow, and five consultancies that compete with you. The [ideal customer profile lesson](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) is the longer version of this step.
2. **Day 2: build the saved searches.** Create the seven Posts-tab searches from the table with your region, industry and platform words added, each filtered to the past week or month as the table says, plus two Jobs searches: the IT Director role and any role that names your platform. Bookmark them. The [hiring-signals routine](/guides/track-hiring-signals-for-sales) goes deeper on the job-post signal, the [job-change guide](/guides/track-job-changes-on-linkedin-for-sales) covers the new-CIO signal, and the [search operators guide](/guides/linkedin-search-operators-for-prospecting) explains the quoting and OR rules.
3. **Days 3 to 5: thirty minutes of reading, thirty of replying.** Run the searches, read the new posts, and for each real buyer do the "what to do" step from the table. Reply in public first, connect second. Send at most 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day; LinkedIn tolerates more, your acceptance rate does not.
4. **Day 6: follow up and find the budget owner.** For every accepted connection with no reply, send one message that adds something: a question about which workloads sit on the 2016 server, a line about the evidence their auditor will ask for first. Never "just checking in". Where the person who posted is a systems administrator rather than the signer, the [decision-maker guide](/guides/find-decision-makers-on-linkedin) has the six steps to find who holds the budget.
5. **Day 7: count.** Signals found, replies, calls booked. Fewer than ten relevant posts in a week means the platform or industry words are wrong, or your buyers do not post; widen the terms and add the Jobs tab before changing the niche.

Message template for the end-of-support signal, for copying:

```
Saw your post about the servers still on Windows Server 2016.
Support ends 12 January 2027, and the two things that usually
surface late are the line-of-business apps nobody has tested
on a newer OS and the insurance questionnaire that asks about
unsupported systems. Happy to run a fixed-scope assessment of
what has to move first, no retainer attached.
```

Finding the right fifteen people each day is where the hour goes; if you want that step done for you, the [LinkedIn signals feature](/features/linkedin-signals) runs the same searches continuously and hands you the list already scored against your customer profile.

## Other ways to get IT consulting clients, and when they beat signals

Signals produce conversations with strangers who have a deadline. Three other routes earn their place, and for most consultancies one of them produces the first client.

**Vendor partner programmes and subcontracting** beat signals when your work is tied to one platform. Microsoft, NetSuite, Salesforce and the rest route implementation work to partners, and a larger consultancy that won a programme it cannot staff will subcontract the delivery. The cost is that the vendor's partner list is long, the referral comes with the vendor's priorities attached, and you never own the relationship. Most small consultancies run the partner channel and signal outreach together: the first fills the calendar, the second fills it with clients who are yours.

**Past clients and former employers** beat signals for the first client, almost always. Every page that ranks for this question says so, and the consensus is right: the company whose migration you ran as an employee, or the client whose ERP you rescued, already trusts you and already has peers with the same server. Ask after every project closes and at every renewal. The ceiling arrives when that network has been asked once; the plan above is what you run when it has.

**Tender portals, marketplaces and content** beat signals for the buyer who has already decided to buy and is now comparing. An RFP on a public portal, a Clutch listing or an article that ranks for "NetSuite implementation partner" all reach a buyer late, when a shortlist exists and price is the comparison. Keep them running; do not wait for them. Cold lists are the one tactic signals replace outright: almost every company of 20 to 500 people could use a consultant in theory, which is why a scraped list converts so badly, and a post says which of them has a date this quarter. ## FAQ

### How do I get my first IT consulting client?
Ask the last two employers and any company whose systems you have fixed whether they have a dated project this quarter: an end-of-support migration, an audit, an ERP review. That is where most consultancies' first contract comes from. Run the week-one plan in parallel from day one, so that when the network has been asked, the companies that posted a deadline this week are already in conversation.

### How long does it take to get clients for a new IT consulting business?
Weeks for the first client when it comes from a former employer or a vendor partner, and a quarter or more for a stranger, because a consulting project needs a scope, a budget owner and often a procurement step. Signal outreach shortens the front of that: a company that posted an end-of-support date or an audit deadline has already committed to spending; the only open question is with whom.

### Should an IT consultancy specialise in one platform or one industry?
Pick the specialisation from where the deadlines are. A platform specialisation (Microsoft, NetSuite, AWS) gives you vendor end-of-support dates and a partner programme to work. An industry specialisation (manufacturing, healthcare, financial services) gives you regulators and auditors who set dates for everyone at once. Either produces searchable posts; "general IT consulting" produces neither, and the searches in the table will tell you within a month which one your buyers post about.

### Do RFPs and tender portals work for small IT consultancies?
They work for a consultancy that has already been talking to the buyer, and badly for one that first meets the buyer in the portal. By the time a tender is published, requirements have been shaped by whoever was in the room, often the vendor's partner. Use the signals to be in the room during the ERP selection or the audit scoping, and treat the eventual RFP as paperwork for a decision already leaning your way.

### Is LinkedIn effective for getting IT consulting clients?
It is effective when you use it to find deadlines and ineffective when you use it to broadcast. The buyers for IT consulting are founders, finance leaders and IT leaders at companies of 20 to 500 people, and they post about the server, the audit, the acquisition and the new role in public. Read those posts and reply with one specific observation. Posting your own content helps the reply land; it does not replace the search.

### How is getting clients for IT consulting different from getting clients for an MSP?
An MSP sells a standing service, so its signals are a support arrangement breaking: an outage, the only technician leaving, a recommendation request. A consultancy sells a dated project, so its signals are deadlines set by someone else: a vendor's support date, a go-live, an auditor, an acquirer. The searches overlap, the offers do not. Lead with a scoped assessment and a date, not a monthly contract.
