> [!SUMMARY]
> - Pick groups by what was posted in the last 30 days, not by member count; most groups are quiet but the members are still real people.
> - Join, open the member list with "Show all", and search it by title, company or location. On Sales Navigator, use the Group Members filter.
> - Add the people who posted or commented in the group feed this month: that is the higher-intent list.
> - Score to 20 to 40 names a week, send connection requests that name the group and the post, and follow up within a week.

Here is how to find prospects in LinkedIn groups without a scraper: search LinkedIn for your buyers' problem and filter to Groups, join the two or three groups with real posts in the last 30 days, search the member list by the titles in your ideal customer profile, add everyone who posted or commented this month, score the shortlist to 20 to 40 people, and send a connection request that names the group and the post. Budget 90 minutes per group in week one, then 30 minutes a week.

## What you need to find prospects in LinkedIn groups

- A LinkedIn account that can send 10 to 15 connection requests a day without a warning. A new or dormant one should run the [four-week warm-up routine](/guides/warm-up-linkedin-account-for-outreach) first.
- A one-line ideal customer profile with a title list, a company-size range and a region, because every filter in Step 3 comes from it. The lesson on [what an ideal customer profile is](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) gets you one in ten minutes.
- A spreadsheet with eight columns: name, title, company, group, where found (list or feed), hook, score, status.
- Sales Navigator is optional. Everything below works on a free account; Navigator adds a Group Members filter and the usual title and headcount filters on top.
- Patience with admins. Private groups need approval, which can take days or never come, so start the join requests on day one and do the rest while you wait.

A LinkedIn group, for this guide, is any group page with a member list: an industry association's group, a vendor's user community, a regional founders group, a job-function group. Membership is a weak signal on its own. Someone who posted a question in the group this month is a [buying signal](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal), and the method below treats the two lists differently.

## Step 1: Pick groups by activity, not by member count

The group decides the list, so the first half hour is about choosing. Skylead's prospecting guide, updated in September 2026, puts it bluntly: most LinkedIn groups are dead, but the members stay listed, and that roster is the asset ([Skylead, updated 14 Sep 2026](https://skylead.io/blog/linkedin-prospecting/)). A quiet group still gives you a filtered list of people who once cared enough to join; a live group gives you that plus posts to reply to.

1. **Search the buyer's problem, not your category.** In the LinkedIn search bar type a phrase a buyer would use, such as `founders hiring first sales rep` or `HR leaders mid-market`, press Enter and choose the Groups filter at the top of the results. Searching your own category ("sales automation") fills the results with groups full of your competitors.
2. **Open the group page and read the feed before you ask to join.** Some groups show their posts to non-members; others show only the description, the rules and the member count until you are in. Where you can see the feed, count posts from the last 30 days that are questions or opinions rather than reposted articles.
3. **Sample ten members.** Look at the member avatars under the group name and open the ones the page lets you see, or check the people who commented on the visible posts. If the headlines are your buyers' titles, keep the group; if they are other vendors, drop it.
4. **Check the rules.** Groups that ban promotional posts are better for you, not worse: the feed is cleaner and the members have not been pitched to death.
5. **Ask to join two or three, not twenty.** LinkedIn caps membership at 100 groups and pending join requests at 20, according to the limits table on Lilach Bullock's guide, updated 21 November 2026 ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/linkedin-groups/)). You will not work more than three groups a week anyway.

| Test | Keep the group | Skip the group |
|---|---|---|
| Posts in the last 30 days | Questions and opinions from members | Only reposted articles, or nothing |
| Ten sampled headlines | Your buyers' titles | Vendors, coaches, recruiters in your category |
| Comments under recent posts | Members answering each other | Only the poster, or spam links |
| Group rules | No promotion allowed | Anything goes |
| Admin | Active in the last month | Last post years ago |

> [!TIP]
> Groups run by associations, regional chambers and software user communities beat "B2B Sales Professionals" style mega-groups. The mega-group has the member count; the association group has the buyers.

## Step 2: Join and open the member list

You will not see the full member list of a LinkedIn group until you are a member of it. Scrupp's tutorial, updated today, answers "can you scrape members if you are not in the group" with a flat no ([Scrupp, updated 27 Nov 2026](https://scrupp.com/blog/linkedin-group-members-r1)), and Kanbox's help page says the same for its Sales Navigator import ([Kanbox help, 22 Jul 2026](https://intercom.help/kbox/en/articles/11081449-how-to-prospect-linkedin-group-members-with-kanbox)). LinkedIn's own help pages on groups could not be opened for this guide, so the rules below are stated as the pages opened on 27 November 2026 describe them.

1. **Click Join or Request to join.** Public groups let you in at once; private ones queue you for an admin. While you wait, work the public groups.
2. **Open the member list.** On the group page, click the member count or the member avatars, then click "Show all" on the right, which is the path HubSpot's messaging guide gives ([HubSpot, updated 8 Sep 2026](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/send-a-linkedin-message-to-someone-youre-not-connected-to)). On mobile, tap the member faces under the group name.
3. **Use the search box at the top of the list.** HubSpot describes it as a name search; try a title such as `VP Sales` or a company name first, and if your account only matches names, scroll the list and use the browser's find (Ctrl+F or Cmd+F) on the headline text instead.
4. **Note the ceiling.** PhantomBuster's export tool states that it can typically extract up to 2,500 visible members per group ([PhantomBuster, updated 8 Jul 2026](https://phantombuster.com/blog/social-selling/how-to-find-and-connect-with-linkedin-group-members/)), which is a sign that the list LinkedIn renders is capped well below the member count on big groups. Search for your titles instead of scrolling.

## Step 3: Filter the list to your ideal customer

The member list search box, or a browser find on the headlines, is the free-account filter. Run one pass per title in your ideal customer profile, then one per target company, and paste the matches into the spreadsheet. The [search operators guide](/guides/linkedin-search-operators-for-prospecting) covers the Boolean forms LinkedIn's people search accepts; inside a member list, keep queries to one or two words, because the box is a simple match, not a full people search.

| What you want | Free account (member list) | Sales Navigator (lead search) |
|---|---|---|
| People with a title | Search or Ctrl+F the title, e.g. `Head of Growth` | Current job title filter, then Group Members filter |
| People at target companies | Search or Ctrl+F the company name | Current company filter, then Group Members filter |
| People in a region | Not available; read the headline or profile | Geography filter |
| People at a company size | Not available | Company headcount filter |
| People who changed jobs recently | Not available | Changed jobs filter |
| Members of several groups at once | One group at a time | Group Members filter accepts several groups |

On Sales Navigator, open Lead filters, scroll to the Group Members filter (in some accounts it sits under "View all filters"), click "Find people in groups", type the group name and pick one or several, then stack title, headcount and geography on top ([Simply B2B help](https://simply-b2b.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/24348797910801-How-to-target-group-members-on-Sales-Navigator); [Skylead](https://skylead.io/blog/linkedin-prospecting/)). The LeadHunter filter guide describes the filter as members of groups you both belong to ([LeadHunter on DEV, updated Feb 2026](https://dev.to/dspv/linkedin-sales-navigator-search-filters-2026-advanced-guide-24dl)), so join first, then filter.

> [!NOTE]
> A 2nd or 3rd-degree result is what you want. A 1st-degree connection is already in your network; message them directly and leave them out of this list.

## Step 4: Add the people who posted or commented this month

The member roster tells you who joined once; the feed tells you who has a problem now. In the group, switch the feed from Recommended to Recent where the option is shown, open every post from the last 30 days, and record the poster plus every commenter whose headline fits your ideal customer. Tag them "feed" in the spreadsheet; they go to the top of Step 5.

Read the post before you write the name down. A member asking "which tool do you use for X" or "how do you handle Y" is showing a pain point in public; a member reposting a company announcement is not. The [buying signals list](/blog/linkedin-buying-signals) sorts the signals worth chasing from the ones to ignore, and the same sorting applies inside a group.

Doing this by hand across three groups costs about 20 minutes a week; across the whole of LinkedIn it is impossible by hand, which is the step [Intentsly's LinkedIn signals](/features/linkedin-signals) replace: it watches keyword posts and competitor-post engagement across the platform, not only inside the groups you joined, and scores each person against your ideal customer profile before you write to anyone.

## Step 5: Score the shortlist to 20 to 40 people

A 2,500-name export is not a prospect list; it is a spreadsheet nobody messages. Score every name and keep the ones that clear a bar. At 10 to 15 connection requests a day, 20 to 40 names is a week of sends with room for follow-ups.

| Criterion | Points | How to check in 20 seconds |
|---|---|---|
| Title matches the ideal customer profile | 3 | Headline |
| Company size in range | 2 | Company page, "employees" line |
| Posted or commented in the group in the last 30 days | 3 | Group feed, newest first |
| Post or comment describes a problem you solve | 3 | Read it |
| Active on LinkedIn in the last two weeks (any post or comment) | 1 | Profile, Activity |
| Region you sell to | 1 | Profile location |

Keep anyone at 7 or more. Names from the feed with a problem statement usually land at 9 or above; roster-only names peak at 7. If the roster gives you more than 40 at 7, raise the bar to 8 rather than sending more.

Scoring is the step people skip, and it is why "I messaged 200 group members and got nothing" is a common complaint. The guide to [qualifying leads before outreach](/guides/qualify-leads-before-outreach) gives a longer version of this sheet if you want more criteria.

## Step 6: Message them with the group and the post as the reason

There are two routes to a fellow group member, and the pages opened for this guide do not agree on how reliable the free one is. HubSpot's guide says you can message a group member you are not connected to only if that person allows messages from group members in their settings; otherwise LinkedIn pushes you to InMail ([HubSpot, updated 8 Sep 2026](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/send-a-linkedin-message-to-someone-youre-not-connected-to)). Neal Schaffer's guide, revised 19 November 2026, says free messaging is limited to 1st-degree connections and fellow group members, and that those group messages often land in the recipient's Other tab, where few people look ([Neal Schaffer](https://nealschaffer.com/how-do-i-contact-2nd-and-3rd-degree-linkedin-connections/)).

1. **Default to a connection request with a note.** It reaches everyone on the list, and the note is where the group reference goes. HubSpot's guide puts the note limit at 200 characters and says free accounts get only a few personalized notes a month ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/send-a-linkedin-message-to-someone-youre-not-connected-to)), so spend the notes on the feed names and send plain requests to roster names.
2. **For feed names, reply in the thread first.** A useful comment under their post, then the request a day later. They will recognise the name.
3. **Test the direct message on five people.** Open the member list, find the person, click Message. If it sends, you have a free channel to that person; if it asks for InMail, move on. Do not build the week around it.
4. **Keep the group out of the pitch.** The group is the reason you noticed them, not the reason they should buy.

```message
Saw your question in the SaaS Founders Network about tracking who's trialling after a webinar. We hit the same wall last year. Happy to share what we settled on if useful.
```

```message
We're both in the Mid-Market HR Leaders group. I work with HR teams at 50 to 300 people on [problem]. No pitch, just keen to compare notes on how you handle it.
```

The guide to [writing a LinkedIn outreach message that gets a reply](/guides/write-linkedin-outreach-message) covers the follow-up sequence; the only change for group prospects is that the first line names the group or the post.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not post "DM me" offers in the group or message every member the day you join. Admins remove people for it, and the account limits in the [warm-up guide](/guides/warm-up-linkedin-account-for-outreach) apply to group messages as much as to any other.

## Step 7: Follow up within a week and log what happened

Record every send in the status column: requested, accepted, replied, meeting, no. After seven days, send one follow-up to accepted-but-silent contacts that refers to the same post, then stop. Once a month, re-run Steps 4 and 5 on each group; the feed changes every week, the roster barely moves.

## Worked example: Harbor Payroll (hypothetical)

Harbor Payroll is a made-up payroll service for companies of 20 to 200 people in the UK, used here to show the counts. Its ideal customer is the finance lead or founder at that size.

1. **Step 1.** Searched `finance leaders SME UK` and `founders payroll` with the Groups filter. Six groups; two had member posts in the last 30 days and buyer headlines in the preview, one was an association group with clear rules. Requested to join all three; the association group approved after four days.
2. **Step 2 and 3.** Member list searches for `Finance Director`, `Head of Finance`, `Founder` and `CFO` across the three groups returned 214 names. Dropping vendors, recruiters and 1st-degree connections left 131.
3. **Step 4.** The feeds held 23 posts from the last 30 days; 9 were questions, and poster plus commenters gave 31 feed names, 14 of them with a problem statement about payroll, pensions or year-end.
4. **Step 5.** Scored: 14 feed names at 9 or more, 17 feed names at 7 or 8, 22 roster names at 7. Kept 36.
5. **Step 6 and 7.** Week one: 12 comments under posts, 36 connection requests with notes on the 31 feed names. Status column after two weeks: whatever the numbers turn out to be, they come from your own log, not from this page.

The point of the example is the funnel shape: a few thousand members become about 200 roster matches, about 30 feed names, and a list of 36 that one person can work in a week.

## Where this goes wrong

1. **Exporting the roster and messaging all of it.** A 2,500-row export of people who joined a group years ago is a cold list with a group name attached. Connection acceptance drops, the account gets a warning, and the feed names who would have replied never hear from you.
2. **Judging groups by member count.** A group with 200,000 members and no posts in two years gives you nothing to reply to and a member list that renders far short of the headline number. A 900-member association group with weekly questions gives you a working list every month.
3. **Leading with the pitch.** The group reference buys ten seconds of attention. A message that spends those seconds on your product reads like every other one in the Other tab.

## What to do with the list

The list is the start of outbound, not the end. Move the accepted contacts into the same sequence you run for everyone else, keep the group as the first-line reason, and track reply rate by source (feed versus roster) so next month's time goes to the groups that produced meetings.

When we launched Intentsly we ran this kind of signal-first outreach with our own product, aimed at founders complaining about cold outreach burnout, and booked 14 qualified demos in 14 days with zero manual outreach and a reply rate 4x our previous cold campaigns ([case study](/case-studies)). The signals came from posts across LinkedIn rather than from groups, which is the part that does not scale by hand.

## FAQ

### Can you see who is in a LinkedIn group without joining?

Only partly. Before joining you can see the member count, the description and, in some groups, the posts, plus a preview of a few member avatars. The full member list with its search box opens after you join, and both Scrupp and Kanbox say their tools cannot pull a list from a group you are not in, so there is no shortcut round the join request.

### Can you message LinkedIn group members without being connected?

Sometimes. HubSpot's guide says the recipient has to allow messages from group members in their settings; Neal Schaffer's says group messages often land in the Other tab. Treat the direct message as a bonus to test on a few people, and use a connection request with a note as the route that reaches everyone on your list.

### How many LinkedIn groups can you join?

Lilach Bullock's limits guide, updated in November 2026, puts the cap at 100 groups per member and 20 pending join requests at a time; Taplio's guide gives the same 100 figure. For prospecting, the cap does not matter: three live groups give one person more names than they can work in a week, and a quiet group costs time without producing posts to reply to.

### Does the Sales Navigator Groups filter work for groups I have not joined?

The filter is described as matching members of groups you both belong to, so join the group first, then search with the Group Members filter. The upside of the paid route is stacking: title, company headcount, geography and changed-jobs filters on top of group membership, and several groups in one search, which the free member list cannot do.

### Are LinkedIn groups dead?

Most feeds are quiet, and Skylead's 2026 guide says so directly, but the member lists remain and a minority of groups, often run by associations or user communities, still carry weekly questions. The 30-day activity test in Step 1 separates the two in five minutes, so you only spend join requests on groups that can produce feed names.

### How do I search inside a LinkedIn group for specific people?

Open the group, click the member count, then "Show all", and type a name, a title or a company into the search box at the top of the list. If your account only matches names, scroll and use the browser's find on the headline text. For region, headcount or job-change filters you need Sales Navigator's lead search with the Group Members filter.
