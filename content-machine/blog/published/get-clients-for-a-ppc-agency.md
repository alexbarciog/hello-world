> - Companies buy PPC help in the week their paid numbers go wrong or change hands, and both moments are public on LinkedIn.
> - Seven posts predict a paid-media budget: cost-per-lead complaints, an agency switch, a paid media job ad, a new marketing leader, funding or a launch, a founder running ads alone, and questions under Google Ads posts.
> - Run the six searches in the table for an hour a day, reply in public first, send 15 connection requests at most, and count replies on day seven.
> - Referrals and partnerships beat signals once you have case studies; the Google Partner directory beats them when you need your first one.

The fastest way to get clients for a PPC agency is to reach a company in the week its paid numbers go wrong or change hands: a post about cost per lead climbing, a job ad for a paid media manager, a new head of marketing, a funding round, or a founder asking which Google Ads agency to hire. All of these are visible on LinkedIn. Message them that week with one observation about their ads and you become the agency the others get compared to.

## Why does most PPC client-finding advice stall?

Most pages on how to get clients for a PPC agency give the same list: pick a niche, get certified, run ads on yourself, ask for referrals, cold email a free account audit, partner with a web design shop, list on a directory, choose between a percentage of spend and a flat fee. None of it is wrong. The problem is timing: every item either waits for a buyer to arrive or sends the same audit offer to a thousand companies that signed a retainer last quarter.

The agencies already know outbound is hard. In SparkToro's [2025 State of Digital Agencies](https://sparktoro.com/blog/digital-agency-new-business-still-a-concern-referrals-still-rule-for-lead-gen-and-are-sales-pipelines-finally-recovering/), 59% of agencies had tried outbound sales, and of those only 9% called it very effective while 33% said it was not effective at all. The same survey found 70% of agencies have nobody working on sales full-time, which rules out volume outbound for most of them.

PPC has one advantage over most services: the buyer's problem is a number they look at every week. In WordStream's [2026 Google Ads benchmarks](https://wordstream.com/blog/2026-google-ads-benchmarks), built from 13,474 US search campaigns between April 2025 and March 2026, the average cost per click was $5.42 and the average cost per lead was $66.69, the first fall in five years, while the average conversion rate reached 8.18%. A business owner whose own cost per lead went the other way notices, and many of them say so in public. A buying signal is a public action that shows someone is in-market now; the [buying signal lesson](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal) has the definition and a test.

| Questions under Google Ads, Meta Ads and PPC tool posts | Comments on posts by Google Ads, PPC tool vendors such as Optmyzr, and the PPC voices your buyers follow | A marketer asking "does this work for lead gen?" or "is Performance Max worth it for a small budget?" is shopping for an answer and often for a vendor | Answer the question in the thread, then connect with a note that continues the answer; your profile does the pitching |

Reddit (r/PPC, r/googleads, r/smallbusiness) carries the same complaints and recommendation requests and is worth a manual search a few times a week; the [Reddit method](/blog/find-b2b-leads-on-reddit) has the queries. Treat it as a manual channel; Intentsly monitors LinkedIn only. Two filters keep the list honest: a signal older than two weeks is cold, and a cost-per-lead rant by another PPC consultant is content, not a buyer.

## How to get clients for a PPC agency by hand: a week-one plan

The plan takes about an hour a day with a free LinkedIn account. Sales Navigator helps with the job search step but is not required.

1. **Day 1: write the one-sentence niche and list the voices.** Pick the business type and the platform, for example "Google Ads for home services companies with two to ten trucks" or "Meta and Google for Shopify stores under five million in revenue". Then list ten competitor agencies in that niche and five tool vendors or PPC voices your buyers follow. The [ideal customer profile lesson](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) is the longer version of this step.
2. **Day 2: build the saved searches.** Create the six Posts-tab searches from the table, each filtered to the past week, and one Jobs search for the in-house role. Bookmark them. Open the comment sections of the ten competitor agencies' latest posts and note who asks questions there.
3. **Days 3 to 5: thirty minutes of reading, thirty of replying.** Run the searches, read the new posts, and for each real buyer do the "what to do" step from the table. Reply in public first, connect second. Send at most 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day. LinkedIn tolerates more; your acceptance rate does not.
4. **Day 6: follow up and find the budget owner.** For every accepted connection with no reply, send one message that adds something, never "just checking in". The person complaining about cost per lead is often a marketing manager; the [decision-maker guide](/guides/find-decision-makers-on-linkedin) has the steps to find who signs.
5. **Day 7: count.** Signals found, replies, calls booked. Under ten relevant posts in a week means the search words are wrong or the niche is too quiet for LinkedIn; widen the terms before changing the niche.

Message template for the cost-per-lead post, for copying:

```
Saw your post about cost per lead climbing since [the month / the change].
I looked at your ads for ten minutes: [one specific, checkable observation,
e.g. your brand and non-brand terms share one campaign, and the landing page
for "emergency plumber" is the homepage]. If you want, I can spend 20 minutes
on a call walking through what I would change first. No audit deck, no proposal.
```

Finding the right fifteen people each day is where the hour goes; if you want that step done for you, the [LinkedIn signals feature](/features/linkedin-signals) runs the same searches continuously and hands you the list already scored.

## Other ways to get PPC clients, and when they beat signals

Signals produce conversations with strangers. Three other routes earn their place.

**Client referrals** beat signals once you have five or more clients who can show a cost-per-lead chart going down. In the [SparkToro survey](https://sparktoro.com/blog/digital-agency-new-business-still-a-concern-referrals-still-rule-for-lead-gen-and-are-sales-pipelines-finally-recovering/), referrals from existing and past clients were by far the biggest source of new business, partner referrals were the top source for 15% of agencies, and 73% named LinkedIn their most effective social channel for new business. The ceiling is the size of your client list, which is why agencies add outbound once the list stops growing. Ask for the referral the month the chart turns, not at renewal.

**Partnerships with agencies that do not do paid** beat cold outreach when you have no case study in a niche yet. An SEO agency's client wants faster results than organic can give; a web design agency finishes a site and the client needs traffic to it next week. Those agencies find their own clients the same way; the [SEO agency page](/solutions/find-clients-for-an-seo-agency) and the [web design agency page](/solutions/find-clients-for-a-web-design-agency) show the signals they chase, and the people posting those signals are your introductions.

**The Google Partner directory and marketplaces** beat signals when you need a first case study more than you need margin. Google's own [Partner requirements](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9702452?hl=en) are a 70% optimization score on the manager account, a 90-day ad spend of $10,000 USD across managed accounts, and 50% of account strategists certified, so the badge is reachable for a small agency with a handful of accounts. Clutch, DesignRush and Upwork deliver buyers with budgets today; the cost is price competition. Use them to get three case studies in a niche, then move the niche to the searches above.

Cold lists are the one tactic signals replace outright: a list says which companies could buy paid media, a cost-per-lead post says who is buying it this month. ## FAQ

### How do I get my first PPC client with no case studies?
Replace the case study with a diagnosis. Find a company showing one of the seven signals, spend twenty minutes on its ads, landing pages and search terms, and send the findings with no proposal. A checkable observation about their own account is more convincing than someone else's chart. Your first client's cost-per-lead graph becomes the case study for the second, so pick an account where a quick structural win is likely.

### Should a PPC agency run ads to get its own clients?
It is the tactic every ranking page recommends, and it works as proof of craft: a buyer who finds you through a tight search campaign has seen you do the job. It is also the most expensive route on this page, because you are bidding against every other agency on the same terms. Run it once you have a niche and a case study to land on; before that, the free searches above cost only an hour a day.

### How do PPC agencies get clients without cold email?
Public replies, referrals and signal outreach are not cold, because each one starts from something the buyer said. Replying under a "our cost per lead doubled" post is a reply, not a campaign. The free LinkedIn searches in the table and the daily sending limits cover it; the cost is an hour a day and the discipline to answer within a day of the post, before three other agencies do.

### Do I need the Google Partner badge to get PPC clients?
No, but it helps with buyers who search the Partner directory and with buyers who ask about it on a call. The requirements are on Google's own help page linked above and are reachable for a small agency once a few accounts are live. Treat the badge as a tie-breaker in an evaluation, not as a source of conversations; the conversations come from the signals.

### How long before signal outreach produces a PPC client?
Week one should produce replies and a call or two, not a signed retainer. A company posting about cost per lead this week is usually choosing help within the month, and a company hiring an in-house paid media manager decides within the quarter. Count signals, replies and calls weekly; once calls book every week, the first retainer is a matter of weeks.

### How do I find local PPC clients with this method?
Add the place to the searches instead of the industry: "Google Ads" plus your city, "head of marketing" plus the region. Local buyers post less about cost per lead and more about launches, new locations, hiring and seasonal pushes, so weight the funding, new-role and job-ad rows of the table, and use the posts to decide who to talk to at the next local business event.
