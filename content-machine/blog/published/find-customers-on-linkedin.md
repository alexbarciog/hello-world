The fastest way to find customers on LinkedIn is to stop searching for people who match your customer profile and start searching for people who are showing, this week, that they have the problem you solve: a post asking for a recommendation, a comment under a competitor's launch, a new hire announcing they own the budget. With a free account and the Posts tab you can find those people today. Below: the six signals, the exact searches, a week-one plan, and when LinkedIn is the wrong channel.

## Why does the usual LinkedIn advice take so long to produce a customer?

Most pages on how to find customers on LinkedIn give the same list: fix your headline, post three times a week, join groups, search by title, send a personalised connection note, follow up. None of it is wrong, but every step is about you, and none of it says which person in your search results wants to buy this month.

Buyers do most of the work before any seller is involved. In its [2024 Buyer Experience Report](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241009142556/en/6sense-Launches-2024-Buyer-Experience-Report-Unveiling-Global-B2B-Buyer-Trends), 6sense found that B2B buyers are nearly 70% through their purchasing process before they engage a seller, that 81% already have a preferred vendor at first contact, and that buyers initiate that contact more than 80% of the time. A title search finds people who could buy someday. A signal search finds people choosing a vendor now.

A buying signal is a public action that shows someone is in-market before they fill in a form; the [buying signal lesson](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal) has the definition and a test for telling a real one from noise. This page covers the LinkedIn version: which actions count, where to look, and what to send.

## Where do customers show up on LinkedIn?

Every row is visible with a free account. The search strings go in the main search bar with the **Posts** tab selected and the date filter on **Past week**. Replace the bracketed parts with your competitor names, problem words and buying titles.

| Signal | Where | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A post asking for a recommendation | Posts tab: `"anyone recommend" [service]`, `"looking for a" [service]`, `"can anyone suggest" [category]` | An open evaluation, usually with a deadline of days | Answer in the thread the same day, vendor-neutral; then connect with a note that quotes the question |
| Comments and reactions on a competitor's launch or product post | The competitor's company page and its founders' recent posts | Follows your category closely; a question in the comment means an active comparison | Reply to the comment first, then connect; offer your own option only in the second message |
| A post describing the pain you remove, in the buyer's words | Posts tab: `"[pain phrase]"` in quotes, such as `"replies dried up"` or `"our CRM is a mess"` | Problem acknowledged in public, vendor not chosen | Comment with a method they can use without you; connect; offer a look in the second message |
| A new role in a buying title | Posts tab: `"excited to join" "[title]"` or `"new role" "[title]"` | New owners review vendors in their first quarter | Congratulate, ask what they inherited, offer a short comparison note rather than a demo |
| A new follow of a competitor page, or category hashtag engagement | The competitor page's follower list; posts under the category hashtag | Early research; weaker than a comment but fresh | Connect with no pitch; let your posts carry the next step |
| A job posting for the role you replace or assist | Jobs tab: the role title, filtered to your segment | Budget exists for the outcome; you are cheaper than the hire | Message the hiring manager with how customers use you instead of, or alongside, that hire |

Two filters keep the list honest. A signal older than about two weeks is cold: the person who asked for a recommendation a month ago has usually chosen. And a signal from someone outside your buying titles belongs to a colleague; the [decision-makers guide](/guides/find-decision-makers-on-linkedin) shows how to walk from the commenter to the person who signs. Reddit and X carry the same questions and are worth a manual search a few times a week; the [Reddit method](/blog/find-b2b-leads-on-reddit) has the queries.

## Which LinkedIn method should you use to find customers?

The methods are not interchangeable: they differ in time to a first conversation and in what they cost. This table is the decision rule.

| Method | Time to first conversation | What it costs | Best for | Where it fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Signal search and reply (this page) | Days | Free account, about an hour a day | Any B2B seller with a defined problem and named competitors | Narrow categories where nobody posts about the problem |
| Title search and connection requests | Weeks | Free, or Sales Navigator for finer filters | Large, well-defined buyer segments | Low acceptance and lower replies, because nobody on the list is known to be in-market |
| Posting content | Months | Hours a week, every week | Founders who write well and sell a considered purchase | No reach until the network exists; inbound arrives unqualified |
| LinkedIn Ads | Days to a lead, longer to a customer | Budget per click, high in B2B | Teams with a conversion page and a process to catch leads | Small budgets; audiences that have never seen you |
| Groups and events | Weeks | Hours | Niche industries with active communities | Most groups are quiet |

With time and no budget, run signals and content together: signals produce conversations now, posts make the note land later. With budget and no time, signals still come first, because ads cannot tell you who is buying this week.

## How to find customers on LinkedIn by hand: a week-one plan

About an hour a day and a free account, built for one person. The example company is hypothetical.

1. **Day 1: write the signal list.** Three competitors, five phrases customers use for the problem (their words from past calls, not your positioning), and the two or three titles that buy. Example (hypothetical): Harbourline Bookkeeping sells monthly bookkeeping to e-commerce brands; its pain phrases are "bookkeeper quit", "books are three months behind" and "Shopify payouts don't match the bank".
2. **Day 1: save the searches.** In the Posts tab, run each pain phrase in quotes with the date filter on Past week, then each competitor name with "anyone using", "recommend" and "alternative". Save every search and follow the competitor pages.
3. **Day 2: build the watch list.** Open each competitor's three most recent posts and record every commenter in a buying title, plus anyone who asked a question: name, company, post, quote, date. The [prospect list guide](/guides/build-b2b-prospect-list) has the sheet layout.
4. **Days 2 to 5: act within a day of the signal.** Reply in the thread first, then send a connection note that quotes what the person wrote and asks one question. No link, no deck. Keep to 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day: LinkedIn restricts accounts that push past its invitation limits, and a restricted account ends the plan.
5. **Days 3 to 5: answer the open questions in full.** For every recommendation request, write the answer you would give a friend, including where you are the wrong choice, then message the poster with the one reason you might fit the job they described.
6. **Day 5: score the week.** Count signals found, messages sent, replies and calls booked. Few replies means the message is pitching; lead with their quote instead. Few signals means the pain phrases are your words, not the buyer's; pull new ones from the comments you read this week.
7. **Day 6: ask every conversation for names.** Everyone who takes a call, buyer or not, knows two people with the same problem.

If the daily searches are the step you want done for you, the [LinkedIn signals feature](/features/linkedin-signals) runs the competitor-post and keyword watches from steps 2 and 3 every day and hands you the list already scored against your customer profile.

## Other ways to find customers, and when they beat LinkedIn

**Referrals.** A warm introduction skips the trust step every LinkedIn message has to earn. If you have ten happy customers and have never asked each for two names, do that first. Referrals beat signals when the sale is large and relationship-led; they fail as a system because you cannot schedule them.

**Communities and events.** Slack groups, associations and niche conferences reach buyers who never post. They beat LinkedIn in industries where people do not post (manufacturing, healthcare, legal) and lose on speed, because you meet people on the community's calendar, not when their problem starts.

**Paid search.** Someone typing "[service] for [industry]" into Google is further along than most LinkedIn posters. Paid search wins when the query exists and you can afford the click; it fails for categories nobody searches for yet.

One reason to keep LinkedIn whatever else you run: Gartner's [March 2026 sales survey](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience) of 646 B2B buyers found that 67% prefer a rep-free experience. A buyer who would rather not talk to a rep will still answer a reply to their own post, and that is the opening this method is built on. The quickest way to see whether your buyers are posting is to look: [try the AI](/try-ai) shows matching buyers on LinkedIn with no account, which tells you whether the signals exist in your category.

## FAQ

### How do I find my first customers on LinkedIn with no network?

Search for signals, not connections. Run your pain phrases in the Posts tab with the Past week filter and reply in the threads before you send any connection request. A useful reply under a stranger's post is read by the poster and by everyone else in the thread, which is the network you do not have yet.

### Does cold messaging on LinkedIn still work?

Cold messages to people selected by title get few replies because nothing about the person says they are buying. The same message sent within a day of a public signal, quoting what the person wrote, is not cold: it answers something they said. Keep the note short, ask one question, and leave the link for the second message.

### Do I need Sales Navigator to find customers on LinkedIn?

Not for the method on this page. The Posts tab, the date filter, company-page follower lists and the Jobs tab are all on a free account. Sales Navigator helps once you move from signals to building a large list of titles and need its company-size and seniority filters; start without it and add it when the free filters run out.

### How many connection requests a day is safe?

LinkedIn restricts accounts that push past its invitation limits, and a restricted account cannot run any of this. Intentsly caps each account at 30 messages and 30 connection requests a day and recommends 15. At 15 a day the limit is never the problem; finding 15 people who showed intent is.

### How long does it take to get a customer from LinkedIn?

Replies and calls can arrive in week one if the signals exist in your category and you answer within a day; customers then follow your normal sales cycle. Our own run produced 14 qualified demos in 14 days. A narrow category where few people post about the problem runs slower, which the day-5 score will show.
