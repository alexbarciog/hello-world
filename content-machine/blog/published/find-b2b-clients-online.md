The fastest way to find B2B clients online is to look for people who are showing, this week, that they have the problem you solve, and to reply to them before anyone else does. On LinkedIn that is a post asking for a recommendation, a comment under a competitor's launch, or a new follow of a competitor's page. Lists, ads and content all have a place, and this page says where. But start with the people already buying.

## Why do most lists of ways to find B2B clients fail small teams?

The pages ranking for how to find B2B clients online give eight to ten channels: SEO, LinkedIn content, cold email, newsletters, communities, partnerships, ads, freelance marketplaces. Each works for somebody. The problem is that they are presented as a menu, and a team of one to ten people cannot run a menu. It can run one loop well.

The loop that produces conversations fastest reaches buyers who are already in-market. In its [2025 B2B Buyer Experience Report](https://6sense.com/report/buyer-experience/), based on nearly 4,000 buyer responses, 6sense found that buyers make first contact with a seller about 61% of the way through their journey, that 94% had ranked their shortlist before engaging any seller, that buyers initiated 79% of those engagements, and that they evaluate 5.1 vendors on average. By the time a buyer fills in your form, the shortlist exists and you may not be on it.

So sort every channel by one question: does it reach people deciding now, or people who might decide someday? A buying signal is a public action that shows someone is in-market now (the [buying signal lesson](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal) has the definition and a test). Channels that surface signals come first; channels that build awareness pay off in months, not weeks.

| Channel | Reaches | Time to first conversation | When it is the right choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn signals (posts, comments, follows) | People deciding now | Days | Your buyers post or comment about their work |
| Referrals and your own network | People who trust you | Days | Fewer than ten clients so far |
| Communities (Slack, Reddit, forums) | People asking in public | Days to weeks | Self-serve or low-ticket offers |
| Cold email to a list | People who fit a profile | Weeks | A proven message, and you need volume |
| SEO and content | People searching later | Months | After the loop above works |
| Paid ads | People matching targeting | Days, at a price | A converting message plus budget |

## Where do B2B clients show up online before they buy?

B2B buyers research in public before they talk to anyone, and LinkedIn is where that research is visible. Each row below is a behaviour you can see with a free LinkedIn account. The last column is the message, written to quote the signal rather than pitch the service.

| Signal | Where | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A post asking "can anyone recommend a [your category]?" | Posts tab search for your category plus "recommend", "looking for", "anyone know" | An open search with a budget, often with a deadline | Answer in the thread within a day, then connect with a note that repeats their exact ask |
| A comment or reaction on a competitor's post | The competitor's company page and its founders' posts | The person follows the category closely enough to react; a question in the comment is an evaluation | Reply to their comment with something useful; connect quoting it; no link in the first message |
| A post describing the problem you remove | Posts tab search for the problem phrases your clients use, filtered to the past week | A problem acknowledged in public, provider not yet chosen | Comment with a method they can use without you; connect; mention your service only if asked |
| A new follow or engagement on a competitor's page or category hashtag | The competitor page's follower list; posts under the category hashtag | Early research, weaker than a comment but fresh | Connect with no pitch; let your posts do the next step |
| A new hire in the role that buys from you | Posts tab search for "excited to join" or "new role" plus the title | New owners audit vendors in their first quarter | Congratulate, ask what they inherited, offer a short comparison note instead of a call |
| A job post for the role your service replaces or assists | Jobs search for the role title at companies in your segment | Budget exists for the outcome; a service is cheaper than a hire | Message the hiring manager with how clients use you alongside or instead of that hire |
| A funding, expansion or new-office announcement | Company page posts; founders' posts with "raised", "opening", "expanding" | New budget and new problems in the same month | Congratulate and name the one problem that follows that announcement for firms like theirs |

Reddit and X carry the same "who do you use for X?" questions and are worth a manual search a few times a week; the [Reddit method](/blog/find-b2b-leads-on-reddit) has the queries. Treat them as manual channels; Intentsly monitors LinkedIn only. One filter applies everywhere: a signal older than about two weeks is cold.

## How to find B2B clients online by hand: a week-one plan

This plan needs a free LinkedIn account and about an hour a day. It is built for one person, so the numbers are small on purpose. The worked example is Ledgerline, a hypothetical outsourced bookkeeping firm for e-commerce brands.

1. **Day 1: write the signal list.** Three competitors, five problem phrases in your clients' words (from past calls, not your website), and the two or three titles that buy. Ledgerline's phrases: "Shopify payouts don't match the bank", "closing the books takes two weeks", "my accountant doesn't understand Amazon fees". Buying titles: founder, COO, head of finance at brands with 5 to 50 staff.
2. **Day 1: save the searches.** In LinkedIn's Posts tab, search your category in quotes plus "recommend", then plus "looking for". Save each problem phrase as its own search with the date filter set to the past week. Follow each competitor's page.
3. **Day 2: build the watch list.** Open each competitor's three most recent posts and list every commenter whose title matches your buying titles, plus anyone who asked a question. Record name, company, post, quote and date in a sheet; the [LinkedIn lead generation guide](/guides/linkedin-lead-generation-guide) has the sheet layout and the Boolean strings.
4. **Days 2 to 5: act within a day.** Reply in the thread first, then send a connection note that quotes what they wrote and asks one question. Keep to 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day; a restricted account ends the plan.
5. **Days 3 to 5: answer the open asks.** For every "can anyone recommend" post, write the answer you would give a friend, including when your service is the wrong fit. Message the poster afterwards with the one reason you might fit the job they described.
6. **Day 5: score the week.** Count signals found, messages sent, replies and calls booked. Fewer than one reply in ten means the message is pitching; open with their quote instead. Fewer than ten signals means the problem phrases are too narrow or you need another competitor.
7. **Day 6: ask every conversation for two names.** Everyone who takes a call, client or not, knows two people with the same problem.

Two templates to copy. Replace the bracketed parts and keep the length.

Connection note after a comment (under 300 characters):

```
Saw your comment under [competitor]'s post about [topic]. "[their exact words]" is the same thing we hear from [type of company] every month. Curious what you ended up doing about it?
```

Message after a "can anyone recommend" post:

```
You asked for a [category] recommendation for [the job they named]. Two honest options: [competitor] is strong if [condition]. If [their stated situation] is the main issue, that is the case we handle most often at [your firm]. Happy to send a two-paragraph note on how, no call needed.
```

Expect week one to produce conversations rather than contracts. The point is a loop you can repeat: watch, quote, reply, book.

## What other ways to find B2B clients beat signals, and when?

Signals are the fastest route to conversations. Three other routes earn their place, each under a condition.

**Your network and referrals** win when you have fewer than ten clients. Steli Efti's [customer acquisition blueprint](https://close.com/blueprint/customer-acquisition-strategy-for-startups) for Close puts the first ten down to any means necessary: friends, former colleagues, introductions, and a targeted list (his example was companies that had just raised funding, which produced seven customers in two weeks of calls). People who already trust you convert faster than any stranger. The ceiling is low, so start the signal loop in week two.

**Communities** win when the offer is self-serve or cheap enough to try without a call. In an [Indie Hackers thread](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-where-did-you-find-your-first-1-10-and-100-customer-f146e8e3d3) on first customers, Reddit was the most-mentioned source, followed by Product Hunt, X, LinkedIn and personal networks. For a service with a proposal and a call in the loop, community posts produce curiosity more than pipeline; answer questions there, and count conversations, not upvotes.

**Paid ads and cold email at volume** win when you already know which message converts and need more of it this quarter. Until then, they pay to learn what a week of signal conversations teaches for free: the problem phrases in your buyers' own words. Bought lists are the one method signals replace outright; a list says who could buy, not who is buying this month.

## FAQ

### Where can I find B2B clients online for free?
LinkedIn's Posts tab with a free account, searched daily for your category plus "recommend" and for the problem phrases your clients use. Add your competitors' recent posts and their commenters. Reddit and niche Slack groups carry the same questions. The cost is an hour a day and the discipline to reply within a day; no tool, ad budget or email domain is needed for week one.

### Cold email or LinkedIn first for B2B clients?
LinkedIn first when the signal is on LinkedIn, because replying where the person asked is the most natural message you can send. Cold email follows for people who accepted a connection but went quiet, and for companies that posted a job for the role you assist. Sending the same message on both channels the same day reads as a sequence, and sequences get ignored.

### How long does it take to find B2B clients online?
Signals shorten the time to a conversation, not the time a buyer needs to decide. Week one by hand should produce replies and a few calls. In our own automated run we booked 14 qualified demos in 14 days. A service with a proposal stage will take longer from call to contract, so count signals, replies and calls weekly and judge the loop on those.

### Should I buy a B2B lead list?
Not to start. A bought list tells you who fits a profile, not who is deciding this month, so most of the messages land on people with no active problem. Signals tell you the opposite: fewer names, each with a quote you can reply to. Use a list later, for cold email at volume, once a converting message exists.

### How many LinkedIn messages a day is safe?
Keep to about 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day from one account, and send them to people who posted or commented, so acceptance stays high. Intentsly caps each connected account at 30 of each per day and recommends 15. A restricted account costs more than a slower week, so treat the lower number as the limit.
