How to get clients for a SaaS company depends on how many you have. Under ten, sell to people who know you and to buyers showing the problem in public this week. From ten to fifty, make those signals the daily routine and ask every client for a name. Past fifty, add content, partners and paid. At every stage the fastest conversations come from trigger events at the buyer's company, visible on LinkedIn, acted on within a day. The rule, the signals, a week-one plan and a scoring table follow.

## Which channel should a SaaS company use at each stage?

Most advice on getting SaaS clients is a list of eight channels with no order. The order matters more than the list, because each channel pays back on a different clock. Pick by client count and contract size, then run one fast channel and one slow one at a time.

| Clients today | Run now | Add later | Skip for now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 10 | Your own network, plus LinkedIn trigger events acted on by hand | Referral ask after each onboarding | SEO, paid social, events, review-site placements |
| 10 to 50 | Daily signal routine, referral ask built into onboarding and renewals | Comparison pages for the competitors your clients left | Paid search until one message is proven on signal conversations |
| 50 or more | Signals as the outbound engine, referrals, partners who already serve your clients | SEO, paid search on category and competitor terms | Nothing by rule; cut what the numbers say to cut |

Contract size shifts the table one row. A tool sold at a few hundred a year can lean on self-serve trials and communities earlier; a contract that needs a security review and three people to sign needs a human conversation at every stage, and that is the case this page is written for. The rest of the page is the "run now" column: how to find the conversations.

## Where do SaaS clients show up on LinkedIn?

Buyers decide before they talk to you. In its [2025 Buyer Behavior Report](https://company.g2.com/news/buyer-behavior-in-2025), a survey of 1,169 B2B decision-makers run in April 2025, G2 found that nearly two out of three buyers prefer to engage vendor salespeople only in later stages, up 17 points on the previous year, and that about one in ten skip the shortlist and go straight to a single vendor. If you wait for the demo request, the list is already drawn. The way in is a trigger event: a public change at the buyer's company that creates the problem your product solves, before the shortlist exists (the [trigger event lesson](/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales) has the definition and more examples).

The sibling page on [finding customers for B2B SaaS](/solutions/find-customers-for-b2b-saas) covers competitor post engagers and "anyone using X?" posts. This table covers the other half: events at the prospect's company. Each one is visible with a free LinkedIn account.

| Signal | Where | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A new hire into the title that owns your category (Head of Sales, RevOps lead, VP Marketing) | Posts tab search: "excited to join" OR "new role" plus the title, past month | New owners audit the stack in their first quarter and have the mandate to change it | Congratulate, ask what they inherited, offer a one-page comparison instead of a demo |
| A job post for the role your product assists or replaces | Jobs search: the role title, filtered to your segment and company size | Budget exists for the outcome; a tool costs less than a hire and ships faster | Message the hiring manager with how clients use your product alongside that role |
| A complaint about the current vendor: "renewal", "price increase", "support ticket", "migrating off" | Posts tab search: the competitor name plus one complaint word, past week | An open evaluation with a reason to switch and often a date | Reply in the thread with something useful, then connect and ask what the renewal date is |
| A post saying "we just moved to [adjacent tool]" or "setting up [platform]" | Posts tab search: the adjacent tool name plus "moved to", "switched", "rolling out" | The stack is changing; integrations and gaps are being noticed this month | Offer the exact integration or the gap your product fills, in one sentence |
| A company milestone post: launch, new market, funding, new office | Company page posts and the founder's posts | New targets create new problems; teams are about to grow | Connect with a specific observation about what the milestone changes for the team you sell to |
| A question about the job to be done: "how are you handling X?" | Posts tab search: the problem phrase in your clients' words, past week | Problem acknowledged in public, no vendor chosen | Answer the question first; mention the product only when asked or in the second message |

Hiring signals have their own routine in the [hiring signals guide](/guides/track-hiring-signals-for-sales). Reddit and X carry the same questions and complaints and are worth a manual search twice a week; treat them as manual channels, because Intentsly monitors LinkedIn only. Two filters keep the list honest: a signal older than about two weeks is cold, and a cheer ("congrats on the launch") is a fan, not a buyer.

## How to get clients for a SaaS company by hand: a week-one plan

One person, a free LinkedIn account, about an hour a day. The example company is hypothetical.

1. **Day 1: write the trigger list.** Three competitors, the two or three titles that sign, five problem phrases in your clients' words, and the adjacent tools your product plugs into. Example (hypothetical): Harbor Ledger sells spend-approval software to finance teams of 50 to 500 people; its titles are Controller and VP Finance, its adjacent tools are NetSuite and Ramp, its phrases include "chasing approvals in Slack" and "month-end close took two weeks".
2. **Day 1: save six searches.** In the Posts tab, one search per row of the table above: the title plus "excited to join"; the competitor plus "renewal"; the adjacent tool plus "moved to"; each problem phrase alone. Set the date filter to the past week. Follow each competitor's page so its comment threads land in your feed.
3. **Day 2: build the sheet.** Columns: name, title, company, signal, quote, date, fit score, next step. Fill it from the searches and from the three most recent competitor posts. The [prospect list guide](/guides/build-b2b-prospect-list) has a template if you want one ready-made.
4. **Day 2: score before you message.** Use the client-fit table below. Message only rows that score at least four; park the rest.
5. **Days 2 to 5: act inside a day.** Reply in the thread first, then send a connection note that quotes what they wrote and asks one question. No link, no deck, no calendar in the first message. Stay at 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day; a restricted account ends the plan.
6. **Days 3 to 5: run the first calls as interviews.** Ask what triggered the search, what they tried, who else decides, and when it has to be solved. Offer a trial or a pilot only after you can repeat their problem back in their words.
7. **Day 5: count.** Signals found, messages sent, replies, calls booked. Under one reply in ten means the message is pitching; rewrite it to open with their quote. Under ten signals a week means the problem phrases are yours, not theirs; replace them with words from the calls.
8. **Day 6: ask for names.** Every call, won or lost, ends with "who else is dealing with this?" Two names a call is a normal answer.

Expect week one to produce conversations, not contracts. A SaaS contract with a security review takes the time it takes; the point is that the clock starts with the right people this week instead of next quarter.

## How do you tell a client from a curious commenter?

Score each signal before you spend a message on it. The table is a copy-paste asset; adjust the point values once you have ten calls of data. The [qualification guide](/guides/qualify-leads-before-outreach) has a longer version with the checks for each row.

| Check | Points | How to verify in under a minute |
|---|---|---|
| Title can sign or recommend the purchase | 2 | Profile headline; "Head", "VP", "Director", "Lead" or founder |
| Company size and segment match your current clients | 1 | Company page: employee range and industry |
| The signal is a trigger event, not a reaction | 2 | They described a change or asked a question; a reaction alone scores 0 |
| The signal is under two weeks old | 1 | Post date |
| A deadline is visible (renewal, hire start date, launch) | 1 | The post or the job ad states a date |
| Your product covers the job they named without a workaround | 1 | Compare their words with your feature list, not your roadmap |

A score of four or more earns a message today. Two or three earns a connection with no pitch and a place on the watch list. Under two is a fan or a peer; let your posts do the work.

## Other ways to get SaaS clients, and when they beat signals

Signals are the fastest route to conversations with strangers. Three other routes earn a place, each at a specific stage.

**Your own network** beats signals when you have no clients yet. In an [Indie Hackers thread](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/share-your-story-how-did-you-find-your-very-first-paying-customer-for-b2b-saas-product-a55e277e4e) asking founders how they found their very first paying B2B SaaS customer, cold outreach and community engagement were each named by three of the nine founders who answered, and one built the product free for a company they already knew. People who trust you convert faster than any stranger. The ceiling is low, so start the signal routine in week two, not month six.

**Referrals and partners** beat signals from about ten clients onward, because each client knows peers with the same trigger events. Build the ask into onboarding and renewal, and look for consultants and agencies who already serve your clients; a partner introduction skips the trust step entirely. The dependence is the risk: a partner channel that supplies most of your pipeline sets your growth rate.

**Content, SEO and paid** beat signals when you know which message converts and need volume. Until then they pay to learn what a week of signal conversations teaches for free: the buyers' own words for the problem. The [SaaStorm guide to first customers](https://saastorm.io/blog/how-to-get-first-customers-saas) is right that content is a slow channel with delayed results; run it alongside signals, not instead of them. Cold lists are the one method signals replace outright. A list says who could buy; a trigger event says who is buying this quarter.

## FAQ

### How do I get my first SaaS clients with no network and no budget?
Pick two trigger events from the table, save the LinkedIn Posts searches for them, and check them every morning. Reply in the thread before you message, quote their words in the connection note, and ask one question. A free account and an hour a day cover week one. Speed matters more than budget: a reply inside a day beats a polished campaign a week later.

### Should I charge the first clients or give the product away?
Charge from the first conversation, even a founder rate for a fixed period. A buyer who posted about a renewal or a new hire already has a budget line, and a free offer makes them doubt the product. Reserve free access for design partners who commit time to feedback, and write down what they owe you in return: calls, data, a quote when it works.

### Cold email or LinkedIn for a SaaS company?
LinkedIn first when the signal is on LinkedIn, because replying where the person wrote is the most natural message you will send. Email follows for people who accept the connection and go quiet, and for hiring managers you found through a job post. The same message on both channels on the same day reads as a sequence, and sequences get ignored.

### How long does it take to get the first ten SaaS clients?
Signals shorten the time to conversations, not the time a buyer needs to run a security review and get a signature. In our own run the method booked 14 qualified demos in 14 days once automated; by hand, week one should produce replies and a few calls. Count signals, replies and calls weekly; once calls book every week, the first ten clients are weeks away, not quarters.

### Does outbound still work for SaaS in 2026?
Untargeted outbound works less every year, because buyers finish most of their research before they want to hear from a seller. Outbound aimed at a trigger event works, because the message arrives while the problem is new and the shortlist is open. What decides the reply is whether the person gave you a reason to write this week, on whichever channel.

### How many messages a day is safe on LinkedIn?
Intentsly caps each connected account at 30 messages and 30 connection requests a day and recommends 15, and the same ceiling is sensible by hand. A restricted account costs more than a slow week. If fifteen a day feels small, the fix is sharper targeting, not more volume: a trigger event and a quote in the first line do more than a second batch.
