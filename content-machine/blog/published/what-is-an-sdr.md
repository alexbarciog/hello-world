> [!SUMMARY]
> - An SDR (sales development representative) opens conversations with potential buyers, qualifies them and books a meeting for a closer. The SDR does not close.
> - Benchmarks from 351 B2B companies: about 112 outreach activities a day, a quota of 10 meetings a month, fewer than two thirds of reps at quota, 1.9 years in the job.
> - A company under 200 people should hire an SDR only after the founder has proven the message by hand. Until then, buying signals plus software cover the job.

**An SDR, or sales development representative, is the salesperson whose job is to open conversations with potential buyers, qualify them and book a meeting for someone else to close.** So the short answer to "what is an SDR" is the person at the top of the sales funnel who turns a name into a booked meeting. Everything the SDR does, from researching a company to sending the fourth follow-up, serves that one handoff. The SDR's output is qualified meetings, not revenue.

## Why does an SDR matter?

Because opening conversations is a full-time job, and the people who close deals are bad at doing it on the side. A closer with eight live deals will always pick the proposal due Friday over forty cold messages, so without someone whose only job is the top of the funnel, the pipeline empties the month after a good quarter.

Most companies that run the model split it one SDR to roughly two closers. The Bridge Group's 2025 survey of 351 B2B companies, most of them SaaS, found an average of one SDR for every 2.4 account executives, a ratio that has held since 2018, and 82% of companies now assign each SDR to specific account executives' territories ([The Bridge Group, February 2025](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)).

> [!EXAMPLE]
> A labelled example. A twelve-person software company has one account executive who runs eight evaluations at a time and books nothing new while doing it. An SDR, or software doing the SDR's job, keeps new first meetings landing in that calendar every week, so the pipeline refills while the AE closes.

## What does an SDR do all day?

Five jobs, in a loop. A good SDR spends most of the day on the third and fourth.

1. **Build the list.** Pick the companies and people who match the [ideal customer profile](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile): right size, right industry, right role. Some SDRs work inbound leads that marketing hands over; most build or refine their own outbound lists.
2. **Research each person.** Find a reason to write that is true for this person this week: a hire, a funding round, a post, a complaint about a competitor. The lesson on [what a buying signal is](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal) covers the reasons worth looking for.
3. **Reach out.** Calls, emails, LinkedIn messages and texts, in sequences that follow up several times. The Bridge Group's median is 112 activities a day: 44 phone, 41 email, 19 LinkedIn and 8 text or other ([The Bridge Group, February 2025](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)).
4. **Qualify.** When someone answers, the SDR checks fit, need, timing and whether this person can get a decision made, then decides whether the conversation deserves a closer's hour. The same survey puts the median at 4.1 quality conversations a day. A conversation that passes becomes a [sales qualified lead](/learn/what-is-a-sales-qualified-lead).
5. **Book and brief.** Put the meeting in the account executive's calendar with notes on who the person is, what they said and what they want. The global median monthly quota is 10 of these first-stage meetings per SDR ([The Bridge Group, February 2025](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)).

```stats
112 | median outreach activities per SDR per day (44 phone, 41 email, 19 LinkedIn) | https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group
10 | median monthly quota of first meetings per SDR | https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group
60% | share of SDRs hitting quota, the lowest in the survey's history | https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group
1.9 years | average SDR tenure | https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group
```

The ratio is the part to remember: roughly 112 touches a day produce about 4 real conversations and, over a month, around 10 meetings. That is what outreach yields when the list is built from job titles rather than from anything the person did.

## What does an SDR cost, and how long do they stay?

More than the salary line suggests, because of ramp and turnover. The same survey puts median SDR on-target earnings at $80K, split $55K base and $25K variable, with an average ramp of 3.0 months before a new rep is productive ([The Bridge Group, February 2025](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)). Coursera, citing Glassdoor in May 2026, gives an average base of $62,000 before commission ([Coursera, May 2026](https://www.coursera.org/articles/sales-development-representative)).

Then they leave. Average tenure is 1.9 years and median annual turnover is 40%, with 16% of reps promoted out, mostly into closing roles ([The Bridge Group, February 2025](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)). Take off the ramp and a company gets about twenty productive months per hire, then starts again.

> [!WARNING]
> Only 60% of SDRs hit their quota in the 2025 survey, the lowest share the study has recorded ([The Bridge Group, February 2025](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)). Budget on the median rep, not on the one in the job advert.

## SDR vs BDR vs account executive vs AI SDR

The titles around the SDR are used loosely, so judge a role by what it owns and what it is measured on.

| Role | What it owns | Measured on | Hands off to |
|---|---|---|---|
| SDR (sales development representative) | Opening and qualifying conversations, usually outbound, sometimes inbound | Qualified meetings booked | Account executive |
| BDR (business development representative) | The same job under another name; at some companies the inbound half, at others the outbound half | Qualified meetings or opportunities | Account executive |
| Account executive (AE) | The deal from first meeting to signature | Revenue closed | Customer success |
| AI SDR | The SDR's loop run by software: picks who to contact, writes, sends, replies, books | Meetings booked within limits you set | A human who runs the meeting |

The SDR and BDR split is not standardised, and the pages that rank for the two terms disagree. LinkedIn's guide and ZoomInfo put inbound follow-up on the SDR and outbound prospecting on the BDR ([LinkedIn Sales Solutions](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/resources/sales-terms/sales-development-representative), [ZoomInfo, July 2026](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/sdr-vs-bdr-difference)). Apollo's page gives the opposite split, outbound SDR and inbound BDR ([Apollo, February 2026](https://www.apollo.io/insights/what-is-an-sdr-in-sales)), and Pipedrive defines the SDR as outbound only ([Pipedrive](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/blog/sales-development-rep)). Read the job description, never the title.

The AI SDR row is new. The lesson on [what an AI SDR is](/learn/what-is-an-ai-sdr) covers it in full; the one-line version is that it runs the same five-step loop and is judged on the same output, booked meetings, with the daily volume set by the channel rather than by a quota.

## When should a small company hire an SDR?

Later than most founders think, and only after the message has been proven by hand. A rule for a company of 1 to 200 people:

1. **Nobody has sent 50 messages by hand yet: do not hire.** An SDR cannot make an unproven offer convert; they can only send it faster. The founder sends the first messages, learns which reasons get replies, and writes down what a qualified meeting looks like.
2. **The message works and the closer is losing meetings to lack of time: fill the job, not necessarily the seat.** The loop is list, research, outreach, qualify, book. Each step can be done by a person or by software, and the expensive part is a person doing step 3 at 112 touches a day to a cold list.
3. **Most of your buyers show up on LinkedIn: start from signals rather than from a list.** A list built from what people did this week (asked for a recommendation, complained about a tool, engaged with a competitor) makes the first line true. If the manual version is more than you can do, Intentsly's [AI SDR](/features/ai-sdr) watches LinkedIn buying signals, scores each person against your ideal customer profile, messages the ones who fit and books the meeting into your Cal.com or Google Calendar, inside limits of 30 messages and 30 connection requests a day per account, with 15 recommended.

It produced 14 qualified demos in 14 days with zero manual outreach and a reply rate 4x our previous cold campaigns. > [!NOTE]
> Hire the human SDR when you need the phone. Software does not make 44 calls a day, and some markets still buy on the phone. If your buyers answer messages, the comparison is between a person and a signal-fed tool on the same channel.

## How to use this lesson this week

Three actions, in order.

1. **Write the handoff definition.** One paragraph: what a qualified meeting means for you (company size, role, a stated problem, a date). Every SDR, human or software, is measured against this sentence, so write it before anyone starts.
2. **Do one SDR day yourself.** Pick 20 people who fit the profile and gave a reason this week, send 20 messages that quote the reason, and count the replies. The free [Buying Signal Playbook](/signal-playbook) lists twelve reasons worth chasing and eight to ignore, with the LinkedIn trigger and script for each.
3. **Decide seat or software.** If the 20 messages got replies and you cannot keep sending them, run the hiring rule above. If they got nothing, the problem is the offer, and no SDR fixes that.

## FAQ

### What does SDR stand for in sales?

SDR stands for sales development representative. The role opens conversations with potential buyers, qualifies them against a fit and need check, and books a first meeting for an account executive or founder to run. SDRs are measured on qualified meetings booked rather than on revenue, which stays with the person who closes.

### Is an SDR an entry-level job?

Usually, yes. Most SDRs are in their first or second sales job, and the common path is promotion to account executive after one to two years. The 2025 Bridge Group survey puts average tenure at 1.9 years, with promotion the largest single reason reps leave the role, which is why SDR teams are always hiring.

### What is the difference between an SDR and a BDR?

At many companies, none: the titles are interchangeable. Where they differ, one common split gives inbound lead follow-up to the SDR and outbound prospecting to the BDR, but several vendors define it the other way round. Both roles are measured on qualified meetings and hand off to an account executive, so read the job description rather than the title.

### How many meetings should an SDR book a month?

The global median quota in the 2025 Bridge Group survey of 351 B2B companies is 10 first-stage meetings a month, with introductory-meeting models set higher and fully qualified models lower. Fewer than two thirds of reps hit their number, so a plan built on the median rep is safer than one built on the quota.

### Do small companies need an SDR?

Not until the founder has proven the message by hand and is losing meetings for lack of time. Before that, an SDR only sends an unproven offer faster. After that, the job can be filled by a hire or by software fed with buying signals, and the choice depends on whether your buyers answer the phone or answer messages.
