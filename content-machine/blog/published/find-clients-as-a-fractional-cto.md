The fastest way to find clients as a fractional CTO is to reach a founder in the week they admit they need one, and that week is visible on LinkedIn: a non-technical founder asking whether their developers are any good, a first "Head of Engineering" job post at a company with no CTO, a seed round announced by a team with no engineer. Message that founder the same day with one specific observation and you are the first CTO in the conversation. Below: six signals and a week-one plan.

## Why does the usual fractional CTO advice stall?

Most pages on how to find clients as a fractional CTO give the same list: define your value proposition, polish the LinkedIn profile, tell your network, offer investors a free technical assessment, publish a few posts, join a marketplace. None of it is wrong. The problem is that every step waits for the founder to come to you, and founders who need a CTO do not know that a part-time one exists until someone tells them. They know they have a problem, and they say so in public.

The market also rewards timing more than most fractional roles. Go Fractional's live demand table, read today, shows [engineering at 121 jobs posted, a 14% share of demand against a 13% share of supply, which it labels balanced](https://www.gofractional.com/insights/talent-map), while finance shows a 15% demand share against 3% supply, a gap it rates at 4.4x. A fractional CFO can wait for inbound; a fractional CTO is competing with other CTOs for every posted role, so the engagement goes to whoever reached the founder before the role was posted.

That is also why the referral statistic every guide quotes is true and insufficient. FractionUS's October 2025 round-up of US research reports that [92.8% of fractional executives get clients through network referrals](https://fractionus.com/blog/fractional-work-statistics-2025-income-market-data), 30.4% use social media and 19.2% rely on cold outreach. Referrals come first for the first client. They also run out, because a network is asked once. The signals below are what you run when it has been. A buying signal is a public action that shows a company is in-market now; the [trigger event lesson](/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales) explains the idea in general, and for fractional CTOs the triggers are easy to see, because the people who hire you are founders, and founders post about hiring, raising and shipping.

## Where do fractional CTO clients show up on LinkedIn?

Each row is a moment you can find with a free LinkedIn account. The search column is what to type into the search bar before switching to the Posts tab (or the Jobs tab, where stated) and filtering to the past week. The last column is the message, written to quote the signal and offer one piece of CTO work, not a retainer. The rows map to the buyer-side signs that CTO coach Stephan Schmidt lists from his coaching conversations ([seven signs you need a fractional CTO](https://www.amazingcto.com/signs-you-need-fractional-cto/), January 2026): a non-technical founder burned by outsourced development, an overwhelmed promoted tech lead, investors asking hard questions, scaling with technology as the bottleneck, confusion about AI.

| Signal | Where | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A non-technical founder asking in public how to judge developers or find a technical partner ("how do I know if my dev shop is any good?", "looking for a technical cofounder", "how much should an MVP cost?") | Posts tab: `"technical cofounder" looking` or `"non-technical founder" developers` or `"dev shop" "how do I know"`, past week | A founder making architecture, hiring and budget decisions alone, with a short list forming in the comments the same day | Answer in the thread with one usable point (the one question to ask the agency, the one thing to check in the repo), then connect with a note that quotes the post; offer a 30-minute look at what they have, not a proposal |
| A first senior engineering job post at a company with no CTO ("Head of Engineering", "VP Engineering", "CTO", "founding engineer" at a company of 5 to 50 people) | Jobs tab: "Head of Engineering" or "CTO", filtered to companies under about 50 people; check the company page and team for an existing technical leader | The founder has admitted the work needs a leader; the hire takes months, and the roadmap, the hiring plan and the architecture decisions cannot wait that long | Message the founder: what a part-time CTO decides while the role is open, and what the hire should inherit on day one (a roadmap, a hiring scorecard, a stable release process) |
| A pre-seed or seed round announced by a team with no technical leader ("we raised our seed", "pre-seed closed", the team section shows a CEO and a sales lead and nobody else) | Posts tab: `"we raised" seed` or `"pre-seed" "excited to announce"` with your industry words, past week; open the company page and count technical titles | Money to build with, investors who will ask technical questions at the next board meeting, and no one to answer them | Congratulate, ask what the money builds first, and offer to write the twelve-week technical plan the investors will expect; the [funding-signals guide](/guides/find-companies-that-just-raised-funding) has the full routine |
| A burned-by-the-agency or rebuild post ("the agency delivered and we can't maintain it", "our app went down at launch", "starting the rebuild", "six months and nothing shipped") | Posts tab: `"dev shop" rebuild` or `"agency" "can't maintain"` or `"nothing shipped" founder`, past week | Budget spent, trust gone, and the next decision (rebuild, repair or replace the team) will be made by someone with no way to judge the options | Do not pile on the agency. Connect, acknowledge the post, and offer a code and architecture review with a written verdict on repair versus rebuild, fixed length, fixed price |
| Investor or board pressure ("preparing for Series A", "tech due diligence next month", "our board asked for an AI strategy", "investors want a roadmap") | Posts tab: `"due diligence" "technical" startup` or `"board" "AI strategy" founder` or `"Series A" "preparing"`, past week; also VCs posting about new portfolio companies | A deadline owned by a founder who cannot produce the document alone; the question is who writes it with them | Offer the due-diligence pack or the AI roadmap as a scoped project with a date, and ask which investor is asking; investors are also the partner route in the section below |
| A CTO leaving, or a developer promoted into the title ("last day as CTO", "stepping down as CTO", "thrilled to step up as CTO" from someone three years into their first developer job) | Posts tab: `"last day" CTO` or `"stepping down" CTO` at companies of 10 to 100 people; `"promoted to CTO"` or `"step up as CTO"`, past two weeks | A gap at the top of engineering, or a new leader doing executive work for the first time who will take a mentor long before they take a replacement | For the departure, message the founder about covering releases, hiring and the board deck for a defined number of weeks; for the promotion, congratulate the new CTO and offer a standing hour a week as a sounding board, which is how many fractional engagements begin |

Two filters keep the list honest. A signal older than two weeks is cold: the founder has either found someone, signed with an agency, or decided to muddle through. And a post from another fractional CTO or a dev agency asking "founders, who needs technical leadership?" is not a buyer, however many comments it has. Reddit and X carry the same founder questions, and the [Reddit method](/blog/find-b2b-leads-on-reddit) has the queries; treat them as manual channels, because Intentsly monitors LinkedIn only.

One more separation matters for this role: a company that could use a CTO is every company with a non-technical founder, which is why cold lists of "startups without a CTO" convert badly. The rows above all require money moving (a hire, a round, an agency fee already spent), a deadline (a board meeting, a diligence date), or a direct question.

## How to find clients as a fractional CTO by hand: a week-one plan

The plan takes about an hour a day with a free LinkedIn account. Sales Navigator makes the headcount and job-post steps faster but is not required.

1. **Day 1: write the one-sentence offer and list the voices.** Pick the company type and the stage, for example "pre-seed to seed B2B software companies in the Netherlands with a non-technical founder and an outsourced team". Then list ten investors, accelerators and founder-community voices your buyers follow, and five fractional CTO practices or dev agencies that compete with you. The [ideal customer profile lesson](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) is the longer version of this step.
2. **Day 2: build the saved searches.** Create the five Posts-tab searches from the table with your industry words added, each filtered to the past week, plus one Jobs search for the Head of Engineering or CTO role at small companies. Bookmark them. The [hiring-signals routine](/guides/track-hiring-signals-for-sales) goes deeper on the job-post signal, which for CTOs is the clearest buying moment of the six.
3. **Days 3 to 5: thirty minutes of reading, thirty of replying.** Run the searches, read the new posts, and for each real buyer do the "what to do" step from the table. Reply in public first, connect second. Send at most 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day; LinkedIn tolerates more, your acceptance rate does not.
4. **Day 6: follow up and find the budget owner.** For every accepted connection with no reply, send one message that adds something: a question about their release process, a line about the investor they mentioned. Never "just checking in". Where the person who posted is a product manager or a lead developer rather than the founder, the [decision-maker guide](/guides/find-decision-makers-on-linkedin) has the six steps to find who signs.
5. **Day 7: count.** Signals found, replies, calls booked. Fewer than ten relevant posts in a week means the industry words are wrong or your founders are quiet on LinkedIn; widen the search terms and the stage before changing the niche.

Message template for the "how do I know if my developers are any good" post, for copying:

```
Saw your question about the dev shop. The two things I would
check this week are [whether anyone outside the agency can run
the build from a clean machine] and [how long a one-line fix
takes to reach production]. Both tell you more than the demo
does. Happy to look at the repo with you for 30 minutes and
give you a written read, no proposal attached.
```

Finding the right fifteen founders each day is where the hour goes; if you want that step done for you, the [LinkedIn signals feature](/features/linkedin-signals) runs the same searches continuously and hands you the list already scored against your customer profile.

## Other ways to find fractional CTO clients, and when they beat signals

Signals produce conversations with founders you have never met. Three other routes earn their place, and for most fractional CTOs one of them comes first.

**Investors and accelerators** beat signals when you serve one stage. A seed fund sees every portfolio company's technical questions at every board meeting and has no engineer on staff to answer them; an accelerator admits a new batch of non-technical founders twice a year. Offering one fund a technical review of one portfolio company, done well, is the route that fractional CTO guides recommend most consistently, and it compounds: one fund has many companies. The cost is that the fund introduces you to the companies it worries about, which are not always the companies you want.

**Dev agencies and fractional CFOs as referral partners** beat signals when you serve one region. An agency needs someone on the client side who can write a specification and judge the delivery, and a good one would rather that person exist than be blamed for its absence. A fractional CFO walks into companies that are raising and gets asked the technical questions too; the [fractional CFO page](/solutions/find-clients-as-a-fractional-cfo) describes how they find their own clients, which helps when you pitch them, and the [software development company page](/solutions/get-clients-for-a-software-development-company) does the same for agencies.

**Former colleagues and the founders you already advised** beat signals for the first client, almost always; the round-up statistic above is right about that. The ceiling arrives when that network has been asked once, and the plan above is what you run when it has.

Marketplaces and cold lists are the two tactics signals replace outright. A fractional marketplace puts you in a list of CTOs for a founder who already decided to hire one, which is a price comparison; the founders in the table have not yet learned a part-time CTO is an option. ## FAQ

### How do I get my first fractional CTO client?
Ask the last two companies you worked for and any founder you have advised informally whether they need one defined project this quarter: a technical due-diligence pack, a hiring plan for the first three engineers, a review of the agency's delivery. The project is the proof. Run the week-one plan in parallel from day one, so that when the network has been asked, founders who posted a signal this week are already in conversation.

### Do I need to have been a CTO to work as a fractional CTO?
The founders in the table are not checking titles; they are asking whether you have made their decision before. A former head of engineering who has hired a team, chosen a stack and survived a rebuild answers every one of the six signals. What you do need is at least one company where you owned the technical decisions end to end, because every founder will ask what you decided and what happened.

### How many clients can a fractional CTO take at once?
Two or three concurrent clients at one or two days a week each is the usual shape, because a CTO's work is decisions and availability rather than hours, and a founder in a crisis needs you the same afternoon. The plan above is sized for that: the goal is one new conversation a week that could become a client, not a pipeline of fifty.

### Should a fractional CTO use a marketplace to find clients?
Use one as a backstop, not as the plan. A marketplace shows the founder a list of CTOs at once, so the comparison is on rate and the founder has already decided what they want. The founders in the table have a problem and no shortlist yet, and a conversation that starts there sets the scope and the price. Marketplaces also take a share or set the terms; a direct engagement does not.

### How do founders usually find a fractional CTO?
Through their investors, their accelerator, a dev agency that wants a technical counterpart on the client side, or another founder who has one. That is why the partner section matters as much as the signals. The founders who do not have those connections ask in public instead, which is exactly what the table is built to catch.

### What should a fractional CTO charge?
Price the first engagement as a scoped project with a fixed end date, not a retainer, because the founder cannot yet judge the value of a day of your time and you cannot yet judge the state of their codebase. Once the project has shown what a decision of yours is worth, a monthly retainer for one or two days a week is the usual shape. Rates vary by region and stage, so ask two fractional CTOs in your market before quoting.
