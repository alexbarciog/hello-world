The fastest way to get clients for a video production company is to reach a business in the week it decides it needs a video, and that week is usually visible on LinkedIn: a founder asking for a videographer recommendation, a hiring push, a product launch, a funding post, a conference booth, a new head of marketing. Message those people within a day with one observation about their situation and you are the first studio in the conversation. Below: eight signals, a week-one plan, and when referrals win.

## Why does the usual video production advice stall?

Every page on how to get clients for a video production company gives the same list: a showreel site, posting clips on social media, SEO, reviews, referrals, networking events, paid ads, agency partnerships, Upwork and Fiverr, directory listings. None of it is wrong. The problem is that all of it waits for the buyer to come to you, and most companies with a video to make never search for a studio. In Wyzowl's 2026 survey of 266 marketers and consumers, [91% of businesses use video as a marketing tool, 59% make their video in-house, 32% use a mix of in-house and outside help, and 10% rely on external vendors only](https://wyzowl.com/video-marketing-statistics/). The in-house majority is your market, and it decides to outsource one project at a time, when a specific video is due and nobody inside can make it. Wistia's 2026 State of Video Report, a survey of over 900 professionals, found [around a quarter of companies outsourcing video to freelancers or production agencies, with outsourced production up 7% on the year, and the share of companies planning customer testimonial videos rising from 17% in 2023 to 47% this year](https://wistia.com/blog/video-marketing-statistics). Those projects are commissioned when a launch, a hire or an event puts a date on them.

The studio owners who have built real pipelines say the same thing from the other side. The Lapse Productions guide to how video companies get clients, updated on 1 November 2026, calls referrals [the default engine with a ceiling](https://www.creativesgrabcoffee.com/guides/how-video-production-companies-get-clients/): they compound, but they are lumpy and concentrated in a few relationships. The same guide reports one studio sending 500 to 600 generic messages and getting nothing back, and another running thousands of cold emails a day with a system behind them. Outbound fails in the middle, where most small studios sit: a few hundred untargeted messages to anyone with "marketing" in their title.

The fix is timing. A buying signal is a public action that shows a company is in-market now; the [buying signal lesson](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal) explains the idea. For video the signals are unusually visible, because the things that create a brief (a launch, a hiring drive, an event, a round, a rebrand) are the things companies announce on LinkedIn.

## Where do video production clients show up on LinkedIn?

Each row is a moment you can find with a free LinkedIn account. The search column is what to type into the search bar before switching to the Posts tab (or the Jobs tab, where stated) and filtering to the past week. The last column is the message, written to quote the signal and offer one video, not a menu of services.

| Signal | Where | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A recommendation request ("anyone know a good videographer?", "looking for a video production company in Austin") | Posts tab: `"video production" recommend` or `videographer "any recommendations"` or `"looking for a" videographer`, past week | An open evaluation with a short list forming in the comments the same day; the budget already exists | Reply in the thread the same day with one usable point about their project (format, length, where it will run), then connect with a note that quotes the post and offers a 15-minute scoping call |
| A hiring push ("we're hiring", "20 open roles", a careers post from HR), or a job post for a video producer, video editor or content producer | Posts tab: `"we're hiring" "open roles"` plus your city or industry words; Jobs tab: "video producer" or "video editor", filtered by company size and region | Recruiting at volume needs a culture film and role videos, and HR owns a budget marketing does not touch; a video job post means video is already a line item and the first projects cannot wait for the hire | For the hiring push, message the head of talent with one observation about how candidates see the company online and offer a single role video to test; for the job post, message the hiring manager and offer to cover the first two projects while the role is open |
| A product launch ("launching next month", "we just shipped", "live on Product Hunt") | Posts tab: `"launching" "next month" product` or `"Product Hunt" "we're live"`, past week | An explainer or demo video is due and the deadline is public; the marketing lead is deciding this week whether to make it in-house | Reply with one point about the launch itself, then offer a short product video scoped to the launch date, with the deadline in your message |
| A funding announcement ("we raised", "excited to announce our seed round") | Posts tab: `"we raised" seed` or `"announce" "Series A"` plus your industry words, past week | A hiring plan, a brand refresh and a content budget arrive together; investors want a story that can be shown | Wait a day for the congratulations, then message the founder with one observation about how the company currently tells its story and offer a brand film or founder story scoped to the next quarter |
| An event ("we'll be at", "see us at booth", "speaking at", "sponsoring") | Posts tab: `"we'll be at" booth` or `"see us at" "booth"` or `"speaking at" conference`, past month | Booth loops, speaker recaps and attendee interviews are needed on a fixed date, and the event team usually books outside crew | Message the event or field marketing manager four to six weeks out: the event by name, the one asset most booths forget, and your availability on the dates |
| A new marketing leader ("excited to join X as head of marketing", "new role as content lead") | Posts tab: `"excited to join" "head of marketing"` or `"new role" "content marketing"`, past week | A first-90-days plan and a new roster of suppliers; the previous studio, if any, has no relationship with this person | Congratulate them, ask what the first content priority is, and offer a look at the company's existing video with one specific suggestion |
| A rebrand or new website ("our new website is live", "new look", "rebrand") | Posts tab: `"new website" live` or `"rebrand" "excited"`, past week | A homepage film, updated testimonials and new social cuts are all missing on launch day | Reply with one thing the new site does well, then offer the one video the site most obviously lacks |
| A "we need more video" post from a marketing manager, often with a phone-shot clip attached | Posts tab: `"more video" "this year" marketing` or `"video content" "struggling"`, past week | The person who will approve the budget has said in public that the work is not getting done | Reply with one practical tip they can use today, connect, and mention what you do only in the second message |

Two filters keep the list honest. A signal older than two weeks is cold; the company has either found a studio or decided to film it on a phone. And a post from another video company or a freelance videographer asking "who needs video this quarter?" is not a buyer, however many comments it has. Reddit and X carry the same requests, and the [Reddit method](/blog/find-b2b-leads-on-reddit) has the queries; treat them as manual channels, because Intentsly monitors LinkedIn only.

## How to get clients for a video production company by hand: a week-one plan

The plan takes about an hour a day with a free LinkedIn account. Sales Navigator makes the company-size and Jobs-tab steps faster but is not required.

1. **Day 1: write the one-sentence offer and list the voices.** Pick the company type, the video type and the region, for example "testimonial and product videos for B2B software companies of 50 to 300 people in the Netherlands". Then list ten people your buyers follow (video marketing writers, B2B marketing voices, event organisers in your niche) and five studios that compete with you. The [ideal customer profile worksheet](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) is the longer version of this step.
2. **Day 2: build the saved searches.** Create the eight Posts-tab searches from the table with your industry words added, each filtered to the past week, plus one Jobs search for "video producer" or "video editor" at companies in your region. Bookmark them. The [funding-round guide](/guides/find-companies-that-just-raised-funding) and the [hiring-signals routine](/guides/track-hiring-signals-for-sales) go deeper on the two signals with the most volume.
3. **Days 3 to 5: thirty minutes of reading, thirty of replying.** Run the searches, read the new posts, and for each real buyer do the "what to do" step from the table. Reply in public first, connect second. Send at most 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day; LinkedIn tolerates more, your acceptance rate does not.
4. **Day 6: follow up and find the budget owner.** For every accepted connection with no reply, send one message that adds something: a question about where the video will run, a reference cut you think fits. Never "just checking in". Where the person who posted is not the budget owner, the [decision-maker guide](/guides/find-decision-makers-on-linkedin) has the six steps to find who signs.
5. **Day 7: count.** Signals found, replies, calls booked. Fewer than ten relevant posts in a week means the industry words are wrong or your buyers are quiet on LinkedIn; widen the search terms before changing the niche.

Message template for the hiring-push signal, for copying:

```
Saw the 20 open roles. Most companies hiring at this pace lose
candidates between the job ad and the first interview, because
nothing online shows what the team is actually like. One role
video usually fixes that for the hardest role first. Happy to
scope one for the engineering posts in 15 minutes, no deck.
```

Finding the right fifteen people each day is where the hour goes; if you want that step done for you, the [LinkedIn signals feature](/features/linkedin-signals) runs the same searches continuously and hands you the list already scored against your customer profile.

## Other ways to get video production clients, and when they beat signals

Signals produce conversations with strangers. Three other routes earn their place, and for most studios one of them comes first.

**Referrals and your existing clients** beat signals for the first few clients and for the biggest ones. The studio owners in the [Lapse Productions guide](https://www.creativesgrabcoffee.com/guides/how-video-production-companies-get-clients/) describe businesses running on roughly 70% word of mouth, and the r/videography thread that ranks for this question tells the same story: a low-paid promo for a local clinic, seen by one of its suppliers, became a yearly contract with that supplier's marketing director. The ceiling is that referrals arrive when someone else decides, and they cluster around a few people. Keep the relationships with people, not companies, because the marketing manager who hired you will move jobs and take the budget with her.

**Google search and Google Ads** beat signals when buyers in your city type "video production company" plus the place name, which is true for corporate, event and real-estate work and much less true for B2B software, where the buyer asks a peer instead. The same guide quotes owners whose businesses run almost entirely on search, and others whose cost per click in a big city makes ads marginal, so the economics depend on your market. Search is slow to build and fast to lose when an algorithm update lands; pair it with the plan above rather than waiting for it.

**Agency and complementary partnerships** beat signals when you would rather produce than sell. Marketing agencies, PR firms, event companies and web studios all have clients who need video and no crew; the [marketing agency page](/solutions/find-clients-for-a-marketing-agency) shows what those agencies are chasing, which is the same list of signals from the other side. The cost is margin and a client relationship you do not own.

Marketplaces and directories are the one route the signal method replaces outright for business work: a listing waits for a buyer who already knows what to search for, and the table above finds the buyer who has not started searching yet. ## FAQ

### How do video production companies get corporate clients?
Corporate video is bought by a handful of roles: marketing managers and content leads (product, brand and testimonial videos), HR and talent teams (recruitment and culture films), event and field marketing managers (booth and recap content) and founders at companies too small to have any of those. Each of them announces the moment they need video on LinkedIn, and the table above gives the search and the message for each.

### How do I get video production clients with no network and no portfolio?
Pick one video type and one industry, film two spec pieces for real companies in that niche and send them to those companies. Run the eight searches above and reply in public before you ask for anything. Replies to a recommendation request or a "we need more video" post do not depend on who you know, only on being useful within a day. The first paid project becomes the portfolio.

### Does cold outreach work for video production companies?
It works when each message quotes something the company just did and offers one specific video, and it fails when it is a generic pitch to anyone with "marketing" in their title. The [Lapse Productions guide](https://www.creativesgrabcoffee.com/guides/how-video-production-companies-get-clients/) reports both outcomes from studio owners: hundreds of generic messages with no reply, and systematic, segmented outreach that fills a pipeline. The signal is what makes the message specific.

### Should a video production company niche down?
For outreach, yes. The searches in the table only work with your buyers' words in them: "we're hiring" returns every company on earth, "we're hiring" plus "SaaS" plus "Berlin" returns yours. Niche by buyer and video type first (recruitment films for scaling tech companies, say), industry second. You can still take a general client who finds you through a referral; you cannot search for one.

### How long does it take to get the first corporate video client this way?
Week one should produce replies and a scoping call or two if your niche posts regularly on LinkedIn. A company that announced a launch, a hiring push or an event this week is usually choosing a supplier within a month, so count weekly and expect a single scoped project before a retainer. Fewer than ten relevant posts a week means the search terms need widening before the niche needs changing.
