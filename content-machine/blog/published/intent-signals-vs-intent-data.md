> [!SUMMARY]
> - An intent signal is one dated action you can quote: a named person commented under a competitor's post this morning.
> - Intent data is a score: many anonymous actions, aggregated per company over weeks, ranked against a baseline.
> - Test any sales page with one question: can I quote the action and name the person? Signal. Is it a number that rose? Intent data.
> - Under 200 employees and deals under six figures: work from signals. Enterprise deals with long cycles: intent data earns its contract.

**An intent signal is a single observed action showing that a specific person or company has a need right now; intent data is many such actions, aggregated and scored over time, usually at company level.** Vendors swap the phrases, but they answer different questions. A signal tells you who to message today and what to say first. Intent data tells you which accounts researched your category this month. The intent signals vs intent data choice comes down to which question you need answered.

## What is the difference between intent signals and intent data?

The difference is granularity and timing. A signal is one event with a date, a source and usually a name attached. Intent data is a trend: it only exists once you have collected enough events to compare this week against last month.

| | Intent signal | Intent data |
|---|---|---|
| Unit | One action ("asked for CRM recommendations on LinkedIn, Tuesday") | A score or stage per company ("Account X surging on topic Y") |
| Names | Usually a person | Usually a company |
| Timing | The moment it happens | Computed over weeks, delivered on the provider's schedule |
| Can you check it? | Yes, open the post or the profile | No, you receive a number |
| First line of the message | Quotes the action | Cannot quote anything specific |
| Where it comes from | Public posts, job changes, funding news, your own site, a reply | Publisher networks, review sites, your own site at volume |
| Typical buyer | A founder or a team of 1 to 20 reps | A revenue team with RevOps and an ABM budget |
| Fails when | You cannot see the action, or you reply late | The score names nobody, or the research was weeks ago |

The lesson on [what intent data is](/learn/what-is-intent-data) covers the account-level versus person-level split in detail. For [what a buying signal is](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal), the short version is that a buying signal is one kind of intent signal: the kind that implies a purchase rather than curiosity.

## How do you tell which one a vendor is selling?

Ask one question of the sales page: **can I quote the action and name the person?** If the product shows you something like "head of sales at a 40-person agency commented 'we are comparing tools for this' under your competitor's post at 09:12" (an invented example), that is a signal. If it shows "Acme Corp: Decision stage, High activity" or "Acme Corp: 74 on 'sales intelligence'", that is intent data, however the vendor labels it.

[SalesIntel's comparison](https://salesintel.io/blog/buying-signals-vs-intent-data/) calls the same split probabilistic versus deterministic: intent data says an account might be researching, a signal confirms something happened. [Common Room's article](https://www.commonroom.io/blog/intent-data-vs-buyer-intelligence/) lists the four limits that follow from a modelled score: no identity, no context, delay, and no clear next step. Both are vendors with a product to sell, but the distinction holds up when you check it against what the big providers actually output.

> [!TIP]
> If a vendor calls its scores "signals", ask what the single underlying event was and when it happened. If they cannot answer, you are buying intent data, which is fine, as long as you are pricing it as intent data.

## What do Bombora and G2 actually deliver?

The two best-known intent data products are useful here because their own documentation shows how far a score sits from a quotable action.

Bombora's Company Surge compares an account's content consumption over the most recent three weeks against a 12-week baseline for that same account, across more than 25,300 topics, and Bombora's own wording is that "account-level intent shows you where to look" ([Bombora, Company Surge](https://bombora.com/intent/)). A company counts as spiking on a topic when its score reaches 60 or more, and Bombora's recommended floor for action is that same 60 ([Bombora customer documentation](https://customers.bombora.com/crc-brand/thresholding)). So the output is: this company read more about this topic than it usually does. Not who read it, not what they wrote.

G2 Buyer Intent reports at the buyer-organisation level and, since 2022, gives each company a Buying Stage (Awareness, Consideration or Decision) and an Activity Level (Low, Medium or High), updated daily, from pageviews on G2 plus Capterra, Software Advice and GetApp ([G2 documentation](https://documentation.g2.com/docs/buyer-intent)). The company name is real and the research is real. You still do not know which of the eight people in that office compared you with a rival, so the first message has to open with a guess.

```stats
60+ | Bombora Company Surge score at which a company counts as spiking on a topic | https://customers.bombora.com/crc-brand/thresholding
12 weeks | baseline Bombora compares the latest 3 weeks of activity against | https://bombora.com/intent/
Daily | how often G2 updates a company's Buying Stage and Activity Level | https://documentation.g2.com/docs/buyer-intent
```

That gap, from "this company is researching" to "this person said this", is the whole intent signals vs intent data debate in one sentence. A score is a reason to look. A signal is a reason to write.

## Why does the gap matter more than it used to?

Because buyers decide earlier and talk to sellers later. In 6sense's 2025 Buyer Experience Report, a survey of more than 4,000 B2B buyers, 94% of buying groups already had a preferred vendor before they contacted any seller, 77% went on to buy from that preliminary favourite, the research-to-seller time split had moved to 60/40, and the average cycle had shortened to about 10 months ([6sense, Business Wire, 12 November 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251112018032/en/)).

Read that against the two definitions. An account-level score that rises over three weeks tells you a shortlist is being written somewhere inside the company. A signal, a named person asking a public question or engaging with a competitor's post, tells you who is writing it and gives you a reason to be in the conversation before the preferred vendor is chosen. If you cannot name the person, the shortlist closes without you either way.

> [!NOTE]
> Account-level intent data is not useless for small teams; it is late and anonymous for them. A ten-person company with a 30-day sales cycle cannot wait three weeks for a topic surge and then spend a week finding the right contact.

## Where is each one the wrong choice?

Every page on this topic ends with "use both". That advice is only useful if you know which one to drop when you cannot afford both, so here is where each fails.

**Intent data is the wrong buy when** your deals close in under 90 days, your buyer is one person rather than a committee, or nobody on your team will own the weekly list. The score arrives at company level; someone still has to find the person, write the message and send it before the surge is stale. If that someone is the founder, the contract goes unused by month three. The [intent data tools for small business](/blog/intent-data-tools-for-small-business) comparison goes through the options and their minimum team sizes.

**Signals are the wrong choice when** your buyer never acts in public. A hospital procurement team does not comment on LinkedIn posts, so post engagement will find nobody, and you would be better served by job-change and hiring signals or by first-party data from your own site. Signals also need a fast reply: a public question gets its answers within a day or two, so a team that batches outreach weekly loses most of the value.

**Public LinkedIn signals, which is what Intentsly watches, are the wrong choice when** your buyers are not on LinkedIn, when you sell to consumers, or when you need email or Reddit monitoring, which we do not offer. We watch post engagement, keyword posts, competitor page followers and hashtag engagement on LinkedIn, and nothing else. The [LinkedIn buying signals](/blog/linkedin-buying-signals) guide shows what you can see with a free account before paying anyone.

## Is a LinkedIn comment an intent signal or intent data?

A single comment is an intent signal. It names a person, it has a timestamp, and you can read it. Fifty comments from the same company over a quarter, counted and ranked, would be intent data you built yourself.

That is the useful loophole for a small team: you can produce your own intent data from signals, but you cannot recover signals from someone else's intent data. The score strips the names out. The lesson on [first-party intent data](/learn/what-is-first-party-intent-data) explains why public signals sit outside both the first-party and third-party categories: you do not own LinkedIn, and nobody aggregated the comment for you.

```message
Saw your question under Maria's post about which tool handles reply tracking. We hit the same wall last year. Happy to send the two-line answer here, no call needed. What's the volume you're at?
```

That message is only possible because a signal, not a score, was the starting point. The example above is a hypothetical opener, not one we sent; it shows the shape.

## How do you use intent signals and intent data together?

If you have both, the order of operations is: data decides the list, signals decide the message and the day. Pull the accounts that are surging or in Decision stage, then watch the people at those accounts for a public action, and write to the person who acted, quoting what they did.

1. **Set the account filter first.** Companies that fit your ideal customer profile by size, industry and geography. Without this, both signals and scores flood you with people who cannot buy.
2. **Pull intent data for the list, if you have it.** Mark the accounts that are surging or in a Consideration or Decision stage this week.
3. **Watch for signals at those accounts.** Post engagement with competitors, questions that match your category, a new hire in the buying role, a follow of a rival's page.
4. **Score the person, not just the account.** Right role, right seniority, acted in the last 48 hours. Intentsly does this step for LinkedIn: it [finds people who engaged with competitor posts or wrote matching posts, scores them against your ideal customer profile, and sends the first LinkedIn message](/features/linkedin-signals), so the daily watching goes away.
5. **Write from the signal.** First line quotes the action. Second line offers something useful. No pitch until they reply.
6. **Measure reply rate on signal-led messages against your cold baseline over the same four weeks.** That number, not a vendor's, is the one to trust.

If you have only signals, skip step 2 and let the account filter do the prioritising. If you have only intent data, step 4 becomes manual: open the account, find the likely buyer on LinkedIn, check their recent activity, and accept that your first line will be generic.

## Which one should a small team use?

The rule in one minute:

- **Under 200 employees, deals under six figures, buyer is one or two people, buyers active on LinkedIn:** work from signals. Start with the free methods in the [signal-based selling](/learn/what-is-signal-based-selling) lesson, then automate the watching when the daily searching eats more than an hour.
- **Deals over six figures, committees of five or more, cycles over six months, a RevOps owner:** intent data earns its contract, because you need to know which accounts to work before anyone acts in public. Add signals on top for the message.
- **Buyers who never act in public:** neither. Use first-party data from your own site and product, plus hiring and job-change signals from company pages and job boards.

## FAQ

### Are intent signals and intent data the same thing?

No. An intent signal is one observed action by a person or company, with a date, that you can read for yourself. Intent data is the aggregate of many such actions, scored per account over weeks. Vendors use the words interchangeably, so test any product with one question: can you quote the underlying action and name the person? If not, it is intent data.

### Which is more reliable, intent signals or intent data?

A signal is more reliable about one person today, because you can verify it by opening the post or the profile. Intent data is more reliable about a company's direction over a month, because it averages out single events. Neither is reliable about a purchase: both show research or interest, and only a conversation confirms budget and timing.

### Is a LinkedIn comment an intent signal or intent data?

A single comment is an intent signal: a named person, a timestamp, readable text. The same comments counted per company across a quarter would be intent data you built yourself. Signals can be rolled up into intent data; a purchased intent score cannot be unrolled back into the names, which is why small teams usually start with signals.

### Can a small team use intent data without buying it from a provider?

Yes, in a limited form. Your own site and product generate first-party intent data: pricing-page visits, repeat logins, feature-page views. Add public signals you collect yourself, such as post engagement and job changes, and you have a working account-level view. You will lack the market-wide coverage that providers such as Bombora or G2 sell, which matters most for long enterprise cycles.

### Should you use intent signals and intent data together?

If you can afford both, yes, in a fixed order: intent data picks the accounts to watch, signals pick the person, the day and the first line of the message. If you can afford one, choose by deal shape. Short cycles, small buying groups and LinkedIn-active buyers favour signals; long cycles, large committees and a RevOps owner favour intent data.

### What makes an intent signal strong?

Three things: it names a person in a buying role, it happened in the last 48 hours, and it states or clearly implies a need, such as asking which tool to use or engaging with a competitor's pricing post. A like from an intern on a general industry article is a signal in the technical sense and worth nothing in practice. Score on role, recency and specificity before you write.
