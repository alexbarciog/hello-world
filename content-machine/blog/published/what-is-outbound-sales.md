**Outbound sales is selling that starts with the seller contacting a specific potential buyer who did not ask to be contacted.** That is the short answer to "what is outbound sales": the seller picks the person, decides when to reach out and makes the first move, by phone, email, LinkedIn or post. Inbound sales is the reverse, where the buyer finds you and asks first. Everything else about outbound, from the lists to the scripts to the reply rates, follows from that one fact about who started the conversation.

## Why does outbound sales matter?

Outbound sales matters because it is the only way to reach buyers who have the problem you solve but are not yet looking for a solution. Inbound only brings you the people already searching. In most markets that is a small minority, and the competitors with bigger marketing budgets are already waiting for them.

Outbound also gives you control that inbound cannot. You choose which companies to approach, so you can go after the accounts that fit best rather than the ones that happen to find you. You choose the timing, so a product launch or a new market does not have to wait for search traffic to build. And you get feedback in days, not quarters: send 30 messages this week and you know by Friday whether the pitch lands.

A labelled example (hypothetical, not a customer): a two-person firm builds onboarding software for accounting practices. Nobody searches for it, because practices do not know such a thing exists. The founder lists 80 practices with 10 to 40 staff, finds the partner who runs operations at each, and writes to them about the hours their team loses re-keying client data. Nine reply, four take a demo, two buy. None of those sales could have come from inbound, because none of those buyers was looking.

## How does outbound sales work?

Outbound sales runs as a repeating sequence of five steps. The steps are the same whether one founder does them in an hour a day or a team of sales development representatives (SDRs, the people who prospect and qualify before handing over to a closer) does them full time.

1. **Define who you sell to.** A one-page [ideal customer profile](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile): company size, industry, the role that buys, the problem they have and one public sign that they have it.
2. **Build the list.** Named people at named companies that match the profile, with a working contact route for each. The [guide to building a B2B prospect list](/guides/build-b2b-prospect-list) gives the steps.
3. **Reach out.** A first message by email, phone or LinkedIn, then follow-ups on a fixed schedule. This is the step people mean when they say "outbound".
4. **Qualify.** In the first reply or call, check that the person has the problem, the authority and the timing. Pass the ones who do into a sales conversation, and drop the rest without a fight.
5. **Measure and adjust.** Replies per 100 messages, meetings per 100 replies, deals per 100 meetings. Change one variable at a time: the list, the first line, the channel.

What a day of this looks like at a staffed team is documented. The Bridge Group's 2025 report on 351 B2B companies puts the median SDR at 112 activities a day (44 phone, 41 email, 19 LinkedIn, 8 text or other), producing 4.1 qualified conversations a day, with an average ramp of 3.0 months, one SDR supporting 2.4 account executives, and 60% of reps at quota, the lowest figure in the study's history ([The Bridge Group, 2025 SDR Metrics Report](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)). Outbound is a volume job when the list is built from a database, and the volume is what wears teams down.

The channels have their own limits. LinkedIn restricts accounts that send too many connection requests and messages, which is why Intentsly caps every connected account at 30 messages and 30 connection requests a day and recommends 15. Email has anti-spam law behind it, covered in the lesson on [cold outreach](/learn/what-is-cold-outreach). Phone is the fastest route to a conversation and the most expensive per conversation.

## Outbound sales vs inbound sales vs signal-based outbound

Most definitions stop at outbound versus inbound. There is a third column worth knowing, because it changes what the first message can say. Signal-based outbound is still outbound, since the seller makes first contact, but the list comes from what buyers just did in public rather than from a database filter.

| | Inbound sales | List-based outbound | Signal-based outbound |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who makes first contact | The buyer | The seller | The seller |
| Where the buyer comes from | Search, content, ads, referrals | A database filtered by company size, industry, role | Public actions: a post about the problem, a comment on a competitor's post, a new role, a funding announcement |
| What the buyer knows about you | Something, they came to you | Usually nothing | Usually nothing |
| What the first line can refer to | Their request | Their company or job title | The thing they just did |
| Timing | Set by the buyer | Set by the seller's quota | Set by the buyer's action |
| Volume available | Limited by your marketing reach | Large | Medium, limited by how much your market says in public |
| Fails when | Nobody is searching for what you sell | The list is broad and the message generic | You act too late or chase the wrong signal |

The one-minute rule: if buyers already search for what you sell and you can afford to wait for them, build inbound and treat outbound as a supplement. If they do not search for it, you need outbound. Within outbound, if your buyers talk about their work in public, start from signals, because a message that refers to what someone wrote yesterday reads as warm even though the recipient has never heard of you. If your buyers are silent in public, run list-based outbound, keep each batch small and follow up.

A [buying signal](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal) is the unit the third column runs on: a public action by a named person that suggests timing. When we launched Intentsly we ran our own LinkedIn signals and AI SDR against founders complaining about cold outreach, and booked 14 qualified demos in 14 days with zero manual outreach and a reply rate 4x our previous cold campaigns, mostly from signals caught in real time (see [case studies](/case-studies)). Every message went to a stranger. Every message referred to something the recipient had just said.

## Does outbound sales still work?

Outbound sales still works, under conditions the data makes clear. Hunter analysed 11 million cold emails sent in 2024 and found an average reply rate of 4.1%, rising to 5.8% for campaigns of 50 or fewer recipients and falling to 2.1% for campaigns of 1,000 or more; campaigns with follow-ups averaged 4.9% against 3% without. In the same report's survey of 217 decision-makers, 71% named lack of relevance as the reason they do not reply ([Hunter, The State of Cold Email 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)). Small lists, specific messages and a follow-up are not tips; they are the difference between the top and bottom of that range.

The buyer's side of the table is moving against generic outbound. In a Gartner survey of 646 B2B buyers fielded in August and September 2025, 67% said they prefer a rep-free buying experience and 45% had used AI during a recent purchase ([Gartner, 9 March 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience)). A buyer who would rather not talk to a rep at all makes an exception for one who clearly knows their situation and wastes none of their time. That favours outbound that starts from what the buyer just did, and punishes outbound that starts from a spreadsheet.

The limit is the reading. A message that refers to something true about one person takes ten to twenty minutes of research first, and a founder running outbound alongside everything else cannot do that for 200 people a month. That is the step where [Intentsly's LinkedIn signals](/features/linkedin-signals) do the reading, surfacing the people in your market who just engaged with a competitor or posted about the problem, so the first line is already there before you open the message box.

## How to use outbound sales this week

Three actions, each under an hour:

1. **Write the profile before the list.** One paragraph naming the company size, the buying role, the problem and one public sign that a company has it. If you cannot name the public sign, you will be running list-based outbound, so plan for small batches.
2. **Pick 25 people and find one fact each.** A post, a hire, a tool they use, an announcement. Write the first line of each message from that fact. If finding the fact takes longer than writing the message, the list is too broad or the channel is wrong.
3. **Set the follow-up and the scoreboard.** Schedule one follow-up three to four working days later that adds something new. Log sends, replies and meetings in a sheet, so that in two weeks you know your own reply rate rather than the industry's.

## FAQ

### What is outbound sales in simple terms?
Outbound sales is selling where the seller makes the first contact with a buyer who did not ask for it. The seller decides who to approach and when, then reaches out by phone, email, LinkedIn or post. Inbound is the opposite: the buyer finds the seller through search, content or a referral and starts the conversation themselves.

### What is the difference between inbound and outbound sales?
Who starts the conversation. In inbound sales the buyer comes to you, usually through search, content, ads or a referral, so they already know something about you and have a need in mind. In outbound sales you go to the buyer, who usually knows nothing about you, so the first message has to earn attention on its own and the list has to be chosen well.

### Is outbound sales the same as cold calling?
No. Cold calling is one outbound channel among several. Outbound sales covers any seller-initiated contact: cold email, LinkedIn connection requests and messages, direct mail and calls. Many teams run two channels in sequence, such as an email followed by a LinkedIn note that refers to it. Which channel to lead with depends on where your buyers actually read and reply.

### What does an outbound sales rep do all day?
An outbound rep, often called an SDR, researches accounts, builds and cleans lists, sends first messages and follow-ups, makes calls, and qualifies the people who reply before handing them to a closer. [The Bridge Group's 2025 report](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group) puts the median at 112 activities a day across phone, email and LinkedIn, producing about four qualified conversations.

### Is outbound sales dead?
No, but generic outbound is dying. [Hunter's analysis of 11 million cold emails](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025) shows reply rates falling from 5.8% for lists of 50 or fewer to 2.1% for lists of 1,000 or more, and [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience) finds 67% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free experience. The version that still works is small, specific and timed to something the buyer just did.

### What is an example of outbound sales?
A labelled hypothetical: a founder selling payroll software for restaurant groups lists 40 groups with five or more locations, finds the finance lead at each, and sends a short email naming the tip-reporting rule that changed this year, followed by a LinkedIn note four days later. The buyers did not ask to hear from her. That is outbound. If they had filled in her demo form instead, that would be inbound.
