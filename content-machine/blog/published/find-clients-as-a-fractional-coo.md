> - Companies hire a fractional COO in the weeks after the founder becomes the bottleneck, and that moment shows up on LinkedIn: a hiring burst, an ops job post, a "nothing moves without me" post.
> - Seven signals below, each with the search to run and the message to send.

The fastest way to find clients as a fractional COO is to reach founders in the week they admit the company has outgrown them: a hiring burst they cannot onboard, a head-of-operations job post, a funding round, a post saying every decision still crosses their desk. All of it is visible on LinkedIn. Message those founders within a day with one observation, not a service list, and you are the first operator in the conversation. Below: seven signals, a week-one plan, and when your network beats all of it.

## Why does the usual fractional COO advice stall?

Every page that ranks for this question gives the same sequence: tell your network privately, announce publicly, post regularly, ask clients for referrals, join a marketplace. None of it is wrong, and the first client almost always comes from the first step. The problem is that the network produces a client only when someone in it happens to need an operator this quarter.

The practitioners say so themselves. In Go Fractional's 2026 survey of 213 fractional leaders, [90% named client acquisition as their biggest challenge, 92% relied on their personal network as a primary channel, and 54% used LinkedIn to source clients](https://www.gofractional.com/docs/2026_State_of_Fractional_Work-Go_Fractional.pdf). Fractional Jobs' own survey data puts it at [84% of fractional leaders finding their first client through their network](https://www.fractionaljobs.io/help/how-to-get-your-first-fractional-clients). The network is where you start; it is not a method for the second and third client.

The demand is real and the buyers are small. Lightcast counted [677 US job postings for fractional roles in 2025 and nearly 1,000 in the first seven months of 2026](https://lightcast.io/blog/rise-of-fractional-leadership), with 97% of the 2026 postings from small and medium companies and business management making up 12% of them. Revelio Labs' data, reported by the Financial Times, says [24 of every 1,000 new executive postings advertised in 2026 were fractional, up from nine five years earlier](https://www.reveliolabs.com/blog/fractional-executive-roles), led by CFO roles and followed by marketing and operations. A buying signal is a public action that shows a company is in-market now; the [trigger event lesson](/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales) explains the idea. For a fractional COO the signals are founder behaviour, and founders post.

## Where do fractional COO clients show up on LinkedIn?

The buyer-side pages agree on the symptoms: the founder approves everything, processes live in people's heads, hiring runs ahead of onboarding, revenue grows while margin does not, projects stall for lack of an owner. Each row below is one of those symptoms as it looks from the outside, with the search to type before switching to the Posts tab (or the Jobs tab, where stated) and filtering to the past week. The last column is the message, written to quote the signal and offer one piece of operations work.

| Signal | Where | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A hiring burst (several roles open at once, "we're hiring across the board", "doubling the team") | Jobs tab: filter to companies of 20 to 150 people in your niche, sort by most recent, look for three or more open roles; Posts tab: `"we're hiring" "roles"` plus your industry words | Headcount is about to outrun onboarding, management cadence and tooling; the founder will feel it in six to eight weeks | Message the founder: what breaks first when a team of that size doubles (onboarding, meeting rhythm, who owns what) and offer a 30-minute look at their hiring plan |
| A job post for a head of operations, operations manager, chief of staff or VP operations | Jobs tab: "head of operations" or "chief of staff", companies under about 150 people | Budget for operations work exists; the company often needs COO-level judgement for two days a week more than a full-time hire it will take months to fill | Offer to cover the role while it stays open, define what the hire should own on arrival, and help interview them |
| The "everything runs through me" post ("I'm the bottleneck", "working in the business, not on it", "I approved 40 things today") | Posts tab: `founder "bottleneck"` or `"working in the business"` or `"every decision" founder`, past week | The founder has diagnosed the problem in public and is open to a fix; the comments fill with other founders, not operators | Reply with one specific mechanism (a weekly operating review, a decision-rights list), then connect with a note that quotes the post |
| A seed or Series A funding announcement | Posts tab: `"we raised" seed` or `"announce" "Series A"` plus your industry words, past week | A hiring plan, a board and a target that doubles the plan, usually with a founder still running operations by hand | Wait a day for the congratulations, then send one observation about what the plan needs in month one (an operating cadence, a hiring sequence) and offer to sketch it |
| A recommendation or how-to request ("anyone know a fractional COO?", "how do you run your weekly leadership meeting?", "EOS implementer recommendations?") | Posts tab: `"fractional COO" recommend` or `"leadership meeting" "how do you"` or `"EOS" recommend`, past week | An open evaluation, with a short list forming in the comments the same day | Answer the question in the thread with one usable point, then connect; offer a look at their current cadence, not a proposal |
| An operations leader leaving, or a new CEO arriving | Posts tab: `"last day" "operations"` or `"stepping down" COO`; also `"excited to join" CEO` at companies of 20 to 150 people | A gap in the operating seat, or a new leader who audits every function in the first quarter | Ask who is covering the weekly rhythm and the hiring pipeline, and offer to bridge the gap for a defined number of weeks |
| A systems rollout ("rolling out EOS", "migrating to NetSuite", "writing our first SOPs", "moving off spreadsheets") | Posts tab: `"rolling out" "EOS"` or `"first SOPs"` or `"migrating" "NetSuite"` founder, past week | A one-off project with a deadline, which is the easiest fractional engagement to start and the usual door to a retainer | Offer the project, scoped and priced for the deadline, with the retainer left for later |

Two filters keep the list honest. A signal older than two weeks is cold; the founder has either found someone or decided to live with it. And a post from another fractional COO asking "who is drowning in operations?" is not a buyer, however many comments it has. Reddit and X carry the same founder complaints, and the [Reddit method](/blog/find-b2b-leads-on-reddit) has the queries; treat them as manual channels, because Intentsly monitors LinkedIn only.

> [!NOTE]
> The company size in the table is a starting point, not a rule. Umbrex's readiness checklist for fractional executives names the fractional COO in its [Series A band of 30 to 100 employees](https://umbrex.com/resources/fractional-executive-playbook/company-readiness-deciding-to-hire-a-fractional-executive/), and its go/no-go questions (revenue or headcount up 50% or more in a year, runway under 18 months, an investor asking for expertise the team lacks) are a good description of what the signals above look like from the inside.

## How to find clients as a fractional COO by hand: a week-one plan

The plan takes about an hour a day with a free LinkedIn account. Sales Navigator makes the company-size and job-post steps faster but is not required.

1. **Day 1: write the one-sentence offer and list the voices.** Pick the company type and the stage, for example "20-to-80-person B2B services firms in the UK that have just hired their first managers". Then list ten investors, founder communities and operations writers your buyers follow, and five operators or firms that compete with you. The [ideal customer profile worksheet](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) is the longer version of this step.
2. **Day 2: build the saved searches.** Create the seven Posts-tab searches from the table with your industry words added, each filtered to the past week, plus two Jobs searches: one for the head-of-operations role, one for companies with three or more open roles. Bookmark them. The [hiring-signals routine](/guides/track-hiring-signals-for-sales) and the [funding-round guide](/guides/find-companies-that-just-raised-funding) go deeper on the two signals with the most volume.
3. **Days 3 to 5: thirty minutes of reading, thirty of replying.** Run the searches, read the new posts, and for each real buyer do the "what to do" step from the table. Reply in public first, connect second. Send at most 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day; LinkedIn tolerates more, your acceptance rate does not.
4. **Day 6: follow up and find the budget owner.** For every accepted connection with no reply, send one message that adds something: a question about their hiring sequence, a number from their own post. Never "just checking in". Where the person who posted is not the founder or CEO, the [decision-maker guide](/guides/find-decision-makers-on-linkedin) has the steps to find who signs.
5. **Day 7: count.** Signals found, replies, calls booked. Fewer than ten relevant posts in a week means the industry words are wrong or your buyers are quiet on LinkedIn; widen the search terms before changing the niche.

Message template for the hiring-burst signal, for copying:

```message
Saw you have six roles open at once. In my experience the part that breaks first at that pace is the first 30 days after each hire: who onboards them, what they own, which meeting they join. Happy to sketch a 30-day plan for your next three hires in 20 minutes, no proposal attached.
```

> [!EXAMPLE]
> Hypothetical, to show the shape: a 45-person logistics software company announces a seed round on Tuesday, posts four engineering and two customer-success roles on Thursday, and the founder writes on Friday that she spent the week in interviews instead of with customers. That is three signals from one company in four days, and the message that lands is about the interview load, not about "operational excellence".

Finding the right fifteen people each day is where the hour goes; if you want that step done for you, the [LinkedIn signals feature](/features/linkedin-signals) runs the same searches continuously and hands you the list already scored against your customer profile.

## Other ways to find fractional COO clients, and when they beat signals

Signals produce conversations with strangers. Three other routes earn their place, and for most fractional COOs one of them comes first.

**Former colleagues, founders you have worked with, and their investors** beat signals for the first client, almost always. Every page that ranks for this question says so, and the surveys above agree. The ceiling arrives when that network has been asked; one consultant's own account, Lilach Bullock's, puts the point at which [referrals run dry at about 18 months](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-to-get-clients-as-a-fractional-consultant/), an observation rather than a study, but a common one. Start the signal routine before that point, not after.

**Marketplaces and talent platforms** beat signals when you want volume fast and can accept a fee and a client who belongs to the platform. Go Fractional's survey found [29% of fractional leaders using talent platforms](https://www.gofractional.com/docs/2026_State_of_Fractional_Work-Go_Fractional.pdf), against 54% sourcing on LinkedIn, and its live data shows operations among the functions with the strongest demand. Fractional Jobs and ExecRoster run the same model. The trade is that a platform listing reaches companies that already know they want a fractional COO, while a signal reaches the founder two weeks before they know.

**Content and speaking** beat signals when the goal is to be the obvious choice once a company starts looking, rather than to start the conversation. A weekly post that shows how you run a leadership meeting or sequence a hiring plan earns replies from founders you would never have searched for. It is slow; pair it with the plan above rather than waiting for it.

Cold lists are the one tactic signals replace outright. Almost every founder-led company between 20 and 150 people could use an operator in theory; a post says which of them has admitted it this month. ## FAQ

### How do fractional COOs get their first client?
Through people who already trust their judgement: a former CEO, a founder they shipped something for, an investor who watched them run a team. The surveys linked above put the network first for most fractional leaders. Ask those people who they know with the problem you fix, rather than asking for a favour. Then start the signal routine, so the second client does not depend on luck.

### Do I need a niche to find fractional COO clients?
For outreach, yes. The searches in the table only work with your buyers' words in them: "we're hiring" returns every company on earth, "we're hiring" plus "logistics" plus "London" returns yours. Niche by stage and company type first (founder-led, 20 to 150 people, first managers just hired), industry second. A referral can still bring a general client; a search cannot find one.

### How long does it take to land a fractional COO client with outreach?
Weeks rather than quarters when the first client comes from your network. Signal outreach adds strangers to that: week one should produce replies and a call or two. A founder who posted a hiring burst or an ops job this week is usually deciding within a month, so count weekly and expect a scoped project, such as a hiring plan or an operating cadence, before a retainer.

### Should a fractional COO use a marketplace like Fractional Jobs or Go Fractional?
Use one if you want listed demand fast and can accept the fee and the platform owning the relationship. Many fractional COOs run a platform profile alongside direct outreach rather than instead of it. The platform reaches companies that already decided; the signal routine reaches founders before they have, which is where the pricing conversation is easiest.

### When does a company hire a fractional COO?
When the founder becomes the bottleneck and a full-time COO is not justified yet: typically a founder-led company that has just raised, is hiring faster than it can onboard, or has grown past the point where processes live in people's heads. Umbrex's readiness checklist, linked above, names the fractional COO at the Series A stage. The table on this page shows what each moment looks like on LinkedIn.

### How many clients can a fractional COO handle?
It depends on the days per week each engagement takes, and the pages that rank for this question disagree, so treat any number as a planning guess. A practical test: if a new client's first month would push your week past what you can run with a proper operating review for each, offer a project instead of a retainer. Capacity is the reason to be selective about signals.
