**A buyer persona in B2B is a written profile of one role inside your target companies: what that person is responsible for, what they are measured on, what makes them go looking for something like what you sell, and what would make them say no.** It describes a job, not a company. The short answer to "what is a buyer persona in B2B" is the second filter in outbound: the ideal customer profile picks the company, the persona picks the person inside it and tells you what to say.

## Why does a buyer persona matter in B2B?

Because a B2B purchase is made by a group, and each member of that group wants a different thing from you. Gartner's survey of 632 B2B buyers, run in August and September 2024, found buying groups of five to 16 people across as many as four functions, and that 74% of those groups showed what Gartner calls unhealthy conflict during the decision ([Gartner, 7 May 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-05-07-gartner-sales-survey-finds-74-percent-of-b2b-buyer-teams-demonstrate-unhealthy-conflict-during-the-decision-process)). Forrester's 2026 buyer research puts the typical decision at 13 internal stakeholders and nine external influencers ([Forrester, 21 January 2026](https://investor.forrester.com/news-releases/news-release-details/forresters-2026-buyer-insights-genai-upending-b2b-buying-leaders/)). One message written for "the company" lands with none of them.

A labelled example. Suppose a six-person agency sells HubSpot clean-up to software companies with 20 to 80 staff. Its profile says which companies to approach. Inside each one, the head of sales wants pipeline she can trust by the next board meeting, the RevOps lead wants nobody touching his workflows without a plan, and the founder wants to know what it costs and when it is done. Three people, three personas, three different first lines. Writing the persona is how the agency stops sending the founder's message to the RevOps lead.

## What goes into a B2B buyer persona?

A useful persona fits on one page and has seven rows. Most templates have the first five and stop. The last two are where the persona starts earning its keep.

| Row | What to write | Example (hypothetical agency above, "head of sales" persona) |
|---|---|---|
| Role and seat | Title, who they report to, team size | Head of sales at a 20 to 80 person software company, reports to the founder, runs two to five reps |
| Measured on | The number they are judged by this quarter | Pipeline coverage and forecast accuracy, reviewed at the board meeting |
| Trigger | The event that makes them look for a solution now | A missed forecast, a new rep who cannot find anything in the CRM, a board question she could not answer |
| Buying role | What they do in the decision: start it, decide it, approve it, use it | Starts the search and champions it; the founder signs; RevOps has a veto |
| Objections | The first two things they say against buying | "We tried a cleanup before and it decayed in a month"; "my reps will not log more fields" |
| Says no when | The veto row: what makes this person kill the deal even when the company fits | Any plan that takes reps out of the CRM for more than a day; a vendor who cannot name a similar customer |
| Public evidence | What this role posts, comments on or changes publicly when the trigger is live | Comments under posts about forecast accuracy; asks for RevOps contractor recommendations; posts a job ad for a "sales ops" hire |

The "says no when" row comes from lost deals and is the one most teams never write. The "public evidence" row turns the persona from a description into something you can watch for, which is the link between personas and [buying signals](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal).

## How do you build a buyer persona without a research budget?

The ranking guides assume customer interviews at scale or a data platform. A company with ten customers can do it from five call notes in an afternoon.

1. **Pull five conversations with people in the same role.** Discovery calls, onboarding calls, support threads, lost-deal notes. Same title or close to it. Five is enough to see a pattern; one is an anecdote.
2. **Ask each of them, or your notes, one question.** "What happened in the month before you started looking?" Adience's guide to persona research makes the same point: ask about a recent real purchase, not how someone thinks they would buy ([Adience, 18 September 2026](https://www.adience.com/blog/how-to/how-to-conduct-buyer-persona-research-in-b2b-with-examples/)). The answers fill the trigger row in the buyer's own words.
3. **Read what the role says in public.** Open ten LinkedIn profiles that match the seat and read the last month of posts and comments. The words they use for the problem go in the objections and public-evidence rows. Your words do not.
4. **Write the veto row from the deals you lost.** Who went quiet after the demo, and what did they say last? Name the trait, not the company.
5. **Check it against your last ten closed deals.** If the persona does not describe the person who signed in at least six of them, it describes who you wish bought, and it goes back to step one.

Write the result as one sentence you can say out loud: "Heads of sales at 20 to 80 person software companies, judged on forecast accuracy, who start looking after a missed quarter and kill any plan that takes reps out of the CRM." If it takes two breaths, cut the weakest clause.

## B2B buyer persona vs ideal customer profile vs buying committee

The three terms get used as if they were one. They answer three different questions.

| | Ideal customer profile | Buyer persona | Buying committee |
|---|---|---|---|
| Describes | A type of company | One role inside that company | The set of roles that decide together on one deal |
| Answers | Is this account worth my time? | Who do I talk to, and what do I say first? | Who else has to agree, and in what order? |
| Built from | Your best and worst customers | Five or more people in the same seat | The roles that showed up in your last few deals |
| Count | One, sometimes two | One per role that starts or stops deals | One map per deal type |
| Changes | Yearly | When the role's measures change | When the product's price or scope changes |

The profile gets you to the right building. The persona tells you which door and what to say when it opens. The committee map tells you which other doors you will need. The lessons on [what an ideal customer profile is](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) and [what a buying committee is](/learn/what-is-a-buying-committee-in-b2b-sales) cover the other two in the same depth.

One more neighbour: the champion. A persona is a type of person; a champion is a specific person inside one account who sells for you internally. You write personas before outreach and find champions during it, which the lesson on [what a champion in sales is](/learn/what-is-a-champion-in-sales) covers.

## How is a B2B buyer persona different from a B2C one?

A consumer persona describes the buyer and the user in one person, spending their own money, usually deciding alone. A B2B persona describes one seat in a group, spending the company's money, judged by a number they did not choose, and often not the person who uses the product. That is why demographic rows (age, hobbies, a stock photo and a first name) do most of the work in B2C templates and almost none in B2B. HubSpot's widely copied definition calls a persona "a fictional representation of a company's ideal customer" ([HubSpot, updated 18 March 2026](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/buyer-persona-definition-under-100-sr)). The fiction is the part to leave out. Keep the rows you can check against a real call.

The group also does more of its own research before it talks to you. Gartner's survey of 645 B2B buyers, run in August and September 2025, found buyers using an average of seven information sources during a recent purchase, 45% using generative AI to gather vendor information, and 69% preferring to validate AI-generated insights with a sales rep ([Gartner, 20 May 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-20-gartner-survey-finds-sixty-nine-percent-of-b-two-b-buyers-turn-to-sales-reps-to-validate-ai-generated-insights)). The persona's objections row is what that rep needs to know before the validation call.

## Where do buyer personas mislead?

Four ways, all common in companies under 200 people.

**Written by marketing alone.** A persona built from what the team imagines about the buyer reads well and sells nothing. Every row should trace back to a call, a lost deal or a public post.

**Too many.** ZoomInfo's guide says most B2B teams need three to five personas, with no reason given ([ZoomInfo, updated 30 June 2026](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/marketing/buyer-persona-guide)). The one-minute rule: write a persona for every role that has started or stopped one of your last ten deals, and no others. For most small companies that is two.

**Never checked against losses.** A persona validated only against closed-won deals describes the people who already liked you. The veto row exists so the lost deals get a vote.

**Used as a timing tool.** A perfect persona match can still be two years from buying. The persona says what to say; it does not say when. That is what the public-evidence row and the [LinkedIn buying signals](/blog/linkedin-buying-signals) that fill it are for.

## How to use a buyer persona this week

Three actions, in order.

1. **Fill the seven-row table for the role that signed your last deal.** From memory and call notes if that is all you have. Read the veto row out loud to whoever writes your outreach.
2. **Rewrite one message from the persona's rows.** Take the first line of your current outreach and replace it with the trigger row in the buyer's words, then the objection you are answering. The guide to [writing a LinkedIn outreach message](/guides/write-linkedin-outreach-message) shows the structure; the persona supplies the content.
3. **Watch for the public-evidence row.** Set a weekly half hour to check the matching profiles at your target companies for the posts, comments and job ads you listed. Intentsly's [LinkedIn signals](/features/linkedin-signals) run this step continuously, catching the posts and engagement in your public-evidence row and scoring each person against the profile you give it, so the list arrives sorted. When we launched, we aimed our own signals at one persona, founders posting about cold-outreach burnout, and booked 14 qualified demos in 14 days with zero manual outreach. The persona was narrow, so every reply was worth taking.

## FAQ

### What is an example of a B2B buyer persona?

A one-sentence version: "Heads of sales at 20 to 80 person software companies, judged on forecast accuracy, who start looking after a missed quarter and kill any plan that takes reps out of the CRM." The full version adds who else decides, the first two objections, and what this person posts or hires for when the problem is live.

### What is the difference between a buyer persona and an ideal customer profile?

An ideal customer profile describes the type of company worth pursuing: industry, size, situation, disqualifiers. A buyer persona describes one role inside that company: what they are measured on, what triggers the search, what they object to and when they say no. You use the profile to choose accounts and the persona to choose the person and the first line.

### How many buyer personas should a B2B company have?

One for every role that has started or stopped one of your last ten deals, and none for roles that only sat in meetings. For most companies under 200 people that is two: the person who feels the problem and the person who signs. Add a third only when a role has killed a deal on its own at least twice.

### Do buyer personas need a name and a photo?

No. A name and a stock photo come from consumer marketing, where the buyer is one person spending their own money. In B2B the useful rows are the seat, the measure, the trigger, the objections and the veto, all of which you can check against a real call. A name you invented cannot be checked, so leave it out.

### What is a negative buyer persona?

A negative persona is a role that looks like a buyer and is not: someone who takes every demo and never has budget, or a user who loves the product but cannot get the signer to care. Rather than writing a separate document, record these in the "says no when" row of the persona they resemble, so the person doing outreach sees the trap next to the target.
