**Cold outreach is a sales message sent to a specific person who has never interacted with you or your company, with the aim of starting a conversation.** That is the whole answer to "what is cold outreach": the contact is cold because the buyer did not ask for it, whatever channel carries it. A cold email, a cold call, a LinkedIn connection note to a stranger and a letter in the post are all cold outreach. A reply to someone who filled in your form is not.

## Why does cold outreach matter?

Cold outreach matters because it is the only way to reach a buyer who does not yet know you exist, and in any market that is most of them. Inbound brings you the people already searching; cold outreach reaches the ones who have the problem but have not started looking.

It also carries the highest cost of getting it wrong. In a Gartner survey of 632 B2B buyers run in August and September 2024, 73% said they actively avoid suppliers who send irrelevant outreach ([Gartner, 25 June 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience)). A bad cold message costs more than the reply it did not get: it removes that buyer from your future pipeline.

A labelled example (hypothetical, not a customer): the founder of a six-person payroll consultancy wants restaurant groups as clients and has no referrals in that niche. She writes to the finance lead at 30 of them, one paragraph each, naming the payroll rule that changed for hospitality this year. Four reply, two take a call, one signs. None had heard of her the week before. That is cold outreach working as intended.

## What counts as cold outreach, and what is spam?

Both are unsolicited. The difference is a test you can apply before you send: **could this message only have been written for this one person?** If the recipient's name and company could be swapped for anyone else's without changing a word, it is spam, whatever the sender calls it. If the message depends on something true about this person (their role, a post they wrote, a hire they made, a tool they use), it is cold outreach.

The law draws its own line, and for email in the United States it applies to B2B senders too. The FTC's CAN-SPAM compliance guide states that the law "makes no exception for business-to-business email" and lists what every commercial email must do: no false header information, no deceptive subject lines, identify the message as an ad, include a physical address, tell recipients how to opt out, and honour opt-out requests promptly. Each separate email in violation is subject to penalties of up to $53,088 ([FTC, CAN-SPAM Act: A Compliance Guide for Business](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)). Other countries set a higher bar, so check the rules where your recipient sits, not where you do.

Platforms add their own limits. LinkedIn restricts accounts that send too many connection requests and messages, which is why Intentsly caps every connected account at 30 messages and 30 connection requests a day and recommends 15. The [guide to warming up a LinkedIn account](/guides/warm-up-linkedin-account-for-outreach) covers how to stay inside those lines by hand.

## What are the main cold outreach channels?

Four channels carry almost all B2B cold outreach. Two have published benchmarks from large datasets, which tell you what "normal" looks like before you judge your own numbers.

**Cold email.** The most common channel because it scales and costs almost nothing per message. Hunter analysed 11 million cold emails sent in 2024 and found an average reply rate of 4.1%. Campaigns sent to 50 or fewer recipients averaged 5.8%, campaigns sent to 1,000 or more averaged 2.1%, and campaigns with follow-ups averaged 4.9% against 3% without ([Hunter, The State of Cold Email 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)). Smaller, more specific lists reply more.

**Cold calling.** The fastest way to a real conversation and the most expensive per conversation. Belkins logged 175,000+ dials across 2025 and found that 9.9% of dials reached a live person, 4.6% of those conversations booked a meeting, and it took roughly 370 dials to book one ([Belkins, cold calling benchmarks](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-calling-benchmarks)). Calling works when the list is tight and fails as a volume play.

**LinkedIn messages and connection notes.** The recipient sees who you are before replying, so a thin profile kills the message before it is read. Daily caps keep volume low by design. The [guide to writing a LinkedIn outreach message](/guides/write-linkedin-outreach-message) has the structure.

**Direct mail and SMS.** Rare in B2B. Direct mail suits a short list of high-value accounts. SMS to a business contact who never gave you their number reads as intrusive and in many places needs consent first.

Most teams run two channels in a sequence: an email, then a LinkedIn note that refers to it, then a call if the account is worth it.

## Cold outreach vs warm outreach vs signal-based outreach

Three terms get mixed up. They differ in what the buyer did before you wrote, and that one fact decides how the message should read and what reply rate to expect.

| | Cold outreach | Warm outreach | Signal-based outreach |
|---|---|---|---|
| What the buyer did first | Nothing involving you | Followed, subscribed, met you or was referred | Did something public that shows timing: commented on a competitor's post, posted about the problem, changed role |
| Who they know | Not you | You or your company | Not you, but you know what they just did |
| Where the list comes from | A database filtered by firmographics | Your CRM, followers, events, referrals | Monitoring of posts, engagement, hashtags and competitor pages |
| What the first line refers to | Their company or role | Your shared history | The thing they just did |
| Volume available | Large | Small, limited by your network | Medium, limited by how much your market says in public |
| Fails when | The list is broad and the copy generic | You run out of warm names | You act too late or on the wrong signal |

The one-minute decision rule: if you have a warm name, use it; a shared history beats everything else. If you have no warm names but your buyers talk about their problems in public, start with signals, because a message that refers to what someone wrote yesterday is cold in the legal sense and warm in the reader's mind. If your buyers are silent in public, go cold, keep each campaign under 50 people, and follow up.

Signal-based outreach is the column most definitions leave out. A [buying signal](/learn/what-is-a-buying-signal) is a public action by a named person that suggests timing, and a [trigger event](/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales) is the company-level version. When we launched Intentsly we ran our own LinkedIn signals and AI SDR against founders complaining about cold outreach, and booked 14 qualified demos in 14 days with zero manual outreach and a reply rate 4x our previous cold campaigns, mostly from signals caught in real time (see [case studies](/case-studies)). The messages still went to strangers. Every one of them referred to something the recipient had just said.

## Does cold outreach still work?

Cold outreach still works, at the rates above and under two conditions: the list is narrow enough that every message can be specific, and the sender follows up. The Hunter data shows the cost of ignoring either: reply rates fall by more than half between a 50-person campaign and a 1,000-person one, and by nearly 40% without a follow-up ([Hunter, The State of Cold Email 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)).

What has stopped working is cold outreach as a numbers game. The same Gartner survey found that 61% of B2B buyers prefer an overall rep-free buying experience ([Gartner, 25 June 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience)). A buyer who would rather not talk to a rep at all makes an exception only for one who clearly knows their situation.

The honest limit is time. A specific message to one person takes ten to twenty minutes of reading first, and a founder running outbound alongside everything else cannot do that for 200 people a month. That is the step where [Intentsly's LinkedIn signals](/features/linkedin-signals) do the reading for you, surfacing the people in your market who just engaged with a competitor or posted about the problem, so the first line is already there.

## How to use cold outreach this week

Three actions, each under an hour:

1. **Write down who you are allowed to contact.** One paragraph: company size, role, the problem they have, and one public sign that they have it. This is your [ideal customer profile](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile), and it decides whether a message can pass the one-person test above.
2. **Build a list of 25, not 250.** Pick 25 people who match the paragraph and for whom you can find one specific fact each: a post, a hire, a tool, an announcement. Write the first line of each message from that fact before anything else.
3. **Schedule the follow-up before any replies arrive.** One follow-up three to four working days later that adds something new (an example, a question) rather than "just bumping this". Log replies by channel so that in two weeks you know which channel earns your time.

If step two is where your week stalls, the free [Buying Signal Playbook](/signal-playbook) lists 12 signals worth chasing and 8 to ignore, with a script for each, so the specific fact in every message comes from what the buyer just did rather than an hour of searching.

## FAQ

### What is cold outreach in simple terms?
Cold outreach is contacting a specific person who has never heard from you, to start a sales conversation. The channel does not matter: a cold email, a cold call, a LinkedIn message to a stranger and a posted letter all count. What makes it cold is that the buyer did not ask for the contact and has no prior relationship with you or your company.

### Is cold outreach the same as spam?
No, although both are unsolicited. Spam is the same message sent to many people regardless of who they are. Cold outreach is written for one person and depends on something true about them, such as their role, a post they wrote or a hire they made. A simple test: if swapping the recipient's name for anyone else's would change nothing, it is spam.

### Is cold outreach legal?
In the United States, cold email is legal when it follows the CAN-SPAM Act, which the FTC says applies to business-to-business email as well: accurate headers and subject lines, identification as an ad, a physical address, a working opt-out that is honoured promptly. Other countries, including the EU and the UK, set stricter rules, so check the law where your recipient is based.

### What is a good reply rate for cold outreach?
[Hunter's analysis of 11 million cold emails](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025) sent in 2024 put the average reply rate at 4.1%, with campaigns of 50 or fewer recipients averaging 5.8%. For cold calls, [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-calling-benchmarks) found 9.9% of dials reached a person and about 370 dials were needed per meeting. Across Intentsly customers in October 2026, AI SDR LinkedIn outreach based on signals averaged a 21% reply rate.

### What is the difference between cold outreach and warm outreach?
Warm outreach goes to someone who has already interacted with you: a follower, a subscriber, someone you met or who was referred to you. Cold outreach goes to someone with no such history. Signal-based outreach sits between them: the buyer does not know you, but you know what they just did in public, and the message refers to it.

### Does cold outreach still work in 2026?
Yes, for narrow lists with specific messages and a follow-up. The published benchmarks show reply rates falling sharply as campaign size grows and when follow-ups are skipped. [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience) found 73% of B2B buyers actively avoid suppliers who send irrelevant outreach, so the version that still works is the researched one, or one where buying signals supply the research.
