> - Each of those moments is a LinkedIn post you can search for; the table below gives the search and the message for all seven.

The fastest way to get clients for a PR agency is to reach a company inside its launch window: the weeks after a funding round, before a launch, after a new marketing leader arrives, or after a founder posts that forty journalists ignored their pitch. Those are the moments a company decides it needs press but has not chosen who to call, and all appear on LinkedIn. Message them within a day, quote the post, offer one piece of work, and you are in before the long list exists.

## Why does the usual PR agency advice stall?

Every page on how to get clients for a PR agency recommends the same set: niche down, publish case studies, post thought leadership, ask for referrals, go to events, build a referral programme. None of it is wrong, and most of it waits for the buyer to come to you. The buyers are coming less often by those routes. RSW/US's 2026 outlook, reported by [Agility PR in January 2026, found that 73% of clients said in 2022 they learned about new agencies through networking, 67% through past relationships and 60% through friends or co-workers; by 2025 those figures were 58%, 48% and 35%](https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-agency-news/new-rsw-us-2026-outlook-report-finds-referrals-and-networking-losing-effectiveness-agencies-must-take-a-more-proactive-approach-to-new-business/).

The pipeline side looks the same. In RSW/US's September 2025 survey of marketing and professional services firms, [only 7% said their pipeline was strong and growing, 50% called it weak but improving, and 75% named referrals as their best lead source](https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-agency-news/pipeline-crisis-93-of-marketing-services-and-professional-services-firms-say-their-growth-engine-isnt-strong-enough/). An agency whose best channel is the one it cannot schedule has a pipeline it cannot plan. The MarketingProfs write-up of the August and September 2026 RSW/US survey adds that [48% of marketing-services executives found new business harder in 2026 than in 2025, and three quarters of those blamed the difficulty of breaking through to new prospects](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2026/56424/the-new-business-outlook-for-marketing-agencies).

Breaking through is a timing problem, not a volume problem. A company that is not launching anything does not want a PR agency this quarter, however good the credentials deck. A company that raised money last week wants one and has not picked it. A [trigger event](/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales) is a public change that makes a company a buyer now; PR has more of them than almost any other service, because announcing things is the product.

## Where do PR agency clients show up on LinkedIn?

Each row is a moment you can find with a free LinkedIn account. The search column is what to type into the search bar before switching to the Posts tab (or the Jobs tab, where stated) and filtering to the past week. The message column quotes the post and offers one piece of work, never a capabilities deck.

| Signal | Where | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A funding announcement ("we raised", "excited to announce our Series A") | Posts tab: `"we raised" seed` or `"announce" "Series A"` plus your sector words, past week | The round itself is a story with a short shelf life, investors expect coverage, and the hiring plan needs an employer story; nobody has been briefed yet | Wait a day for the congratulations, then message the founder with one angle the announcement missed (the customer story, the category point) and offer a 20-minute call on what the round can still earn in coverage |
| A pre-launch post ("launching next month", "something big coming", "we're going live on Product Hunt") | Posts tab: `"launching" "next month" founder` or `"coming soon" "we're launching"` or `"Product Hunt" "next week"`, past week | A launch date exists, a press list probably does not, and the founder is planning to do the pitching personally | Reply with one specific journalist or outlet that covered a comparable launch, then offer a launch-week media plan with a fixed scope |
| A new marketing or comms leader ("excited to join X as CMO", "new role as Head of Communications") | Posts tab: `"excited to join" "head of communications"` or `"new role" CMO`; also job-change notifications from your connections | A first-90-days plan and a review of every supplier; the previous agency has no relationship with this person | Congratulate them, ask what the first priority is, then offer a one-page read of how the company currently shows up in the press and in AI answers |
| A comms hire (job post for PR manager, communications manager or head of comms) or "we're hiring our first comms lead" | Jobs tab: "PR manager" or "communications manager", filtered by company size and region; Posts tab: `"hiring" "communications lead"` | PR is already a budget line and the first announcements cannot wait the months a hire takes | Message the hiring manager and offer to carry media relations while the role is open, or to work alongside the new hire on launches |
| A recommendation request ("anyone know a good PR agency for a B2B launch?", "looking for a PR firm") | Posts tab: `"PR agency" recommend` or `"looking for a" "PR firm"` or `"PR firm" "any recommendations"`, past week | A shortlist is forming in the comments the same day and the budget already exists | Reply in the thread with one scoping question (launch date, markets, who decides), then connect with a note that quotes the post and offers a 15-minute call |
| A "the press is ignoring us" post ("pitched 40 journalists, no reply", "how do startups actually get coverage?") | Posts tab: `"journalists" "no response" founder` or `"get press" startup "how do"` or `pitched "zero replies"`, past week | The founder has tried to do PR alone, named the failure in public and is testing whether anyone can do better | Reply with one practical fix they can use today (the subject line, the timing, the angle), connect, and mention what you do only in the second message |
| A thought-leadership resolution ("I'm going to post every week this year", "speaking at SaaStr next month", "we won the award") | Posts tab: `"speaking at" conference founder` or `"post every" week "this year"` or `"we won" award`, past month | The CEO wants to be visible and has just made that public; executive profiling is the PR service with the shortest sales cycle | Reply with one angle their talk or win could earn in trade press, then offer a profiling sprint built around the event |

One PR post is too late to chase: the coverage announcement ("we're in TechCrunch", "featured in Forbes this morning"). By the time a company posts its coverage, the agency has been chosen and thanked in the comments. The post is still worth reading for two reasons: the comments tell you which agency won and what the buyer praised, and the founders congratulating the company are often the ones who want the same coverage next quarter. The [competitor-monitoring routine](/guides/monitor-competitors-on-linkedin) turns a rival's coverage posts into a list of those people.

> [!WARNING]
> A company in a live crisis (a breach, a recall, a public resignation) is a signal, and it is the one not to chase. A message that arrives mid-crisis reads as ambulance chasing, and the comment thread is watched. If crisis communications is your service, be visible before the crisis: the comms leader will search for who was useful last month.

Two more filters keep the list honest. A signal older than a month is cold for launch work, because the launch has usually happened. And a post from another agency or a freelance publicist asking "who is launching this year?" is not a buyer, however many comments it collects. Reddit and X carry the same requests, and the [Reddit queries](/blog/reddit-search-queries-to-find-customers) have the phrasing; treat them as manual channels, because Intentsly monitors LinkedIn only.

## How to get clients for a PR agency by hand: a week-one plan

The plan takes about an hour a day with a free LinkedIn account. Sales Navigator makes the company-size filter and the job-change alerts faster but is not required.

1. **Day 1: write the one-sentence offer and list the voices.** Pick the company type, the PR work and the region, for example "launch and funding announcements for B2B software companies of 20 to 200 people in the DACH region". Then list ten people your buyers follow (venture investors in your niche, trade journalists who post on LinkedIn, founders known for building in public) and five agencies you lose pitches to. The [ideal customer profile worksheet](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) is the longer version of this step.
2. **Day 2: build the saved searches.** Create the seven Posts-tab searches from the table with your sector words added, each filtered to the past week, plus one Jobs search for "PR manager" or "communications manager" at companies in your region. Bookmark them. The [funding-round guide](/guides/find-companies-that-just-raised-funding) and the [job-change routine](/guides/track-job-changes-on-linkedin-for-sales) go deeper on the two signals with the most volume.
3. **Days 3 to 5: thirty minutes of reading, thirty of replying.** Run the searches, read the new posts, and for each real buyer do the "what to do" step from the table. Reply in public first, connect second. Send at most 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day; LinkedIn tolerates more, your acceptance rate does not.
4. **Day 6: follow up and find the budget owner.** For every accepted connection with no reply, send one message that adds something: a journalist who covered a comparable launch, a question about the announcement date. Never "just checking in". Where the person who posted is not the budget owner, the [decision-maker guide](/guides/find-decision-makers-on-linkedin) has the six steps to find who signs.
5. **Day 7: count.** Signals found, replies, calls booked. Fewer than ten relevant posts in a week means the sector words are wrong or your buyers are quiet on LinkedIn; widen the search terms before changing the niche.

Message template for the funding signal, for copying:

```
Congratulations on the round. One thing I noticed: the
announcement led with the number, and the customer story
underneath it is the part trade press would actually run.
If useful, I can send three angles the raise can still earn
over the next six weeks, no call needed. When is the next
announcement due?
```

Finding the right fifteen people each day is where the hour goes; if you want that step done for you, the [LinkedIn signals feature](/features/linkedin-signals) runs the same searches continuously and hands you the list already scored against your customer profile.

## Why is a launch the moment a company buys PR?

Knowing why buyers move explains why the signals above work. The founder who tries to do PR alone meets the same wall every agency knows. In Muck Rack's State of PR 2026, a survey of 971 PR professionals reported by [Bizcommunity in July 2026, 71% named low response rates from journalists as the biggest obstacle to earned media, 61% pointed to shrinking media lists, and 49% still pitch more than 20 journalists per announcement](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/muck-rack-finds-journalists-are-harder-to-reach-despite-ai-advances-620831a). A founder with no relationships and one announcement gets the same silence, which is what the "pitched 40 journalists" post is reporting.

The same survey explains the thought-leadership signal: [60% of PR professionals named LinkedIn the most valuable social platform for their work, and 51% said thought leadership accounts for at least a quarter of their role](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/muck-rack-finds-journalists-are-harder-to-reach-despite-ai-advances-620831a). On the agency side, IPRN's April 2026 survey of its member agencies in 22 countries found [67% forecasting growth, 19% naming CEO profiling and thought leadership as a primary opportunity, and 22% naming corporate reputation; the technology sector led growth prospects at 16%](https://iprn.com/iprn-public-relations-agencies-forecast-growth-for-2026/). A CEO who has just announced a speaking slot or a weekly posting habit is that opportunity, at a company of any size.

## Other ways to get PR agency clients, and when they beat signals

Signals produce conversations with strangers who are in-market now. Three other routes earn their place, and for most agencies one of them comes first.

**Referrals and past clients** beat signals for the first few clients and for the largest. RSW/US is right that a referral is the best lead when it arrives and the worst plan when it does not; [75% of firms in its September 2025 survey named referrals their best source, and only 7% had a strong pipeline](https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-agency-news/pipeline-crisis-93-of-marketing-services-and-professional-services-firms-say-their-growth-engine-isnt-strong-enough/). Ask at the moment the coverage lands, name the introduction you want, and keep the relationship with the person rather than the company, because the marketing leader who hired you will change jobs and take the budget along.

**Partnerships with investors, accelerators and other agencies** beat signals when you would rather pitch journalists than prospects. A fund's portfolio announces rounds on a schedule you can see, and an accelerator's demo day is a launch window for a whole cohort. Branding and marketing agencies win clients who need a story before the campaign can start, and they have no media team; the [branding agency page](/solutions/get-clients-for-a-branding-agency) and the [marketing agency page](/solutions/find-clients-for-a-marketing-agency) show what those partners are chasing, which is the same list of signals from the other side. The cost is margin and a client relationship you do not own.

**Inbound, content and AI visibility** beat signals when buyers in your market search for "PR agency" plus a city or a sector, which is true for consumer brands and much less true for B2B, where the founder asks an investor instead. The new version of inbound is being found in AI answers: [15% of IPRN's member agencies already generate business from generative engine optimisation and 41% are testing it](https://iprn.com/iprn-public-relations-agencies-forecast-growth-for-2026/). Case studies and sector commentary compound slowly and keep working while you sleep; pair them with the plan above rather than waiting for them. ## FAQ

### How do PR agencies get clients?
Mostly through referrals, partnerships with investors and other agencies, inbound from case studies and commentary, and outbound to companies inside a launch window. Referrals bring the biggest retainers but cannot be scheduled; outbound can, provided each message quotes something the company just announced (a round, a launch date, a new comms leader) and offers one piece of work rather than a capabilities deck.

### How do I get my first PR client with no case studies?
Pick one sector and one type of PR work, then run the seven searches above and reply in public before asking for anything. A founder who has just posted that forty journalists ignored them does not check how long you have been trading; they check whether your reply fixed the pitch. Offer a scoped first project, such as a launch-week media plan or a funding-announcement angle sheet, before proposing a retainer.

### Does cold outreach work for PR agencies?
It works when the message is tied to a visible change and offers one specific deliverable, such as three angles after a funding round or a media plan before a launch. It fails when it is a generic introduction sent to anyone with "founder" in their title, and it fails loudly when it arrives during a crisis. Expect weeks, not days: PR decisions involve the founder, the marketing lead and often the investors.

### Should a PR agency niche down?
For outreach, yes, because the searches only work with your buyers' words in them: "we raised" returns every funded company on earth, "we raised" plus "fintech" plus "Berlin" returns yours. Niche by buyer and type of work first (launch and funding PR for B2B software, say), industry second. A general client can still find you through a referral; you cannot search for one.

### What do startups look for in a PR agency?
Proof that you know their sector's journalists, a scope they can understand, and a reporting method they can show an investor. Lead with the angle their last announcement missed, name two outlets that covered a comparable company, and put scope, timeline and measurement on one page. A founder who has tried pitching alone already knows the problem; they are deciding whether you can do it faster.

### How long does it take to land a PR retainer this way?
Week one should produce replies and a scoping call if your niche posts regularly on LinkedIn. A company that announced a round, a launch date or a new marketing leader this month is usually choosing help within the quarter, so count weekly and expect a scoped first project (a launch plan, an announcement, a profiling sprint) before a retainer.
