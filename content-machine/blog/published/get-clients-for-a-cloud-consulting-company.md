> - Cloud consulting is bought at a decision point: a new technical leader, a funding round, a migration someone has just announced, a bill nobody can explain, a customer asking where the data lives.
> - Seven of those moments are visible on LinkedIn with a free account; the table below gives the search and the message for each.
> - Run the week-one plan by hand for an hour a day, or let Intentsly watch LinkedIn and open the conversations for you.

The fastest way to get clients for a cloud consulting company is to reach the technical leader in the week a platform decision opens: a new VP of Engineering in her first month, a founder posting that the company is leaving its data centre, a CTO asking why the cloud bill doubled. All three post on LinkedIn before the decision is made. Message them within a day with one observation about their setup, not a service list, and you are the first consultancy in the room.

## Why does the usual cloud consulting advice stall?

Most pages on how to get clients for a cloud consulting company give the same list: define the ideal customer, run outbound to IT directors, join the AWS or Azure partner programme, list on the marketplace, publish technical content, ask for referrals. None of it is wrong, and the pages ranking for it are written by lead-generation agencies, a sales data vendor, a phone system company and a project-management tool. The problem is timing. A partner-programme lead arrives when the hyperscaler's rep chooses to share it, and a referral arrives when a past client happens to know someone deciding this quarter.

The spend is there. Gartner's April 2026 forecast puts [worldwide IT spending at $6.31 trillion in 2026, up 13.5%, with IT services past $1.87 trillion and data center systems up 55.8% on hyperscale demand](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-04-22-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-it-spending-to-grow-13-point-5-percent-in-2026-totaling-6-point-31-trillion-dollars). The pain is there too: in Flexera's 2026 State of the Cloud survey of more than 750 cloud decision-makers, [85% called managing cloud spend a top challenge and wasted cloud spend rose to 29%, the first increase in five years, while 81% said they are using generative AI](https://www.flexera.com/about-us/press-center/flexera-finds-cloud-value-is-rising-while-ai-waste-grows).

What a consultancy usually lacks is the moment: the quarter in which one specific company decides to migrate, re-platform, bring the bill under control or move data into a region. A buying signal is a public action that marks that moment; the [trigger event lesson](/learn/what-is-a-trigger-event-in-sales) explains the idea, and for cloud work the triggers are unusually public, because technical leaders announce new roles, migrations and post-mortems on LinkedIn as a matter of habit.

## Where do cloud consulting clients show up on LinkedIn?

Each row is a moment you can find with a free LinkedIn account. The search column is what to type into the search bar before switching to the Posts tab (or the Jobs tab, where stated) and filtering to the past week. The last column is the message, written to quote the signal and offer one scoped piece of work, not a capabilities deck.

| Signal | Where | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A new CTO, VP of Engineering or Head of Infrastructure starting ("excited to join as VP of Engineering", "starting a new position as CTO", "new role: Head of Platform") | Posts tab: `"new position" CTO` or `"excited to join" "VP of Engineering"` or `"Head of Platform" "new role"`, past week; job-change alerts on saved leads | New technical leaders review the platform in the first quarter, usually find a cost, reliability or lock-in problem, and own a budget they have not spent yet | Congratulate, ask one question about what they inherited, and offer a two-week review of one workload as a second opinion for their 90-day plan |
| A first cloud, platform or infrastructure job post at a company with no platform team ("Cloud Engineer", "Platform Engineer", "Site Reliability Engineer", "FinOps Analyst") | Jobs tab: "Platform Engineer" or "Cloud Engineer" or "FinOps", filtered to companies under about 500 people; check the People tab for existing infrastructure titles | The work exists before the hire does; a role that stays open for months is a backlog the engineering lead is carrying between other jobs | Message the hiring manager with a fixed-scope engagement that clears the backlog until the hire starts, and hand over documentation on day one |
| A funding round with an infrastructure hiring plan ("we raised our Series A", "closed our Series B", "hiring across engineering and infrastructure") | Posts tab: `"we raised" "Series A"` or `"closed our Series B" engineering`, past week; the [funding signal guide](/guides/find-companies-that-just-raised-funding) covers the other sources | The platform built for ten engineers has to carry forty, and the board expects a cost line and a security posture an enterprise customer will accept | Congratulate, ask what the platform needs to look like in a year, and offer a landing-zone or account-structure review scoped to the hiring plan |
| A migration or modernisation announced in public ("we're moving to AWS", "leaving our data centre", "migrating off Heroku", "going all-in on Azure", "our multi-cloud strategy") | Posts tab: `"migrating to" AWS` or `"data center" leaving cloud` or `"moving off" Heroku`, past week | The decision is made and the plan is not; the first ninety days of a migration set the account structure, the network and the bill for years | Reply in the thread with one specific question about sequencing or the first workload, connect with a note that quotes the post, and offer a migration plan review |
| A cloud-bill post ("our AWS bill doubled", "cloud costs are out of control", "reserved instances", "savings plans", "egress fees") | Posts tab: `"AWS bill"` or `"cloud costs" doubled` or `"savings plans" confused`, past week | Someone is already explaining the bill to a CFO and would rather an outside party found the waste than a finance team did | Offer a fixed-fee cost review of one account with a written list of findings; lead with one observation about the service they named |
| A data residency, sovereignty or compliance requirement ("customers asking where their data lives", "EU data residency", "sovereign cloud", "SOC 2 audit next quarter", "ISO 27001") | Posts tab: `"data residency" customers` or `"sovereign cloud" we` or `"SOC 2" "next quarter"`, past week | A sales deal or a regulator is forcing an architecture decision with a date on it, and the engineering team has not done one before | Offer a region and account design review with the controls mapped to the requirement; the deadline is the budget |
| Someone asking for a partner by name ("can anyone recommend an AWS partner for a migration?", "looking for a Terraform contractor", "need an Azure consultancy") | Posts tab: `recommend "AWS partner"` or `"Terraform" contractor looking` or `"Azure" consultancy recommend`, past week | An open evaluation with the shortlist forming in the comments the same day | Answer in the thread with one usable point about their situation, connect, and offer a scoped first engagement; the thread closes in days |

Two filters keep the list honest. A signal older than three weeks is cold: the leader has either picked a partner, hired the engineer, or pushed the migration to next year. And a post from another consultancy asking "who needs cloud help?" is not a buyer, nor is an engineer venting about a bill she does not own. The sixth row is the one most consultancies have no search for. Gartner forecasts [sovereign cloud IaaS spending of $80 billion in 2026, up 35.6%, with Europe growing 83% and set to pass North America in 2027, and says 80% of that spend will come from net new digital solutions or legacy workloads waiting to be migrated](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-09-gartner-says-worldwide-sovereign-cloud-iaas-spending-will-total-us-dollars-80-billion-in-2026). A CTO whose enterprise customer just asked where the data lives is making a platform decision with a contract date attached, and the consultancy that answers the post first gets to design it. ## How to get clients for a cloud consulting company by hand: a week-one plan

The plan takes about an hour a day with a free LinkedIn account. Sales Navigator makes the job-change and job-post steps faster but is not required.

1. **Day 1: write the one-sentence offer and list the voices.** Pick the company and the platform, for example "software companies of 50 to 500 people on AWS with no platform team and an enterprise customer base". Then list ten infrastructure tool vendors, hyperscaler evangelists and engineering authors your buyers follow, and five consultancies that compete with you. The [ideal customer profile lesson](/learn/what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile) is the longer version of this step.
2. **Day 2: build the saved searches.** Create the Posts-tab searches from the table with your platform words added (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, Kubernetes, Terraform), each filtered to the past week, plus two Jobs searches: one for platform and cloud engineering roles, one for FinOps and infrastructure leadership roles. Bookmark them. The [hiring-signals routine](/guides/track-hiring-signals-for-sales) goes deeper on the job-post signal, and the [job-change method](/guides/track-job-changes-on-linkedin-for-sales) covers the new-leader signal.
3. **Days 3 to 5: thirty minutes of reading, thirty of replying.** Run the searches, read the new posts and listings, and for each real buyer do the "what to do" step from the table. Reply in public first, connect second. Send at most 15 connection requests and 15 messages a day; LinkedIn tolerates more, your acceptance rate does not.
4. **Day 6: follow up and find the budget owner.** For every accepted connection with no reply, send one message that adds something: a question about the first workload, a line about the region they mentioned. Never "just checking in". Where the person who posted is an engineer rather than the leader, the [decision-maker guide](/guides/find-decision-makers-on-linkedin) has the steps to find who signs.
5. **Day 7: count.** Signals found, replies, calls booked. Fewer than ten relevant posts and listings in a week means the platform words are wrong or your buyers are quiet on LinkedIn; widen the search terms before changing the niche.

Message template for the new technical leader, for copying:

```
Congratulations on the VP of Engineering role. The first thing
most new leaders find is that the account structure was set up
for the team of five, and the bill and the permissions show it.
If it would help, I can spend 30 minutes on what you inherited
and where a two-week review of one workload would pay back
fastest. No deck, no proposal.
```

```message
Saw your post about leaving the data centre next year. Which workload goes first, and has anyone priced the egress yet?
```

Finding the right fifteen people each day is where the hour goes; if you want that step done for you, the [LinkedIn signals feature](/features/linkedin-signals) runs the same post searches continuously and hands you the list already scored against your customer profile.

## Other ways to get cloud consulting clients, and when they beat signals

Signals produce conversations with strangers. Three other routes earn their place, and for most consultancies one of them comes first.

**Hyperscaler partner programmes and marketplaces** beat signals once you have the certifications and a few named references, because the buyer who is already on the platform searching for implementation help has a budget and sometimes credits to spend. The cost is control: the rep decides which partner gets the lead, the tier requirements take time, and every other consultancy in your region is in the same programme. Run it alongside the plan above rather than instead of it.

**Referrals and past clients** beat signals once you have shipped a few migrations. A platform lead you worked with becomes CTO somewhere else and brings you in; an engineer who used your landing zone recommends you when her new company raises. Keep a list of every client contact and watch their job changes, because this network compounds and a cold list never does. It pays in year three, not month three.

**Subcontracting for larger consultancies and ISVs** beats signals when you are small and the deals are large. A systems integrator with an enterprise migration needs a team for the Kubernetes work or the data layer; a software vendor needs a partner to deploy its product in customers' accounts. The margin is lower and the relationship is not yours, which is why most firms use it to fill gaps rather than to build a pipeline.

Cold lists are the one tactic signals replace outright. Almost every company with fifty engineers could use cloud help in theory, which is why a scraped list of them converts so badly; a new-leader post, a job listing or a migration announcement says which of them has decided to spend this quarter. ## FAQ

### Who should a cloud consulting company target first?
The technical leader at a company that has just changed something: a new CTO or VP of Engineering, a funding round with an engineering hiring plan, or an announced migration. Those companies have a decision open and a budget attached. A company of 50 to 500 people with no platform team is usually the fastest to close, because the engineering lead is doing the infrastructure work between other jobs.

### How do you find companies that still run on-premises or on legacy infrastructure?
Search LinkedIn posts for the announcement rather than the state. Phrases such as "leaving our data centre", "migrating to AWS" or "our Azure migration" mark companies that have decided to move, and job posts for a first cloud or platform engineer mark companies about to. The [technographic data lesson](/learn/what-is-technographic-data) covers the tools that list a company's current stack when you want to build a list from the other direction.

### What is the best first offer for a cloud consultancy?
A fixed-scope, fixed-fee review of one thing: one workload's migration plan, one account's cost, one region design for a residency requirement. It takes two weeks, ends in a written document the buyer keeps, and answers the question they posted. Larger engagements follow once the review has shown them something their own team missed.

### Do AWS or Azure partner programmes bring cloud consulting clients?
Yes, once you have the certifications, the tier and a few named references, and the leads tend to be real because the buyer is already on the platform. They arrive on the hyperscaler's schedule, not yours, and every partner in your region competes for them. Treat the programme as one channel beside signal outreach and referrals, not as the pipeline.

### How do you qualify a cloud consulting lead before a call?
Four things, in the thread or the first message: a named workload or account, a date (a lease ending, an audit, a customer contract, a hire starting), a person who owns the budget, and the trigger that started it. A post that names the workload and the date is already half qualified; a post that only complains about the bill needs the other three before it is worth a proposal.

### How long is the sales cycle for cloud consulting?
Weeks for a scoped review bought by a technical leader who posted a signal this month; quarters for an enterprise migration that goes through procurement. The week-one plan is built for the first kind, which funds the second: a two-week review that finds something the team missed is the usual route into the migration that follows.
